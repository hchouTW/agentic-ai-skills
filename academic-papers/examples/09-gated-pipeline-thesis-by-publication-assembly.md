## Phase 1: Input Extraction & Gap Formulation

Per `references/thesis-by-publication-assembly.md` point 1, the graduate
school's current thesis-by-publication requirements were retrieved rather
than assumed: a minimum of three included papers, a mandatory overarching
introduction and conclusion, an explicit per-chapter contribution statement,
and copyright-permission documentation for any verbatim reuse.

Gaps found extracting the candidate's three papers against those
requirements:
- Paper A is published (journal holds copyright); Paper B is accepted but not
  yet in print; Paper C is still under review at a second journal.
- No per-chapter contribution statement exists yet for Paper A, which has
  four co-authors.
- Reuse permission for Paper A's published figures has not been checked
  against that specific publisher's self-archiving policy.

**Gate:** proceed to Phase 2 only once each paper's exact status (published/
accepted/under review) is labeled, a contribution statement draft exists for
every multi-author paper, and Paper A's publisher's copyright-transfer
agreement has actually been read rather than assumed to permit reuse.

## Phase 2: Draft Synthesis

Checked Paper A's copyright-transfer agreement directly per point 2: the
publisher's self-archiving policy permits reuse of the accepted manuscript
version (not the publisher-typeset final version) in a thesis, so the
assembled chapter uses that version with a footnote citing the published
DOI. Drafted the overarching Introduction and Conclusion per point 3, using
`references/literature-review.md`'s synthesis-vs-listing guidance: the
Introduction states the thesis's unifying question (how does detector
mis-calibration propagate into a measured cross-section's systematic budget)
and how each of the three papers contributes a distinct piece of that
question, rather than three independent summaries; the Conclusion
synthesizes across all three papers' results into one combined statement
about calibration-driven systematics, not three restated abstracts. Drafted
per-chapter contribution statements per point 4, sourced from an email from
each co-author confirming the candidate's specific role, not inferred from
author-list order.

**Gate:** proceed to Phase 3 only once every chapter's copyright/reuse status
is documented with its actual source (publisher policy or explicit
permission), every contribution statement is confirmed by the relevant
co-authors rather than drafted unilaterally, and each chapter is labeled with
its real publication status.

## Phase 3: Red-Team Review & Final Artifact Packaging

The assumption most worth stress-testing was: "the notation is consistent
enough across the three papers, written over four years, that no reconciling
note is needed." Per point 5, this was checked explicitly rather than
assumed, and found **false**: Paper A (the earliest) uses ε for detector
efficiency, while Papers B and C (written after a collaboration-wide notation
change) use ε for a different quantity (a systematic-uncertainty scale
factor) and denote efficiency as η instead. As a result, a reconciling
footnote was added to Paper A's chapter explicitly flagging this notation
difference for the reader, per point 5's instruction to flag such
inconsistencies in connective material rather than silently harmonizing
notation in a way that misrepresents what was actually published in each
paper.

**Gate:** submit to the committee only once every chapter's publication
status label, contribution statement, and copyright-permission source is
final and cross-checked against the graduate school's actual current
requirements a second time, and the cross-paper notation discrepancy is
flagged in the connective material rather than smoothed over - this is the
final packaging criterion, not an intermediate one.
