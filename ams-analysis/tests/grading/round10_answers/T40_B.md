No. A constant ratio is consistent with a common origin, but it does not prove one.

**What AMS measured**
- [Documented, C77] The Phys. Rept. review (S01) reports that above 60 GeV the positron-to-antiproton flux ratio is consistent with a constant. A constant fit over 60-525 GeV (in energy) gives 2.00 ± 0.035 (stat.) ± 0.06 (syst.), with χ²/d.o.f. = 7.2/12.
- [Documented, C95, C74] The samples behind this are the review's 1.9 million positrons and 5.6×10⁵ antiprotons.
- [Documented, C77] The review reads the result as nearly identical energy dependence and suggests a possible common source. It also says continued data taking is needed to confirm the origin.
- [Documented, C75, C06] The shape similarity of the antiproton-to-proton ratio is a different result. It is in rigidity, above about 60 GV, and comes from different papers. Do not merge it with the energy-based ratio above.

**Why a constant does not prove a common origin**
- [General method] A flat ratio over 60-525 GeV only says the two spectra have similar shapes in that range. It does not identify the source.
- Different mechanisms can give the same shape. For example, a common production process plus similar propagation can do it, as can an accidental match between a secondary-production component and a primary source.
- The constant fit has finite precision. The fit uncertainty is roughly 2%, and the systematic uncertainty is about 3% of the ratio. A small energy dependence, or a cutoff outside 60-525 GeV, would not be excluded.
- [Proposal] Treat a common source as a hypothesis to test against specific models. Fit the positron and antiproton fluxes together within one model, carry the joint covariance, and vary the secondary-production and propagation modelling as nuisance parameters.
- [General method, invariants 5 and 6] The ratio does not cancel acceptance, efficiency and systematics automatically. Classify each nuisance as correlated, partial or independent, and check that the uncertainties are not treated as Gaussian where counts are low.

**What would change the picture**
- [Documented, C77] The review says a cutoff in the antiproton spectrum is expected if the high-energy antiprotons come from dark-matter annihilation. It claims no such cutoff as observed.
- [Documented, C77] The review also states that antiprotons are not produced by pulsars. That is an interpretation, not a measurement.
- [Documented, C75] The review is explicit that interpretation is not yet definitive. It says modelling uncertainties must improve, and that all AMS data (electrons, positrons, protons, nuclei) should be used to constrain models.
