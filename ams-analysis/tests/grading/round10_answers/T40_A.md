No. A constant ratio is consistent with a common source, but it does not prove one.

**What AMS reported.** [Documented, C77, Phys. Rept. 894 review] Above 60 GeV the positron-to-antiproton flux ratio is consistent with a constant. A constant fit over 60-525 GeV (in energy) gives 2.00 ± 0.035 (stat.) ± 0.06 (syst.), with χ²/d.o.f. = 7.2/12. The review reads this as nearly identical energy dependence for positrons and antiprotons, and says it "suggests a possible common source". It adds that continued data taking is needed to confirm the origin.
- The samples behind this are the review's: 1.9 million positrons (C95) and 5.6×10⁵ antiprotons over 1-525 GV (C74).
- The antiproton-to-proton shape similarity (C75) is a separate result in rigidity. The earlier near-identical rigidity dependence of p̄, p and e⁺ at about 60-500 GV (C06) is from a different paper and variable. Neither is this ratio.

**Why the constant ratio is not proof.** [General method]
1. A fit that is consistent with a constant is not a test against every alternative. It shows that a constant is not excluded within the errors and range, which are 3.5% statistical and 6% systematic on the level. A slow trend or a shape difference at that level, or outside 60-525 GeV, would not show up. Proof would need a stated metric and a trigger (invariant 10), not just reasonable agreement.
2. Different physical mechanisms can produce a similar energy dependence. For example, a common propagation or energy-loss shape applied to different source terms could give a flat ratio. The value 2.00 is the product of the production, propagation and energy-loss effects of both species, so a constant ratio does not by itself identify one source.
3. Ratios do not cancel nuisances automatically (invariant 5). Positron and antiproton acceptance, efficiency, charge-confusion and template systematics must each be classed as correlated, partial or independent. The positron and antiproton analyses use different selections and backgrounds (S04 and S08 respectively).
4. Positron and antiproton fluxes are measured separately. Their errors are only partly correlated, so check which parts are shared before treating the ratio's uncertainty as a single number.

**The review's own interpretive statements.** [Documented, C77 and C75; collaboration interpretation, not measurements]
- The review says antiprotons are not produced by pulsars.
- It says a cutoff in the antiproton spectrum is expected if high-energy antiprotons come from dark-matter annihilation.
- It reports no such cutoff as observed.
- The antiproton-to-proton shape is described as not expected if antiprotons come only from cosmic-ray collisions with the interstellar medium. The review also says modelling uncertainties must improve before a definitive interpretation. One model shown both with and without a dark-matter contribution agrees qualitatively with the data in both cases.

**What would strengthen the claim.** [Proposal]
- Extend the range, for example to higher energy where a cutoff would show.
- Add statistics.
- Use the full covariance of the shared systematics.
- Test the constant against explicit alternatives, such as a slope or a break, and report the exclusion level.
- Fit the positron, antiproton and proton data jointly with propagation uncertainties.
