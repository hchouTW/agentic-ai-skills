**Verdict: the assumption does not hold as stated, and a constant fit that uses only the quoted bin errors is not justified.** Acceptance and efficiency do not cancel automatically in a ratio. Each effect has to be classified as correlated, partial or independent, and that has to be checked [General method; SKILL invariant 5].

**Why the cancellation assumption fails for e+/e- in 27-day bins**

1. **The two species do not share one selection.** The positron and electron samples differ in several ways [General method, with the published practice from C24, C25, C26]:
   - They have different backgrounds. The positron sample carries proton contamination and charge-confused electrons. The electron sample has a small antiproton background and no comparable charge-confusion contamination.
   - They use different templates, with separate positron and electron template provenance.
   - Their data/MC efficiency corrections are not identical.
   - Their charge-confusion handling is sign-specific.
   - The cutoff and exposure treatment can differ by sign at low rigidity.

   Only part of the acceptance and trigger efficiency is common to both. The proton-rejection, charge-confusion and template systematics are partial or independent.

2. **Statistical errors and time-dependent systematics are not independent across bins.** Systematics fall into three groups:
   - Persistent ones (template shape, cross-section model, calibration) are correlated across all bins.
   - Slowly varying ones are correlated between neighbouring bins.
   - The rest are independent per period.

   If you fit using the quoted bin errors alone, you are in effect assuming that every systematic is independent between bins. This is the same as treating the errors as diagonal. The result is an understated uncertainty on the fitted constant and a misleading chi2 and p-value.

3. **The ratio is not a pure efficiency ratio in time.** Livetime, acceptance, efficiency and detector state can each drift with time, and each bin has its own exposure and geomagnetic cutoff transmission. Cutoff sensitivity differs by charge sign, so it does not cancel at low rigidity or energy. A drift that is corrected in one category must not also be corrected in another.

4. **A constant is a physical hypothesis you are testing, not a given.** Solar modulation is charge-sign dependent because of drifts in the heliospheric field, and the polarity reversal makes opposite signs follow different time variations [General method]. AMS reported that positrons and electrons vary differently in time. These results are at abstract level [Documented, C40, C41]. Daily e+ and e- show a hysteresis below about 8.5 GV [C40, C41]. Separately, positrons are modulated more than protons below about 7 GV [C41]. The reported e+/e- transition across the 2013 polarity reversal took about 830 +- 30 days [Documented, C39, C90]. So a flat ratio over a low-rigidity or low-energy range would be a result needing validation, not an assumption.
   - Check your variable first. The Bartels-rotation lepton results are in energy (C39, C48). The daily lepton results are in rigidity (C40, C41). Use the same variable for your bins as the comparison you are making.
   - Bartels-rotation (27-day) binning is itself a documented AMS choice for the leptons (C48, C49). It does not make your error model valid.

2. **Write each time bin k and each energy bin i as a measured ratio.**
   - Use R_ki = [(N+_ki - b+_ki)/(E+_ki dE)] / [(N-_ki - b-_ki)/(E-_ki dE)], where E_ki is the exposure for that species.
   - Keep the exposures separate for the two species, and do not assume eps+/eps- = 1.

3. **Classify every nuisance** by the effect on the numerator and denominator, with a stated numerator-denominator correlation rho. Examples are ECAL/TRD selection efficiency, charge confusion, template shape, energy scale, trigger, and exposure and cutoff. Then classify each one across time as persistent, slowly varying or independent per period.

4. **Build one covariance over time and rigidity or energy.**
   - Use a documented ordering.
   - Do not use a Kronecker product unless you have tested that the correlation factorizes.
   - If you normalize every bin to a reference period, only the persistent part cancels, and it correlates the statistical errors of all bins with the reference. Carry that covariance as well.

5. **Run the checks.** If you have measured joint numerator-denominator covariances, run `scripts/statistical_toys.py ratio-measured`. It compares the full-covariance constant-ratio fit with a diagonal-only fit in chi2 and p-value, and reports the sigma ratio. If you only have declared correlation structures, run `ratio-cov`. Run `ratio-toys` with `--sys name:sigma_num:sigma_den:rho` to measure the residual after cancellation. Validate the covariance with `scripts/validate_covariance.py`.

6. **Fit the constant with the full covariance,** not the quoted bin errors, and report the chi2 and its toy-calibrated p-value.
   - Do not rely on Wilks if any bin has low counts or the constraints are weak.
   - Also fit a model with a time-dependent term, so the constant is tested against the charge-sign modulation described in point 4.

7. **Add stability checks** with a stated metric and trigger.
   - Use a control species' rate or an efficiency series versus time, flat after livetime and cutoff correction.
   - Run the same series on exposure and efficiency, so an apparent time structure is not a systematic.
   - "Reasonable agreement" is not validation: define the tolerance beforehand, and add a period split, a scale factor or a systematic if it is exceeded.
   - Fix the binning, range and metric before you look at the time dependence.
