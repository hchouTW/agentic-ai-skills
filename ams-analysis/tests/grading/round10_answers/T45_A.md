**Defects, in priority order**

1. **Most of what remains does not cancel.** For e+ and e-, these effects are species- or sign-specific:
   - **Charge confusion.** It is a Tracker tail and is sign-specific. It contaminates the positron sample with electrons.
   - **Proton background.** Protons misidentified as leptons are the dominant background in the positron sample, so the two samples have different contamination. The TRD and ECAL estimators and their templates are shared, so this is partially correlated rather than independent.
   - **Selection and template efficiencies.** The TRD, ECAL and E/p selections and the template provenance differ between the signs.
   - **Geomagnetic transmission.** At low energy it differs by charge sign.
   - **Material interaction and bremsstrahlung response.** These are also sign-dependent. [General method; Proposal: classify each as correlated, partial or independent.]
   - **What can be common.** Geometric acceptance, trigger efficiency, livetime and exposure can be largely common if you use the same time bins, the same fiducial region and the same energy or rigidity bins. This has to be verified. It is not automatic.

2. **Quoted bin errors are statistical only, so they are too small for a constant fit.**
   - Systematics are not independent across time bins. The persistent ones are correlated across all periods: calibration, template shape and cross-section model. The slowly varying ones are correlated between neighbouring bins. [General method, `time-dependent-analysis`.]
   - A diagonal-only chi-square for a constant fit is wrong when bins share systematics. It underestimates the uncertainty on the fitted constant and gives a wrong goodness of fit.
   - The fix is a full (time × energy or rigidity) covariance with a stated ordering, including the numerator-denominator cross-covariance. Check it with `scripts/validate_covariance.py`. If you have a measured joint covariance, run `statistical_toys.py ratio-measured`. If you only have declared correlation structures, use `ratio-cov`. Use `ratio-toys` to test each "cancels" claim with a stated numerator-denominator correlation rho, and keep the residual. [General method]
   - If you normalise to a reference period, only the persistent part cancels. That also correlates the statistical errors of all periods with the reference, so carry that covariance.

3. **Constant versus time-varying.** A constant ratio is a hypothesis to test, not an assumption. Charge-sign-dependent solar modulation is physics, so e+ and e- need not follow the same time variation. AMS reported that e+ and e- time structures differ at the daily level [Documented, C40, C41, abstract level]. It also reported a smooth e+/e- transition across the solar polarity reversal over about 830 days [Documented, C39, C89, C90]. The ratio's time dependence therefore depends on energy or rigidity and on the solar-cycle phase. A constant is only plausible in a narrow range and period, and then only if you show it with the full covariance. A good chi-square with diagonal errors proves nothing.

4. **Per-bin construction issues to check.**
   - **Variable.** Use the same variable and bin edges for both signs. Published AMS lepton products use energy, whereas the Tracker measures rigidity and the ECAL measures energy. State which variable you bin in.
   - **Exposure.** Each time bin has its own exposure, and the cutoff transmission must be computed per bin and per sign. Do not average ratios across bins of different exposure.
   - **Boundaries.** Bins must not straddle hardware or reconstruction-version boundaries.
   - **Low counts.** Use Poisson-exact or toy-validated errors, not Gaussian ones, in sparse bins. At low counts the ratio of two Poisson counts is biased, so check it with `ratio-toys`.
   - **Calibration drift.** Correct a drift in one category only. Do not apply it as both a veto and an efficiency.
   - **Template fits.** Use finite-template statistics (Barlow-Beeston) in the likelihood.
   - **Time-dependent systematics.** AMS evaluated efficiencies per day and added the time-dependent systematics in quadrature, separate from the time-independent ones [Documented, C44, S43, daily proton flux only]. This is one analysis's choice, not a rule. How AMS correlated systematics across days or rotations is [Unknown/needs input]: no covariance model appears in the text the ledger read.
   - **Bin length.** A 27-day (Bartels-rotation) bin is a fixed count. Aligning to rotations makes bins comparable but does not remove within-bin exposure differences.

5. **Periodicity.** If your goal is to look for a periodicity, do not use a single-frequency p-value. Report local and global significance, and run the same search on exposure and control series. [General method]

**What would change the verdict.** Your energy or rigidity range, your period and hardware eras, whether you have a joint covariance or only per-bin statistical errors, how you split the systematics, and whether the e+ and e- samples are built with the same selection and templates. With a measured covariance and a demonstrated per-effect correlation, a constant fit is acceptable. If you have none of that, the minimum is to refit with the declared-correlation covariance via `ratio-cov`. Also report the constant-fit chi-square and the fitted-constant uncertainty with and without systematics. A large change between the two is the trigger to drop the diagonal-only claim.
