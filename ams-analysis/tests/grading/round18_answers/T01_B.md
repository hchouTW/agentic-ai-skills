**How AMS-02 determines charge sign**

Charge sign comes from the Tracker and the permanent magnet only. [Documented, C21; General method for the physics]

- **Response chain.** A charged particle crosses the magnetic field. Its trajectory curves in the bending projection, with the radius set by `R = pc/(Ze)`. It ionizes the silicon layers, and the strip signals become clusters and then hit coordinates. The track fit with the field map gives the curvature, hence a signed rigidity.
- **Sign convention.** `sign(R) = sign(Z)` under a stated convention: which projection bends, the field direction, and the fit orientation. The analysis quotes `|R|` for fluxes and the signed `R` for charge-sign work.
- **Magnitude is separate from sign.** `|Z|` comes from energy loss and photon yield, `dE/dx ∝ Z^2` at a given `β`. The Tracker, TOF and RICH each give a `|Z|` estimator, but none of these gives the sign.
- **Failure mode.** Charge confusion happens when the curvature is measured with the wrong sign. The causes are finite resolution (large-angle scattering, delta rays, interactions and hit loss, and alignment or rigidity-scale errors at high `R`). It is a tail phenomenon: an estimate built on the Gaussian core of `δR/R` is not acceptable. It needs a data-driven or calibrated-simulation tail check. Published AMS numbers exist for specific analyses (for example C21 for the maximum detectable rigidity), and they do not transfer to other species or selections.
- **Cross-checks.** Independent partial fits, such as upper and lower track segments, should agree on the sign. The rigidity scale is checked with a physical control.

**Why the TRD cannot do it**

1. The TRD's signal depends on the Lorentz factor `γ`, not on the sign of the charge. Relativistic particles of either sign cross radiator layers and emit transition radiation X-rays with the same yield for the same `γ`. The X-rays are absorbed in gas straws, so the TRD sees energy deposition and no bending.
2. It has no field and no curvature measurement. Charge sign is a property of the direction a trajectory bends in a magnetic field, and only the Tracker plus magnet measures that.
3. Its strength is lepton/hadron separation. For example, it separates e from p by `γ`, so e+ and e- look alike in the TRD, as do p and p̄. The TRD can say "lepton" or "hadron". The Tracker must say "+" or "-".
4. The same holds for TOF, ECAL and RICH, which also do not measure charge sign. The TOF gives direction, `β` and `|Z|`. The ECAL gives shower energy and shape. The RICH gives `β` and `|Z|`. So the TRD is never a standalone charge-sign detector.

**Consequence for e+/e- and p/p̄.** The TRD and ECAL reject the wrong species (for example protons in a positron sample), and the Tracker supplies the sign. Charge-sign errors are a Tracker problem and cannot be fixed by lepton/hadron ID. They are controlled by tail validation of the rigidity resolution and charge-confusion migration. The TRD cannot supply that control.
