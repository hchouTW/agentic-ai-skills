# Measurement brief: helium-4 flux, 2 GV to 3 TV, public AMS data

## 0. Two issues to settle first

1. **"Helium-4" and 2 GV-3 TV do not match any published AMS measurement.** [Documented, C59, C62, C81] AMS has published the *elemental* helium flux (all He isotopes, charge Z=2) over about 1.9 GV-3 TV. [Documented, S12; nuclei-and-isotopes] The isotope-resolved He-4 sample is 100 million 4He nuclei at 2.1-21 GV (3He: 1.9-15 GV, May 2011-Nov 2017); the velocity measurement limits the isotope range. Above about 21 GV there is no isotope-separated 4He flux in the public record.
   - **Proposal:** the estimand for 2 GV-3 TV is the **elemental He flux**, Phi_He(R). Report an "4He flux" only as Phi_He x f_4(R), with f_4 = 4He/(3He+4He) taken from S12 below 21 GV and an assumed composition above. That assumption carries its own uncertainty. [Documented, C60] The published helium analysis varied the 3He/4He ratio from 0 to 0.2 as a systematic (0.3%).
   - If the user really wants the isotope, the target is 2.1-21 GV, estimated with a mass fit from R, |Z| and beta (RICH/TOF), not 3 TV.
2. **"Public AMS data" means published results, not event-level data.** [Unknown/needs input] I have no public AMS event files, calibration, or selection constants. The work is either (a) reproduce or compare against the published He fluxes and tables, or (b) design the analysis symbolically for someone with data access. Compiled databases (CRDB at LPSC, ASI SSDC) hold published values only and are secondary, never an AMS result. Check them with `crdb_query.py` before use.

## 1. Estimand
Differential flux at the top of the instrument, Phi_He(R) in m^-2 sr^-1 s^-1 GV^-1, in true |R|, for nuclei with |Z|=2 and charge sign +1. No solar-modulation correction to the local interstellar spectrum. Level: flux. Target: flux. Variable: rigidity (GV). [General method] Edges converted with `ams_kinematics.py` for |Z|=2, A=4, m=3.7274 GeV (the same bins in T/A are only for display): R = 2 GV is pc = 4 GeV/c and T/A = 0.435 GeV/n. R = 3 TV is pc = 6 TeV/c and T/A = 1499 GeV/n. Conversion to T/A needs an assumed A or composition model and a Jacobian, not only relabeled edges.

## 4. Selection (one conditional denominator per cut)
[Documented, C59, C62; this is what the cited papers did, not a standard] Downward-going events, normal-operation time only, pointing within 40 degrees of zenith, ISS outside the South Atlantic Anomaly. Inner-tracker track with charge compatible with Z=2, full-span track (L1-L9) with a good chi^2 in the bending plane, and charge measured by L1, TOF and L9 compatible with Z=2 to remove interacting candidates. Rigidity above 1.2 times the maximum geomagnetic cutoff in the field of view.
[Documented, C54, C56] Other He analyses used other selections (L1 and inner tracker only, data-derived cutoff), so no single selection is the AMS standard. All cut values and efficiencies for my design are [Unknown/needs input] and stay symbolic. Each cut gets a named conditional denominator, a metric, a threshold and an action.

## 5. Backgrounds
- Wrong-Z leakage from neighbours (proton and Li/Be).
- Helium from fragmentation of heavier nuclei at the top of the instrument.
- Charge-sign confusion at high R (a tail effect).
- Albedo and secondaries near cutoff.
Each needs an estimator, a control region and a closure test. [Documented, C59] The first-30-month paper reports proton contamination below 1e-4 and heavier-nuclei fragmentation below 1e-3. These are scoped to that paper's selection and period, so do not transfer them.

## 6. Efficiency, acceptance, livetime, response (kept separate)
- n_i = observed counts and b_i = background, per reconstructed bin.
- eps_j = trigger x reconstruction x charge-selection efficiency, with data/MC scale factors. Do not multiply conditional factors as if independent.
- A_j = effective acceptance (m^2 sr) from the generation record, including survival against upstream material (He inelastic cross-section model).
- T_j = livetime x geomagnetic transmission (exposure already contains acceptance and livetime; count each once).
- R_ij = P(R_meas in i | R_true in j, selected), tails included.
- Do not use the diagonal estimator Phi_i = (n_i - b_i)/(A eps T dR) under migration. Use unfolding or forward-folding: mu_i = sum_j R_ij A_j eps_j T_j Phi_j dR_j + b_i.
- [Documented, S01, C63] The review gives the tracker maximum detectable rigidity for He as 3.2 TV (nine-layer configuration). The 3 TV edge sits at about 94% of that. Above a few hundred GV the resolution tail and the rigidity-scale error dominate, so the top bins need tail-validated response models. The Gaussian core alone is not acceptable.

## 7. Inference
Counts are plentiful at low and mid R, so Gaussian errors are fine there. The highest bins have low counts: use Poisson or toy-validated intervals, not Gaussian or S/sqrt(B). [Documented, C62] The published He, C, O analysis used 67 bins from 2.2 GV to 3.0 TV, with unfolding done separately for each species. [General method] Choose bin edges and check migration as a function of R before fixing them. Choose the estimator after the response is defined, not before.

## 9. Validation (metric, threshold, action)
- MC closure with an injected spectrum. If the closure pull exceeds the stated threshold, change the unfolding or add an uncertainty.
- Stability versus time, detector region and track configuration (L1-L9 versus inner tracker).
- Cutoff-factor scan 1.0-1.4.
- Loose versus tight charge cuts.
- Charge-sign split.
- Comparison with the published He flux, as a plausibility check only. Agreement is not validation.
