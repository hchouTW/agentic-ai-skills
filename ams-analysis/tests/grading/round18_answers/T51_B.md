**1. Hysteresis significance, antiproton versus proton, bins below 11 GV** [Documented, C178; Table SA, values checked against the rendered page]

Table SA has six rigidity bins below 11 GV. The significance is the collaboration's sigma_hys, the significance of the difference of Phi_pbar^A/Phi_pbar^B from unity:

| Rigidity bin (GV) | Phi_pbar^A/Phi_pbar^B | sigma_hys |
|---|---|---|
| 1.00-2.97 | 1.22 +- 0.04 | 5.5 |
| 2.97-4.88 | 1.14 +- 0.02 | 5.8 |
| 4.88-5.90 | 1.12 +- 0.02 | 6.9 |
| 5.90-7.09 | 1.07 +- 0.02 | 4.0 |
| 7.09-8.48 | 1.10 +- 0.02 | 5.6 |
| 8.48-11.0 | 1.06 +- 0.01 | 4.2 |

Caveats on these numbers:
- Per bin, the paper picks two 13-rotation intervals with the same normalized proton flux, one before and one after 2014-2015, and takes the pair with the most significant difference in the normalized antiproton flux. The method is cited only by reference to an earlier AMS paper.
- The text does not say how the correlation between the intervals or the choice of the most significant pair (a look-elsewhere effect) enters. Treat these as the collaboration's numbers, not validated global significances.
- The ratio is not monotonic in the table (1.07 at 5.90-7.09 GV, 1.10 at 7.09-8.48 GV), although the text says it decreases with rigidity.
- The text summary: more than 5 sigma at 1.00-2.97 GV, falling to 4 sigma at 8.48-11.0 GV.

I will not give a number. The per-rotation flux values are in Tables S1-S139 of the Supplemental Material (SM-antip-BR-Tables.pdf). The ledger records only the layout of those tables (C180, C122), and the table values were not read or transcribed. A figure from memory would be invented.
- Rotation 2426 is probably the first rotation, May 2011. The ledger gives 2426 as the first number in the nuclei supplement (C106, a different paper). The antiproton tables' own rotation numbering is not recorded, so which table holds BR 2426 is unverified.
- Where to look: open the table for that rotation and read the 1.00-1.92 GV row.
- What each row has: the flux in [m^2 sr s GV]^-1, a statistical error, a time-dependent systematic error and a total systematic error.
- Time-dependent systematic: it has contributions from background subtraction, trigger efficiency and unfolding.
- Total systematic: the quadrature sum of the time-dependent systematic, geomagnetic cutoff, effective acceptance, unfolding and rigidity scale.
- Correlations: the introductory text read does not mention any correlation between bins or rotations. That is not evidence that none exists.

**3. Antiproton-electron slope k at 1.00-1.92 GV** [Documented, C179]

k(e-, pbar) = 1.78 +- 0.12 in 1.00-1.92 GV. Notes on its definition and caveats:
- Definition: it comes from the fit V_e- = k(e-, pbar) V_pbar (Eq. S1). Here V = (Phi - <Phi>)/<Phi> is the relative variation of each flux per Bartels rotation, and <Phi> is the 11-year time-averaged flux.
- Reading: k is greater than 1 with significance over 4 sigma, so the antiproton flux is modulated less than the electron flux.
- Trend: k decreases with rigidity, reaching 1.31 +- 0.08 at 5.90-7.09 GV.
- Caveats: the per-bin fit errors and chi2/d.f. (Fig. S6) were not read. The relation is stated to be compatible with linear in the six bins from 1.00 to 7.09 GV.
