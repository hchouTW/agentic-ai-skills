# D/p flux ratio, 2-20 GV: measurement design

Labels: [Documented, Cxx] = ledger claim, [General method], [Proposal], [Unknown/needs input]. Assumption: public-paper-level design, data not MC, no internal cuts or constants.

## 1. Estimand
Ratio of the deuteron to proton differential fluxes, in **rigidity** |R| (GV), Z = 1 for both, in the same bins and the same period: D/p(R) = Phi_D(R)/Phi_p(R). Rigidity is not momentum or kinetic energy per nucleon. At a given R, D and p have different T/A and beta (General method, ams_kinematics.py):

| R (GV) | p: beta, T/A | D: beta, T/A |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | 0.905, 1.27 GeV/n | 0.729, 0.433 GeV/n |
| 20 | 0.9989, 19.1 GeV/n | 0.9956, 9.11 GeV/n |

So a ratio in R is not a ratio at equal T/A. Quoting it vs T/A needs a stated A assumption (A = 2 for D, A = 1 for p) and the flux Jacobian (about 2.74 for D at 2 GV, about 2.01 at 20 GV; 1.10 and 1.00 for p). Rigidity is the native variable. Decide first whether the product is D/p vs R or vs T/A. Do not pick the algorithm first.

## 3. Detector roles
- **Tracker**: signed |R|, |Z| = 1, direction. Sole charge-sign source (not an issue here beyond rejecting Z = -1 and negative R).
- **TOF**: beta at low velocity, |Z|, direction. [Documented, C28] TOF Delta(1/beta) Gaussian core is 0.02 in the helium-isotope paper. That is a core width, not a tail, and not a D paper value.
- **RICH (NaF, then aerogel)**: precise beta at higher velocity. [Documented, C70, review level] thresholds beta > 0.75 (NaF) and 0.953 (aerogel). [Documented, C28] RICH-Agl core width 7e-4 at beta = 1 (helium-isotope paper).
- **TRD, ECAL, ACC**: no isotope power. ACC is only a veto.

Consequence (General method, computed from the table): a 2 GV deuteron has beta = 0.729, below the NaF threshold, so its beta there comes from TOF alone. A 2 GV proton (0.905) is above the NaF threshold. The core separation Delta(1/beta) between p and D at 2 GV is about 0.27, against a core 0.02, which is comfortable, but tails decide. At 20 GV the separation is Delta(1/beta) about 0.0033, compared with a RICH-Agl core of 7e-4 (a different species and paper), so the high edge is the sensitivity edge. Mass follows (dm/m)^2 = (dR/R)^2 + (gamma^2 d beta/beta)^2 (+ correlation). gamma^2 is about 115 for D at 20 GV, which grows with R. The upper rigidity limit is set by the beta resolution, not statistics. The rigidity-beta correlation rho must be verified, not assumed zero. At 20 GV D is only barely above the aerogel threshold range limit of the RICH acceptance, so check acceptance and radiator-region coverage separately for D and p.

## 4. Selection (proposal, tied to what S13 documents)
[Documented, C27] The deuteron analysis requires downward-going, a track through tracker L1, and Z = 1 on L1, upper TOF, inner tracker and lower TOF. I propose the same Z = 1 chain for both D and p, so charge selection cancels as far as it is identical (verify, do not assume). Add: geomagnetic selection with R above a safety factor times the maximum cutoff for the direction and time [Documented, C31 for the proton paper's R > 1.2 times maximum cutoff; its value for this analysis is an Unknown]. Livetime, SAA exclusion and zenith-angle conditions are common inputs [Unknown/needs input for this design]. Ring quality and containment per radiator region. Choose the discriminant: mass, or beta at fixed R, or 1/beta vs R.

## 5. Signal and background model
1. **Proton sample is almost pure p; the D sample is about 10^-2 of it (order of magnitude not sourced; user to supply).** Any proton tail of the beta (or mass) distribution that falls on the D region is the dominant background to D. Build templates for p, D, and tails (beta-resolution tails, wrong-ring, secondary particles, accidental hits) and validate the tails in data in the same beta range with an independent sample. Tails must come from data, not from the Gaussian core.
2. **Helium fragmentation**: [Documented, C27] the dominant background is He -> D (and T) fragmentation above L1, estimated from data using 4He + (C, Al) -> T + X (bounded at 4% there). For D/p add its effect on the proton side (He -> p is small relative to p but not zero) and vary the cross-section model.
3. **Wrong-Z and charge confusion** (D with Z = 1 selected, p/D charge sign): tail phenomenon; require tail validation, not core-based estimates.
4. **Albedo and upward-going** at low R: TOF direction and misassignment probability.
5. Mis-assigned ring from a secondary particle.

## 6. Efficiency, acceptance, exposure
Efficiencies, acceptance, exposure and livetime are distinct. Per species and per bin: Phi_i = N_i / (A_i eps_i T_exp dR) (symbols; do not multiply conditional efficiencies as independent). Using the same period and bins, exposure and the geometric acceptance largely factor out of the ratio. Ratios do not cancel efficiency automatically: velocity-detector efficiency (ring reconstruction, NaF vs aerogel, TOF) is species and beta dependent, hadronic survival differs for D and p (different cross-sections, material above L1 and L2), and trigger and tracking efficiency may differ at low beta. Classify each nuisance as correlated, partial or independent and verify. Do not correct one effect in two categories (a veto or ring-quality cut in both efficiency and response).

## 7. Response and inference
Define the response from true R to reconstructed R per species, with tails. Isotope separation requires an explicit procedure. [Documented, C27; C28] AMS unfolds the Z = 1 sample using rigidity and inverse-velocity resolution functions (TOF and RICH) from MC, validated at beta about 1 with data, in velocity bins per detector. [General method] I propose a binned template fit of D fraction in each R bin (finite-template nuisances, full Barlow-Beeston with scripts/template_fit.py), then unfold bin-to-bin migration, or equivalently forward-fold the D and p models. Report the template-overlap diagnostic and the correlation between D fraction and resolution nuisances as a per-bin sensitivity flag. Bin width at least about twice the resolution where it applies. Report the covariance, with the correlation structure of shared systematics (C33).

## 8. Systematics (ledger)
Beta-resolution tails and the core width (vary by 10% as a pattern; [Documented, C28] for the helium analysis), template statistics and shape, ring-selection and radiator-boundary effects, He -> D feed and cross-section model, hadronic survival difference between D and p, rigidity scale (steep spectrum amplifies by the local index), alignment, geomagnetic cutoff factor, charge selection, time stability. Combine independent sources in quadrature [Documented, C33]. Cross-sections and material model: state their provenance, do not attribute to AMS.

## 9. Validation and triggers
Closure with pseudo-data with injected D fractions (bias below a stated fraction of the statistical error, else add an uncertainty or change the template); tail validation on a known-mass control (abundant p and 4He in the same beta range); alternative discriminant (mass vs beta at fixed R); loose/tight ring quality; NaF vs aerogel overlap region; detector-half and time-period splits (solar modulation affects low R); blinded comparison with the published D flux over the common range as plausibility only. "Reasonable agreement" is not validation: set a metric (e.g. chi2 or pull of the split-sample ratio) and a trigger to add an uncertainty.
