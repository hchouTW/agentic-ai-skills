**Result: at R = 10 GV the first-order mass resolution is about 12.5% for a proton and about 5.8% for a deuteron, so the two are separated by about 8 sigma in the Gaussian core. That is a core-only figure.** [General method; user-supplied inputs dR/R = 5% and dbeta/beta = 0.1%. These are not AMS performance claims.]

**Method.** Mass is m = |Z|R/(gamma*beta). Take Z = 1 and assume the two error terms are uncorrelated (rho = 0). Then (dm/m)^2 = (dR/R)^2 + (gamma^2 dbeta/beta)^2. At fixed R the velocity depends on the mass hypothesis, so the numbers differ for each species. I used PDG masses and ran `scripts/ams_kinematics.py mass-resolution` for each hypothesis.

| Hypothesis | beta | gamma | gamma^2 dbeta/beta | R term | dm/m | sigma_m |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Proton (0.938 GeV) | 0.9956 | 10.70 | 0.115 | 0.050 | 0.125 | about 0.117 GeV |
| Deuteron (1.876 GeV) | 0.9829 | 5.42 | 0.029 | 0.050 | 0.058 | about 0.109 GeV |

**Separation.** The mass difference is 1.876 - 0.938 = 0.937 GeV. With sigma_m about 0.11-0.12 GeV, that is roughly 8 sigma between the peaks, or about 4 sigma each if you cut at the midpoint.

**What limits each species.**
- **Proton:** the velocity term dominates because gamma^2 is large.
- **Deuteron:** the 5% rigidity term dominates because gamma^2 is smaller.
- **Higher rigidity:** the velocity term grows roughly as gamma^2, so separation degrades as R rises. The upper rigidity limit for isotope separation is set by the beta resolution, not by statistics.

**Caveats.**
- This is a Gaussian-core, first-order estimate. In a real D/p analysis the proton background is orders of magnitude larger than the deuteron signal.
- Even a small tail of the proton mass distribution at +0.9 GeV (the proton beta-resolution tail, wrong rings) can therefore swamp the deuterons. That tail must be validated with data and templates, not inferred from the core.
- I assumed rho = 0. If the R and beta errors share information (a shared trajectory), verify rho. A positive correlation reduces the combined error.
- The 0.1% velocity figure is a Gaussian core. Check whether it applies at your beta, radiator and ring quality. Published RICH values are quoted per radiator and Z range in the source-index (C21, C28).
- The 5% dR/R is also a core value. At 10 GV the real Tracker resolution is partly multiple-scattering dominated.
- A mass-estimate "separation" is only a discriminant. The actual measurement is a template fit of isotope fractions per rigidity bin with finite-template nuisances. See the nuclei-and-isotopes reference for the workflow.
