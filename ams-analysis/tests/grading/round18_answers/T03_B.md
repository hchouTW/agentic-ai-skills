**e/p separation.** The ECAL is one of the two strong lepton/hadron handles, together with the TRD. [Documented, C71/C72; detector-and-observables]

- It images the shower in 3D. It has 17 X0 (C50, C71). It gives the shower energy, the longitudinal and lateral shape, and the shower axis.
- An electron deposits E_ECAL ~ pc, so E/(|Z|·|R|) with Z = 1 is near 1, within bremsstrahlung and leakage effects. A proton has a hadronic response, so E/p < 1 on average with wide tails.
- The shower-shape estimator Λ_ECAL and the E/p cut combine into the proton rejection (C72, 1-2000 GeV/c). Any rejection number must be quoted with its efficiency, range and claim ID. I give no rejection value here.
- ECAL shower shape and E/p share the same ECAL energy. Do not count them as independent handles.
- Do not use the same ECAL variables both to select and to define the template that measures the selection efficiency, because that is circular.
- Hadronic showers with a large electromagnetic component (charge exchange, π0) can fake electrons. Validate the hadronic tails on control data. [General method]
- Charge sign is not an ECAL observable. For e+ versus e-, use the Tracker sign together with ECAL/TRD identification. [Documented, invariant 2]

**Charge estimator for nuclei: no.** The ECAL is not the standard nucleus charge estimator. [Documented, cross-subsystem matrix]

- Its measured quantity is shower energy. Hadronic response is species-dependent, and E ≈ p·c holds only for electrons. For He and heavier nuclei, p = |Z|R/c, so an E/R = 1 expectation has no physical basis.
- It gives no |Z| calibration comparable to the dedicated detectors, and the leakage and tail behaviour of an ECAL-based Z is not documented.
- Use the dedicated |Z| estimators:
  - Tracker cluster amplitude (dE/dx ∝ Z²).
  - TOF amplitudes.
  - RICH photoelectron yield (∝ Z²).
  - Cross-check these against each other. Tails feed adjacent-element leakage, so validate them.
- The ECAL can only play a supporting role for nuclei. Examples are the shower axis for matching, or a veto of interacting events. Any such use needs its own validation. [Proposal]
- If you intend to use the ECAL for Z, treat it as a [Proposal] and validate it against the Tracker/TOF/RICH estimators.
