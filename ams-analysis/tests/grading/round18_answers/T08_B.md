## Defects in priority order

**1. Charge confusion from the Gaussian rigidity resolution is invalid (invariant 4).**
- Charge confusion (p -> negative sign) is a tail phenomenon: large-angle scattering, wrong hit assignment, interactions and secondary tracks. The Gaussian core says nothing about it, and it typically underestimates the rate by orders of magnitude.
- The antiproton sample is signal-poor and sits on an abundant proton flux, so this is the dominant systematic for the result.
- Smallest fix [Proposal]:
  - Estimate the sign-flip probability as a migration `M(p-bar <- p)(R)`, using a data-driven tail or a calibrated MC tail.
  - Validate the tail on abundant samples. Use independent partial track fits (different layer subsets or upper/lower segments), hit-pattern and track-quality variables, and positive-rigidity protons under varied cuts, then check the extrapolation to the tight selection.
  - Add a template for charge-confused protons in the fit. Use a charge-confusion estimator, with templates checked against high-rigidity test-beam or flight data.
  - Set a metric and a trigger: for example, if the tail normalization differs between data and MC beyond a stated threshold, add a nuisance or switch to the data-driven estimate.
- [Documented, C22] The published antiproton template fit includes charge-confused protons with templates checked against 400 GV proton test-beam data. Other collaboration analyses use a Tracker-based charge-confusion estimator (C24). Scope each claim to its own paper (S08 for antiprotons; S04 for positrons), not to the other.

**2. The "TRD cut" as the signal definition is incomplete, and a TRD cut is not a sign or antiproton selector (invariant 2).**
- The negative sign comes from the Tracker/magnet only.
- The TRD gives lepton/hadron separation through the gamma-dependent transition radiation. It rejects electrons but leaves hadronic negatives (pi-, charge-confused protons, secondary or interacting particles) untouched.
- A single cut on one estimator is a cut-and-count design with no stated efficiency, no conditional denominator and no control region.
- The signal region must also require |Z| = 1, a beta-rigidity consistency check and sensible track quality. It should use independent evidence, with ECAL shape and E/|p| as a second electron veto. Account for the correlation: ECAL shape and E/p share the ECAL energy, and Tracker charge and E/p share the track.
- Do not use an ECAL variable both to define the sample and to derive the template that measures it.
- Smallest fix [Proposal]:
  - Define the selection ledger with a named conditional denominator for each cut. Do not multiply conditional efficiencies as if independent (invariant 3).
  - Derive the TRD and ECAL efficiencies on data where possible, with a stated tail check.

**3. Subtracting the electron background "by MC" is not enough, and it is a counting-style design where the problem is a template-fit problem.**
- At some rigidities the electron background is dominant in the negative-sign sample, and its tail after the cut is exactly where MC is least reliable.
- This is a one-shot subtraction with no uncertainty, no closure test and no control region.
- Smallest fix [Proposal]:
  - Move to a template fit of a discriminant in each rigidity bin (a TRD or ECAL estimator, or a combination) with templates for antiprotons, electrons, pi-, charge-confused p and others.
  - Take the signal shape from proton data, the electron template from data and MC, and the charge-confused template as above.
  - Include finite-MC-statistics nuisances (Barlow-Beeston style), template-provenance checks, cross-contamination of the proton template, an injection test, a pull and goodness-of-fit check, and a fit-stability test.
  - Add a data-driven electron control, such as a reversed TRD/ECAL cut, with a transfer-factor validation.
- [Documented, C22] The published analysis fits templates to the negative-rigidity sample in overlapping rigidity regions rather than subtracting MC. This is the collaboration's method, not an endorsement of any specific implementation here. Do not present this fit as the only AMS practice.

## Further gaps
- No estimand: the flux or the antiproton-to-proton ratio, in rigidity?
- Rigidity is the variable. State |R| and the sign convention, and keep the abundant-p denominator's acceptance and efficiency separate. Partial cancellation is to be verified rather than assumed (invariant 5).
- Exposure, acceptance, livetime and geomagnetic selection are not mentioned. Keep them distinct, and use the exposure that already contains acceptance and livetime so nothing is corrected twice.
- Migration and unfolding are not mentioned. Use a response with a stated orientation and normalization, or a forward fold.
- No systematic ledger. Each effect, each with its `applied_via` (migration, nuisance, alternate sample), a bin-to-bin correlation model and a double-counting check.
- Low rigidity-bin counts at high rigidity need exact Poisson or toy-validated intervals, not S/sqrt(B) or Gaussian errors (invariant 6).
- No validation metrics: "agrees with MC" is not validation (invariant 10). State a metric, a threshold and an action.
- No blinding or cross-check plan. Use alternative discriminants, loose/tight variations, period and charge-sign splits, and compare with the published result only as a plausibility check.
