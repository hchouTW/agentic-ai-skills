**Iron (PRL 126, 041104, 2021)** [Documented, C149]
- Sample: 0.62 x 10^6 Fe nuclei out of 1.50 x 10^11 cosmic-ray events (the 1.50 x 10^11 is the total sample, not the Fe sample).
- Rigidity range: 2.65 GV to 3.0 TV, 49 bins.
- Period: first 8.5 years of ISS operation, 19 May 2011 to 30 Oct 2019.
- Purity > 97% [C151].

**Fluorine (PRL 126, 081102, 2021)** [Documented, C156]
- Sample: 0.29 x 10^6 F nuclei after background subtraction, out of 1.50 x 10^11 events.
- Rigidity range: 2.15 GV to 2.9 TV, 49 bins.
- Period: the same, 19 May 2011 to 30 Oct 2019.

Both are main-article readings only. The Supplemental Material was not read, so per-cut definitions and tables are unchecked.

**What F/Si versus B/O implies** [Documented, C160, C161; S58 main article]
- F/Si was fitted over 28.8-2900 GV with a broken power law at 175 GV. The slopes are Delta1 = -0.34 +- 0.02 and Delta2 = -0.19 +- 0.07, so the hardening is 0.15 +- 0.07.
- The AMS B/O ratio (data from the earlier AMS boron paper, not re-measured) was fitted with the same form. It gives Delta1 = -0.405 +- 0.005 and Delta2 = -0.26 +- 0.03.
- The double ratio (F/Si)/(B/O) above 10 GV is described by a single power law R^delta with delta = 0.052 +- 0.007. The paper quotes this as a 7 sigma departure from zero.
- The paper's conclusion is that the rigidity dependence of F/Si is distinctly different from that of B/O (or B/C). It infers that the propagation of heavy secondaries (F, from Ne/Mg/Si progenitors) differs from that of light secondaries (He-to-O progenitors). Secondary cosmic rays then also form two classes, like the primaries.
- Both ratios harden above about 175-200 GV. The hardening is similar within errors (0.15 +- 0.07 for F/Si against the light-ratio value of 0.140 +- 0.025, quoted from earlier papers).
- The F/Si ratio falls less steeply than B/O. This means the heavy secondaries have a different effective rigidity-dependent path length or escape.
- These statements are the paper's own claims.

**Caveats**
- [Proposal] The two-class reading is an interpretation. It does not by itself fix the cause: grammage, spallation cross sections, source composition, or different progenitor mixes.
- The significance and trial factor details are not given in the main text.
- The Fe flux paper separately finds Fe behaving like the He-C-O primary class (Fe/O constant above 80.5 GV, 0.155 +- 0.006 [C155]).
- Do not read the Fe paper as a heavy-secondary result. Iron is a primary.
