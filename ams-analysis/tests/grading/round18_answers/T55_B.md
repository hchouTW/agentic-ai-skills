Source: AMS Ne, Mg, Si paper (PRL 124, 211102, 2020; main article only, Supplemental Material not read) [Documented, S56].

**Sample, range, period** [Documented, C143]
- 1.8 x 10^6 Ne, 2.2 x 10^6 Mg and 1.6 x 10^6 Si nuclei, counted after background subtraction.
- Rigidity range 2.15 GV to 3.0 TV, in 66 bins with identical widths for the three nuclei.
- Period: May 19, 2011 to May 26, 2018 (first 7 years on the ISS), from 120 x 10^9 cosmic-ray events.
- The collection time reaches 1.74 x 10^8 s above 30 GV.
- Total flux error is about 5% at 100 GV for each nucleus.

**Charge purities** [Documented, C145]
- Purity is > 98% for Ne and Mg, and > 99.7% for Si.
- Background from interactions between L1 and L2, fitted with charge templates, is < 0.3% for all three.
- Background from interactions above L1, estimated from simulation, is < 2% for Ne and < 1.5% for Mg. It is negligible for Si.
- The overall background-subtraction uncertainty is < 0.5%.

**Fitted breaks** [Documented, C147]
- The ratio is fitted as k (R/R0)^Delta for R <= R0 and as the constant k above R0.
- Ne/Mg: k = 0.84 +- 0.02, R0 = 3.65 +- 0.5 GV, Delta = 0.19 +- 0.08, chi2/d.o.f. = 42/64. The fit covers the entire rigidity range.
- Si/Mg: k = 0.89 +- 0.02, R0 = 86.5 +- 13 GV, Delta = 0.069 +- 0.005, chi2/d.o.f. = 29/53. The fit covers rigidities above 6 GV.
- The paper concludes that Ne and Mg have an identical rigidity dependence above 3.65 GV, and that all three fluxes are identical above 86.5 GV.
- The break values are parameters of the chosen functional form, with sizable uncertainties.
- "Identical above R0" is a statement about that fit, not an independent test.

**Different class from He, C, O?** [Documented, C148]
- The paper states this as its own conclusion. I would not describe it as shown independently of the paper's own assumptions and fit.
- The evidence is a fit of Ne/O, Mg/O and Si/O above 20 GV with a double power law whose break is at 86.5 GV.
- Above 86.5 GV the indices are delta(Ne/O) = -0.046 +- 0.010, delta(Mg/O) = -0.049 +- 0.011 and delta(Si/O) = -0.040 +- 0.011.
- Their average is -0.045 +- 0.008, which the paper quotes as more than 5 sigma from zero. From this it concludes that Ne, Mg and Si are a different class of primary cosmic rays from He, C and O.
- The He, C, O comparison and the O flux come from earlier AMS papers (the He/C/O paper, S11), not from this one.
- Treat this as the paper's empirical statement about rigidity dependence. Which uncertainties are correlated between the Ne, Mg, Si and O fluxes in that average was not checked, because the Supplemental Material was not read.
- Any physical interpretation (source, propagation) is a model step beyond the measurement.
- "At least two classes" is a minimum, not a count.
- I cannot check from here whether the paper has been superseded. No web access, and the skill index was last verified 2026-09-20.
