**Short answer: no to both.**

**1. Positron cutoff: not 5 sigma.** [Documented, C35, C97] The positron flux fit in the AMS positron paper (PRL 122, 041102, 2019) uses a diffuse term plus a source term with an exponential cutoff. It gives a source-term cutoff E_s = 810 (+310, -180) GeV. The abstract states "more than 4 sigma", and the body gives 4.07 sigma. That is the point where 1/E_s = 0 lies in the six-parameter confidence region. The Phys. Rept. review restates the same 4.07 sigma. A 5 sigma claim is not supported.

Caveats on the 4.07 sigma:
- It is a model-dependent fit result. It is not a spectral break seen directly in the data.
- It comes from a six-parameter region scan.
- It has no look-elsewhere correction.
- The 810 GeV value is the cutoff of a parametrized source term. It is not a measured break position.
- Pulsar and dark-matter readings are the collaboration's interpretation. They are not measurements.

**2. Electron flux: no, it does not show the same cutoff.** [Documented, C98, C99] The electron flux is measured to 1.4 TeV (28.1 million events). It is described by a power law with a gentle transition (E0 = 42.1 GeV) and no cutoff. In the review's fits over 41.61-1400 GeV:
- The data neither require nor exclude a charge-symmetric copy of the positron source term. The fitted normalization is f = 0.5 (+1.2, -0.6), with no chi2 improvement over f = 0 or f = 1.
- A power law with an exponential cutoff fitted to the electrons gives 1/E_s = 0.00 (+0.08, -0.00) TeV^-1. Cutoffs E_s < 1.9 TeV are excluded at 5 sigma. This applies to that model and that energy range only. It is an exclusion, not a detection, and it does not cover every cutoff shape.
- The review states that the electron excess is of a different nature from the positron excess.

The "5 sigma" in the electron context is therefore an exclusion of a low electron cutoff. It is not a positron-cutoff significance.

**Scope.** These numbers are in energy, not rigidity. The positron figures are from the 2019 paper and the Phys. Rept. review, covering data to 2017 (about 6.5 years). I had no web access, so I did not check for newer AMS updates. The skill's index was last verified 2026-09-20, so treat "current" status as unconfirmed.
