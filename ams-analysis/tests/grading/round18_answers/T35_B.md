**Short answer: do not assume a single TRD proton rejection for a 100 GeV positron analysis. The number depends on the e± efficiency you accept, and your own analysis has to measure it.**

**[Documented, C67]** The AMS review (Phys. Rept. 894, S01) reports a TRD proton rejection above 1000 at 90% e± efficiency, measured on orbit over 2-200 GeV/c. The review says it improves when the e± efficiency is tightened to 65%. The review gives no number for that tighter point. 100 GeV falls inside the quoted range. Three caveats apply:
- 1000 is a lower bound for that specific selection and efficiency. It is not a general performance figure.
- It is review-level evidence for the review's data set, and it is not tied to your selection.
- I do not quote a figure for the improved rejection at 65% efficiency, because the source does not give one as a number.

**[Documented, C72]** The ECAL gives an independent handle. Λ_ECAL combined with E/p > 0.7 gives a further factor of about 3 in proton rejection when the e± efficiency is tightened from 90% to 65%. The review states this is independent of the TRD rejection. For your analysis, validate that independence yourself.

**[Documented, C24, C25]** Published AMS positron analyses do not rely on a fixed rejection factor. They use an energy-dependent ECAL cut, then a template fit in the (Λ_TRD, Λ_CC) plane. The proton background is determined in each energy bin by that fit.

**[General method / Proposal]** For your analysis:
1. State the target e⁺ efficiency. Rejection is only meaningful as a pair, rejection versus efficiency.
2. Treat the remaining proton contamination as a measured quantity. Take it from a proton-rich control sample (reversed cuts) and a transfer factor, or from the template fit.
3. Validate the proton tails in the lepton-selected sample with orthogonal selections. The protons that matter are the tail, so a Gaussian-core assumption is not acceptable.
4. Keep the TRD training or calibration sample separate from the fit sample. Check stability of the rejection against time, gas and gain conditions, and energy.
5. The TRD does not measure charge sign. The Tracker/magnet alone supplies the e⁺ versus e⁻ distinction, and charge confusion (e⁻ reconstructed as e⁺) is a separate background to estimate from the Tracker tail.
6. Check how the TRD and ECAL information is shared between selection and template. Using the same variables to select and to build the template that measures the result is circular.

**[Unknown/needs input]** Your selection, efficiency working point, data period and TRD calibration state. The rejection that applies to your analysis cannot be determined without them.
