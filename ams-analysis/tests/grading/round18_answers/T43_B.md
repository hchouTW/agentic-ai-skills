**Short answer: neither is fully right; it depends on the construction. The "3.0 whatever the background" claim is true only for a specific construction (CLs, or a flat-prior Bayesian limit), not for the classical limit.**

**What 3.0 is.** For n_obs = 0 the one-sided 95% bound on the total Poisson mean is -ln(0.05) = 3.00 [General method]. This is the B = 0 limit on the signal. It is also the CLs limit, which is background-independent for n = 0 by construction (CLs = CLs+b/CLb = exp(-s)). It is the flat-prior Bayesian limit too.

**Where your colleague is wrong.**
- The classical (frequentist) limit with known background b is s_UL = -ln(0.05) - b, so it shrinks as b grows. For b = 0.5 it is 2.50 (script `poisson_diagnostics.py upper-limit`).
- Feldman-Cousins (95%, n = 0, b = 0.5) gives [0, 2.63], also not 3.0.
- So "3.0 for any background" is wrong for the classical or FC limit. It is right for CLs, which is a different, deliberately conservative convention. It over-covers, so it is not a frequentist 95% interval. Quote it only if you label it CLs.

**Where you are partly right.** The background does matter in the classical and FC constructions. But with n = 0 and b = 0.5 the dependence is weak, and a quoted limit of about 2.5 to 3.0 is not misleading.

**What to do about the 30% background uncertainty (sigma_b = 0.15).**
1. For n = 0 the uncertainty barely matters. Marginalizing the background (Cousins-Highland, a truncated-normal prior with sigma_b = 0.15) gives a CLs limit of 2.996, identical to the known-background value. FC with marginalization gives 2.62, against 2.63 known. Both are [General method] script outputs.
2. A profile-likelihood construction (single bin, Gaussian constraint, seeded toys, 4000 toys, seed 1) gives about 2.67. The asymptotic value (1.35) is far too low at zero counts. Do not use it.
3. Recommended reporting: state the construction and its nuisance treatment explicitly (profile versus marginalized), and vary sigma_b to show the stability.
   - Quote the CLs limit with its expected band as the headline conservative number: observed 3.0, expected median 3.0. The +1 and +2 sigma bands are 4.4 and 5.8.
   - Optionally add the FC or profile result for the frequentist reading.
   - Put the observed and expected sensitivity side by side. At n = 0 the observed limit is at the best-case end, so the expected sensitivity is needed to read it correctly.
4. Do not use S/sqrt(B), Gaussian errors or Wilks here (invariant 6).
5. Check where the 0.5 +/- 30% comes from. If it is a MC or data-control estimate, its tail and its systematic matter more than the Gaussian width, and the prior shape should follow the origin of the uncertainty. A lognormal or gamma prior is better than a Gaussian truncated at 0.
6. Count limit to flux limit: Phi_UL = N_UL/(E*DeltaX), with the exposure uncertainty propagated as a separate nuisance.

**Caveats.** The 0.5 events and the 30% are user-supplied numbers. I did not look up any AMS antinucleus result. There is no published AMS antinucleus candidate count that this answer relies on. The numbers above are generic Poisson arithmetic (poisson_diagnostics.py and likelihood_limits.py, seed 1), not AMS performance.
