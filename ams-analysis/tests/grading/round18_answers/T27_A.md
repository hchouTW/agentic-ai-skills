Not as stated. The published AMS deuteron paper (Properties of Cosmic Deuterons, PRL 132, 261001, 2024) describes an unfolding procedure, not a template fit on a mass distribution. [Documented, C27; main article only, full-text level, Supplemental Material not read]

What the paper says it did:
- Sample: downward-going events with a track through tracker layer L1 and Z = 1 required on L1, upper TOF, inner tracker and lower TOF. It covers 26 rigidity bins from 1.9 to 21 GV, May 2011 to April 2021.
- Separation: an unfolding procedure splits D from the Z = 1 sample (mostly protons). It uses the rigidity and inverse-velocity (1/beta) resolution functions of the TOF and the RICH. These functions come from MC and were checked against data at beta ~ 1, with corrections below 5%. [Documented, C27]
- Main background: He to D fragmentation above L1. It is computed from data, using the 4He + (C, Al) to T + X reaction, and is at most 4%. [Documented, C27]
- The helium-isotope paper (PRL 123, 181102) likewise unfolds the rates with resolution functions in velocity bins for the TOF and the two RICH radiators. [Documented, C28]

Two cautions:
- "Mass distribution" is a general-method framing: m = |Z|R/(gamma*beta), built from R (Tracker) and beta (TOF/RICH). A template fit on mass or 1/beta at fixed R is a sensible textbook design for isotope separation. [General method] I cannot attribute template shapes, fit variables or tail models to AMS. The papers I read describe resolution-function unfolding. [Unknown/needs input beyond that]
- Unfolding with resolution functions and fitting templates built from the same resolution functions are related but not identical. Do not call the published method a template fit unless the paper's text or Supplemental Material says so.
