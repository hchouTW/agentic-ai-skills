No. A constant ratio is consistent with a common source, but it does not prove one.

**What AMS reported.** [Documented, C77] In the AMS review (S01), above 60 GeV the positron-to-antiproton flux ratio is consistent with a constant. A constant fit over 60-525 GeV gives 2.00 +- 0.035 (stat.) +- 0.06 (syst.), with chi2/d.o.f. = 7.2/12. The review reads this as nearly identical energy dependence and "suggests a possible common source". It also says continued data taking is needed to confirm the origin. These are the review's samples: 1.9 million positrons (C95) and 5.6e5 antiprotons (C74). The statement is about the review's energy range and data set, not a general result.

**Why it is not proof.**
- **Consistency is not discrimination.** The fit has chi2/d.o.f. = 7.2/12, so a constant is acceptable. That does not exclude other shapes. Any alternative that predicts a nearly flat ratio over 60-525 GeV fits equally well.
  - Examples are different sources with similar spectra, or a secondary production component that is nearly flat in the ratio.
  - The statistical power to separate such alternatives is limited by the antiproton sample, and the flat-ratio model has not been tested against them.
- **Common origin is not the only way to get a flat ratio.** Matching spectral shapes can arise from similar propagation and energy-loss behaviour combined with a similar injection spectrum. They need not come from one physical source. Positrons lose energy by radiation and antiprotons do not, and the two particles have different masses and different production channels.
- **Different physics could mimic it.** The quantity is a flux ratio in energy, so the ratio of two different physical processes can look constant over a limited range by coincidence.
- **Modelling uncertainties limit any interpretation.** [Documented, C75, C06] The antiproton-to-proton shape similarity is in rigidity, a different variable from the energy used in the positron/antiproton ratio. The review concludes that modelling uncertainties must improve before a definitive interpretation. The review's own statements are interpretation, not measurement.
- **The ratio's uncertainty is not the whole story.** Acceptance, efficiency and systematics do not cancel automatically in a ratio. Each nuisance must be classed as correlated, partial or independent before the quoted systematic can be read as a test of constancy. [General method]

**What would strengthen or weaken the claim.**
- [Proposal] Define a specific alternative (for example a ratio with a free slope or a break) and compare it with the constant model. Report the slope's interval, not only the constant fit's chi2.
- [Proposal] Compare a source-model prediction for the ratio, including its uncertainties from production cross sections and propagation.
- [Proposal] Extend the energy range and statistics, and check the ratio's behaviour at the highest energies, where the antiproton sample is smallest.

[General method] "Constant within errors" is a statement of compatibility. Making it evidence for a shared origin needs a stated metric, a competing hypothesis and a threshold for rejecting it.
