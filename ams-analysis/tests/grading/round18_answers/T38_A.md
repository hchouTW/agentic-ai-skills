Short answer: only with qualification. Quote it as what AMS reports, not as a propagation-free source abundance.

**What the number is.** [Documented, C91; C142; C167] AMS fits the nitrogen flux over the whole rigidity range as a weighted sum of a primary reference (oxygen) and a secondary reference (boron): Phi_N = (0.092 +- 0.002) x Phi_O + (0.61 +- 0.02) x Phi_B, with chi2/d.o.f. = 59/64. The review (Phys. Rept. 894) calls the 0.092 +- 0.002 the N/O abundance ratio at the source, and compares it with the Solar-System value of 0.135 (+0.051, -0.047), which it calls consistent. The AMS nitrogen PRL (121, 051103) gives a slightly different fit, (0.090 +- 0.002) x Phi_O + (0.62 +- 0.02) x Phi_B with chi2/d.o.f. = 51/64 (C142). So the value depends on the paper and the fit version. The Na/Al paper (PRL 127, 021101) repeats the 0.092 and 0.61 as a comparison entry taken from the review; it is not a new result there (C167).

**Why you cannot quote it as a plain source abundance.**
1. The 0.092 is a fit coefficient, not a measured abundance ratio. It is a descriptive two-component fit to measured fluxes (C142).
2. It is conditional on the template choice. Oxygen is assumed to have a purely primary shape and boron a purely secondary shape. The review itself notes that O has the lowest secondary content among He, C and O, and that B is made mostly from primary C and O. The "source abundance" reading rests on this assumption, and chi2/d.o.f. = 59/64 does not test it. A fit with a different reference pair, or a different rigidity range, would give a different coefficient. The same PRL's fit above 60 GV gives 0.083 +- 0.005 (C142).
3. It is called a ratio at the source "without Galactic propagation" by the papers. That is the authors' interpretation, not something this analysis re-derived. Propagation effects (secondary production, energy-dependent escape) are absorbed into the boron term only to the extent that B mimics them.
4. The quoted +-0.002 is the fit's statistical-plus-systematic error under those assumptions. It does not include the model uncertainty from the choice of references.
5. It is a ratio of fluxes at equal rigidity, not of abundances per nucleon. Converting to an abundance comparison involves the Z/A of the two species. Do not equate it with a Solar-System number-abundance ratio without stating this.

**How to quote it.**
- Safe: "AMS finds the nitrogen flux is described by (0.092 +- 0.002) x Phi_O + (0.61 +- 0.02) x Phi_B (Phys. Rept. 894, chi2/d.o.f. = 59/64); the authors interpret 0.092 +- 0.002 as the N/O abundance ratio at the source, consistent with the Solar-System value 0.135 (+0.051, -0.047)."
- Not safe: "AMS measured the source N/O to be 0.092" as a model-independent result. If you use it as a constraint in a propagation or abundance paper, state that it is conditional on O being primary and B secondary, and cite the paper and the fit version (0.092 vs 0.090) you took it from.
