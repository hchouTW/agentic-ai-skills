# D/p flux ratio, 2-20 GV: measurement design

## 0. Framing and what is documented
- Estimand [Proposal]: Phi_D(R)/Phi_p(R) in rigidity bins, 2-20 GV, |Z| = 1, same data period, same R definition (|R|, positive-charge sign), so a ratio of fluxes in R (not in T/A). Quoting vs T/A needs an explicit A (A = 2 for D, A = 1 for p), and the two species then land in different bins; that is a different measurement, not a bin-edge relabelling.
- [Documented, C27, S13] AMS has published the deuteron flux (21x10^6 D, 1.9-21 GV, 26 rigidity bins, May 2011-April 2021, main article only; Supplemental Material not read). The requested 2-20 GV is essentially that range, so this design is a re-description of a published analysis type, not a new one. If the user wants a new result they must say what is new (period, binning, ratio treatment). For "latest/current" status I have no web access, so I cannot claim currency (index last verified 2026-09-20).
- Everything below beyond the C27/C28/C29 clauses is [General method] or [Proposal]. I do not know the internal AMS cuts, templates, bins, efficiencies or systematic sizes [Unknown/needs input], and I do not invent them.

## 1. Why this is an isotope problem, and where the range limits come from
- Mass m = |Z||R|/(gamma beta) [General method]; D/p mass ratio about 2 (the easiest isotope pair, vs 4/3 for He and 7/6 for Li). R from the Tracker only; beta from TOF and RICH. Tracker gives R and charge sign; TOF/RICH give beta; TRD/ECAL play no role in D/p separation.
- Mass resolution: (dm/m)^2 = (dR/R)^2 + (gamma^2 dbeta/beta)^2 - 2 rho (dR/R)(gamma^2 dbeta/beta); gamma^2 growth makes the upper rigidity limit a beta-resolution limit, not a statistics limit. Tails and rho must be validated, not assumed.
- Kinematics for Z = 1 (arithmetic by hand with PDG masses, [General method]; confirm with `scripts/ams_kinematics.py` before quoting):
  - D at R = 2 GV: beta about 0.73 (below the NaF threshold 0.75 [Documented, C70, S01 review scope]), so the lowest bins are TOF-only; D crosses beta = 0.75 near R about 2.1 GV and the aerogel threshold beta = 0.953 near R about 5.9 GV. Protons at the same R are faster (beta about 0.905 at 2 GV), so the species can sit in different velocity detectors in the same R bin; the response must be built per species.
  - At R = 20 GV: D beta about 0.9956, p about 0.9989; 1/beta differs by only about 3x10^-3, i.e. about 5 core widths if the RICH Gaussian-core width 7x10^-4 at beta = 1 [Documented, C28, S12, He isotope paper, cores only, not D-specific] applies. That is a marginal separation: template overlap, tails and the rigidity-beta correlation dominate there, and the upper edge 20 GV needs a demonstrated sensitivity, not an assumption. Expect strong template correlation near the top bins (a diagnostic of the sensitivity edge).
  - Low edge: TOF Delta(1/beta) core width 0.02 [Documented, C28, S12] vs D-p 1/beta difference of order 0.2 at 2 GV, so the separation is easy there; low-R issues are instead albedo, multiple scattering, fragmentation and solar modulation.

## 2. Selection (what AMS did vs what I propose)
[Documented, C27/C29]: downward-going, Tracker track through L1, the same |Z| = 1 required on L1, upper TOF, inner Tracker and lower TOF; rigidity above 1.2 x maximum geomagnetic cutoff in the field of view, with the factor varied (1.0-1.4 in the D paper; effect < 0.1%).
[Proposal]: quality cuts for normal detector conditions, ISS outside the SAA, zenith pointing (defined as in S06, C31 only if the user adopts them); ring containment and quality in the velocity bins using the RICH; a single common selection for D and p so that most selection efficiencies are shared, but each remains a conditional efficiency measured per species and not multiplied as if independent.

## 3. Signal, backgrounds, controls
1. Signal-extraction discriminant [Proposal]: per rigidity bin, fit/unfold in 1/beta (or mass) with isotope templates from the TOF and RICH (NaF, aerogel) resolution functions. [Documented, C27/C28]: AMS unfolded the rates using rigidity and 1/beta resolution functions from MC, validated at beta about 1 with data (corrections < 5% in the D paper); I do not claim the exact template forms.
2. Proton contamination into D (and the reverse) is the tail migration; the D sample is about 10^-2 to 10^-3 of the protons in this region [Unknown/needs input; do not use a number], so proton resolution tails in 1/beta and a wrong-ring or secondary-hit component are the key systematic. A Gaussian-core resolution is not acceptable for this tail (Invariant 4): validate on a pure abundant proton control in the same beta range, with tails.
3. Fragmentation/secondary D: the dominant background [Documented, C27]: He -> D (also 4He + (C, Al) -> T + X, used to calibrate it, up to 4%) above L1; plus heavier-nucleus interactions. Treat as a background with its own data-driven control and cross-section/material variation. Beyond L1, charge-changing losses (survival probability) differ between D and p, so ratio cancellation is not automatic.
4. Charge-sign: positive R only; deuteron-like negative-charge contamination (antiprotons are |Z| = 1 with sign flipped) requires the Tracker sign alone, with a tail check on charge confusion; TRD/ECAL do not help with sign.
5. Albedo/up-going mis-direction at low R (TOF direction confusion tail).

## 4. Efficiency, acceptance, exposure, response
Flux per species: Phi_i = (N_i unfolded)/(A_i eps_i DeltaR T_i), with exposure containing acceptance and livetime (do not apply livetime or the ACC veto twice). For the ratio, acceptance and exposure cancel to leading order only if the same geometrical selection is applied; the species-dependent pieces (trigger, TOF/RICH ring efficiency vs beta, charge-selection efficiency, survival/interaction losses, cutoff/time exposure when D and p are binned in different T/A or beta ranges) must be classified as correlated / partial / independent and verified, not assumed to cancel (Invariant 5). Response: a migration matrix in R and an isotope-misidentification matrix (D <-> p) per species, both with tails; unfold simultaneously or forward-fold, defined before choosing the algorithm; avoid starting from a preferred algorithm.

## 5. Inference and uncertainties
- Template fit with finite-MC statistics (Barlow-Beeston; `scripts/template_fit.py`) per bin or an unfolding of the 1/beta distribution [Proposal]; resolution nuisances (varied, e.g. the width by +-10% as AMS did for the rigidity resolution in S12, C28) with their correlation to the D fraction reported.
- Systematic ledger (one effect per family, no double counting): resolution/tail model (TOF, RICH region, rigidity); selection and ring-quality; D-from-He and heavier-nucleus fragmentation (cross-section, material); survival/charge migration; rigidity scale; geomagnetic cutoff factor; MC acceptance data/MC correction; unfolding; time period/solar modulation; statistical error of templates. Sources combined in quadrature only where independent [Documented practice, C27/C33], with the ratio correlation matrix stated.
- Low counts at the top bins (small D samples): Poisson/toy-validated intervals; no Gaussian errors there.

## 6. Validation and decision triggers
- Closure: inject known D/p into pseudo-data with the actual templates and tails; recovered fraction within a stated bias (for example pull mean < 0.2 sigma, my proposal) per bin, otherwise add an uncertainty or merge bins.
- Cross-checks: alternative discriminant (mass vs 1/beta at fixed R); loose/tight ring quality; NaF vs aerogel region and detector-half splits; time-period splits (also tests solar modulation, relevant in the low-R bins since time dependence enters differently with R); alternative templates. [Documented, C27]: four independent analyses agreed in the published D paper; a second independent analysis is the standard I would aim for here.
- Comparison with the published D/p is plausibility only, not validation, and "reasonable agreement" is not a metric: define a threshold (e.g. consistency within the combined uncertainty, 2 sigma, [Proposal]) and what changes if it fails.

## 7. Output, reporting and unknowns
Report D/p vs |R| with bin-by-bin and correlated uncertainties, the data period and range of validity (the top bins flagged as the sensitivity edge), and the A and R definitions. Unresolved inputs the user must give (they change the design): public vs internal work; data (which period, ISS data only) vs MC; whether the result is vs R or T/A; available per-species resolution functions with tails; control samples in the same beta range; whether the aim is to reproduce S13 or extend it.
