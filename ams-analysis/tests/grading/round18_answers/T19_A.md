**Part 1: TRD proton rejection at 100 GeV**

There is no single "TRD proton rejection factor at 100 GeV". A rejection factor is only defined together with a signal (e±) efficiency, an energy range and a sample.

- [Documented, C67] The AMS review (Phys. Rept. 894, section 1) states that the TRD proton rejection measured on orbit at 90% e± efficiency is **above 1000 in 2-200 GeV/c**. 100 GeV lies inside that range, so ">1000 at 90% e± efficiency" is the most you can quote.
- 1000 is a lower bound for the whole range, not a value at 100 GeV. The source gives no point value there.
- [Documented, C67] The rejection improves if the e± efficiency is tightened to 65%. The source gives no number for this, so I will not supply one.
- The TRD estimator is Lambda_TRD. It is the ratio of the e± to proton log-likelihood over the 20 layers, with values about 0.4 for e± and about 1 for protons in 10-100 GV [Documented, C67].
- Rejection is not a universal property of the detector. It depends on the selection, the e± efficiency, the momentum range and whether the proton sample is clean (Tracker rigidity and ECAL-selected samples differ).
- [Documented, C72] ECAL rejection (Lambda_ECAL with E/p > 0.7) is a separate handle. The review states it is independent of the TRD rejection for its samples. A new analysis should still validate that independence, and should not multiply the two rejections without checking.
- If you need a value at 100 GeV for a design, estimate it yourself from the Lambda_TRD distributions in a proton-enriched data sample (for example ECAL-selected or Tracker-rigidity-selected). Quote it with its efficiency, and validate the tails. [Proposal]

What the public record does say, for the proton flux paper (PRL 114, 171103, published 2015) [Documented, C31]:
- The data are the first 30 months, 19 May 2011 to 26 Nov 2013. Despite the "2015" in your question, the 2015 paper does not use 2015 data.
- The flux covers 1 GV to 1.8 TV in 72 rigidity bins.
- Event selection:
  - live time above 50%;
  - the AMS z axis within 40° of zenith;
  - outside the South Atlantic Anomaly;
  - R > 1.2 × the maximum geomagnetic cutoff;
  - iterative unfolding to correct bin-to-bin migration.
- The abstract quotes 300 million events [Documented, C05].
- No pass number or run list is given.
