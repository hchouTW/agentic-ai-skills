**Why CLs, and why not the classical number.** The observed count (3) sits below the expected background (4.2), a downward fluctuation. The classical limit (3.55) is then tighter than the experiment's sensitivity, because the data happen to fluctuate low. The observed CLs limit (4.90) is lower than the expected median (5.79), but it is still inside the background-only band. The CLs construction divides by CLb, which protects against excluding signals the experiment cannot test. CLs over-covers by design. It is a convention, not a frequentist 95% confidence-level statement, and it should not be labeled as one. The Feldman-Cousins result (lower bound 0, upper 4.18) is a frequentist interval, not a CLs limit. A lower bound of 0 is the expected behavior at small N and is not evidence for zero signal. Report the two as different constructions, and never quote one under the other's name.
