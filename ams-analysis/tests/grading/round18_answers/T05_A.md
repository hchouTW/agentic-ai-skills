**Verdict: not publishable as described; the estimator is incomplete and, depending on what the "bin-by-bin factor" contains, may double count or be biased.** The skeleton is a recognizable diagonal flux estimator, but it needs several additions and one structural change (migration) before the numbers can be trusted. [General method] throughout; I have not seen your cuts, bins, or MC, so all quantities stay symbolic.

## What you describe, written explicitly

Φ_i = C_i · (n_i) / (A_i · T_i · ΔR_i), with C_i the MC bin-by-bin correction.

The target is a differential flux Φ(R) at the top of the instrument, in m^-2 sr^-1 s^-1 GV^-1, as a function of **true** rigidity. Say this estimand first. Then check that every term in the formula maps to it.

## Defects, in priority order

1. **No background subtraction.** The numerator is raw counts after cuts. The estimator should use (n_i − b_i), with b_i a data-driven yield and covariance, for example from template fits. Candidates are charge-migration from neighboring |Z|, fragmentation from heavier nuclei, and lighter-species or lepton contamination (e/p at low R, and p vs d/He). An MC factor does not replace this unless the background is explicitly inside the claimed correction; if it is, it is MC-only and unvalidated against data.

2. **A bin-by-bin factor is a diagonal estimator, and it is invalid under appreciable migration.** The spectrum falls steeply and rigidity resolution has tails, so lower true bins feed higher reconstructed bins. The bias is rigidity dependent and grows with the local spectral index and with the resolution tails. It also depends on the MC input spectrum: if the MC spectrum differs from the true one, the factor is wrong in a circular way. You need a response matrix R_ij = P(R_meas in i | R_true in j, selected), including tails, and either unfolding or forward folding (μ_i = Σ_j R_ij A_j ε_j T_j Φ_j ΔR_j + b_i). Validate on MC closure with an injected spectrum, and check sensitivity to prior, regularization and binning.

3. **Undefined and potentially double counted "factor from MC".** State exactly what C_i contains: selection efficiency, trigger efficiency, migration, survival or interaction losses, or all of them. Then:
   - Acceptance is not efficiency and not exposure. If A is geometric acceptance only, selection and trigger efficiencies must be separate factors, each with a named conditional denominator. If A is already an effective acceptance (A_geo times efficiency), the MC factor must not include efficiency again.
   - Do not multiply marginal efficiencies; use conditional ones, with ordering closure and a combined-efficiency closure in MC. Two 99% cuts only bound the joint efficiency to 98–99% (a floor, not a measurement).
   - A single effect must be treated in exactly one category (veto, calibration, efficiency, response). For example, a time period removed via livetime must not also be corrected as an efficiency.

4. **MC efficiency is not validated against data.** ε_data = SF · ε_MC requires data/MC scale factors from control samples (tag-and-probe, orthogonal subsystem selectors, unbiased or prescaled triggers), measured per rigidity bin, with their statistical and systematic uncertainty. Include the tail regions (charge-estimator and track-quality tails). "Reasonable agreement" is not validation: give a metric and a threshold that triggers an added uncertainty or a method change.

5. **Livetime and exposure are not defined correctly.** T must be quality-approved livetime (not calendar time) and must include the geomagnetic transmission in each rigidity bin: the fraction of time each rigidity is above the local cutoff, with a stated safety factor and field model, varied as a systematic. Acceptance and livetime vary together, so integrate exposure Σ_t A_eff(t) T_live(t) over intervals rather than multiplying averages. Also handle prescales and dead time, and split by hardware era or reconstruction version if the data span one.

6. **Acceptance and material.** State the generation record (surface, solid angle, generated spectrum and weights, geometry and material model, MC and reconstruction version). Survival probability against upstream material and in-detector interactions depends on a cross-section model; vary it and record the variation. Avoid a circular acceptance in which the generated spectrum is tuned to the measured one.

7. **Bin width and bin center.** Dividing by ΔR is correct for a differential flux, but for a steep spectrum the value belongs at a prescribed position (where the flux equals the bin average), not at the arithmetic bin center. State the convention; the exact bin-average factor depends on the spectral shape.

8. **No uncertainty model.** Statistical errors need to come from the unfolded or fitted covariance, not Poisson sqrt(n) on the raw count. Systematics need a ledger with correlations: trigger and selection efficiency, acceptance and material, background and charge confusion, rigidity scale and resolution (scale shifts propagated through migration, not applied to the final spectrum), unfolding and prior, template statistics, cutoff and time dependence, MC statistics and generator. Combine covariances, not variances; never add unknown correlations in quadrature. Adjacent bins are correlated through unfolding and shared systematics; publish the covariance or a reproducible correlation model.

9. **Charge confusion.** Charge-sign contamination (for example e−, p̄, or wrong-sign migration at high R) is a tail phenomenon. Do not estimate it from the Gaussian core of the rigidity resolution; validate the tail on data with a loosened-selection sample.

## Inputs that would change the verdict

- What the MC factor contains and whether the MC response is already normalized to the generated count (if it is a full response-based correction with data-validated efficiencies and background-subtracted counts, defects 1, 3 and 4 shrink).
- The strength of migration: resolution and tail size versus bin width and spectral index. With very wide bins and small tails, a diagonal factor could be adequate for a plausibility check, but that must be shown with MC closure and not assumed.
- Whether the data are real or MC, the rigidity range, and the track configuration and time period.

## Minimum fix list

State the estimand, then use: background-subtracted counts, response-based unfolding or forward folding, a separate conditional efficiency chain with data/MC scale factors, exposure with geomagnetic transmission and a safety factor, the bin-center prescription, a systematic ledger propagated through the response, and closure and stability checks (time, geometry or detector region, track configuration, cut variation, injected spectrum), each with a stated trigger for action. A comparison with published spectra is a plausibility check only. (No AMS-specific numbers are quoted, so no source claims are needed; I have not drawn on documented AMS practice here.)
