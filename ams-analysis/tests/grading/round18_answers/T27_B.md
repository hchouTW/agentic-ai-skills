**Short answer: not as described in the main article. [Documented, C27 (S13, PRL 132, 261001, 2024; main article read, Supplemental Material not read)]** AMS separated deuterons from protons with an **unfolding procedure**, not a template fit on a reconstructed-mass distribution. Do not call it a "mass-distribution template fit".

What C27 documents for the deuteron flux (1.9-21 GV, 26 rigidity bins, May 2011-April 2021):
- Selection: downward-going, track through tracker layer L1, and |Z| = 1 required on L1, upper TOF, inner tracker and lower TOF.
- Separation: D is separated from the Z = 1 sample by unfolding. It uses the rigidity and inverse-velocity (1/beta) resolution functions of the TOF and RICH. These come from MC and are checked at beta ~ 1 with data (corrections < 5%).
- Dominant background: He -> D fragmentation above L1. It is computed from data using 4He + (C, Al) -> T + X (<= 4%).
- Other documented items: acceptance data/MC corrections (< 10% total); cutoff factor varied 1.0-1.4 (< 0.1%); four independent analyses agree.

The helium-isotope paper (S12, C28) used the same style. Its rates were unfolded with resolution functions in velocity bins for the TOF, RICH-NaF and RICH-Agl. The quoted widths are Gaussian cores (TOF Delta(1/beta) 0.02; RICH-Agl 7e-4 at beta = 1), not tails.

[General method, a generic design and not AMS practice] A mass-template fit would use m = |Z||R|/(gamma*beta). It would have templates for D, p and backgrounds, tail validation in data, and a diagnosis of template overlap. Overlap grows as beta -> 1 because of the gamma^2 amplification of the beta error. That is why the velocity resolution, not statistics, sets the upper rigidity limit.
