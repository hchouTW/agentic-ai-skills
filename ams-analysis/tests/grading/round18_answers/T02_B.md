**How the RICH helps.** Mass comes from two independent measurements: the Tracker gives rigidity R and the RICH gives velocity beta, so m = |Z||R|/(gamma beta) [General method]. The RICH measures beta from the Cherenkov opening angle, cos(theta_c) = 1/(n beta), in two radiators. These are NaF (index about 1.33, threshold beta > 0.75) and aerogel (index about 1.05, threshold beta > 0.953) [Documented, C70, S01 review level]. The RICH covers the higher-beta range where the TOF is no longer adequate. The TOF Delta(1/beta) core width is about 0.02 [Documented, C28].

**Isotope analyses.** The AMS isotope analyses (He isotopes C28, deuterons C27) do not cut on a reconstructed mass. They bin in velocity per detector (TOF, RICH-NaF, RICH-Agl) and unfold the rates using rigidity and 1/beta resolution functions. These functions come from MC and are validated at beta of about 1 with data [Documented, C27, C28]. The He-isotope paper varied the rigidity-resolution width by 10% as a systematic [Documented, C28]. The RICH-Agl Gaussian-core width is 7e-4 at beta = 1, falling to 6.3e-4 at beta about 0.953 [Documented, C28]. These are core widths only, not tails. The usual RICH velocity resolution is better than about 0.1% at beta about 1 [Documented, C21 for |Z| = 1 in the analysis papers, C70 for |Z| > 1 in the review]. Quote the Z range with it.

**What limits it at high rigidity.** It is the resolution, not the statistics.
- Error propagation gives (dm/m)^2 = (dR/R)^2 + (gamma^2 dbeta/beta)^2 - 2 rho (dR/R)(gamma^2 dbeta/beta) [General method].
- At fixed mass, beta tends to 1 as R grows and gamma^2 grows with it. A fixed dbeta/beta (the RICH Gaussian core of about 0.1% or better) therefore blows up the mass term. The isotope mass distributions overlap, because the rest-mass ratios are small: about 2 for D/p, 4/3 for He and 7/6 for Li [General method].
- This sets an upper rigidity or T/A limit for each isotope pair. The published ranges are consistent with this: D 1.9-21 GV (C27) and He isotopes 1.9-21 GV (C28). I make no further claim about which term sets each upper edge.
- The rigidity resolution (dR/R, which grows with R at high R) adds to the mass error. The R-beta error correlation rho must be validated, not assumed zero [General method].
- The relevant resolutions are non-Gaussian tails: wrong ring, ring from a secondary particle, radiator tile boundaries, ring quality and Tracker-extrapolation error. They are not the Gaussian core. A core width alone is not a validation. The tails need data validation with an abundant known-mass sample (p, 4He) in the same beta range [General method].
- Also limiting are the radiator coverage and thresholds, and the small isotope samples.
- Fragmentation backgrounds matter for D and 3He. An example is He to D above L1 (C27). They are a background with its own control, not a resolution effect.
