**Cite PRL 113, 121101 (AMS, 2014), "High Statistics Measurement of the Positron Fraction in Primary Cosmic Rays of 0.5-500 GeV" (S03).**

**Sample** [Documented, C113]
- Data: the first 30 months, 2011-05-19 to 2013-11-26. That is 1.7 times the statistics of the 2013 paper.
- Bin fit yields: 10.9×10^6 primary positrons and electrons, 3.50×10^6 protons, and 0.64×10^6 positrons.
- Method [Documented, C114]: over 41×10^9 events were analyzed, with a reference-spectra fit per energy bin. The acceptance is the same for e+ and e- and cancels in the fraction.

**Behaviour above about 200 GeV** [Documented, C113]
- The fraction no longer increases with energy.
- The slope fit, slope = c·log(E/E0) above 30 GeV, gives E0 = 275 ± 32 GeV.
- The minimal model with an exponential cutoff has chi2/d.o.f. = 36.4/58 and 1/Es = 1.84 ± 0.58 TeV^-1. The version without a cutoff is excluded at 99.9% C.L.
- The collaboration lists pulsar and dark-matter classes of interpretation. These are its qualitative readings, not measurements of an origin.

**Comparison with the 2013 first result, PRL 110, 141102 (S02)**
- **Range:** 0.5-350 GeV in 2013 against 0.5-500 GeV in 2014.
- **Sample:** 6.8×10^6 e± events in 2013 [C03], taken 2011-05-19 to 2012-12-10, against 10.9×10^6 in 2014 [C113]. The 2014 paper states the earlier result is consistent with the new one.
- **Rise:** the 2013 paper says the rise from 10 to 250 GeV is "not consistent with only secondary production" [C110]. Below 10 GeV the fraction falls, as expected for secondaries. The collaboration also wrote that the behaviour above 250 GeV needs more statistics.
- **Flattening above ~200 GeV:** this appears only in the 2014 paper [C113].
- **Cutoffs:** 760 (+1000, -280) GeV in 2013 [C109] and 1/Es = 1.84 ± 0.58 TeV^-1 in 2014 [C113]. These are model-dependent and defined with different parameters, so do not compare them directly.
