Both papers say there were no earlier rigidity measurements of their flux. The quoted total flux errors are 4.8% for Fe and 5.9% for F, each at 100 GV only.

**Where the F/Si versus B/O comparison comes from.** It is in the fluorine paper, not the iron paper.
- The B/O data are the earlier AMS measurement (the paper's Ref. [11]). Only the paper's fit to them is new.
- The iron paper contains Fe/O, and its slab-model fit also gives a Fe/Si result. It has no F/Si versus B/O comparison.

**What the paper reports** [Documented, C160, C161]
- F/Si was fitted over 28.8-2900 GV with a broken power law, C (R/175 GV)^Delta. Delta1 = -0.34 +- 0.02 below 175 GV and Delta2 = -0.19 +- 0.07 above, so F/Si hardens by 0.15 +- 0.07 above 175 GV.
- The paper compares this hardening with the 0.140 +- 0.025 hardening of the light ratios (Li/C, Be/C, B/C, Li/O, Be/O, B/O). That 0.140 figure comes from earlier AMS papers, not from the fluorine measurement.
- The same fit applied to B/O gives Delta1 = -0.405 +- 0.005 and Delta2 = -0.26 +- 0.03.
- The double ratio (F/Si)/(B/O) was fitted over the full rigidity range with a break at R0 = 9.8 +- 0.9 GV. Above 10 GV it is described by a single power law R^delta with delta = 0.052 +- 0.007, which the paper states is 7 sigma from zero.

**What the paper concludes** [Documented, C161]
- The F/Si rigidity dependence is distinctly different from that of B/O (or B/C).
- The propagation properties of heavy cosmic rays from F to Si therefore differ from those of light cosmic rays from He to O.
- Secondary cosmic rays also have two classes, as primaries do.

**Caveats on the interpretation**
- The result is a difference between two flux-ratio shapes. It is not a measurement of a propagation parameter, and it does not show which mechanism causes it.
- Si is used as the heavy primary because Ne, Mg and Si share the same rigidity dependence above 80.5 GV [Documented, C160]. F is treated as secondary, but fragmentation of heavier species also feeds it. Part of the F/Si versus B/O difference could therefore reflect different parent composition and different path lengths for the heavy species, not only a different propagation law. Separating these needs a propagation model (grammage, cross sections, source terms) [Proposal].
- The paper states the 7 sigma but gives no trial factor or significance details [Documented, C161]. The Si/O input above 86.5 GV comes from the earlier Ne, Mg, Si paper.
- The paper's own caveat applies: the F/Si versus B/O comparison with GALPROP-type predictions is shown only in a figure of the paper. I did not read it, so I make no claim about it.
- Separately, the iron paper's slab-model fit finds the Fe/Si ratio at the source not constant at the 3 sigma level [Documented, C155]. That is a model-dependent statement about sources, not about propagation.
