Source: AMS antiproton paper, PRL 134, 051002 (S09; Supplemental Material text read 2026-10-02, per the skill's source index). Antiproton selection and template-fit text: C177. Trigger efficiency: C175. Scope: the text read, not the 140 flux tables.

**Trigger efficiency** [Documented, C175; Supplemental Material, Detector]. The antiproton trigger efficiency is 80% above 10 GV, rising to 83% at 1 GV. This is a different quantity from the trigger-efficiency systematic in the main article (below 1%, C121).

**Signal template: definition and reason** [Documented, C177]. Only the negative-rigidity sample is used. It contains antiprotons, electrons, light negative mesons (pi-, with a negligible amount of K-) and charge-confusion protons. Mesons occur only below 10 GV, and electrons occur at all rigidities. A charge-confusion estimator reduces charge-confusion protons to below 0.1% in all bins. Signal and background yields are found per rigidity bin by fitting templates to data with floating normalizations. The signal template is always defined from the high-statistics proton data sample. The reason given is that the template variables have the same distribution for antiprotons and protons when both are reconstructed with the correct charge sign. This was verified with Monte Carlo and with antiproton and proton data.

**Templates below and above 2.97 GV** [Documented, C177]. There are two regions, 1.00-2.97 GV and 2.97-41.9 GV.
- Below 2.97 GV: the fit variable is the mass distribution computed from the inner-tracker rigidity and the TOF velocity. A TRD-estimator cut is applied. Electron and pion templates are defined per rigidity bin from data selected with the TRD, RICH and ECAL.
- Above 2.97 GV: the fit variables are the TRD estimator and the RICH velocity. The pi- background is removed by a rigidity-dependent RICH velocity cut. The electron template is defined per bin from ECAL-selected data. Monte Carlo is used to check that the electron template shapes inside and outside the ECAL acceptance are identical.
