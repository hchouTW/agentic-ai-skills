**Short answer: not by itself. "Four iterations because a paper used it" has no validity for your analysis.** [General method]

**Why.** The iteration count is the bias-variance knob of D'Agostini unfolding. Few iterations keep the result near the prior (bias) but give small variance. Many iterations reduce the bias but amplify noise and the anticorrelations between neighbouring bins. The best value depends on your response matrix, binning, statistics, prior spectrum and the steepness of the spectrum. A number tuned for another analysis's response, bins and counts has no meaning for yours.

**What to do** (declare the criterion before looking at the unfolded result, and do not tune it on the final spectrum):
1. **Define the setup first:** the estimand, truth and reconstructed bins, the response orientation and normalization, where the inefficiency lives (in R or in A, not both), and under/overflow and feed-in.
2. **Scan the iteration number on MC pseudo-data.** Use Poisson-fluctuated data and a fluctuated response, with several truth spectra (including distorted, kinked ones not in the prior). Choose the iteration count that minimizes total expected error (bias² + variance), or stop when successive iterations agree within a stated fraction of the statistical error. Note that the scan is circular if you use only the one truth that also supplies the prior.
3. **Closure tests:** nominal MC, reweighted spectrum, distorted spectrum, response perturbations (resolution, tails, scale), and pulls across pseudo-experiments. Trigger for changing the method or adding an uncertainty [Proposal]: a pull width well above 1, or a mean pull beyond about 3 standard errors.
4. **Vary the prior and the iteration count** (for example 3, 4, 6, 10) and quote the spread as a systematic. Check stability against binning.
5. **Propagate the full covariance by toys through the whole unfolding.** Include statistical, finite-MC response, systematic and unfolding-induced bin correlations. Check the adjacent-bin correlations: strongly negative values mean the unfolding is under-regularized (too many iterations).
6. **Compare with a forward-folded fit** if your estimand is a parameter rather than a model-independent spectrum.

If you have the response matrix, `scripts/unfolding_diagnostics.py` (`regularized-scan`, `closure`) and `scripts/statistical_toys.py unfold-scan` can do these scans. All of them are seeded toys, and the results are conditional on the truth you supply.

**What AMS does** [Documented, C23]: in the antiproton flux analysis, the iterative unfolding is stopped when the fluxes of two consecutive steps agree within 0.1%. That is a declared convergence criterion, not a fixed number of iterations. It applies to that paper's analysis (S08, antiproton flux, 1-450 GV), and I do not extend it to other species or to your analysis. Whether your paper's "4 iterations" came from a similar criterion or from a tuning on its own response is [Unknown/needs input]. I would not assume a fixed count carries over.

**Inputs that would change this answer:** your response matrix and its statistics, the number of events per bin, the species and rigidity range, the prior spectrum, and whether the target is a spectrum or a model parameter.
