Do not pick a width: the cut itself is not valid for helium, so there is no width to tune. [General method; detector-and-observables, ECAL `E/|p|` and `E/|R|`]

**Why `E_ECAL/R = 1` does not select clean helium**
- `E/R ≈ 1` is an electron/positron relation. For a lepton, `E_ECAL ≈ pc`, and `E/(|Z|·|R|)` with `Z = 1` is near unity within resolution, bremsstrahlung and leakage.
- For helium, `Z = 2`, so `p = |Z|R/c = 2R/c`. Even for a fully contained electromagnetic shower, `E ≈ 2R` would be the equivalent expectation, not `E = R`.
- Helium does not deposit its momentum in the ECAL as an electromagnetic shower. It makes a hadronic shower (or a minimum-ionizing-like deposit), so the response is a species-dependent hadronic fraction with a wide, non-Gaussian tail. The ratio `E/p` is below 1 on average, with broad tails.
- Hence `E/R = 1` has no physical basis for He, and any width you choose is arbitrary. A narrow cut mostly removes helium; it does not isolate it.
- It is also not a helium discriminant. A cut on `E/R` selects an arbitrary slice of hadronic response and biases the sample, for example toward interacting events, leaving a sample that depends on the cut width and the energy.

**What selects helium**
- Helium identification is by `|Z| = 2`. The Tracker charge estimate is the primary handle, with an independent confirmation from the TOF (and the RICH, where it applies) through the same |Z| on the required layers. Select on the charge-peak window and validate it. [General method]
- Check the leakage from neighbouring charges (H below, Li above) with loosened-cut tests, including the tails of the charge estimator. Charge-peak position and width stability against time, rigidity and geometry must be shown. Mis-ID is a tail phenomenon, so validate the tails and not only the core. See `nuclei-and-isotopes`.
- Rigidity, and its sign if needed, comes from the Tracker/magnet only. The ECAL measures neither charge nor charge sign.
- Use the ECAL, if at all, only as an optional veto or cross-check, never as an `E/R = 1` selector.

**If you still want an ECAL-based cut**
- Define the variable with `Z` stated explicitly (`E/(|Z|R)` with `Z = 2`).
- Take the efficiency from helium data or MC and from a control sample, not from an electron-calibrated width.
- Include it as a conditional efficiency, `ε_ECAL | (other cuts)`. Because its energy is shared with other ECAL variables, treat the correlation explicitly.
- Do not pick the width by tuning it on the sample you are measuring. That is circular.

**Inputs that would change this answer**
- Which analysis you are doing (flux, a He/p ratio, isotope separation).
- Whether you are using data or MC.
- Whether the ECAL cut is meant as a purity cut or as an energy-scale check.
- The rigidity range.
- Which charge and TOF estimators you already use.

If the goal was a clean helium sample, the next step is the charge-window selection and its tail and leakage validation, not an ECAL width.
