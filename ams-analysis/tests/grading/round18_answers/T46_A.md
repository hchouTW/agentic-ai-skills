**Cite PRL 113, 121101 (AMS Collaboration, 2014), "High Statistics Measurement of the Positron Fraction in Primary Cosmic Rays of 0.5-500 GeV".** [Documented, C113; main article only, read 2026-10-02; the Supplemental Material and complete tables were not read.] I had no web access, so I cannot confirm that nothing newer or superseding exists. The skill's index was last verified 2026-09-20.

**Sample [Documented, C113].**
- The data are the first 30 months, 2011-05-19 to 2013-11-26. That is 1.7 times the statistics of the 2013 paper.
- The bin fits give 10.9×10^6 primary positrons and electrons, of which 0.64×10^6 are positrons. They also give 3.50×10^6 protons.
- The 0.5-500 GeV range is in **energy**, not rigidity.
- Over 41×10^9 events were analysed (C114).

**Behaviour above 200 GeV [Documented, C113].**
- The paper says the positron fraction "no longer increases with energy" above about 200 GeV.
- A sliding-window slope fit and a fit of slope = c log(E/E0) above 30 GeV give E0 = 275 ± 32 GeV, with chi2/d.o.f. = 3.9/12.
- The minimal model, a diffuse power law for each lepton plus a common source with exponential cutoff, was fitted over 1-500 GeV.
  - It gives chi2/d.o.f. = 36.4/58 and 1/Es = 1.84 ± 0.58 TeV^-1.
  - The version without a cutoff is excluded at 99.9% C.L.
- These fit results are model-dependent. The pulsar and dark-matter readings are the collaboration's interpretation, not measurements. The paper says the origin needs data up to the TeV region and the antiproton-to-proton ratio.

- The 2014 paper states that the earlier result is consistent with it. It extends the range and statistics rather than contradicting the first result.
- The 2013 and 2014 cutoff values are in different forms and are model-dependent, so do not compare them directly.
- In 2013 the sample was 6.8×10^6 e± plus about 7×10^5 protons, about 8% of the expected data.

**Caution for your framing as a "pure-secondary-production test" [Documented, C110].**
- The explicit secondary-production statement is in the 2013 paper. Below 10 GeV the fraction decreases with energy, as expected from secondary production. From 10 to 250 GeV the rise is "not consistent with only secondary production".
- The 2014 paper does not give a standalone secondary-production test. Quote its measured shape (flattening above about 200 GeV) and its model fits, and state that the excess over secondary production is the collaboration's reading.
- Any secondary-production prediction (propagation model, cross sections, solar modulation) is your own input. Vary it as a nuisance. Do not present it as an AMS result.
- Do not extrapolate beyond 500 GeV.
- The fraction shares acceptance and proton contamination between numerator and denominator. The acceptance cancels in the fraction (C114), but this does not carry over to the separate fluxes, which are in PRL 113, 121102.
- If you also fit the separate fluxes, do not treat the fraction and the fluxes as independent data. They come from the same events and share systematics.
- A later AMS paper, PRL 122, 041102 (2019, S04), gives the positron flux up to 1 TeV. It is a different observable from the fraction.
