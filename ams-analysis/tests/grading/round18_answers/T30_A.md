**Verdict: no, do not clip eigenvalues and move on. Both symptoms point to construction errors, and clipping would hide them.** [General method]

**1. Correlation of -1.3 (priority defect).**
- A valid covariance has |rho| <= 1 for every pair, so -1.3 means the matrix is not PSD. A 2x2 sub-block alone is already invalid.
- Do not clip a violation this large. Clipping is acceptable only when the most negative eigenvalue is at round-off level, about machine precision times the largest eigenvalue. A -1.3 correlation is far above that, so it is a construction error.
- Run `scripts/validate_covariance.py`. It symmetrizes (C + C^T)/2, reports the eigenvalue spectrum, and can show the effect of a clip as a labeled [Proposal] without changing your matrix. Use it to see how large the negative eigenvalue is and which bins it involves.
- Likely causes to check, in this order:
  - Mixed conventions: absolute vs relative entries, bin ordering, or units.
  - Rounded or hand-typed correlation tables, or a correlation computed from the wrong pair of entries.
  - A subtracted term with an implausible correlation.
  - Components added twice.
  - Unfolding-induced anticorrelation between neighbouring bins from an under-regularized inversion. Bins 4 and 5 being adjacent fits this.
- Fix: rebuild the covariance from coherent variations, so that each block is PSD by construction. Propagate through the full unfolding with toys; the sample covariance of the toys is PSD. If the cause is under-regularization, revisit the regularization strength or iteration count. Choose it with a pre-declared criterion, not by tuning on the final spectrum.
- Never repair by dropping correlations, or by combining components in quadrature when their correlations are unknown.
- If the checker shows the negative eigenvalue is only round-off level after you fix the real error, a clip is then fine, but record the change and confirm the fit or chi2 result does not move.

**2. Response column sums of 0.85 plus a separate trigger efficiency (possible double counting).**
- The question to settle is whether the matrix normalization is `conditional_on_selected` (columns sum to 1, inefficiency lives outside R) or `includes_efficiency` (columns sum to the efficiency, at most 1).
- A column sum of 0.85 has two legitimate readings:
  - Inefficiency, meaning true events that are not reconstructed in the selected sample.
  - Migration of true events into reconstructed bins outside the tabulated range, when under/overflow rows are not stored in R.
- Check whether your matrix stores under/overflow rows before calling the 0.85 inefficiency. Run `scripts/validate_response.py` with the declared metadata (orientation, normalization, inefficiency, acceptance, and whether efficiency is applied separately).
- If 0.85 is genuine inefficiency inside R, and you also multiply the counts by a trigger efficiency, you are correcting the same effect twice. Each effect must be applied once. If the trigger efficiency is one factor of the 0.85, then it is partly inside R and partly outside, which is also double counting.
- Keep one `effect_id` per effect, and make sure `response.includes` and `estimator.applies` do not overlap. Exposure, if already a single number, already contains acceptance and livetime, so do not multiply those again.
- Also confirm that the trigger efficiency is not already folded into the exposure or livetime.

**What I would do, in order:**
1. Run `validate_covariance.py` and `validate_response.py` on your actual matrices.
2. Decide the response normalization and apply the inefficiency in exactly one place.
3. Find and fix the source of the -1.3, and rebuild the covariance with toys.
4. Only then check that the covariance is PSD, and clip only if what is left is round-off.

**Inputs that would change the verdict:** the most negative eigenvalue relative to the largest; whether the -1.3 appears before or after unfolding, and whether it is relative or absolute; the metadata of the response matrix (under/overflow stored or not, normalization); and where the trigger efficiency is already applied (R, exposure, or acceptance).
