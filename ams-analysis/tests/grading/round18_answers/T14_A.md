**Short answer: not automatically. Treat cancellation as a hypothesis to test for each factor, not as an assumption.** (I assume you mean the public-level positron/electron ratio or fraction, in energy, with e+ and e- selected by the same cuts.)

**Documented context.** [Documented, C114] The AMS positron fraction paper (S03) states that the acceptance is the same for positrons and electrons and cancels in the fraction. That is a property of that fraction and that analysis. It is not a licence to cancel everything. [Documented, C115, C116] The separate e+ and e- fluxes in PRL 113, 121102 are built with `Φ = N / (A_eff ε_trig T ΔE)`, a two-step TRD-template fit and a tracker charge-confusion correction. The fluxes therefore carry their own acceptance and efficiency terms. Do not generalize the fraction statement to them.

**Why cancellation is only partial.** [General method] A ratio `N⁺/N⁻ = (Φ⁺ A⁺ ε⁺ T⁺)/(Φ⁻ A⁻ ε⁻ T⁻)` reduces to `Φ⁺/Φ⁻` only if every factor is equal for the two signs. For each factor:

1. **Geometric acceptance and the generation record.** Opposite charges bend opposite ways in the magnet. This is approximately symmetric, but the acceptance is not exactly symmetric if there are detector asymmetries such as dead or noisy channels, alignment, or the layer layout. Test it in MC with e+ and e- generated on the same surface.
2. **Geomagnetic transmission and exposure.** The cutoff and the east-west effect are charge-sign dependent. At low energy e+ and e- see different allowed directions and different livetime-weighted exposure. This is not a safe cancellation below the cutoff-dominated range (see the geomagnetic selection in `charged-cosmic-rays`).
3. **Tracker charge confusion.** This is not a cancellation at all. It is a charge-sign migration with `M(e⁺←e⁻) ≠ M(e⁻←e⁺)` in general, and it is a tail phenomenon. Take it from data-driven or tail-validated MC, not from the Gaussian core of the resolution. Because e- are much more abundant than e+, even a symmetric flip probability moves the e+ sample more.
4. **Background contamination is asymmetric.** Protons (positive) populate the e+ sample, which is the dominant background. Antiprotons are far rarer in the e- sample, so the TRD and ECAL residual contamination differs between the two. The template fit needs separate proton and charge-confusion templates in each sign sample, with shared parameters where justified.
5. **Identification efficiencies (TRD, ECAL shape, E/p).** Physically they are almost sign-blind for leptons, but they are conditional efficiencies sharing the Tracker track and ECAL energy. Do not multiply marginals. Measure the joint efficiency and check closure in MC for both signs. Small differences from material, bremsstrahlung and e+ annihilation (position-dependent) enter here.
6. **Energy-scale and migration.** The energy-scale uncertainty and the energy response (bremsstrahlung tail) are common to both signs, so they cancel in the ratio only if they are fully correlated. The two sign samples have different spectra, so a common scale shift moves the ratio. Classify it as partial, not cancelled.

**Classification to adopt.** [Proposal] Put each nuisance in a ledger as correlated (cancels in the ratio, for example common luminosity or livetime), partial (energy scale, shared selection, geomagnetic cutoff), or independent (sign-dependent charge confusion, proton contamination, statistics). Verify each class with MC symmetry tests (e+ and e- generated identically), data splits, and closure. Propagate the numerator-denominator covariance. Do not combine the pieces in quadrature.

**What to do.**
- State the estimand (fraction `e⁺/(e⁺+e⁻)` or ratio `e⁺/e⁻`) and the variable (energy, with the binning related to the resolution).
- Show the cancellation per bin and per factor, using the symmetry and closure tests above.
- Fix the dominant sign-dependent terms (proton and charge confusion) explicitly instead of hoping for cancellation.
- Quote an acceptance-ratio systematic from the residual MC asymmetry, with a stated trigger for changing the method (for example, an asymmetry that exceeds the statistical error in a bin).
