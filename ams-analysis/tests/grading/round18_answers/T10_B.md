**Setup [General method; user-supplied: dR/R = 5%, dbeta/beta = 0.1%, R = 10 GV, |Z| = 1, errors uncorrelated]**

Mass is m = |Z||R|/(gamma*beta). First-order propagation (detector-and-observables): (dm/m)^2 = (dR/R)^2 + (gamma^2 dbeta/beta)^2 (+ cross-term, rho = 0 assumed; verify). The gamma^2 factor matters because beta depends on the mass hypothesis. At fixed R = 10 GV, p = 10 GeV/c for both species (PDG masses 0.93827 and 1.87561 GeV). The numbers below come from `scripts/ams_kinematics.py mass-resolution`.

| Species | beta | gamma | gamma^2 dbeta/beta | dm/m (quadrature) | dm (GeV) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| proton | 0.99563 | 10.70 | 0.115 | 12.5% | about 0.117 |
| deuteron | 0.98286 | 5.42 | 0.029 | 5.8% | about 0.109 |

**Result.** The mass resolution is about 12.5% for a proton and about 5.8% for a deuteron, or about 0.11 GeV/c^2 in absolute terms for both. The mass difference is 0.937 GeV, so the D and p peaks are about 8 sigma apart in the Gaussian core. Mass is a poor discriminant here.

**Where each term dominates.**
- For the deuteron, the 5% rigidity term dominates and the velocity term is small (2.9%).
- For the proton, the velocity term (11.5%) is larger than the rigidity term (5%) because gamma^2 is 115.

**Caveats.**
- This is a Gaussian-core, first-order estimate. It is not AMS performance. The inputs are your numbers, not documented AMS values.
- A D/p measurement is a tail problem. The deuteron flux is only of order 1e-2 of the proton flux (my recollection, not a ledger value), so the proton tails under the deuteron peak, from non-Gaussian beta and rigidity tails, wrong rings and fragmentation, decide the result more than the 8-sigma core separation does.
- Validate the tails in data, for example with abundant protons in the same beta range. Do not accept "reasonable agreement" as validation.
- Check rho between dR/R and dbeta/beta. A shared track fit would make it non-zero.
- Verify that 0.1% is the right beta resolution for the radiator and beta range used. In AMS documents it is a core value for |Z| = 1 or higher charge, not a tail.
- The separation degrades quickly with rigidity because gamma^2 grows. At fixed R it is easier for heavier particles.
- A proposal: a template fit in 1/beta at fixed R, or in mass, with finite-template nuisances, is better than a mass cut.
