# Design: 10-500 GV positron flux analysis (AMS-02)

Labels: [Documented, Cxx] = claim in source-index; [General method]; [Proposal]; [Unknown/needs input]. No AMS-internal numbers (rejection factors, charge-confusion rates, bins, livetimes, systematic sizes) are supplied; they are symbolic or user inputs.

## 0. Framing and assumptions

- **Estimand.** The "10-500 GV" range mixes variables. Published AMS lepton fluxes are in energy, not rigidity [Documented, C25, C115; S04/S05 use energy at the top of AMS]. Positron rigidity is `R = pc/(Ze)`, and for `e+` (|Z|=1, m negligible) `E ≈ |R|·(1 GeV/GV)`, but ECAL measures energy and the Tracker measures rigidity, with bremsstrahlung and energy loss making them differ. Decide first: flux binned in true energy `E` (the published convention, recommended for comparison), or in `|R|`. Below I treat it as a differential flux `Φ_{e+}(E)`, with 10-500 GV interpreted as roughly 10-500 GeV. This is an assumption to confirm. [Unknown/needs input: energy vs rigidity; public-data-only or collaboration work; data period and hardware era; separate flux vs fraction.]
- A **positron flux** is distinct from the **positron fraction** `e+/(e+ + e-)`. Acceptance cancels in the fraction only for a same-selection, same-acceptance construction [Documented, C114]; it does not cancel automatically in the separate flux, which needs its own `A_eff`, efficiencies, exposure and charge-confusion correction [Documented, C115, C116]. Deliberately a flux here, with the fraction as a cross-check.
- Plan: start from the estimand, observable space and response, then the algorithm. Do not start from "a BDT / a template fit".

## 1. Measurement target and chain

`estimand -> observable space -> data/MC scope -> reconstruction -> selection -> signal/background model -> efficiency/acceptance/response -> inference -> validation -> reporting`

Flux per bin (symbolic) [General method, consistent with C115, C95]:
`Φ_i = N_i^{e+} / ( A_i ε_i (1+δ_i) T_i ΔE_i )` with `N_i` the corrected signal count, `A_i` acceptance, `ε_i` conditional efficiency product, `δ` correction factor, `T_i` exposure time including livetime and geomagnetic selection, and `ΔE_i` bin width. If exposure is already `A·T`, do not multiply twice. Exposure, acceptance, livetime and efficiency are different quantities.

## 2. Subsystem roles (independent evidence)

- **Tracker + permanent magnet:** the only source of charge sign, plus rigidity and |Z|=1. TRD, ECAL, TOF, RICH do not measure charge sign.
- **TRD:** e/p separation via transition radiation (Λ_TRD, a log-likelihood ratio); not a charge-sign detector.
- **ECAL:** shower shape (Λ_ECAL), energy, `E/|p|` compatibility between ECAL energy and Tracker rigidity.
- **TOF/ACC/RICH:** direction, β, |Z|, veto; RICH is limited for lepton ID.
- Correlations to track: ECAL shape and `E/p` share the ECAL energy; Tracker charge and `E/p` share the track; TRD and ECAL are largely independent physics.

## 3. Reconstruction and selection [Proposal, framed on documented practice]

1. Run/data quality, livetime accounting, geomagnetic selection (cutoff with safety factor) so that the sample is primary at the chosen energy; at 10 GeV and above the geomagnetic effect matters mostly at the low edge (secondary/albedo leptons). Selection must be stated and varied.
2. Single downward-going track, |Z|=1 (Tracker, cross-checked with TOF), track quality and fiducial cuts (geometry defines `A_geo`, not efficiency; do not count a geometry cut also as an efficiency loss).
3. Lepton identification: energy-dependent cut on Λ_ECAL and `E/p` compatibility (documented practice removes most hadronic background before the fit [Documented, C24, C26]); keep TRD for the fit variable, not both defining the sample and the template (ECAL variables used both to define the sample and to derive the template that measures it is a listed failure mode).
4. Charge-sign: positive rigidity sample for the signal; the negative sample is needed for the electron-derived templates and efficiency corrections.

## 4. Signal and backgrounds: ledger

| Background | Origin and entry | Estimator | Control / transfer | Closure |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Protons as leptons (dominant; orders of magnitude above positrons) | hadronic showers with large EM fraction, TR fluctuations | template fit in the TRD estimator (and ECAL) plane per energy bin | proton-rich sample with reversed ECAL/TRD cuts; check signal contamination of that control | inject-and-recover; alternate proton shapes |
| Charge confusion `e- -> e+` | Tracker tail (not the Gaussian core), bad hit assignment, secondary tracks | charge-confusion estimator (e.g. BDT Λ_CC, as published practice [Documented, C24]) + MC template validated on test-beam e± and flight e- | abundant electron sample; tail probability vs energy; extrapolation to tight selection tested | injected confusion; sign-flip tail vs rigidity; rate must be validated in the tail |
| Secondary/albedo leptons | geomagnetic, interactions | cutoff-based selection; time/geometry splits | low-energy edge | stability vs cutoff safety factor |
| Bremsstrahlung / photon conversion | changes the track-shower matching and energy response | MC with data validation; part of the energy response matrix | e- control | response perturbation |

Documented method for orientation [Documented, C24, C25; S04]: template fit in the two-dimensional (Λ_TRD, Λ_CC) plane with three templates (correct-sign lepton, correct-sign proton, charge-confusion lepton); positron signal template from electron data at low energy and MC at high energy; charge-confusion template from MC checked with test-beam e± and flight e-; flux in energy at the top of AMS with data/MC efficiency corrections from the electron sample; bin widths at least twice the energy resolution; unfolding for small migration; energy-scale uncertainty as an uncertainty on bin boundaries. This is the published S04 approach; my design follows it in spirit and is not claimed to be identical to it, and Supplemental details are not reproduced.

## 5. Efficiency, acceptance, response

- Conditional efficiencies `ε_total = ε_1 · ε_2|1 · ε_3|12 ...` (trigger, reconstruction, track quality, charge/|Z|, ECAL/TRD ID); never a product of marginals without a factorization-bias check, ordering closure, and combined closure in MC. Fréchet bounds are bounds, not estimates.
- Methods: tag-and-probe or orthogonal selectors; electrons are the natural control (same acceptance, opposite sign), but do not transfer an electron scale factor to positrons without justifying that charge-asymmetric effects (charge confusion direction, e+/e- interaction differences, proton contamination differences) are accounted for as a separate nuisance.
- Acceptance/exposure from a documented MC generation record (surface, solid angle, generated spectrum, physics list, geometry); reweight to the measured spectrum without circularity; apply once, not both in the response matrix and in `A`.
- Energy response: includes bremsstrahlung tail; build `R_ij` (true energy to reconstructed energy), declare orientation/normalization and whether inefficiency is in `R` or in `A`, not both. Include charge-sign migration `e-` to `e+` as an explicit row/column.
- Bin centering: state a spectral-shape-dependent prescription rather than the arithmetic center.

## 6. Inference

`μ_i = Σ_j R_ij A_j T_j Φ_j Δx_j + Σ_k b_ik`, with the binned Poisson likelihood and auxiliary constraints from control regions (not priors unless Bayesian). Template fit per energy bin: Barlow-Beeston finite-template nuisances (full BB or light variant), template provenance without circular use of the fitted data, smoothing/morphing in energy, cross-contamination of the proton template by signal, identifiability (parameter correlations near ±1), goodness of fit (pulls, toy-based p-value).

- Positron flux needs a model-independent spectrum: unfold (iterative Bayesian with a declared stop criterion, as AMS practice stops at 0.1% change [Documented, C23, for S06/S08 flux analyses; do not attribute it to the lepton papers]; or regularized likelihood) vs forward-fold if the aim is model parameters (index, break, source-term cutoff). Bin-by-bin correction only if migration is shown negligible (a steep spectrum can hide migration).
- Unfolding validation: nominal and reweighted closure, distorted-spectrum stress tests, response perturbations, pseudo-experiment pulls/coverage, binning stability, bias-variance vs iterations, toys through the full unfolding; vary the prior and quote the spread. Choose iterations before looking at the final spectrum.
- High-energy bins (low counts at the upper edge, tens to hundreds of GeV): use exact Poisson or toy-calibrated intervals, no Gaussian errors or Wilks; a trial factor for any spectral-feature claim (break/cutoff position is non-regular, so toys over a predefined range) [General method].

## 7. Systematics ledger (classify each as correlated, partial or independent; verify)

Acceptance and MC generation; trigger and reconstruction efficiency (data/MC scale factors); ID selection (repeat the analysis with varied cuts; the published approach does this many times [Documented, C108]); proton-template shape/normalization; charge-confusion tail normalization (the dominant high-energy concern); template statistics; energy scale (bin-boundary shift) and resolution/unfolding; geomagnetic selection; material and interaction cross sections; time stability across the data period (split by period and geometry). Do not count one effect in two categories (veto, calibration, efficiency, response). Do not quadrature-combine components whose correlations are unknown; carry the covariance and check it is PSD.

## 8. Validation and triggers

Each check needs a metric and a trigger for changing the method or adding an uncertainty ("reasonable agreement" is not validation): (a) closure in MC per energy bin: |data-derived/truth| within the statistical error, else add a closure systematic; (b) selection variation stability across loose/tight cuts: change beyond the statistical expectation becomes a systematic; (c) alternate discriminant (ECAL-only vs TRD-only chain) and alternate templates; (d) electron flux through the same chain compared to the published electron measurement as a plausibility check only; (e) positron fraction from the same events (shared covariance), compared with the published fraction as plausibility only; (f) period/geometry/charge-sign splits; (g) signal injection including zero; (h) charge-confusion tail validated on abundant electron data with extrapolation tested; (i) fit-bias across the signal-strength range.

Blinding/iteration: predefine the selection, binning and discriminant; record all iterations. Repeated tuning on the same data inflates agreement.

## 9. Reporting

Report the flux as a function of the stated variable with statistical, systematic and, if relevant, joint covariance (not per-bin percentages as if independent), the response, exposure, and the model-dependent bin-center prescription. Interpretation (pulsar, dark matter, source-term cutoff) is a separate model step; the published cutoff statements are the collaboration's model-dependent results [Documented, C35/C97 in the review, minimal-model, not directly comparable across papers] and must not be extrapolated outside the measured range. If the upper edge (500 GV/GeV) has few events, say so and give exact intervals.

## 10. Inputs that would change the design

Energy vs rigidity variable; public vs internal work; data period/hardware era; whether templates and a generation record exist; charge-confusion control sample and tail validation; binning and resolution; whether the target is the flux, the fraction, or both (joint covariance); expected counts per bin at the top edge.
