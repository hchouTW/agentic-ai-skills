There is no single universal number: the maximum detectable rigidity (MDR) is defined as the rigidity where delta R/R = 1, and it depends on the track configuration (which layers are used) and on the particle charge |Z|. It also depends on how it is defined, so a value without its configuration is not an AMS performance claim.

Published values, for the full nine-layer span L1 to L9 (about 3 m lever arm; L1 at the top, L2 above the magnet, L3-L8 in the bore, L9 above the ECAL):

- Protons and other |Z| = 1 particles: 2 TV [Documented, C21; C63]
- Helium: 3.2 TV, quoted as 3.2 +- 0.16 TV (first 30 months) [Documented, C61]. It is also 3.2 TV with 5% uncertainty in the later He/C/O paper [Documented, C62].
- Carbon 3.7 TV, oxygen 3.4 TV, iron 3.7 TV [Documented, C63; carbon and oxygen also C62].

Why it varies with charge: the MDR is larger for higher |Z|. Rigidity resolution at high R is set by spatial resolution and alignment, with delta R/R growing roughly linearly with R. At low R it is dominated by multiple scattering. [General method]

Caveats:
- These numbers are for the L1-L9 configuration. Inner-tracker-only tracks (L2-L8) or L1-L8 and L2-L9 subsets have a smaller lever arm and a lower MDR. I do not quote those numbers because they are not in the source ledger. [Unknown/needs input]
- The MDR is not the usable range. The flux results reach about 3 TV (for example helium up to 3 TV), where the rigidity resolution has non-Gaussian tails and the rigidity-scale systematic is large. The helium paper gives a Gaussian-core width of 64% in 1/R at 2 TV, with tails and an absolute rigidity-scale uncertainty that dominate at the highest R [Documented, C61, C60].
- Do not use a single MDR to assess charge confusion or tail behavior. Those are tail phenomena that need their own validation.
