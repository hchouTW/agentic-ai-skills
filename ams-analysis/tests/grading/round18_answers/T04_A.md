The Tracker measures signed rigidity R = pc/(Ze) from track curvature, so the relative error is δR/R = δ(1/R)·R. Two effects with different R-dependence set it, which is why it degrades at both ends. [General method, with detector description from detector-and-observables]

**Low rigidity: multiple Coulomb scattering.** Material in the track path (silicon, supports, and other material crossed) scatters the particle. The scattering angle scales as about 1/(βp), with p = |Z|R/c. The resulting curvature error gives δR/R that is roughly independent of R at high β. It grows at lower β, because scattering goes as 1/(βp) and β falls below 1 for slow particles. This term sets a floor on resolution at low R. Secondary effects are hard scatters, delta rays and nuclear interactions. These create non-Gaussian tails. They matter for charge confusion and for the migration at low R, and the Gaussian core does not describe them. Particles near the geomagnetic cutoff are also slow and affected most.

**High rigidity: finite spatial resolution and alignment.** A stiff track has a small sagitta s ~ B L^2 / (8 p). A fixed hit position error σ_x, plus alignment error, gives δR/R ∝ R (roughly σ_x·R/(B L²), reduced with more layers N). The larger the lever arm and the more layers, the better the resolution. It degrades linearly with R, and at the maximum detectable rigidity (MDR, where δR/R = 1) the curvature is comparable to its own error. Beyond that, the measured curvature can be consistent with zero or with the wrong sign. This is the origin of charge confusion at high R, a tail effect. Alignment and field-map errors act coherently and also shift the rigidity scale.

**Crossover and where the numbers sit.** The minimum of δR/R lies where the two terms are equal. Its position depends on |Z|, β and the track configuration (inner tracker versus full span, which layers are used). There is no universal value. [Documented, C21, C63] The published MDR is 2 TV for |Z| = 1 with the full nine-layer span (L1 to L9, 3 m lever arm). The review lists 3.2 TV for He, 3.7 TV for C and Fe-like heavier nuclei (C63), and ΔR/R of about 0.1 below 20 GV. These values apply only to that nine-layer configuration and those species. **Consequences for analysis.**
- Resolution is a function P(R_meas | R_true, |Z|, config) with tails, not a single sigma. Unfold it, or fold it, in the response. Do not use the Gaussian core.
- A rigidity-scale shift or a resolution bias is amplified by the local spectral index of a steep spectrum. Validate the scale with a physical control, not MC agreement alone.
- Check partial-track consistency (for example upper versus lower segments) and residual tails per layer, region and time.
- For ratios or charge-sign work (e+/e-, antiprotons), the high-R tail is the dominant systematic. Require a data-driven or calibrated-simulation tail check.
