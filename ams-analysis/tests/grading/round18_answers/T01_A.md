**Charge sign in AMS-02, and why the TRD cannot provide it**

**How the sign is determined [Documented, C13; General method for the physics].** The sign comes only from the Tracker inside the permanent magnet. A charged particle ionizes the silicon layers, and the strip signals become clusters and hit coordinates. A track fit using the field map gives the curvature, so the rigidity is R = pc/(Ze) = p/Z in GV, a signed quantity. The sign of the curvature in the bending projection, under a stated field and orientation convention, is the charge sign. The same convention fixes sign(R) = sign(Z).

|Z| is a separate observable. It comes from energy loss and photon yield in the Tracker, TOF and RICH (dE/dx ∝ Z²), and it carries no sign. In an analysis, TOF, RICH and the TRD supply direction, β, |Z| and particle identification. They never supply the sign. The sign comes from the Tracker alone, and the other subsystems are used to confirm |Z| = 1 and to separate particle species.

**Why the TRD cannot do it.**
1. Transition radiation yield depends on the Lorentz factor γ. It is the same for e+ and e- at the same γ, and the same for p and p̄. So the TRD estimator (a per-layer log-likelihood ratio, e hypothesis over p hypothesis) separates species, for example lepton from hadron. It is blind to the sign of the charge.
2. The TRD has no magnetic bending of its own, so it has no curvature measurement from which a sign could be derived. Its ionization samples give a quantity that depends on |Z| and γ, again with no sign.
3. Consequently, an e+ versus e- measurement needs the TRD for e/p rejection and the Tracker for the sign. Both are needed, and neither replaces the other. The same holds for antiprotons: the TRD rejects electrons in the negative-rigidity sample, and the Tracker supplies "negative".

**Consequence.** Wrong-sign events are a Tracker problem, called charge confusion. They arise from limited rigidity resolution, hit-position errors, large-angle scattering and interactions in the material. They cannot be removed by lepton/hadron ID. They are a tail phenomenon, so any estimate must be validated on the tails of the resolution and not on its Gaussian core. This is done with data-driven or calibrated-simulation checks, for example a charge-confusion estimator built from track χ², rigidities from different layer combinations, and nearby hits, as the published e±/p̄ analyses use (C22, C26).
