There is no single AMS "safety factor". The public AMS flux papers most often require the particle rigidity to be above 1.2 times the maximum geomagnetic cutoff in the field of view. The cutoff is computed from the IGRF field. The factor is varied as a systematic.

- [Documented, C20; C31] The proton and other primary-flux analyses (S08, S06) require rigidity above 1.2 times the maximum geomagnetic cutoff.
- [Documented, C24] The positron paper (S04) states the same requirement in energy.
- [Documented] The factor is then scanned as a systematic. The antiproton analysis scans 1.2 to 1.4 (C23). The deuteron analysis and the proton and helium time-structure analysis scan 1.0 to 1.4 (C27, C49). The helium papers use 1.2 times the IGRF cutoff and a 1.0 to 1.4 scan (C59, C60, C62).
- [Documented] This is not universal. The daily proton and helium analyses use a cutoff derived from AMS data (C51, C54). The Bartels-rotation nuclei analysis uses 30-degree pointing (C56). So no single selection is the AMS standard.
- [Unknown/needs input] The exact internal cutoff-computation settings are not public here.

The 1.2 factor is a documented choice in specific papers and must not be treated as a universal AMS rule. For a particular result, check that paper's own selection.
