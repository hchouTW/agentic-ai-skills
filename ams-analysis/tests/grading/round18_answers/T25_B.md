**Short answer.** Charge-confusion protons (positive-rigidity protons reconstructed with negative rigidity) were separated in AMS by a dedicated Tracker-based charge-confusion estimator, and any residual was handled as a template in the fit to the negative-rigidity sample. Charge sign comes only from the Tracker and magnet. TRD, TOF, ECAL and RICH do not measure charge sign, so they cannot separate these events; they suppress electrons and pions. The three published sources differ in detail, so I keep them apart. Everything below is from the main articles and the Supplemental Material text. Tables and figures were not transcribed.

**What the published analyses did**

- **2016 paper (PRL 117, 091103; [Documented, C22], main article only):**
  - The fit used the negative-rigidity sample.
  - The fit variables were the TRD estimator Λ_TRD (a per-layer log-likelihood ratio of the electron to proton hypothesis) and a BDT charge-confusion estimator Λ_CC.
  - Λ_CC combines track χ², rigidities from different layer combinations, hits near the track, and the TOF and Tracker charges.
  - Templates were used in three overlapping rigidity regions: 1.00-4.02, 2.97-18.0 and 16.6-450 GV.
  - The antiproton signal template was taken from high-statistics proton data. This is valid because the template variables are the same for antiprotons and protons when both have the correct charge sign. The paper checked this against MC and against p̄/p data.
  - Electron/π⁻ and charge-confusion-proton templates came from data and MC.
  - The charge-confusion MC was checked with 400 GV proton test-beam data.
  - That test-beam check addresses the tail directly. The Gaussian core of the rigidity resolution is not enough to estimate charge confusion.
- **2025 solar-cycle paper (PRL 134, 051002; [Documented, C177], Supplemental Material text only):**
  - Only the negative-rigidity sample is used. It contains antiprotons, electrons, π⁻ (below 10 GV) and charge-confusion protons.
  - The charge-confusion estimator, using the technique of an earlier AMS paper, reduces charge-confusion protons to a negligible level, below 0.1% in all rigidity bins. This bound holds after the estimator cut. It is not the rate before the cut.
  - Charge confusion matters only at higher rigidity. Electrons are present at all rigidities.
  - The template-fit regions differ from the 2016 ones: 1.00-2.97 and 2.97-41.9 GV.
  - Low rigidity uses the mass distribution from the inner-tracker rigidity and the TOF velocity, plus the TRD estimator. High rigidity uses the TRD estimator and the RICH velocity.
  - A RICH velocity cut removes π⁻.
  - Electron templates are defined per bin from ECAL-selected data.
- **Residual systematics:** the finite statistics of the background template are a separate uncertainty. They are 1.5% at 1 GV and below 1% above 3 GV in the 2025 paper [Documented, C121]. In the 2016 paper, template shape contributes 12% at 450 GV [Documented, C23]. These belong to their own papers and should not be mixed.

**How the separation works, step by step**

1. Select the negative-rigidity, |Z| = 1 sample with the TRD, TOF and Tracker charge consistency requirements.
2. Reject electrons and pions with the TRD, ECAL and RICH. The antiproton is hadron-like and also has β consistent with its rigidity.
3. Suppress charge-confusion protons with the Λ_CC cut. This uses redundant Tracker information: rigidity from different layer combinations, χ², and extra hits near the track.
4. Fit the remaining templates for signal, electron/π⁻ and residual charge confusion, with floating normalizations, per rigidity bin.
5. Convert yields to a flux by iterative unfolding and acceptance folding, which also corrects migration [Documented, C23, C120].
