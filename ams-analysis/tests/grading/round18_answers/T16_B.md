**Short answer: forward-fold.** The estimand is a model parameter (the break position, with index change and smoothness), and the high-rigidity bins have few counts. [General method; the method table in `inference-and-unfolding`]

**Why forward-folding fits this case**
- Forward folding uses the raw counts, so there is no regularization or iteration bias on a feature as subtle as a break. Unfolding at low statistics trades variance for bias, and regularization smooths exactly the kinks you want to locate.
- It uses a Poisson likelihood per bin. This is valid at low counts, where the Gaussian errors from an unfolded spectrum are not.
- It includes the response and the tail migration explicitly. Steep spectra push abundant low-R events upward into higher bins, so the migration matrix matters at high rigidity.
- Unfolding adds strongly anticorrelated bins, and a fit to the unfolded spectrum needs the full covariance. Dropping it or combining per-bin errors in quadrature is a known failure mode.

**Model**
- Use `mu_i(theta, eta) = sum_j R_ij A_j T_j Phi_j(theta) dx_j + sum_k b_ik`.
- `Phi(theta)` is a smoothly broken power law with parameters for the break position, the index change and the break smoothness.
- Apply the response once. Choose either conditional `R` with inefficiency in `A`, or `R` that includes efficiency, and not both. Exposure already contains acceptance and livetime.
- Make the nuisances `eta` explicit: acceptance, efficiency, rigidity scale, resolution and tails, and background.

**Caveats**
- The result is conditional on the parametrization. A broken power law cannot represent unmodeled structure, so compare alternative families (smooth versus sharp break, a curvature-only model, a single power law) and treat the break position as model-dependent.
- A forward-folded fit does not give a model-free spectrum. If a spectrum is also needed, publish the response and the counts so others can forward-fold, or unfold separately and do not fit the break to the unfolded points without the covariance.
- Wilks does not apply to the break significance, because the break position is not identifiable under the no-break hypothesis. Use toys and a trial factor over a predefined range of break positions.
- Use profile-likelihood or constructed intervals for the break position and check their coverage with toys. Near the edge of the range or with few counts, the interval may be asymmetric, unbounded or multimodal, so do not use Gaussian errors or asymptotics unless toys validate them.
- Validate the whole chain with seeded pseudo-experiments:
  - Injection and recovery of the break position and its bias.
  - Pulls and coverage.
  - Response perturbation (resolution, tails, scale).
  - Stability against binning and the fit range.
- Set the criteria that trigger a change of method or an added uncertainty before looking at the data.

**When to unfold instead**
- Unfold only if a model-independent spectrum is the deliverable and the statistics support it.
- If you do, declare the iteration or regularization criterion in advance, and choose it on pseudo-data that include break-like and distorted spectra, not on the final spectrum.
- Vary the prior and quote the spread, test closure, and carry the full covariance.
