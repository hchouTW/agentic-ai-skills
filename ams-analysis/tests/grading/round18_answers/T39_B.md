**Method [Documented, C80, review-level summary of Phys. Rept. 894]:** AMS measures inelastic cross sections in flight, from nuclear survival probabilities, not from a dedicated beam.

- Because of the ISS attitude, AMS points horizontally in this sample. Nuclei therefore cross the detector in both directions, L8 to L1 and L2 to L9.
- The seven inner tracker layers L2-L8 define the particle (dE/dx charge and rigidity). The charge distributions in L2-L8 are compared with those in L1 (or in L8 and L9), which gives the survival probability.
- The interaction probability between L2 and L8 (below 3%) is obtained by comparing upper- and lower-TOF charges. It is combined with the L1-L2 and L8-L9 survival probabilities to give the L1-L9 survival.
- The material is path-length averaged over the acceptance: about 73% carbon and 17% aluminium by mass, with small amounts of Si, O, H, Na, Au and others. This is not a material budget.
- A dedicated Geant4 Monte Carlo with the Glauber-Gribov inelastic cross sections varied by ±10% is compared with the data, and the best-agreeing value is chosen.
- For helium, a few-percent discrepancy at low rigidity (below about 30 GV) led to a modified Glauber-Gribov model matched to the data over the full rigidity range. Below 30 GV the measured He rigidity dependence is used to modify the model for heavier nuclei.
- In the helium flux paper [Documented, C60] the Glauber-Gribov cross sections were scaled by 1.05-1.20.

**What was obtained [Documented, C80]:**
- Survival probabilities were measured for Li, Be, B, C, N, O, Ne, Mg and Si.
- The inelastic cross sections on carbon, averaged over 5-100 GV, are constant within the accuracy.
- Before AMS, only He and C projectiles had been measured, and only below 10 GV. The review says no other measurements exist except He-on-C and C-on-C.
- The ledger says the cross-section values appear only in figures and are not transcribed.
- I therefore do not quote numerical cross sections (mb) here. Any number I gave would be invented. Take them from the dedicated AMS paper or the review's figures.
- The Li survival measurement is a separate result [C29]. It complements C80, and I do not claim the two agree.

**Use in the flux analyses [Documented]:**
- The resulting inelastic-interaction systematic is a few percent in the flux papers, for example:
  - He: 1% under 100 GV, 2% at 3 TV [C60].
  - C and O: below 2.2% and 2.7% to 100 GV, rising to 3% and 3.5% at 3 TV [C62].
  - Fe: below 4% to 100 GV, rising to 4.5% at 3 TV [C153].
- These are systematic sizes on fluxes, not the cross sections themselves.
