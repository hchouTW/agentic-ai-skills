**No cut width can be recommended, because the premise does not hold: E_ECAL/R = 1 has no physical basis for helium, so it will not select clean helium.** [General method; see the ECAL entry in detector-and-observables]

**Why the cut is ill-defined**
- Rigidity is not momentum: p = |Z|R/c. For He, |Z| = 2, so p·c = 2R (GeV, with R in GV). Even in the electron-like limit where E ≈ pc, the quantity near unity would be E/(|Z|R) = E/(2R), not E/R.
- E/R = 1 equals E/(|Z|R) = 1/2 for helium. That is not a physical calibration point.
- The E/p ≈ 1 condition is an electron/positron property. The ECAL is a 17 X0 calorimeter, and a lepton shower contains essentially all of its energy.
- A helium nucleus is not an electromagnetic probe. Its ECAL response is a hadronic, species-dependent fraction with wide tails, set by the interaction point and the charge-exchange and pi0 content of the shower. This is also true for protons, where E/p < 1 on average with wide tails.
- A helium nucleus can interact in the Tracker, TOF or TRD material above the ECAL and fragment, so the E/R peak is smeared further.
- A cut at E/R = 1 therefore has no defined width. Any width you picked would be a tuning of an arbitrary variable and would sculpt the sample in a species- and energy-dependent way.

**Why it also biases the measurement**
- It selects on shower fluctuations, and it removes or keeps events depending on whether they interacted and where.
- The ECAL acceptance, the shower containment and the hadronic interaction probability then all enter the efficiency, so the selection efficiency becomes rigidity-dependent in a way that is hard to model in MC.
- Using an ECAL variable to select also biases any ECAL-based efficiency or template measurement (circularity).
- It correlates with the ECAL geometric acceptance, and so with the incidence direction and the exposure. Do not fold that into an "efficiency" without defining the response chain.

**What to use instead for clean helium** [Proposal]
- Define the estimand first: for example He flux versus rigidity, with the |Z| = 2 selection from the Tracker (inner and L1/L9 charge) and the TOF, and with RICH charge as an independent cross-check where its acceptance applies.
- Select charge from Tracker/TOF |Z| estimators, with loose and tight charge-selection variations.
- Reject interacting or fragmenting events with consistency between the upper and lower charge measurements.
- Use track quality and rigidity-consistency cuts.
- Estimate the residual contamination from neighbouring charges, such as Li/Be/B fragmentation and H, by template fits in the charge variables or by data-driven methods.
- Treat the ECAL as optional, not a helium selector. If it is used at all, use it as a validation or control quantity with the |Z| and species stated, not through an E/R = 1 cut.

**Inputs that would change this**
- The rigidity range, the data period, and whether you have an ECAL-containment requirement for acceptance reasons (a geometric requirement, not an energy-ratio cut).
- If you need an ECAL-based variable for a specific reason, say so. Then the cut would be defined from the data-measured E/(|Z|R) distribution, with a stated efficiency and its systematic, not from E/R = 1.
