**Short answer:** do not assume a single number. TRD proton rejection is not a fixed figure. It depends on the lepton efficiency you accept, the energy, and the selection. For a 100 GeV positron analysis, treat it as an input you measure, not a constant you adopt.

**What the public record supports**
- [Documented, C67] The AMS review (Phys. Rept. 894) states that the on-orbit TRD proton rejection at 90% e± efficiency is **above 1000 in 2-200 GeV/c**. That is a lower bound for that selection, and it improves when the e± efficiency is tightened to 65%. 100 GeV lies inside this range.
- No exact rejection value at 100 GeV is given in the ledger. The rejection curve is a figure, and I will not quote a number read off it.
- [Documented, C72] The ECAL adds a separate handle. Λ_ECAL together with E/p > 0.7 gives a further factor of about 3 in proton rejection when the e± efficiency goes from 90% to 65%. The review states this is independent of the TRD rejection for its samples.
- [Documented, C24, C26] Published AMS lepton analyses do not apply a fixed TRD rejection. They fit the (Λ_TRD, Λ_CC) plane with templates (positron paper, C24) or apply an ECAL-estimator cut and then a template fit (electron paper, C26).

**Why you cannot just assume it**
1. The rejection is defined at a stated e± efficiency. Quoting it without that efficiency is meaningless.
2. A rejection of order 10^3 for the TRD alone is not enough on its own. Protons outnumber positrons by orders of magnitude at 100 GeV, so you need TRD and ECAL together, plus the Tracker tail control.
3. A mean rejection factor says nothing about the tail. The proton contamination that matters comes from the non-Gaussian tail of the discriminant, and it needs validation (invariant 4).
4. The TRD and ECAL are not fully independent, because the ECAL shape and E/p share the ECAL energy. Do not multiply their rejections without checking correlations (invariant 3).
5. The TRD is not a charge-sign detector (invariant 2). Charge confusion (e⁻ reconstructed as e⁺) is a separate background that comes from the Tracker. It is not suppressed by TRD rejection.

**What to do instead [Proposal]**
- Write the contamination symbolically: N_p^contam(E) = N_p^sel(E) / R_p(E, ε_e), where R_p is the proton rejection at the chosen lepton efficiency ε_e. Keep ε_e explicit.
- Measure R_p on data. Use a proton-enriched control (for example, inverted ECAL cuts) and an electron or positron-enriched control, and validate the transfer to the signal region.
- If the template fit is the primary method, the TRD estimator template for protons comes from data or MC with a stated provenance. Include finite-template statistics and a signal-contaminated-template check.
- Vary the TRD cut and the ECAL cut and test whether the result is stable. Name a metric and a trigger for adding an uncertainty. "Looks consistent" does not count.

**Bottom line**
- Assumable from public sources: at least 1000 at 90% e± efficiency across 2-200 GeV/c (C67), with the ECAL giving a further factor of about 3 at 65% efficiency (C72).
- Not assumable: a specific value at 100 GeV, a combined TRD x ECAL rejection, or a tail contamination level. These are [Unknown/needs input]. Take them from your own data-driven measurement, or from the published figures if you have the paper.

Inputs that would sharpen this: data or MC, the e± efficiency target, whether you want a fraction or a flux, and whether the analysis is public or internal.
