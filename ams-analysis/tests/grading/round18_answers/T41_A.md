**Short answer:** about 830 +- 30 days (roughly 2.3 years) for the positron-to-electron ratio R_e = Phi(e+)/Phi(e-) to go from 10% to 90% of its change across the 2013 solar polarity reversal. The transition is smooth, and the duration does not depend on energy. The midpoint of the transition is delayed by an amount that does depend on energy. [Documented, C39 (S42 abstract, 1-50 GeV in energy, 79 Bartels rotations, May 2011 to May 2017), C90 (Phys. Rept. review fit, S01), C182 (S42 supplement caption)]. This is the AMS published scope; I have no web access, so I make no claim about later updates (the index was last verified 2026-09-20).

**How AMS got the number**
- **Data.** Electron and positron fluxes per Bartels rotation (27 days), in energy (not rigidity), 1-50 GeV. The review (S01) quotes 23.5 million events in 49 energy bins [Documented, C89]. The flux in each time bin follows the AMS flux definition, Phi = N/(A eps T dE), with unfolding and exposure per time bin [Documented, C43-C49 pattern; C48 for the lepton Bartels-rotation product].
- **Ratio.** R_e is formed from the 3871 independent (energy, time) measurements. The short-term (months-scale) structures appear in both e+ and e- with about the same relative amplitude, so they largely cancel in the ratio. What remains is the long-term, charge-sign-dependent evolution [Documented, C39, C89]. The review states that only particle drift gives charge-sign-dependent solar modulation. That is the review's physics context, not a measurement.
- **Logistic fit.** For each energy, R_e(t, E) = R0(E)[1 + C(E)/(exp(-(t - t_1/2(E))/(dt/Delta_80)) + 1)] with Delta_80 = 4.39, so that dt is the 10%-90% transition time. There are four parameters per energy: R0, C, t_1/2 and dt. The fits over 1-21 GeV have chi2/dof about 1. The fitted dt is independent of energy at 830 +- 30 days [Documented, C90; the same value appears in C39 and in the S42 supplement caption C182].
- **Energy-dependent delay.** t_1/2(E) - t_rev = tau (E/GeV)^rho, with t_rev = 1 July 2013 (a stated choice, the centre of the period with no well-defined solar polarity), rho = -0.33 +- 0.04 (stat) (+0.08, -0.15) (syst), tau = 580 +- 19 (stat) +- 136 (syst) days. t_1/2 shifts by 260 +- 30 days from 1 to 6 GeV [Documented, C90]. The amplitude C is close to 1 at 1 GeV and consistent with zero above 20 GeV.

**Caveats**
- The logistic form is a descriptive four-parameter fit chosen by the collaboration, not a physical model. dt and t_1/2 are only well determined at low energy, where C is large. Above 6 GeV the review fixes dt to 830 days and uses the t_1/2(E) relation.
- The delays t_1/2 - t_rev depend on the chosen t_rev, and rho and tau carry large systematics, so quote both errors.
- The index does not describe how errors are correlated across bins, or any trial factor, for this fit [Unknown/needs input].
- The data are in energy 1-50 GeV (the fit 1-21 GeV). Do not apply them to rigidity, or to the daily-flux papers, without saying so.
- Attribute the number to the S42 abstract (C39) and the review's fit (C90), not to a model of heliospheric transport. Agreement with drift-including solar-modulation models is qualitative.
