**Verdict: the method does not hold as stated.** The assumption that acceptance and efficiency cancel in a positron-to-electron ratio is not established, and fitting a constant with only the quoted bin errors is not a valid test or estimate. Below, "e+/e-" means your ratio in 27-day (Bartels-rotation) bins at a fixed energy or rigidity range, which I assume. Please confirm that reading. I have no knowledge of your internal numbers, so everything is symbolic.

## Defects, in priority order

1. **Cancellation is assumed, not shown (invariant 5).**
   - [Documented, C114] AMS stated that the *acceptance* is the same for positrons and electrons and so cancels in the positron *fraction*. That is a stated property of that fraction, not a general rule. It does not extend to *efficiencies*, and not to your selection, binning or time bins.
   - [Documented, C115, C116] The separate e+ and e- fluxes carry their own two-step TRD-template fit and a tracker charge-confusion correction.
   - Efficiencies and backgrounds differ by charge sign:
     - The positive sample has a large proton contamination, while the negative sample's hadronic background (antiprotons) is far smaller.
     - Charge confusion is asymmetric: e- into e+ and e+ into e- are different migrations, `M(e+<-e-)` and `M(e-<-e+)`, and are tail phenomena (invariant 4).
     - The geomagnetic cutoff transmission is sign-dependent.
     - Any sign-asymmetric selection or ECAL/TRD efficiency also enters.
   - Classify each effect as correlated, partial or independent with a stated `rho`, and keep the residual. A ratio of two Poisson counts with partially correlated multiplicative systematics does not reduce to the quoted statistical errors.

2. **"Only the quoted bin errors" is ambiguous, and either reading is wrong for a constant fit.**
   - *If the errors are statistical only:* systematic effects that move the constant are omitted. The error on the fitted constant is then too small and the chi2 is meaningless as a systematic test.
   - *If the errors are total (stat plus syst), added in quadrature per bin:* persistent systematics (template shape, calibration, charge-confusion model) are correlated across all bins and do not average down as 1/sqrt(N). A diagonal chi2 then underestimates the uncertainty on the constant and can hide real structure.
   - *Time-dependent systematics are only partly independent per bin.* Slowly varying ones are correlated between neighbouring bins. AMS split systematics into time-independent and time-dependent parts and added the time-dependent part in quadrature [Documented, C44, daily proton flux only; not a rule for you].
   - Statistical errors are independent between disjoint 27-day bins, but a ratio normalized to a reference period correlates all bins through that reference.
   - **Fix:** build one covariance over (time, rigidity or energy) with persistent, slowly varying and independent components, and numerator-denominator correlations. Fit the constant with the full covariance. Use `scripts/validate_covariance.py` on the matrix.
   - [General method] `statistical_toys.py ratio-measured` compares a constant-ratio fit with the full covariance against a diagonal-only treatment. `ratio-cov` is the fallback if no measured covariance exists.

3. **Gaussian errors fail at low counts.**
   - At high energy the positron count per 27-day bin is small. The ratio of two small Poisson counts is biased, and Gaussian errors and a chi2 are invalid there (invariant 6).
   - Use a Poisson likelihood on the counts, with toy-validated intervals and p-values. Then check coverage of the constant fit with seeded toys, recording the seed. Alternatively merge bins in time or energy until counts support the inference.
   - Do not average ratios across bins with different exposure. Combine through counts and exposures.

4. **A constant is a physics hypothesis, not a null you may assume.**
   - Solar modulation depends on charge sign through heliospheric drifts, so e+ and e- need not share the same time variation. This is [General method].
   - [Documented, C41, abstract level] AMS reported positron variations similar to protons and unlike electrons in the daily fluxes (rigidity, below about 7 GV).
   - [Documented, C39, C90] AMS reported a smooth e+/e- transition across the 2013 polarity reversal, 830 +- 30 days with an energy-dependent midpoint delay. A logistic fit of that transition is the review's (C90).
   - So at low energy a constant ratio over a long span is expected to fail physically. A poor chi2 may be real signal or a hidden systematic, and the chi2 alone cannot distinguish them.
   - Use the same time bins, energy or rigidity variable, exposure treatment, and sign-specific cutoff and charge-confusion handling for both signs.

5. **Variable and binning.**
   - State whether you bin in energy or rigidity. [Documented, C48, C24, C25] AMS's Bartels-rotation lepton product is in energy, while the daily e+/e- fluxes (C40, C41) are in rigidity.
   - Do not let a 27-day bin straddle a hardware-era or reconstruction-version boundary.
   - Compute the geomagnetic transmission per bin and per sign.

## What to do

- **Cancellation check, [General method].**
  - Run `statistical_toys.py ratio-toys --n1 N+ --n2 N- --sys name:sigma_num:sigma_den:rho` for each systematic. Compare the "cancels" assumption against the "independent" assumption and the residual.
  - Validate with an abundant control (an e- sample and a time-independent e+/e- control).
  - Show efficiency stability versus time after livetime and cutoff correction. Stability needs a stated metric and a trigger for adding a period split, a scale factor or a systematic.
  - Correct each drift in one category only, never twice.
- **Fit.** Poisson likelihood on counts, full covariance, constant versus a physically motivated alternative (for example a smooth or logistic time dependence). Report the constant, its total error and the goodness of fit with a toy-calibrated p-value, not a bare chi2.
- **Periodicity trap.** If you also look for a periodic signal in this ratio, account for the look-elsewhere effect with toys that reproduce the sampling, exposure pattern and correlated systematics.
