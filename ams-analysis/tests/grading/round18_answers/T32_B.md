# Positron-to-electron ratio R_k = N(e+)/N(e-) in 27-day bins, 5-50 GV, with a one-year periodicity search

Assumptions (user inputs to confirm): public-level design, flux-level ratio (not a detector study), time span = AMS data taking from May 2011 (user to fix end date), variable = rigidity as requested. Everything below is [General method] or [Proposal] unless tagged. [Unknown/needs input]: per-period livetime, efficiency, calibration and trigger records, and any AMS trial-factor or systematic covariance treatment across days or rotations (the ledger does not describe one).

## 1. Estimand and variable
- Estimand: R_k(R_j) = Phi_k(e+) / Phi_k(e-) per time bin k and rigidity bin j, with R = pc/(Ze). Charge sign comes only from the Tracker/magnet; TRD, ECAL, TOF and RICH do not give sign. [General method]
- Variable warning: the published Bartels-rotation lepton paper bins in energy (C39, C48; S42), whereas the daily e-/e+ papers bin in rigidity, 1.00-41.9 GV (C40, C41; abstract level). A 5-50 GV rigidity ratio is therefore an extension in range beyond the 41.9 GV of the daily rigidity papers; it is not a reproduction of any published product. State the variable and, if energy is wanted, convert with explicit |Z|=1 and mass (scripts/ams_kinematics.py). [Documented, C40, C41, C48; scope-limited]
- A 27-day bin is one Bartels rotation, which matches the AMS Bartels-rotation lepton and nuclei products (C48, C49) but is a choice, not a rule. [Documented, C48]

## 2. Binning (fix before looking at the time dependence) [Proposal]
- Time: bin edges aligned to Bartels rotations; no bin may straddle a hardware-era or reconstruction-version boundary. Record the rigidity edges and the stability metric up front.
- Rigidity: bin widths at least about twice the rigidity resolution, with the resolution tail validated (not the Gaussian core). Positron counts per 27-day bin fall steeply with rigidity, so the 30-50 GV region may need merged rigidity bins or a lower time resolution; state the rigidity range over which the time resolution is actually supported, by a count criterion (Poisson-exact or toy-validated intervals, not Gaussian errors, when counts are low).

## 3. Per-bin measurement chain
1. Selection: |Z|=1, Tracker charge sign, quality time only (normal conditions, zenith angle cut, outside the South Atlantic Anomaly) and an explicit geomagnetic cutoff safety factor, applied to both signs. The conditions in the proton and deuteron papers (C31, C27) and in the Bartels-rotation lepton analysis (C48) are those analyses' choices, not defaults.
2. Cutoff transmission and exposure computed per bin and per sign: E_k = sum of A_eff x livetime x transmission inside bin k. Never average flux across bins of unequal exposure. [General method]
3. Signal extraction in each (k, j): template fit (TRD estimator and a charge-confusion estimator, as in the published lepton analysis, C24; template provenance, finite-MC statistics and Barlow-Beeston-type treatment as nuisances) or cut-and-count with control samples. Backgrounds: protons faking e+ (dominant, orders of magnitude more abundant), charge confusion e- to e+ (Tracker tail, a migration in sign, M(e+<-e-)), antiprotons in the e- sample, secondaries and albedo leptons. Validate the tail with test-beam or flight control samples; do not extrapolate from the core resolution. [Proposal, consistent with C24, C25]
4. Response: migration is unfolded or forward folded independently in each time bin, as in the AMS time-resolved fluxes (C43, C46, C49); the charge-sign migration is part of the response, and the correction is made in one category only (veto, calibration, efficiency or response), never twice.
5. Ratio: R_k = [N+ - b+]/[E+ ...] over [N- - b-]/[E- ...]. In the ratio the acceptance and exposure largely cancel, but cancellation must be shown per effect, not assumed: the cutoff and charge-confusion treatment are sign-specific and do NOT cancel; the proton contamination differs between signs; efficiencies for e+ and e- differ at the percent level through TRD/ECAL selection and sign-dependent tracking. Classify every nuisance as correlated, partial or independent. [General method]

## 4. Systematic and covariance structure over (time, rigidity)
- Statistical errors independent between disjoint-event time bins; the ratio's numerator and denominator are independent samples (different charge-sign events) so no stat correlation between them within a bin, but charge confusion and shared selection create correlated systematics.
- Systematics: persistent (template shape, cross-section model, calibration: correlated across all periods), slowly varying (neighbouring bins), independent per period (per-period efficiency fluctuations). AMS split its systematics into time-independent and time-dependent parts, adding the latter in quadrature (C44, daily proton flux only); how AMS correlated systematics across days or rotations is not documented in the text read [Unknown/needs input], so propose and test your own model rather than citing AMS.
- Build one covariance over (k, j) with a documented ordering; a Kronecker time x rigidity factorization only if tested. Normalizing to a reference period cancels only the persistent part and correlates the statistical errors of all periods with the reference. Check with scripts/validate_covariance.py.

## 5. Solar-cycle physics and what the ratio is for
- Modulation depends on charge sign through drift in a heliospheric field whose polarity reverses over the cycle, so e+ and e- need not follow the same time variation; the time dependence of the ratio is a measurement, a drift or force-field interpretation is a separate model step. [General method]
- Published context: positron variations are similar to proton and unlike electron, with positrons modulated more than protons below about 7 GV [Documented, C41, abstract level]; the electron-proton and electron-positron hysteresis appears below about 8.5 GV [Documented, C40, C41, abstract level] (do not merge the two thresholds). Over 5-50 GV these effects are strongest at the low edge, so expect a rigidity-dependent time structure in the ratio and consider splitting the range, for example 5-10 GV and above 10 GV, rather than a single band.
- The smooth e+/e- transition across the 2013 polarity reversal over 830 +- 30 days with an energy-dependent midpoint delay is documented in energy for the Bartels-rotation lepton paper [Documented, C39, C90, C89, C182]; it is a collaboration fit, not a template for your rigidity analysis. Months-scale e+ and e- structures that are not visible in the ratio are reported there (C39), which is directly relevant: part of the periodicity may cancel in the ratio, so a null in the ratio does not imply a null in each species.

## 6. One-year periodicity search [Proposal; all General method]
Define before unblinding: period band, observable, test statistic, trial range, significance threshold.
- Sampling: 27-day bins give about 13.5 samples per year. The Nyquist period is 54 days, so a 365-day period is resolved, but only with a modest number of cycles (about 11 over the full span), and sampling is uneven if bins are lost to exposure or data-quality gaps. Use a method that handles uneven sampling and weights by bin uncertainties (generalized Lomb-Scargle or a likelihood fit of a cosine plus sine term at each trial period; amplitude is sqrt(a^2 + b^2), not a^2 + b^2). Fit jointly across charge signs and separately per sign (separate e+ and e- fluxes, not just the ratio) and compare.
- Detrend the solar-cycle trend and the polarity-reversal transition first (low-order polynomial or smooth spline, or a fit with the logistic term included), fitted simultaneously with the sinusoid; otherwise the large slow variation leaks power into low frequencies and inflates a one-year signal. Apply the identical detrending to toys.
- Period band: a band centred on one year (a priori) is a single-trial test; if you search a wide band, the look-elsewhere effect applies. Report local and global significance (trial factor in the sense of Gross and Vitells, S37) calibrated with toys that reproduce the sampling, exposure pattern, correlated systematics (the covariance in section 4) and red noise from the solar variations. Do not use Wilks or a single-frequency p-value. AMS used a wavelet analysis with 95% confidence levels from lag-1 red-noise Monte Carlo surrogates for the daily proton flux [Documented, C45, C52; daily proton flux only]; any trial-factor treatment is not described in the text read, and the wavelet method is not a rule for this analysis [Unknown/needs input].
- Nuisance periodicities: a one-year signal is exactly where instrumental and orbital effects live (seasonal exposure and geomagnetic-cutoff transmission changes tied to the ISS orbit and the solar beta angle, and temperature or detector-state cycles). Run the same search on (i) the exposure and livetime series per bin, (ii) efficiency and cutoff-transmission series, (iii) a control species such as protons with a ratio-matched control (a p-based pseudo-ratio, for example p/p from two independent halves), and (iv) the individual e+ and e- series. A coincident signal in any of these is a systematic, not a discovery. Consider also removing the annual exposure variation by a cutoff-safety-factor variation (the public analyses varied this, C27, as a systematic).
- Sensitivity: report an upper limit on the amplitude, not just a p-value, with exact or toy-calibrated intervals when the amplitude is small; inject known sinusoids with realistic covariance to measure efficiency and bias of the estimator. Note that the ratio of low-count bins is a biased estimator; use a Poisson likelihood for e+ and e- counts directly (or a binomial for the fraction) rather than Gaussian errors on R.
- Claims: a periodicity near one year would need to survive the control series, the detrending choice (varied as a systematic), alternate bin alignments and a split-sample test (odd and even rotations, or first and second half of the span). A local significance alone does not support a physics claim.

## 7. Validation (metric and trigger, not "reasonable agreement") [Proposal]
- Closure: injected e+/e- ratios with charge confusion in each bin, recovered within a stated tolerance; a bias beyond the statistical error in a bin triggers a larger charge-confusion systematic or a coarser rigidity binning.
- Stability: an abundant control species rate versus time, flat after livetime and cutoff correction; beyond tolerance, a period split, a scale factor with uncertainty or a systematic.
- Variation tests: cutoff safety factor, template shape, selection (repeat with varied cuts, as the early lepton fraction did, C108, C114), bin alignment.
- Joint-likelihood option for all bins: L = prod_k prod_j Poisson(n | mu(theta_shared, phi_k, eta)) x constraints, tested against separate per-period fits with toys; do not combine periods with different definitions.
