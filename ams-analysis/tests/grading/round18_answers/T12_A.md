**Short answer: no, not as it stands.** [General method] A Wilks (chi-squared) p-value is not reliable when the best-fit signal sits at 0 and the signal is bounded to be non-negative. Your template likelihood also treats the MC proton template as exact, which makes the problem worse.

**Why Wilks fails here**
1. **Boundary.** The null (signal = 0) lies on the edge of the allowed region (signal strength >= 0). Wilks' theorem assumes the null is an interior point. For a one-sided test, the asymptotic null distribution of q0 is a half point-mass at 0 plus half chi2 with 1 d.o.f. (Cowan et al.), not chi2 with 1 d.o.f.
   - A fit that prefers 0 gives q0 = 0 and p = 0.5. That is the boundary behaving as expected, not evidence about the signal.
   - Even this mixture holds only under regularity conditions and enough counts. With few counts in the bin, or weak nuisance constraints, check it with toys. [Proposal: toys]
2. **Low counts.** In a positron-fraction bin, positrons are a small fraction of a proton-dominated sample, so the count that carries the signal may be small. Use Poisson-based inference, not Gaussian or Wilks approximations.
3. **Finite proton template.** An MC proton template has its own statistical uncertainty. Treating it as exact gives p-values and intervals that are too optimistic. Use a Barlow-Beeston-type likelihood, with per-bin template nuisances and weighted-MC effective entries. Check for empty or low-population template bins, which also bias the fit.
4. **Template systematics.** The shape and normalization of an MC proton template (data/MC mismatch in the TRD or ECAL discriminant, tails) are systematic nuisances. Profile them, or vary them with alternative templates. Do not fix them at the MC nominal. If the shape is wrong, the fit can be pushed to 0 for the wrong reason.
5. **Shared proton model.** If the template is also used in other bins or in the negative-charge sample, those fits are correlated. A per-bin p-value then ignores that the nuisances are shared.

**What to do**
- Define the test statistic (profile-likelihood q0 with mu >= 0). Generate pseudo-experiments from the background-only fit, including Poisson fluctuations of data and of the template. Take the p-value from the toy distribution of q0.
- For an upper limit or an interval, use a stated construction (Feldman-Cousins, CLs, or profile likelihood calibrated with toys) and check coverage. State that CLs is a convention, not a confidence-level statement. Do not quote Gaussian errors near the boundary.
- Account for the look-elsewhere effect if you scan many energy bins. A local p-value in one bin is not a global one.
- Validate with signal-injection and pull tests, and report the goodness of fit and the fit-parameter correlations.
- Seeded diagnostics are available in the skill's `scripts/statistical_toys.py` and `scripts/likelihood_limits.py` (boundary p-value, finite-template effect), if you want them run.

**Also check**
- A fit preferring 0 signal is only a boundary result if the fit is otherwise sensible. Check that the proton template is not contaminated with signal, which would absorb it. Check that the fit is not degenerate with the charge-confusion template.
- For AMS, [Documented, C24, C107] the published positron fits are done in a two-dimensional (TRD estimator, charge-confusion estimator) plane or in the [TRD estimator - log(E/p)] plane per energy bin, with the charge-confusion template from MC verified on data. A single proton MC template is a simplification of that practice, so treat your design as a proposal, not as AMS practice.

**Inputs that would change this answer**
- Expected counts in the bin.
- Whether the template uncertainty is already in the likelihood.
- The test statistic you used.
- Whether the p-value is for signal vs background-only, and whether it is local or global.
