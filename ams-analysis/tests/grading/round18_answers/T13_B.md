**Short answer: no. 98% is not a measured combined efficiency; it is only a lower bound.** [General method]

- 0.99 x 0.99 = 98.01% is valid only if A and B are independent for the population you care about. That has to be shown, not assumed. The correct form is a chain of conditionals: ε_total = ε_A · ε_B|A, where ε_B|A is measured on events that already pass A.
- Selections that use related information are correlated. Examples are two cuts that both use the Tracker charge estimate, a shower-shape cut and an E/p cut that share ECAL energy, or a TRD cut and a rigidity cut that share rigidity dependence. For such cuts the product of marginals can be wrong in either direction.
- Without independence, the efficiencies only bound the joint (Fréchet): max(0, ε_A + ε_B − 1) ≤ ε_{A∧B} ≤ min(ε_A, ε_B). For two 99% cuts the joint efficiency lies between 98% and 99%. So 98% is a floor, not an estimate, and quoting it as the efficiency is wrong. If the cuts are positively correlated, the true value is closer to 99%.
- "99% on MC" is also not a data efficiency. The efficiency is a function of species, rigidity or energy, time and geometry, not a single number. To use it for data you need a data/MC scale factor ε_data = SF · ε_MC, measured in a control sample, with its own statistical and systematic uncertainty. Do not apply a scale factor measured on one species or period to another without justification.

What to do:
1. State the denominator and numerator of each efficiency, and the conditional population for the second cut.
2. Measure the joint efficiency directly in MC (and in data where possible) and compare it with the product. The difference is a factorization-bias systematic, or a reason to measure the two cuts jointly.
3. Run an ordering closure: apply A then B, and B then A, in MC. The totals must agree within statistical precision, while the individual factors change.
4. Run a combined-efficiency closure: compare the product of the measured factors, with data-derived corrections, to the truth-level combined efficiency, per rigidity bin and time slice.
5. Measure the factors with tag-and-probe or an orthogonal-subsystem control, with a signal-free control sample and the tag bias evaluated. Give exact binomial intervals, not Wald, for efficiencies near 1.
6. Do not also correct the same loss elsewhere, for example in a response matrix that already includes inefficiency. That would double count it.

If you can show that A and B are independent, 98.0% is acceptable as a product. You would still need to give its uncertainty and the closure test. Otherwise quote the measured joint efficiency, or at most the bound "between 98% and 99%".
