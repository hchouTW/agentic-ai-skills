**Verdict: forward-fold.** The estimand is a parameter, the break position, and the regime is low counts with strong migration at high rigidity. Both favor a forward-folded Poisson likelihood fit over unfolding. [General method]

**Why**
- The inference-and-unfolding method table pairs "model parameters (index, break, cutoff)" with a forward-folded likelihood fit. It works on raw counts and has no regularization bias. [General method]
- At high rigidity bins have few counts, so Gaussian errors and Wilks fail. Charge-confusion and resolution tails also matter there. An unfolded spectrum adds regularization/iteration bias and strongly correlated, even anticorrelated, bins. Fitting a break to it needs the full unfolded covariance, which includes unfolding-induced correlations and the response covariance. Chi2 with a diagonal covariance is wrong.
- Cost: the result is conditional on the parametrization (for example a smoothly broken power law). Goodness of fit tests only that family. A smooth spectral feature the family cannot represent will be absorbed or misplaced, so the fit does not give a model-free spectrum.

**Setup** (symbolic; I do not supply AMS numbers)
- Expected counts: `mu_i(theta, eta) = sum_j R_ij A_j T_j Phi_j(theta) Dx_j + sum_k b_ik`, with `Phi(theta)` a broken power law whose parameters include the break position `R_b`. Poisson likelihood per bin `n_i`.
- Declare the orientation and normalization of `R`. Do not put inefficiency in both `R` and `A`. Include under/overflow and feed-in from below the fitted range, because upward migration of abundant low-R events matters for a steep spectrum.
- Acceptance, efficiency, scale and resolution-tail, charge-confusion and background nuisances are constrained by auxiliary measurements, not by the fitted data. Put finite-MC response and template statistics in the likelihood. Check the response by running `validate_response.py`. Check the response statistics with the multinomial `response-covariance` and the efficiency systematic.

**The break significance and uncertainty are non-regular**
- `R_b` is unidentifiable under the no-break hypothesis, so Wilks does not apply. Use toys with a trial factor over the predefined `R_b` range (Gross-Vitells). Declare the range in advance.
- Do not use profile-likelihood or Gaussian intervals on `R_b` without toy calibration, since the likelihood can be flat or multimodal in `R_b`. Use toy-calibrated, Feldman-Cousins-style or profile intervals, and say which.

**Validation** (triggers to change the method or add an uncertainty are [Proposal])
1. Poisson-fluctuated pseudo-experiments, with the response redrawn, give pulls and coverage for `R_b` and the index change. A pull width above 1 or a mean pull beyond about 3 standard errors means adding an uncertainty or changing the method.
2. Injection test: inject breaks at several positions and sizes, including none, and recover them. Also fit a smooth curved spectrum without a break to check for a false break.
3. Stress tests: alternative parametrizations (sharp versus smooth break, log-curvature), alternative bin edges and range, and response perturbations (resolution scale, tails).
4. Run `fold-compare` to see the statistical cost of each route, and `nonparam-fold` as a cross-check. A smoothness prior on log t favors falling spectra and can wash out a break, so repeat with a different penalty. These scripts assume the spectral form is known, so they isolate cost and are not proof for the real data.

**If you also need a publishable spectrum:** unfold separately, with a declared iteration or regularization criterion not tuned on the final spectrum, a prior-variation systematic, closure on reweighted and distorted truths, and the full covariance. Do not fit the break to it with a diagonal covariance. Publishing the response and data lets others forward-fold.

**Needed inputs [Unknown/needs input]:** counts per bin in the break region, the response matrix with its orientation and normalization, the predefined search range for `R_b`, and which systematics are correlated across bins. No AMS-specific numbers are quoted here.
