There is no single AMS-wide value, but the published AMS flux papers most often use a factor of 1.2: the measured rigidity (or energy for leptons) must exceed 1.2 times the maximum geomagnetic cutoff in the field of view. The cutoff comes from backtracing in the IGRF field model (and Stormer cutoff for the e+/e- papers).

- [Documented, C20] Antiproton analysis: R > 1.2 x maximum geomagnetic cutoff (backtracing, IGRF).
- [Documented, C31] Proton flux (first 30 months): R > 1.2 x maximum cutoff.
- [Documented, C24] Positron flux: E > 1.2 x maximum Stormer cutoff.
- [Documented, C132, C138, C144] Nuclei papers (Li/Be/B, N, and a further nuclei paper): 1.2 x maximum cutoff with IGRF backtracing.

The factor is then varied as a systematic:
- [Documented, C23] Antiproton paper: scan 1.2-1.4.
- [Documented, C27, C49, C60] Deuteron, proton/He time-structure and helium papers: scan 1.0-1.4. The effect is below 0.1% for helium and many nuclei below 30 GV.

It is not one uniform standard:
- [Documented, C51, C54] The daily proton and daily helium analyses use a cutoff computed from AMS data, raised by 10% in the systematic, instead of the IGRF 1.2 factor.
- [Documented, C56] The Bartels-rotation nuclei analysis uses 30-degree pointing, in contrast to the 40 degrees of the other analyses.

For any other AMS paper, check that paper. I could not check for newer results here (no web access; the index was last verified 2026-09-20).

"Safety factor" in general: [General method] a primary selection requires |R| > SF x R_c, and the choice of SF and field model is analysis input that should be varied as a systematic.
