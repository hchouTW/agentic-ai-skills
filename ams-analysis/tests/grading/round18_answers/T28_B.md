There is no single universal number: the maximum detectable rigidity (MDR, where the rigidity resolution dR/R reaches 100%) depends on the charge |Z| and on which tracker layers are used. In the Tracker's multiple-scattering regime dR/R is roughly independent of R, and at high R it is set by spatial resolution and alignment (dR/R grows in proportion to R).

For the full nine-layer configuration (L1 to L9, 3 m lever arm), the published values are:

- Protons (|Z| = 1): about 2 TV. [Documented, C21, C63]
- Helium: 3.2 TV. The uncertainty is +-0.16 TV in the first-30-months paper [Documented, C61] and 5% in the five-year paper [Documented, C62].
- Carbon: 3.7 TV. [Documented, C62, C63]
- Oxygen: 3.4 TV. [Documented, C62, C63]
- Iron: 3.7 TV. [Documented, C63]

Context for these numbers:

- They apply only to the nine-layer L1-L9 span. The inner tracker alone (L2-L8) has a shorter lever arm and a lower MDR, and this note does not give a value for it.
- The MDR is higher for larger |Z| in these papers.
- The MDR is not the limit of the usable flux range. The published proton and helium fluxes stop at 1.8 TV and 3 TV, in bins where the resolution tails and the absolute rigidity-scale uncertainty dominate.
- The numbers come from simulation checked against test-beam and flight data (C61, C63). They are not a statement about a single event's measurement.
- Do not quote a universal MDR without stating the species and the layer configuration.
