**Short answer.** Tracker rigidity is measured from the track curvature in the permanent magnet field (R = pc/(Ze)). Two different error sources dominate at the two ends of the range, so the relative resolution δR/R is U-shaped in R.

**Low rigidity: multiple Coulomb scattering.**
- [General method] The scattering angle scales as θ0 ∝ (1/(βcp))·|Z|·sqrt(x/X0). The curvature error it produces is a fractional error on R that is roughly independent of R. It grows as β drops (a 1/β factor) and with the amount of material in the track path.
- Hit resolution is not the limit here, because the sagitta is large.
- Other low-R effects: the particle is slow (β < 1), so the β-dependent scattering term matters. Energy loss and delta rays add non-Gaussian tails. Interactions or fragmentation in the material can produce a wrong or kinked track. Albedo and re-entrant particles are a background in this regime.
- Net effect: δR/R flattens at a floor set by material and β, which does not improve with a better hit resolution.

**High rigidity: spatial resolution and alignment.**
- [General method] The sagitta shrinks as 1/R. The fixed hit position error (cluster position resolution, plus alignment and field-map errors) therefore gives δR/R ∝ R (δ(1/R) is roughly constant). The coefficient scales with the position resolution σ_x, falls with the lever arm L (as 1/L^2) and falls with the field strength. The number of hit layers also enters.
- Alignment, time-dependent drifts, and systematic distortions are as important as the intrinsic hit resolution here. They also produce a rigidity-scale error. On a steep spectrum, that error is amplified by the local spectral index.
- The resolution function acquires non-Gaussian tails. This matters for charge confusion, which is a Tracker-only problem (see below).

**Where the two meet.** [Documented, C63, S01 Phys. Rept. 894 ch. 1] The resolution is ΔR/R = 0.1 below 20 GV for the nine-layer configuration. The maximum detectable rigidity (MDR, where δR/R = 1) is 2.0 TV for p, 3.2 TV for He, and 3.7 TV for C (and 3.4 TV for O, 3.7 TV for Fe). [Documented, C21] The MDR is 2 TV for |Z| = 1 with the 3 m L1-L9 lever arm. These values depend on the track configuration and on the definition, so they are not a universal MDR.

**Why it matters.**
- Both ends make the rigidity response a migration matrix, not a delta function. At the low end, bin migration and tails matter. At the high end, steep spectra smear toward higher R. This has to be folded or unfolded, not corrected by a bin-by-bin factor.
- Charge confusion (wrong curvature sign) is a tail effect. It must not be estimated from the Gaussian core of δR/R. It needs a data-driven or calibrated-simulation tail check. Lepton/hadron ID (TRD, ECAL) cannot fix it.
- [Proposal] Validate the rigidity scale with a physical control (for example E/p in a pure-lepton sample, or the consistency of independent partial fits), not with MC agreement alone. Track the resolution and tails against time.
