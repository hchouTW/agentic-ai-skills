[General method] No AMS-specific content is needed here; this is standard statistics (Wilks 1938; Cowan, Cranmer, Gross, Vitells 2011).

**Profile likelihood ratio.** Take a likelihood L(mu, theta) with a parameter of interest mu and nuisance parameters theta. Let (mu_hat, theta_hat) be the global maximum-likelihood estimates, and let theta_hat_hat(mu) be the best theta at a fixed mu. The profile likelihood ratio is

lambda(mu) = L(mu, theta_hat_hat(mu)) / L(mu_hat, theta_hat),  with 0 < lambda <= 1.

It is usually used as the test statistic t_mu = -2 ln lambda(mu). Profiling replaces each nuisance parameter by its best-fit value at each fixed mu. The nuisance parameters are thereby eliminated without a prior, and their uncertainty is carried into the interval for mu. A confidence interval is the set of mu with t_mu below a critical value. A discovery test uses q0, which is the same construction at mu = 0.

**Wilks' theorem.** Suppose H0 fixes k parameters, or constrains them to a nested sub-model, and the regularity conditions hold. Then, as the sample size goes to infinity, -2 ln lambda under H0 follows a chi-square distribution with k degrees of freedom. For a single parameter of interest, k = 1.

- The critical value does not depend on the nuisance parameters or on the data. It is chi2_1(0.683) = 1 for a 1 sigma interval and 3.84 for 95% CL.
- This gives a fast interval of the form -2 Delta ln L <= 1, with no toy Monte Carlo.
- The significance is roughly sqrt(q0).

**Conditions for the theorem.** The true value must lie in the interior of the parameter space, not on a boundary. The models must be nested and identifiable under H0. The sample must be large enough for the likelihood to be approximately Gaussian near its maximum, so that the asymptotic (Gaussian-likelihood) regime applies.

**Where it fails.**
- At a boundary such as a signal strength mu >= 0. The distribution of q0 is then a half chi-square, with half of the toys at q0 = 0. The naive Wilks p-value is a factor of 2 too large, so the result is conservative.
- When a parameter is not identifiable under H0, for example the position of a peak or spectral break, which is the look-elsewhere or trial-factor problem. Use toys over the predefined range.
- With low counts, weakly constrained nuisance parameters or non-nested models. Use exact Poisson, Feldman-Cousins or toy-calibrated profile constructions, and check coverage.

**Practical rule.** Do not quote the asymptotic result by default. Check it against toys, or against the Asimov-dataset approximation, in the regime you are in. Do not use naive S/sqrt(B) or Gaussian errors for small counts. Use Wilks when you can justify that the conditions hold, and use toys otherwise.
