**Verdict: not justified as stated.** Acceptance and efficiency do not cancel automatically in a positron-to-electron ratio, and bin errors alone are not a valid error model for a constant fit in 27-day bins. Treat the fit as unreliable until the points below are addressed. [General method]

**1. Cancellation has to be shown effect by effect.** Classify each nuisance as correlated (cancels), partially correlated, or independent between numerator and denominator, and verify it. A ratio does not cancel these things by itself.
- Geometric acceptance and trigger efficiency may be nearly sign-symmetric. That is a property to demonstrate, not assume. [General method]
- For the *fraction* e+/(e+ + e-), the public AMS papers state that acceptance is the same for positrons and electrons and cancels (C114, PRL 113, 121101, energy bins). That is specific to that fraction. The separate fluxes carry their own acceptance, trigger efficiency and charge-confusion corrections (C115, C116). [Documented, C114, C115, C116]
- These do not cancel and are sign-specific:
  - **Charge confusion.** It is a Tracker tail effect and is asymmetric. Electrons are far more abundant than positrons, so e- to e+ migration contaminates the positron sample much more than the reverse. It must be validated in the tails, not from the Gaussian core of the rigidity resolution.
  - **Proton contamination.** Protons contaminate the positive sample only. It must be subtracted or fitted, for example in the TRD/ECAL template fit (C24, C25).
  - **Geomagnetic cutoff and transmission.** These are sign-dependent at low energy or rigidity.
  - **Selection and template systematics.** Sign-specific components of these do not cancel either.
  - **Different statistical weights.** The two samples have different statistics, so the errors do not cancel.

**2. Bin errors alone are the wrong error model.**
- Statistical errors on the numerator and denominator should be propagated properly. They are independent if the bins use disjoint events. With a fraction, the numerator is part of the denominator, so they are correlated.
- Systematics are **not independent between 27-day bins**. Classify each as persistent (template shape, charge-confusion model, cross-section model: correlated across all bins), slowly varying (correlated between neighbouring bins), or independent per bin. If you fit using only the bin errors, you treat all of this as independent. That overstates the information, gives a chi-square that is too small or too large depending on which effect dominates, and gives an understated parameter error on the constant.
- Build one covariance over time, and rigidity or energy if you use several bins. Check that it is positive semidefinite, for example with `scripts/validate_covariance.py`. Fit with the full covariance (generalized chi-square), or fit nuisance parameters explicitly. A persistent systematic should be a shared normalization nuisance, not an independent error per bin. [General method, Proposal]
- Normalizing to a reference period removes only the persistent part of the systematics. It also correlates the statistical errors of all periods with the reference, so that covariance has to be carried too.

**3. A constant is a hypothesis and the physics may not support it.**
- Charge-sign-dependent solar modulation (drifts in the heliospheric field, whose polarity reverses over the solar cycle) means e+ and e- need not follow the same time variation, so the ratio can vary with time. [General method]
- AMS has reported daily and Bartels-rotation lepton results. These show 27-day recurrent variations, positron variations that resemble protons more than electrons, hysteresis below about 8.5 GV between electrons and positrons, and months-scale structures. The e+/e- ratio also changes smoothly across the 2013 polarity reversal over about 830 days (C39, C40, C41, C90; abstract or review level). So a constant ratio is a hypothesis to test, not an expected outcome. [Documented, C39, C40, C41, C90]
- Choose the same time bins, the same variable (energy or rigidity) and the same exposure and cutoff treatment for both species. Exposure is bin-dependent, so average through counts and exposures, not by averaging ratios.

**4. How to fit and test it.**
- Report the chi-square with the full covariance, the number of degrees of freedom, and a p-value. "Reasonable agreement" is not validation. State a metric and a trigger for changing the model. [General method]
- Make the test sensitive to the likely alternatives (a slope, a step at a polarity change, a 27-day modulation) and compare with the constant.
- Check for spurious structure from exposure, orbit, season or detector-state cycles by running the same fit on an exposure series, an efficiency series and a control species.
- If a period search is involved, define the band before the fit and correct for the look-elsewhere effect with toys that include the sampling and the correlated systematics.
- At high energy the counts per bin are low. Use Poisson-exact or toy-validated intervals, not Gaussian errors.
