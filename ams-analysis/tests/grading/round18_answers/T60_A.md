**Short answer.** I cannot give you the per-interval spectral-index numbers. In the AMS Li, Be, B paper (PRL 120, 021101, 2018) they appear only in a figure and in Supplemental Material tables. This skill's ledger did not read either (main article only), so I will not supply values from memory. Take them from the paper's Fig. 2 and its Supplemental tables, or from the review (Phys. Rept. 894), and cite the interval and its error.

**What the ledger documents.**

- **Definition and intervals** [Documented, C136]. The spectral index is gamma = d log(Flux) / d log(R). It was computed over rigidity intervals bounded by 7.09, 12.0, 16.6, 22.8, 41.9, 60.3, 192 and 3300 GV. The same boundaries are used in the review [Documented, C84].
- **Li, Be, B indices** [Documented, C136]. Their magnitude and rigidity dependence are nearly identical to each other but distinctly different from He, C, O. Above about 200 GV, Li, Be, B harden more than He, C, O. The He, C, O indices come from an earlier AMS paper, not this one.
- **Flux shapes** [Documented, C135]. Li and B have identical rigidity dependence above 7 GV. All three fluxes (Li, Be, B) are identical above 30 GV. All three deviate from a single power law above 200 GV, in the same way.
- **Flux ratios** [Documented, C135]. Li/B = 0.72 ± 0.02 above 7 GV. Be/B = 0.36 ± 0.01 above 30 GV. Li/Be = 2.0 ± 0.1 above 30 GV.
- **Secondary-to-primary hardening** [Documented, C136]. Delta = d log(Flux_S/Flux_P) / d log(R) was fitted over 60.3-192 GV and 192-3300 GV for Li/C, Be/C, B/C, Li/O, Be/O and B/O. The average hardening above about 200 GV is 0.13 ± 0.03 in the 2018 paper.
- **Review value** [Documented, C84]. The review, with its larger data set, quotes 0.140 ± 0.025, stated to exceed 5 sigma. Do not mix the two numbers or attach one's errors to the other. How the review's significance was computed is [Unknown/needs input].

**What it says about propagation (the paper's reading, not a measurement of mechanism).**

- The paper says the hardening of the secondary-to-primary ratios is consistent with expectations if it is due to propagation properties in the Galaxy. The review adds that source-injection hardening would give similar hardening for secondaries and primaries, so the primary-only hardening of He, C, O would not produce a change in the ratio [Documented, C136, C84]. This is the authors' interpretation; I did not read third-party model papers.
- The Be flux's different shape below 30 GV is described by the paper as most likely due to the radioactive 10Be isotope (half-life 1.4 My). That is an attribution, not a measurement, and the elemental fluxes are not isotopic [Documented, C135].

**Caveats.**

- A single average Delta is a fit over two chosen intervals. The boundaries are the authors' choice, not a prescription.
- Using the secondary-to-primary ratio for propagation conclusions requires the cross sections and the 10Be and other isotope content to be handled in a model. That is a model step beyond the data.
- "Latest" status is not checked: no web access here, and the index was last verified 2026-09-20. Later papers on the nuclei may supersede these.
