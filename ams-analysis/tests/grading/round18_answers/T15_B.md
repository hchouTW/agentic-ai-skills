Not on that basis. "A paper used 4" has no meaning for your analysis: the iteration count is a bias-variance knob tied to the response matrix, prior, binning, resolution and statistics it was tuned for, so a number copied from another analysis does not transfer. [General method]

What to do instead:
1. Fix the criterion in advance and do not tune on your final unfolded spectrum. For example, minimise expected bias^2 + variance over an ensemble of MC pseudo-data that includes stress-test spectra (different shapes from your nominal prior), and check how sensitive the result is around the chosen count (say 2, 4, 8, ...). [Proposal]
2. Vary the prior or reweight the MC truth, unfold with each, and quote the spread as a systematic. Do not choose the iteration count from a scan whose truth is also its prior, because that is circular.
3. Run closure and stress tests: unfold MC pseudo-data with known truth, and look at pulls and bias per bin. Name a metric and a trigger for adding an uncertainty or changing the method, since "looks fine" is not validation.
4. Propagate the full covariance (statistical, response-statistics and systematic). Bin-to-bin anticorrelations grow with iterations, so report the covariance and do not drop it.
5. Check that the response includes resolution tails and migration, that the bins are sensible against the resolution, and that efficiency and acceptance are not corrected twice (once in the response and once outside it).
6. A fixed convergence rule is a legitimate alternative to a fixed count. Published AMS flux analyses are documented to stop an iterative unfolding when successive steps agree within 0.1% [Documented, C23]. That is a declared stopping criterion, not "4 iterations", and it still needs closure validation. Do not present your choice as AMS practice unless that criterion is what you apply.

Verdict: 4 may turn out fine, but nothing you have said shows it. Inputs that would change the answer are your response matrix and its migration, the bin widths relative to the resolution, the number of events per bin, and the prior's sensitivity.
