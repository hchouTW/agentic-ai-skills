# Design: 27-day-bin e+/e- ratio, 5-50 GV, search for a one-year periodicity

Status labels: [Documented, C##] = ledger claim at its own scope; [General method]; [Proposal]; [Unknown/needs input]. No AMS internal numbers are supplied; counts, efficiencies and systematics are symbols. Review verdict first: the design is feasible as a public-style study, but three things must be fixed before it is meaningful (range, definition of the ratio, and what counts as a "one-year signal").

## 0. Verdict and the main defects to fix first

1. **Variable and range mismatch.** [Documented, C40, C41] AMS daily e- and e+ papers bin in rigidity over 1.00-41.9 GV; [Documented, C39, C48] the Bartels-rotation lepton paper bins in energy (1-50 GeV). Neither covers "5-50 GV" in rigidity, so 41.9-50 GV is beyond any documented AMS lepton time-resolved range. Treat the 41.9-50 GV part as [Unknown/needs input] and extrapolation, not as published practice. State up front whether bins are in rigidity |R| (Tracker) or energy (ECAL); they are not interchangeable (positrons and electrons lose energy differently, and E is not |Z|R·c at the ECAL).
2. **"Ratio" is not a fraction.** e+/e- is a flux ratio; e+/(e+ + e-) is the fraction. Pick one; the error propagation and the correlations differ (the fraction is bounded in [0,1]).
3. **A one-year period is not a clean physics frequency here.** It coincides with orbit/season/exposure cycles and with the beta angle of the ISS orbit, so any apparent annual signal is first a candidate systematic (see section 6).

## 1. Estimand

R_k(Δ) = Φ_{e+,k}(Δ) / Φ_{e-,k}(Δ) for time bin k (27 days) and rigidity (or energy) bin Δ within 5-50 GV, plus a periodicity statistic (amplitude and phase at T = 1 yr) of R_k(t) after removing the slow solar-cycle trend. The periodicity target must be fixed in advance: one fixed period (1 yr) is a single-frequency test, but if the search band is wider than 1 yr (for example 0.5-2 yr), a trial factor is required.

## 2. Binning

- [General method] A 27-day bin gives about 13.5 bins per year and about 148 bins over 11 years; a 1-yr period is resolved (more than 2 bins per cycle), 27-day structure itself is not a target. [Documented, C48, C49] AMS used Bartels rotations (27 days) for the lepton and nuclei products, so 27-day bins are consistent with that practice; the deuteron analysis instead used 108-day bins (C27), so this is a choice, not a rule. Align bins to Bartels rotations, and split any bin that straddles a hardware-era or reconstruction-version boundary.
- [General method] Statistics decide the rigidity binning. e+ is roughly an order of magnitude or more rarer than e-, and the e+ count per 27 days falls steeply with rigidity; at the top of 5-50 GV each bin may hold few e+ per rotation. Merge rigidity bins until the least populated (time, rigidity) cell supports the inference (Poisson-exact or toy-validated, not Gaussian); state the rigidity range where 27-day resolution is supported. [Unknown/needs input] actual e+ counts per rotation.
- [Proposal] Fix binning, ranges and the periodicity metric before looking at the time dependence; log any change.

## 3. Per-bin measurement (inherit the blueprint, per time bin)

Φ_k = (N_k - b_k) / (E_k ΔR), E_k = Σ A_eff · ε · T_live · transmission accumulated inside bin k, with migration unfolded or forward-folded per time bin [Documented, C43, C46, C49: AMS did this for its time-resolved products; those are the analyses' choices]. Never average fluxes of bins with unequal exposure; average through counts and exposures.

- Charge sign: Tracker/magnet only. TRD and ECAL separate leptons from protons but do not give sign.
- Leptons vs protons: TRD estimator and ECAL (E/p) cuts; the proton background to the positron sample rises with rigidity. Template fit per (time, rigidity) bin in the (TRD estimator, charge-confusion estimator) plane is the natural design, following the structure of [Documented, C24] (positrons: three templates, 2-D Λ_TRD, Λ_CC fit) as a pattern, not as an AMS recipe for this analysis.
- Charge confusion is a tail effect: e- reconstructed as e+ contaminates the (small) e+ sample, increasingly with rigidity because the Tracker sign resolution degrades toward 50 GV. Validate the tail (data-driven, abundant e- sample, MC with test-beam check), not the Gaussian core. Carry it as a migration in charge sign M(e+←e-) per rigidity bin; it must be checked for time dependence (tracker alignment/ageing changes the tail) and is a candidate for a slow drift mimicking an e+/e- trend.
- Geomagnetic cutoff: [General method] compute transmission per time bin; at 5 GV and above the cutoff dependence is small relative to the 1-5 GV region but the cutoff safety factor still matters in the lowest bin. [Documented, C31, C27] other analyses used safety factors of 1.2 or 1.0-1.4 as systematics; scope-limited, not a rule. The cutoff differs for the two signs at fixed |R| only through geometry; propagate per bin and per sign.
- Exposure: livetime, acceptance, efficiency are distinct; each drift corrected once (veto, calibration, efficiency or response, not two).

## 4. What cancels in the ratio (classify, do not assume)

- Acceptance: for a fixed |R|, the geometric acceptance is the same for both signs to first order, but the effective acceptance in a sign-asymmetric apparatus (the magnetic field bends them opposite ways, so the same |R| populates different parts of the detector, plus asymmetry from material, ISS structure and geomagnetic east-west) is not guaranteed equal. [Documented, C114] the positron-fraction paper states the acceptance cancels in the fraction for that analysis; that is a property of that fraction in energy, not a rule for your rigidity bins. Classify: correlated (cancels), partial, or independent, and test with a control (e.g. the e- sample split by orbit phase, or MC).
- Livetime and exposure: cancel in the ratio if the same events/time window are used, but only if both numerator and denominator use the same exposure definition and cutoff; this is a check, not an assumption.
- Not cancelling: proton contamination (different for the two signs), charge confusion (asymmetric by construction), Tracker sign-dependent efficiency, TRD/ECAL efficiency if lepton identification differs, template-shape systematics for e+ vs e-.
- Statistical errors: numerator and denominator are disjoint samples only if the charge-sign split is by the selected sign; but the charge-confused events and the shared fit parameters make the e+ and e- yields correlated. Carry the joint covariance (a simultaneous fit of the positive and negative samples sharing the charge-confusion parameters).

## 5. Systematics and covariance across time and rigidity

Classify each nuisance as persistent (calibration, template shape, cross-section model: correlated across all bins), slowly varying (correlated between neighbouring rotations), or independent per rotation; and across rigidity as normalization, smooth shape, or independent. [Documented, C44] AMS split systematics into time-independent and time-dependent parts (the latter, efficiencies evaluated each period, added in quadrature) for the daily proton flux; [Unknown/needs input] AMS does not state how systematics are correlated across days or rotations in the text read, so there is no public covariance model to copy. Build one covariance over (time, rigidity) with a documented ordering, validated with `scripts/validate_covariance.py` (PSD check). A Kronecker product is valid only if the correlation factorizes; test it. Normalizing to a reference period cancels only the persistent part and correlates the statistical errors of all periods with the reference; carry that covariance.

## 6. Periodicity search (the actual inference)

**Pre-defined (before unblinding the time series):** observable (R_k per rigidity band, and a rigidity-integrated version), the period or band (1 yr fixed vs a band), the test statistic, the detrending, the significance threshold.

1. **Detrend the solar-cycle/polarity-reversal trend first.** The ratio carries a slow time variation across the 2013 polarity reversal. [Documented, C39, C89, C90] the S42 abstract and the review report a smooth e+/e- transition over 830 +- 30 days (the logistic fit is the review's, C90, and is energy-based, 1-50 GeV scale for C39); do not describe it as a rigidity result in 5-50 GV. Removing the trend by a smooth model (spline, moving average longer than 1 yr, or a logistic plus polynomial) changes what survives at 1 yr; the filter must not itself have response at 1 yr. Better: include the trend as a nuisance in the same fit as the sinusoid (joint fit), so its freedom is propagated.
2. **Statistic.** Fit a(t) = a cos(2πt/T) + b sin(2πt/T) plus trend to R_k, weighting by the full covariance (not by independent bin errors); amplitude A = sqrt(a² + b²), not a² + b²; phase from atan2(b, a). For unevenly spaced or unequal-exposure series (gaps, bins dropped for quality) use a method that handles the sampling: a generalized (Lomb-Scargle-type, weighted) periodogram, or a likelihood fit, not an FFT of an assumed even grid. Fit the e+ and e- series jointly and separately and compare; a real heliospheric effect need not be identical for the two signs (charge-sign-dependent modulation, [General method]).
3. **Resolution of the period.** With a span of about 11 years, the frequency resolution is about 1/11 yr^-1, so a 1-yr period is well resolved from the 11-yr cycle but not from, say, 0.8-1.2 yr; state the band.
4. **Look-elsewhere and significance.** For a fixed period, report the single-frequency p-value and say so. If the period was selected from a scan (or if several rigidity bins, e+/e-/ratio, and several detrendings were tried), report local and global significance with a Gross-Vitells trial factor (S37) and calibrate with toys that reproduce the 27-day sampling, the exposure pattern and the correlated systematics; do not use Wilks or a single-frequency p-value for a global claim. Count the rigidity bins and detrending choices in the trial factor. Red-noise (lag-1 autoregressive) surrogates are the appropriate null for a solar-modulated series [Documented, C45, C52: AMS' wavelet analysis of the daily proton flux used red-noise Monte Carlo surrogates at 95% C.L.; that is the daily-proton analysis only, and no AMS trial-factor treatment is described in the text read, [Unknown/needs input]].
5. **Control series (mandatory).** Run the identical search on (a) the exposure/livetime series, (b) the geomagnetic-cutoff transmission, (c) ISS beta angle / orbit season, (d) a control species (the e- or p flux alone, the e+/e- ratio at 1-5 GV where the strong effect is known, and the proton flux), and (e) the efficiency series. A coincident 1-yr signal in any of these is a systematic, not a discovery. A 1-yr period is also where the exposure, the ISS orbit-precession (beta-angle) cycle and any seasonal temperature-dependent detector state can leave a signature; the cutoff-safety factor and the lowest-rigidity bin are the most exposed.
6. **Expected sensitivity.** [General method] The documented time variation is large at low rigidity and small at high rigidity: [Documented, C40, C41, abstract level] the electron/proton and electron/positron hysteresis appears below about 8.5 GV, and positrons are modulated more than protons below about 7 GV (do not merge the two thresholds). So above about 10 GV most of the 5-50 GV range may be nearly time-independent in the ratio, and a one-year amplitude there is expected to be small relative to the statistical error per rotation; compute the expected sensitivity (injected-sinusoid toys: amplitude vs rigidity band) before looking at the data, and decide in advance what amplitude would be detectable. If the sensitivity is poor above ~20 GV, say so and restrict the search band rather than quote a null for 50 GV.
7. **Injection and closure.** Inject sinusoids of known amplitude and phase into the per-bin counts (not into the final ratio), propagate through the template fit and unfolding, and check amplitude bias, phase recovery and the coverage of the quoted interval. Check that the detrending does not absorb or fabricate a 1-yr term.

## 7. Cross-checks

Splits by charge-sign handling (loose/tight lepton ID, alternative proton templates), alternate time-bin lengths (27 vs 54 days), alternate detrending, rigidity-band splits (5-10, 10-20, 20-50 GV), period splits (before/after the 2013 reversal, and by hardware/reconstruction era), e+ and e- separately, orthogonal ID chains, signal-injection, pulls and goodness of fit. Agreement with a published figure is plausibility only, not validation: state a metric (for example amplitude bias below a fraction of its uncertainty) and a trigger (add a systematic or split the period if exceeded).

## 8. Reporting

Report the per-bin R_k with the covariance and exposures so others can refit; report amplitude, phase, local and global significance, the search band, the trend model, all control-series results, and what was fixed in advance. Interpretation (a drift or modulation model) is a separate model step. Do not present another experiment's method as AMS practice; the design above is a proposal.

## 9. Inputs that would change the answer

Rigidity vs energy bins; e+/e- ratio vs fraction; whether the range really extends to 50 GV (documented AMS daily lepton range ends at 41.9 GV); e+ counts per rotation in the top bins; whether a fixed 1-yr period or a band is searched; whether this is public-data work or internal (public: only published AMS results can be compared; internal data, run lists and calibrations are not available here); per-rotation efficiency, livetime and charge-confusion records; hardware eras and reconstruction versions inside the span.
