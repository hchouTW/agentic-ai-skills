**Verdict: not defensible as stated. All three elements are flawed and need replacing before the analysis can be trusted.** [General method / Proposal unless tagged; no AMS numbers are quoted]

**Defects, in priority order**

1. **Charge confusion from the Gaussian rigidity resolution (fatal).** Charge confusion (positive-sign protons reconstructed with negative sign) is a tail phenomenon, not the Gaussian core (invariant 4). Sources: large-angle multiple scattering, hard interactions and delta rays, hit-reconstruction/mis-association, and alignment/ladder effects. The Gaussian core estimate can be orders of magnitude too low. Because protons outnumber antiprotons by a very large factor, a tiny tail rate is a leading background at high |R|. Required: a dedicated charge-confusion estimator (variables sensitive to track quality and to inner-tracker versus full-span consistency), a template for charge-confused protons, tail validation on data (for example the track-splitting consistency of the inner-tracker and full-span rigidity measurements, or an independent charge-sign control such as positive-rigidity protons with the same selection) and with high-rigidity test-beam protons. State the metric and a trigger for adding an uncertainty. [Documented, C22: the published antiproton analysis includes a charge-confused proton template and checks charge-confusion MC with 400 GV proton test-beam data; this is documentation of S08, not a recipe for your analysis.]

2. **Electron background "subtracted by MC".** Electrons are the dominant negative-sign background at low rigidity. A pure MC subtraction inherits the MC modelling of TRD/ECAL response, bremsstrahlung and the rigidity-dependent electron-to-antiproton ratio, with no data constraint and no uncertainty. Replace it with data-driven or data-constrained templates (for example an electron-enriched sample selected with TRD/ECAL, whose shape is then used in the fit), and a fit of the electron yield per rigidity bin rather than a fixed subtraction. Add template-shape and finite-MC-statistics uncertainties. Also treat other negative-sign backgrounds (π⁻ and secondaries/interacting particles). Cross-check the e⁻ contamination by varying the TRD/ECAL selection and checking stability of the flux.

3. **"Signal = negative tracks passing a TRD cut".** A TRD cut alone cannot define the signal. The TRD does not measure charge sign (invariant 2); sign comes only from the Tracker/magnet. The TRD only separates hadron-like from lepton-like. The signature also needs |Z| = 1 (Tracker and TOF charge), a consistent β from TOF/RICH, and an ECAL/TRD hadron-like response. A single cut gives no signal estimate, only a sample. A template fit of a charge-sign-sensitive variable (for example signed rigidity or the charge-confusion estimator combined with a TRD estimator) in reconstructed bins is a better design [Proposal; not AMS practice].

**Further gaps to close**
- No estimand, observable space or response is defined (flux versus p̄/p, in |R|), and no efficiency, acceptance, exposure or livetime ledger, so the analysis starts from a cut instead of from the estimand. If p̄/p is the target, do not assume acceptance and efficiency cancel; classify each nuisance as correlated, partial or independent and verify.
- Migration and unfolding of the rigidity response, with a stated stopping criterion.
- Do not multiply conditional efficiencies as if independent, and do not correct one effect twice (for example a veto and an efficiency correction).
- Statistics: use Poisson/exact or toy-validated intervals at high rigidity, where antiproton counts are low; no S/sqrt(B) and no Gaussian errors there.
- Validation: "reasonable agreement" is not validation. State a metric (for example data/MC pull of the control samples, or stability of the flux versus the cut) and a trigger for changing the method.

**Inputs that would change the verdict**
- The rigidity range. At low rigidity the electron background dominates, and at high rigidity the charge-confusion tail dominates.
- Whether this is a public-MC study or an internal data analysis, and what charge-confusion control sample is available.
- Whether the target is the flux or the p̄/p ratio.
- Whether templates are data-driven or MC, and what their provenance is.
