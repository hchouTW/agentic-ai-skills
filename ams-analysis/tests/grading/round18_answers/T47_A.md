**Source and currency.** The paper is S09, "Antiprotons and Elementary Particles over a Solar Cycle", PRL 134, 051002 (published 2025). Its data run from May 2011 to June 2022. Publication date and data period are different things. It is distinct from the earlier time-integrated antiproton paper S08 (PRL 117, 091103, 2016; 1-450 GV, 3.49e5 antiprotons), so do not mix their numbers [Documented, C06]. I have no web access here. The index was last verified 2026-09-20, so I cannot say there is no newer or superseding paper.

## What the main article gives

- **Data set and method [Documented, C07, C120].**
  - It has 1.1e6 antiprotons in 11 rigidity bins from 1.00 to 41.9 GV, with one flux per Bartels rotation (27 days each) over 139 rotations.
  - The flux is Phi = N / (A (1+delta) eps T DeltaR). N is corrected for background and migration by unfolding. The delta correction is 8% at 1 GV, falling to 3% at 10 GV.
- **Systematics [Documented, C121].**
  - The total is about 9% at 1 GV and about 4% above 5 GV, with time-independent and time-dependent parts added in quadrature.
  - These percentages belong to this paper's bins and are not typical of other analyses.
- **Time dependence [Documented, C122].**
  - The antiproton flux varies significantly in time below about 10 GV. The relative variation falls with rigidity and is not visible above about 10 GV.
  - Antiprotons, protons, electrons and positrons all reach a minimum around 2014-2015 and a maximum around 2020. The data cover most of solar cycle 24 (including the 2013 polarity reversal) and the start of cycle 25.
  - Below about 4 GV the antiproton variation is significantly smaller than that of the other three.
  - These statements use 13-rotation moving averages.
- **Correlations [Documented, C07, C123].**
  - Antiprotons and protons show a hysteresis in every rigidity bin below 11.0 GV.
  - Below 4.88 GV the antiproton-proton hysteresis differs from the electron-positron one by more than 4 sigma.
  - Antiprotons and electrons have a linear relation from 1.00 to 7.09 GV, with the antiproton flux modulated less than the electron flux.
- **Spectral shape versus variation [Documented, C124].**
  - The antiproton spectral index is larger than the electron index, and the proton index larger than the positron index.
  - Using M, the relative magnitude of the flux variation, DeltaM/Deltagamma is negative and the same within errors for (e-, antiproton) and (e+, p).
  - The collaboration calls this a universal relation independent of charge sign. It is their reading, not a transport-model result.
- **Interpretation.** The collaboration concludes there is a clear charge-sign effect and that spectral shape matters. Those are interpretations, not measurements.

## What you can get without the supplement

- You can use the method, the quoted systematic percentages at 1 GV, 10 GV and above 5 GV, the qualitative variation by rigidity, the hysteresis and linear-relation statements, and the sign and ordering of the index and M results.
- You can only read plots, not tables. Figures show 13-rotation moving averages.

## What you cannot get without the supplement

- **Per-rotation fluxes and the time-averaged flux.** These are in Tables S1-S140 (S1-S139 per rotation, S140 the 11-year average) [Documented, C122, C180]. Without them you cannot refit, rebin or recompute anything from the paper.
- **Selection and template-fit details.**
  - The cutoff factor of 1.2 [Documented, C176] and the signal template taken from proton data [Documented, C177] are in the Supplemental Material text. Neither is in the main article, so you cannot reconstruct the selection or the background model from the main article alone.
  - The detector description and trigger efficiency are also there [Documented, C175].
- **Per-bin hysteresis significances.** The values in Table SA (C178) and the fit factors k (C179) are in the supplement. The main text gives only the thresholds above.
- **Index and M values per bin.** They are in the supplement figures. The main text gives only the qualitative ordering [Documented, C124].
- **Correlated errors.**
  - The index records no covariance across rigidity bins or rotations, in either the main article or the supplement table introduction [C121, C180].
  - So you cannot propagate correlated systematics for a fit across time or rigidity. Treat the correlation model as [Unknown/needs input].
- **Significance caveat.** Whether a look-elsewhere correction applies to the hysteresis significances is not described in the text read. Treat those as the collaboration's local numbers, not validated global significances.
- **Missing values.** The index records no collection-time value per rotation and no bin-by-bin events or purities. I will not supply any.

## Boundaries

- Do not extend these claims to the daily-flux papers (S43-S46) or to energy-binned lepton results. S09 is in rigidity.
- The caveat on any numbers beyond those above applies equally to the supplement.
