**Short answer.** AMS reports that above about 200 GV the secondary-to-primary flux ratios (Li/C, Be/C, B/C, Li/O, Be/O, B/O) harden: their spectral index becomes less negative. On average the index changes by 0.140 ± 0.025, and the review quotes a significance above 5 sigma. I could not check how that 5 sigma figure was built, so treat the significance as a quoted statement and not as a number I verified.

**What was measured** (no web access; source index last verified 2026-09-20)

- **Method [Documented, C84, S01 Phys. Rept. 894 review].**
  - The Li, Be and B fluxes were divided by the C and O fluxes.
  - A single power law, Phi_S/Phi_P proportional to R^Delta, was fitted in two non-overlapping rigidity intervals: 60.3-192 GV and 192-3300 GV.
  - The hardening is the difference Delta[192-3300] - Delta[60.3-192], averaged over the six ratios. It is 0.140 ± 0.025.
  - The 192 GV boundary is the review's choice, not a prescription.
- **Earlier paper [Documented, C136, S54 PRL 120, 021101 (2018), main article only].**
  - It reports an average hardening of 0.13 ± 0.03 above about 200 GV, using the same intervals and the same six ratios.
  - The 0.140 ± 0.025 value in the review is the later, more precise number.
  - The fluorine paper quotes 0.140 ± 0.025 only as a comparison value from earlier AMS papers [C160, S58]. Do not cite it as that paper's own result.
- **Individual spectra [Documented, C135, C136, C84].**
  - Li, Be and B have nearly identical spectral indices, and these differ distinctly from the He, C and O indices.
  - Above about 200 GV Li, Be and B harden more than He, C and O.
  - The Li, Be and B fluxes deviate from a single power law above 200 GV, in the same way as the primary He, C and O fluxes.
- **Interpretation [Documented as the collaboration's statement, C84, C136; not a measurement of mechanism].**
  - The papers say this is consistent with expectations if the hardening comes from propagation in the Galaxy.
  - They say hardening from source injection would harden secondaries and primaries similarly.
  - The review adds that no model predicted the observed behaviour of the primaries or the secondaries.
  - Third-party model papers were not read.

**How significant is it?**

- The only significance figure I can give is the review's statement that the average hardening exceeds 5 sigma [Documented, C84].
- [Unknown/needs input] I did not read these inputs, so the quote may not reflect them:
  - how the fit and systematic errors were combined or correlated across rigidity bins;
  - whether any correction was applied for the choice of interval boundaries or for the six ratios;
  - whether 5 sigma applies to the average over ratios or to each ratio separately.
- The ratios share C and O in the denominator and B in the numerator, so they are strongly correlated. Their average is not six independent confirmations.
- The per-rotation nuclei supplement tables list no covariance across bins and no trial factor [C106, a different paper]. I did not check whether the hardening papers' own supplements do.
- The simple ratio of the central value to its error is 0.140 / 0.025, about 5.6. That is my own arithmetic, [General method], and assumes Gaussian errors. It is consistent with the quoted >5 sigma but does not replace the paper's treatment.

**Caveats**

- The 0.140 ± 0.025 and >5 sigma values come from the review-level summary. The review is full text, but its numbers were checked against the PDF pages only for the quoted values.
- The 2018 PRL source index also records only main-article reading, with its Supplemental Material not read.
- Currency: I cannot verify "latest" without web access. Check INSPIRE, the journal and ams02.space for newer Li/Be/B or secondary-to-primary results before calling this the current value. The AMS paper list as of 2026-09-20 shows no newer dedicated paper on this ratio hardening.
- The first Li, Be and B paper was based on data from about the first 5 years. Do not extend the 0.140 value to a larger data set or to other species, such as N or F.
