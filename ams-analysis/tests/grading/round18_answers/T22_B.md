[General method; not AMS-specific, no AMS numbers used.]

**Profile likelihood ratio.** For data x, parameter of interest mu and nuisance parameters theta, with likelihood L(mu, theta):

lambda(mu) = L(mu, theta_hat_hat(mu)) / L(mu_hat, theta_hat)

- The numerator maximizes over theta at fixed mu (conditional MLE, theta_hat_hat).
- The denominator is the global maximum (unconditional MLEs).
- 0 < lambda <= 1. Values near 1 mean mu is well supported by the data.
- The test statistic is t(mu) = -2 ln lambda(mu). Profiling removes the nuisance parameters by re-optimizing them at each mu.
- Intervals: {mu : t(mu) <= c}. Hypothesis tests: reject mu_0 when t(mu_0) is large.

**Wilks' theorem.** If the hypothesis is true and regularity conditions hold, then as the sample size goes to infinity, t(mu_0) = -2 ln lambda(mu_0) is distributed as chi-square with k degrees of freedom. Here k is the number of parameters fixed by the null hypothesis, that is, the difference in free parameters between the full model and the constrained model.

- For one parameter of interest, the 68.27% interval is t <= 1, and the 95% interval is t <= 3.84 (1.96 squared).
- The result does not depend on the nuisance parameters or on the true value of mu_0. This is why the profile likelihood ratio is so convenient.
- Intuition: near the maximum, ln L is approximately quadratic (Gaussian MLE), so -2 ln lambda is a squared standardized deviation.

**Regularity conditions (when Wilks fails).**
1. The null value must be an interior point of the parameter space. For a boundary such as mu >= 0 tested at mu = 0, the limit is a mixture, 0.5 chi2(0) + 0.5 chi2(1), not chi2(1). The same applies to variances and to physical bounds.
2. All parameters must be identifiable under the null. A nuisance parameter that exists only under the alternative breaks it (Davies problem). Examples are the position of a spectral break or a peak, or a bump search over a mass range. These need a look-elsewhere or trial factor, with toys.
3. The model must be smooth, with a nondegenerate Fisher information, and the sample large enough. At small counts, or with weakly constrained nuisances, the distribution of t is not chi-square. Use exact Poisson or toy-calibrated (Monte Carlo) distributions, Feldman-Cousins or CLs constructions, and check coverage.
4. The nuisance parameters must not sit at boundaries, and the hypotheses must be nested.

**Practical rule.** State the construction used. Use the asymptotic chi-square only after checking the conditions above. Otherwise calibrate t with seeded toys and report the coverage check.
