# Limit setting for a rare antinucleus search in AMS-02, zero observed events

Scope: public-domain design only. No AMS-internal counts, cuts, livetimes, acceptances, or systematic sizes are used or invented. All steps below are [Proposal] unless labeled otherwise. Zero observed events is the observation, not the result: the result is a flux (or ratio) limit with an expected sensitivity next to it.

## 1. Estimand first (before any algorithm)

- Target: an upper limit on the flux (or on the antinucleus-to-matter ratio) of one species (e.g. antideuteron or antihelium), per predefined rigidity range. Fix species, `|Z|`, `A`, charge sign, and whether the limit is in rigidity or in kinetic energy per nucleon (state `Z` and `A` for every conversion; `p = |Z|R/c`).
- Fix the confidence level (e.g. 95%), one-sided, and the construction before looking at the signal region.
- Status [Documented, C14, C19, search-limited, last verified 2026-09-20]: no peer-reviewed AMS-02 antihelium paper and no AMS antideuteron search paper were found in INSPIRE-HEP. The only AMS-authored antihelium result in the index is the 1999 AMS-01 limit (S22). So there is no published AMS-02 number to quote here, and nothing below may be presented as AMS practice. For current status I cannot browse; I do not claim currency.

## 2. Observable space and blinding

- Signal topology: sign from Tracker/magnet only (TOF, TRD, ECAL, RICH do not measure charge sign); `|Z|` from several subsystems; mass from `R` and `β`.
- Mask the signal region in plots, logs, and optimization; define selection, bins, and diagnostics before unblinding; state who may unblind and when.

## 3. Background model (this is where the limit is won or lost)

The relevant backgrounds are tail phenomena (invariant 4). A Gaussian-core resolution estimate is not acceptable.

- Charge-sign confusion of the abundant same-`|Z|` matter (`d → anti-d`, `He → anti-He`) from Tracker tails, bad hit assignment, and secondary tracks.
- `|Z|` confusion from fragmentation and charge-changing interactions; wrong mass from `β` errors; interactions producing antiparticles.
- Estimate the sign-flip tail probability `p_flip(R)` from abundant matter samples under looser cuts and extrapolate to the tight selection. Test the extrapolation assumption explicitly. Use redundant signed-rigidity fits (different layer subsets, upper/lower segments), subsystem charge consistency before and after the magnet, and hit-pattern checks, validated on data and MC.
- Background ledger per component: origin, entry mechanism, control region and its signal contamination, transfer model, closure test, nuisances and correlations. Expected background `b` is then a number with an uncertainty `σ_b`, not an exactly known value.

## 4. Exposure and conversion to a flux limit

- Exposure `E(R) = A_eff(R) · T_live · geomagnetic transmission` for the signal species. Acceptance, efficiency, exposure, and livetime are distinct; do not apply any factor twice (if `E` already contains acceptance and livetime, do not multiply again).
- Efficiencies as conditional factors, with combined-efficiency closure (not a product of marginals). The signal efficiency for the antinucleus must be estimated for that species (the matter-sample efficiency is not directly transferable: different interaction cross sections, different `β`-`R` relation). Generation record, with a spectrum assumption that is varied, not tuned to the result.
- Flux limit: `Φ_UL = N_UL / (E · Δx)`, with the uncertainty on `E` propagated as a nuisance. A ratio limit relative to matter must classify each systematic as correlated, partial, or independent (invariant 5), not assume cancellation.

## 5. The count limit with zero observed events

[General method]
- `n_obs = 0`, `b = 0`: the one-sided 95% classical upper limit on signal counts is `-ln(0.05) ≈ 3.00`.
- `n_obs = 0`, known `b > 0`: the classical limit is `-ln(0.05) - b`. It shrinks as `b` grows and can reach zero or go negative. That is not a result and not a measure of sensitivity.
- The CLs limit is the `b`-independent `-ln(0.05) ≈ 3.00` for `n_obs = 0`. CLs over-covers by design and is not a confidence-level statement. Do not call the classical `-ln α` limit "independent of the background"; that holds for CLs (or a flat-prior Bayesian limit), which answers a different question. State which construction you report.
- Feldman-Cousins is the unified frequentist alternative. For `n = 0` it is never empty or negative, but it is an interval, not a CLs limit; do not mix the labels.
- Because `n_obs = 0` is below the expectation whenever `b > 0`, always report the expected sensitivity (median and 1 and 2 sigma bands under background only) next to the observed limit, and the construction chosen before unblinding. A tight observed limit from a downward fluctuation is not extra sensitivity.
- Uncertain background and nuisances (`σ_b`, efficiency, exposure, tail probability): use a profile-likelihood or toy-calibrated construction, with a CLs variant if protection against downward fluctuations is wanted, and compare with the marginalized treatment (a difference is itself a method systematic). Berger-Boos (guaranteed coverage over the background nuisance) is the option if coverage must be guaranteed; it is longer. Asymptotic formulae are not trusted at these counts without toy validation; check coverage in the low-count regime and scan the true mean, since coverage of a discrete distribution oscillates.
- Likelihood (symbolic): `L(μ, η) = Poisson(0 | μ·s_exp(η) + b(η)) × Π_a C_a(η_a)`, with `μ ≥ 0` the signal strength, and `C_a` auxiliary measurements (control-region counts, tail-probability measurement on matter, efficiency calibration) rather than arbitrary priors. Do not constrain a nuisance with the data being fitted.
- Multiple rigidity bins or species: a search over a predefined range needs a stated local versus global treatment (trial factor) if a significance is ever quoted; for a pure limit, state whether the limit is per bin or combined and the correlation of nuisances across bins.

Tools: `scripts/poisson_diagnostics.py` (`upper-limit`, `fc-interval`, `cls-limit`, `coverage`) and `scripts/likelihood_limits.py` (`profile-limit`, `profile-cls`, `neyman-limit`) provide the numbers once real inputs (`b`, `σ_b`) exist, with seed and toy count recorded. No inputs are supplied here, so I give no AMS-specific numerical limit.

## 6. Validation and sensitivity (before unblinding)

- Expected sensitivity and coverage studied on background-only toys before the signal region is opened.
- Signal injection with a known `μ`; fit-bias test across `μ` including zero; pulls of the nuisances; stability versus rigidity and time.
- Closure of the tail-probability extrapolation on matter samples; cross-checks with orthogonal identification chains, alternate track fits, period and geometry splits, charge-sign splits.
- Predefine a metric and a trigger for changing the method or adding an uncertainty ("reasonable agreement" is not validation; invariant 10). For example, a nuisance pull width above 1 or an uncovered injection is a trigger (thresholds are [Proposal], not AMS values).

## 7. Reporting

- Report: the limit (confidence level, one-sided, construction, nuisance treatment), the expected sensitivity band, the exposure and its uncertainty, the assumed signal spectrum or the rigidity range, and what a limit means (a bound on flux in that range for that species, not a statement about other species or ranges).
- If any candidate survives, keep the levels separate: candidate, excess (with local and global significance), evidence, discovery. A candidate is not an excess, and an excess is not a discovery. Zero observed events needs none of this, but the wording must stay a limit and must not be read as a measurement of absence outside the stated range.

None is available in public sources, and none is invented here.
