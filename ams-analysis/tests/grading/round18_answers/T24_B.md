# Limit setting for a rare antinucleus search in AMS-02 with zero observed events

Label key: [General method], [Proposal], [Unknown/needs input], [Documented, Cxx]. No AMS candidate counts, cuts, livetimes, acceptances, or systematic sizes are supplied here; all are symbolic inputs.

- Choose the species first (antideuteron, antihelium-3, antihelium-4). Z, A, sign and mass hypothesis change the observables, the dominant background, and the exposure. Symbols below are species-generic.

## 1. Estimand (define before choosing a method)

- Primary: a one-sided upper limit on the antinucleus flux Phi_UL in a predefined rigidity range (or on the ratio to the matching matter species, e.g. anti-He/He). State range, species, data period, and confidence level.
- Counts become a flux limit only through exposure: Phi_UL = N_UL / (E * DeltaR), with E = acceptance x efficiency x livetime x geomagnetic transmission for the signal species. [General method]
- A limit on a rigidity-integrated flux or a ratio is a different estimand with different cancellations. Do not mix them.

## 2. Sequence (blinding and everything else fixed before looking)

1. Define the signal topology: |Z|, negative sign, and mass hypothesis region in (R, beta, |Z|). Sign comes from the Tracker/magnet only; |Z| from several subsystems; mass from R and beta. TRD, TOF, ECAL and RICH do not measure charge sign.
2. Define and mask the signal region before any optimization. Record who may unblind and when.
3. Build the background ledger (origin, entry mechanism, control region and its signal contamination, transfer model, closure test, nuisances). Dominant paths:
   - Charge-sign confusion of abundant same-|Z| matter (e.g. He to anti-He) through Tracker non-Gaussian tails and bad hit assignment.
   - |Z| confusion from fragmentation and charge-changing interactions; secondary tracks.
   - Wrong mass from beta errors; antiparticles produced in interactions.
   These are tail phenomena: reject any estimate from the Gaussian core of the rigidity resolution.
4. Measure the sign-flip tail on abundant matter samples (looser cuts), as a function of rigidity, and validate the extrapolation to the tight selection. Redundant signed-rigidity fits (different layer subsets, upper/lower segments), hit-pattern checks, and subsystem |Z| consistency are the selection logic and also the control regions.
5. Define candidate-level diagnostics (event display checklist, residuals, partial-fit consistency) before unblinding.
6. Compute the expected sensitivity and coverage before opening the box.

## 3. Inference with zero observed events

- Counting model: n_obs = 0 in the signal region; mu = s + b, where s = Phi * E * DeltaR (efficiency-folded) and b is the expected background from the tail estimate.
- Do not use S/sqrt(B), Wilks, or Gaussian errors. [General method, invariant 6]
- Zero-count bound with b = 0: the one-sided 95% CL upper limit on signal counts is -ln(0.05) = 3.00 [General method]. This is arithmetic, not an AMS number.
- With expected background b > 0 the constructions differ and must be named:
  - Classical: N_UL = -ln(1 - CL) - b. It shrinks as b grows and can reach zero or go negative, so it must not be quoted alone. It is not "independent of the background".
  - CLs: the b-independent -ln(0.05) = 3.00 for n_obs = 0 with known b. It over-covers by design and is a convention, not a confidence-level statement.
  - Feldman-Cousins: never empty or negative. A zero lower bound is expected and not a failure.
  Pick one construction in advance and state it. Reporting FC or CLs with the expected sensitivity band is the usual safe choice; report the expected limit next to the observed one, because with n_obs = 0 the observed limit mostly reflects a downward fluctuation of b.
- The background b is uncertain (tail probability times the abundant-matter flux). Treat it as a nuisance with an auxiliary measurement from the abundant-sample tail study (Poisson or gamma-type constraint from its own count, not an arbitrary prior), not as exactly known. Use a profile-likelihood or toy-calibrated construction (scripts/likelihood_limits.py: profile-limit, profile-cls, neyman-limit with Berger-Boos if coverage must be guaranteed), and compare against marginalized results varying sigma_b. The difference between constructions is itself a method systematic.
- Other nuisances: efficiency and acceptance (exposure E is a nuisance, possibly correlated with the background model), template or tail extrapolation, mass-region definition. Propagate E into Phi_UL; do not multiply efficiencies as if independent (combined-efficiency closure, ordering closure, Frechet bounds), and do not correct one effect in two categories.
- Check coverage with seeded toys at small counts and scan the true mean, since coverage of a discrete distribution oscillates. Record seed, toy count, CL, and script version. [General method]
- If the search spans several rigidity bins or several species, a look-elsewhere trial factor applies to any significance statement; a limit-only report across a predefined range avoids global-significance claims but should state that the bins are not independent if they share nuisances. Joint bins share one signal-flux model and common nuisances; do not combine bin limits quadratically.

## 4. Reporting

- Report: construction, CL, rigidity range, species, exposure with uncertainty, expected limit and band, observed limit (zero events), the background estimate and its validation, and the seed.
- Zero observed events means no candidate. A later candidate is not an excess, an excess is not evidence, and evidence is not discovery; thresholds predefined. Never quote or invent candidate counts, significances, or sensitivities.
- Cross-check: signal injection with closure, alternative tail models, charge-sign and period splits, comparison with published limits (e.g. AMS-01, S22) as plausibility only, never as AMS-02 practice.
