**Short answer.** The ECAL is a strong e/p discriminator, but it is not a charge estimator for nuclei, and it never gives charge sign.

**What the ECAL contributes to e/p separation** ([Documented, C50, C71, C72] for the description; the mechanism is [General method])
- It is a 17 X0 lead/scintillating-fibre sampling calorimeter with 3D shower imaging (9 superlayers, 1296 cells). Electrons and positrons shower fully and electromagnetically. Protons mostly shower hadronically, with deeper, broader and more irregular profiles.
- Shower shape. AMS uses an estimator, Lambda_ECAL, that compares the cell energy depositions and shower parameters with an electromagnetic shower [Documented, C72]. It is built on the same shower parametrization as the energy measurement.
- Energy-momentum match. For an electron, E_ECAL is about pc, so E/(|Z||R|) with Z = 1 is near 1 within resolution, bremsstrahlung and leakage. For a proton the response is a hadronic fraction, so E/p is below 1 on average with wide tails. AMS combines Lambda_ECAL with E/p > 0.7 [Documented, C72].
- A published proton-rejection curve exists, as a function of rigidity for 90% and 65% e± efficiency (1-2000 GeV/c). It is a figure, so I give no numbers here. Any rejection figure is only valid with its efficiency and range [Documented, C72].
- The ECAL is largely independent of the TRD (transition radiation versus shower development). Shower shape and E/p share the ECAL energy, so they are correlated and must not be treated as independent handles.
- The ECAL gives no charge sign. Positron versus electron comes from the Tracker/magnet only (invariant 2). Charge confusion is a tail problem in the Tracker and cannot be fixed by e/p ID.
- Caveats:
  - Hadronic showers with a large pi0 component fake electrons.
  - Leakage, bremsstrahlung and upstream conversions matter.
  - Do not use ECAL variables both to select and to define the template that measures the selection efficiency (circularity).
  - Validate the proton tails with orthogonal selections, and check stability against time and energy.

**Can it be a charge estimator for nuclei? No, not as a standard one.**
- In the detector matrix, |Z| comes from the Tracker, TOF and RICH. The TRD gives limited, context-dependent |Z| information. The ECAL is not the standard nucleus charge estimator [General method, detector matrix].
- Physics reasons:
  - Nuclei interact hadronically, mostly in the upper part of the calorimeter, so the response is a species-dependent hadronic fraction. Fragmentation in the ECAL material changes the deposit further.
  - The deposited energy is not a function of Z alone. It depends on the kinetic energy per nucleon, A, the interaction depth, containment and leakage. Calibration is EM-based (electrons, e.g. a CERN test beam [Documented, C50]).
  - E/R = 1 has no physical basis for He or heavier nuclei, because p = |Z|R/c. Always state Z in any E/R variable.
  - Large dynamic range effects (saturation, nonlinearity) add further problems.
- Using ECAL energy to infer Z would be circular and badly conditioned. It would also fold hadronic-response uncertainty into the charge assignment.
- Recommended use [Proposal]:
  - Take |Z| from the Tracker (inner tracker or L1), TOF and RICH. Cross-check their consistency and treat charge-estimator tails and adjacent-element leakage explicitly.
  - Use the ECAL at most as a supporting handle, for example to flag fragmentation or interactions, or for particle-type consistency. Validate it on a control sample and do not call it a charge measurement.
  - If someone wants to test an ECAL-based charge proxy, treat it as a research study. It would need an independent control sample with known Z, a resolution and tail model per element, and a stated metric. I know of no published AMS use of this, so nothing is documented.
