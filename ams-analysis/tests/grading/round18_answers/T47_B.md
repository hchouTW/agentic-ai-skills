**Paper:** AMS, "Antiprotons and Elementary Particles over a Solar Cycle", PRL 134, 051002 (2025) (S09). Index status: the main article and the Supplemental Material text were read on 2026-10-02. The tables S1-S140 were not transcribed and the supplement figures were not interpreted.

## What AMS published (main article)

- **[Documented, C07, C120]** Continuous antiproton fluxes per Bartels rotation (27 days). The data cover 139 rotations from May 2011 to June 2022, which is most of solar cycle 24 including the 2013 polarity reversal, plus the start of cycle 25. There are 11 rigidity bins from 1.00 to 41.9 GV and 1.1x10^6 antiprotons after selection and background subtraction.
- **[Documented, C120]** The flux is `Phi = N / (A (1+delta) eps T DeltaR)`. N is corrected for background and bin-to-bin migration by unfolding. A is the Monte Carlo effective acceptance, delta is a data-minus-MC correction, eps is the trigger efficiency and T is the collection time.
- **[Documented, C121]** Total systematic error is about 9% at 1 GV and about 4% above 5 GV. It is the quadrature sum of time-independent and time-dependent parts.
- **[Documented, C122]** The antiproton flux varies significantly in time below about 10 GV. The relative variation decreases with rigidity and is not visible above about 10 GV (13-rotation moving averages). The flux reaches a minimum around 2014-2015 and a maximum around 2020, as do the proton, electron and positron fluxes. Below about 4 GV the antiproton variation is significantly smaller than that of the other three.
- **[Documented, C07, C122]** Antiproton variation is smaller in magnitude than proton, electron and positron variation.
- **[Documented, C123]** For 1.00-2.97 GV with 13-rotation averages, the antiproton-proton fluxes show a hysteresis. It is seen in every rigidity bin below 11.0 GV. Below 4.88 GV its detailed form differs from the electron-positron hysteresis by more than 4 sigma. Antiproton and electron fluxes show a linear relation in all bins from 1.00 to 7.09 GV, with the antiproton modulated significantly less than the electron. The charge-sign-effect and spectral-shape conclusions are the collaboration's interpretation.
- **[Documented, C124]** A model-independent analysis relates the flux-variation magnitude M to the spectral index gamma. Delta M / Delta gamma is negative, approaches zero with rigidity up to 11 GV, and is the same within errors for (e-, antiproton) and (e+, p). The collaboration calls this a universal relation independent of charge sign and takes it to mean the modulation differences are mainly due to spectral shape. That is their reading, not a transport model. [General method] A drift or force-field interpretation is a separate model step.

## What you can get from the main paper alone

- The method, the binning, the systematic budget at the level above, and the qualitative and rigidity-threshold statements.
- The 11-year-averaged behaviour: the antiproton flux peaks between 2 and 4 GV with a turnover near 3 GV.
- The conclusions drawn from the correlations.

## What you cannot get without the supplement

- **Per-rotation flux values with their errors.** They are in Tables S1-S139 and the 11-year average is in S140. Each table has a statistical error, a time-dependent systematic error and a total systematic error. Without them you cannot refit, recompute moving averages or re-derive M.
- **Per-bin spectral indices and the figures S1-S9.** These are not in the main text.
- **Per-bin hysteresis and linear-relation numbers.** [Documented, C178, C179, supplement text] Table SA gives the per-bin hysteresis significances, and the supplement gives the k(e-, antiproton) values. They are not in the main article.
- **Full selection, cutoff, template-fit and trigger details.** [Documented, C175-C177, supplement text] These are in the supplement text: the 1.2 times IGRF cutoff factor varied to 1.4, the template fit in two rigidity regions, and the trigger efficiency.
