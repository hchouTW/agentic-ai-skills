**Verdict: not acceptable as described.** The L-curve choice is a defensible start, but the fit to diagonal errors is the real defect. (The AMS tie is only the unfolding and fit practice; I quote no AMS numbers.)

**1. Fitting the unfolded points with diagonal errors is wrong [General method].**
- Unfolded bins are correlated. Neighbouring bins are typically anticorrelated, and regularization adds smoothing correlations on top. Dropping the off-diagonal terms gives a wrong chi2 and a wrong index uncertainty. Either can be too small or too large, depending on the correlation structure and the bin range.
- The fit also ignores correlated systematics (acceptance, efficiency, scale) and the finite response-matrix statistics.
- If you fit unfolded points, use the full covariance matrix. It needs the data statistics, the MC/template statistics, the systematic components and the unfolding-induced correlations. Build it by toys through the whole unfolding, not by linear propagation. Record bin ordering, units and absolute versus relative convention, and check it is positive semidefinite (`scripts/validate_covariance.py`). Never combine components with unknown correlations in quadrature.
- Even with the full covariance, a Tikhonov-regularized unfolding biases the spectrum towards smooth shapes. A fit to it inherits that bias, which the covariance does not capture.

**2. The estimand is the spectral index, so forward folding is the better tool.**
- Fit the index directly with a forward-folded likelihood on the raw counts: `mu_i(theta, eta) = sum_j R_ij A_j T_j Phi_j(theta) dx_j + b_i`.
- That needs no regularization and no unfolded covariance. Nuisance parameters enter naturally, and the fit tests the parametrization. Its cost is that the result depends on the assumed functional form.
- If you also publish the unfolded spectrum, treat it as a separate deliverable and publish its covariance and the response.

**3. The automatic L-curve choice needs validation.**
- Declaring the criterion in advance is acceptable. But the corner is a heuristic. It is not a bias-variance optimum, and it can be ill-defined or unstable for noisy or smooth data.
- Check it on MC pseudo-experiments that include distorted and kinked spectra and varied priors. Quote the sensitivity of the result to the regularization strength and to the prior, and add the spread as a systematic.
- Run these tests, with a stated metric and a trigger for changing the method (`scripts/unfolding_diagnostics.py`):
  - MC closure.
  - Reweighted-spectrum closure.
  - Response perturbations.
  - Pulls and coverage on the fitted index, from toys through unfold-then-fit.
  - Stability against binning.
- Do not tune the strength on the final data spectrum. Do not copy a value from another analysis.

**4. Further checks.**
- Confirm the response matrix is declared as conditional or as including efficiency, so inefficiency is not counted twice.
- Check for migration at the range edges.
- Keep truth and reconstructed variables, and rigidity versus energy, explicit.

**Inputs that would change the verdict.**
- Whether you have the full unfolding covariance.
- The migration strength and the number of bins.
- The counts per bin.
- Whether the goal is a model-independent spectrum or only the index.

With the full covariance and validated toys, fitting the unfolded points is acceptable. With diagonal errors it is not.
