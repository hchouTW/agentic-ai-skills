**What AMS found (Li, Be, B paper, PRL 120, 021101, 2018; main article only).**

- **Interval definitions.** [Documented, C136] The spectral index is gamma = d[log Phi]/d[log R]. It is computed over rigidity intervals bounded by 7.09, 12.0, 16.6, 22.8, 41.9, 60.3, 192 and 3300 GV.
- **Compared with He, C, O.** [Documented, C136] The magnitude and rigidity dependence of the Li, Be and B indices are nearly identical to one another. They are distinctly different from He, C and O, whose comparison indices come from an earlier AMS paper (Ref. 14, not this one). Above about 200 GV, Li, Be and B harden more than He, C and O.
- **Flux shapes.** [Documented, C135]
  - Li and B have an identical rigidity dependence above about 7 GV. All three fluxes (Li, Be, B) are identical above about 30 GV.
  - Be differs from Li and B at lower rigidity. The paper says this is "most likely" due to radioactive 10Be (half-life 1.4 MY). That is the paper's own statement, not a measurement.
  - Fits to a constant give Li/B = 0.72 ± 0.02 above 7 GV and Be/B = 0.36 ± 0.01 above 30 GV. Li/Be = 2.0 ± 0.1 above 30 GV.
  - All three fluxes deviate from a single power law above 200 GV in an identical way, as AMS also saw in the primary He, C and O fluxes.
- **Secondary-to-primary ratios.** [Documented, C136]
  - AMS defines Delta = d[log(Phi_S/Phi_P)]/d[log(R)] over the intervals 60.3-192 GV and 192-3300 GV. It does this for Li/C, Be/C, B/C, Li/O, Be/O and B/O.
  - The average hardening above about 200 GV is 0.13 ± 0.03. The paper states this is consistent with expectations if the hardening comes from propagation properties in the Galaxy.

**What this says about propagation.**

- [Documented, C136, the paper's own interpretation] The secondary-to-primary hardening is consistent with the hardening arising from propagation in the Galaxy, not from the sources.
- [General method] The reasoning is that secondaries are produced by fragmentation of primaries during propagation. If the secondary and primary spectra both harden but the secondary hardens more, the rigidity dependence of the escape or diffusion is the natural suspect.
- [General method] This is model-dependent. The data show an empirical hardening, and the mechanism is a model step. Not-yet-excluded alternatives include source effects, reacceleration and spallation cross-section uncertainties. The Be-Li difference below 30 GV is attributed by the paper to 10Be, so low-rigidity ratios also depend on isotopic and cross-section assumptions.
