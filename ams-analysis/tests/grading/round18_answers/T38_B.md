Yes, but only as AMS's own conditional, model-dependent fit coefficient, not as a propagation-free measurement of the source N/O.

**What 0.092 is.** [Documented, C91, C167] The value comes from fitting the nitrogen flux over the whole rigidity range as a weighted sum of a primary reference (oxygen) and a secondary reference (boron): Phi_N = (0.092 +- 0.002) x Phi_O + (0.61 +- 0.02) x Phi_B. The quoted uncertainty is +-0.002 with chi2/d.o.f. = 59/64. The AMS Physics Reports review (S01) describes the 0.092 +- 0.002 as the N/O abundance ratio at the source and compares it with the Solar-System value 0.135 (+0.051, -0.047), which it calls consistent. Table I of the Na/Al paper (S59, C167) lists the same N coefficients and attributes them to the review. So 0.092 is not a new result of that Letter.

**Caveats to state when quoting it.**
- [Documented, C91] The "source" reading depends on the assumption that O has a purely primary shape and B a purely secondary shape. The review itself notes that O has the lowest secondary content among He, C and O, and that B is mostly made from primary C and O.
- The fit quality (59/64) does not test that assumption. The 0.092 is therefore a coefficient conditional on that choice. "Source abundance" is the authors' interpretation and is not a propagation-free measurement in the strict sense.
- [Documented, C142] The dedicated nitrogen paper (S55, PRL 121, 051103) gives a different number. Its entire-range fit is 0.090 +- 0.002 x Phi_O + 0.62 +- 0.02 x Phi_B, chi2/d.o.f. = 51/64. Above 60 GV the fit gives 0.083 +- 0.005 x Phi_O + 0.66 +- 0.04 x Phi_B. The value therefore depends on the rigidity range and the data set. The paper itself says the fit allows the N/O abundance ratio at the source to be determined without Galactic propagation. That is the authors' statement and is not independently evaluated here.
- 0.092 is the review's (S01) number, which overlaps the nitrogen paper's result. Cite it to the review (C91) or to the Table I attribution (C167). Do not attribute it to the 2018 nitrogen paper, whose own value is 0.090.

**Suggested wording.** "AMS fits the nitrogen flux as (0.092 +- 0.002) x Phi_O + (0.61 +- 0.02) x Phi_B (Phys. Rept. 894). It interprets the primary coefficient as the N/O abundance ratio at the source, under the assumption that O is purely primary and B purely secondary. The dedicated PRL 121, 051103 gives 0.090 +- 0.002."

**Limits.**
- [General method] Do not treat 0.092 as a model-independent or propagation-free source abundance, and do not compare it with the Solar-System value as a precision test without stating the O and B assumption.
- Do not drop the uncertainty or the primary/secondary context.
- [Unknown] The Supplemental Material was not read for S55, so any systematic breakdown of the coefficient is not available here.
