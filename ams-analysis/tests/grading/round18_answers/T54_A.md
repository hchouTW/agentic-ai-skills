**Source:** AMS nitrogen Letter, PRL 121, 051103 (2018), S55, main article only (Supplemental Material not read). Index last verified 2026-09-20; no web access, so I make no claim about currency or later papers.

**Sample, range and period** [Documented, C137]
- The flux uses 2.2 x 10^6 nitrogen events. The paper's own note is that this is the count after background subtraction.
- The rigidity range is 2.2 GV to 3.3 TV, in 66 bins.
- The period is May 19, 2011 to May 26, 2016, the first five years on the ISS. In that period AMS collected 8.5 x 10^10 cosmic-ray events.
- Total flux error is 4% at 100 GV.

**Two-component fit** [Documented, C142]
- The flux over the entire rigidity range is fitted as a weighted sum of a primary reference (oxygen) and a secondary reference (boron):
  Phi_N = Phi_PN + Phi_SN = (0.090 +- 0.002) x Phi_O + (0.62 +- 0.02) x Phi_B, with chi2/d.o.f. = 51/64.
- The same fit above 60 GV gives (0.083 +- 0.005) x Phi_O + (0.66 +- 0.04) x Phi_B, with chi2/d.o.f. = 18/25.
- The secondary contribution falls from about 70% at a few GV to below 30% above 1 TV.
- The model-independent spectral index (above 8.48 GV) lies between the primary (He, C, O) and secondary (Li, Be, B) indices. It hardens above about 100 GV and becomes identical to the He, C, O indices above about 700 GV.

**What comes from the nitrogen paper itself, and what does not**
- From this Letter:
  - the nitrogen flux and its event count, range, period and errors;
  - the nitrogen spectral index;
  - the fitted coefficients 0.090 +- 0.002 and 0.62 +- 0.02, and the chi2 values above.
- Not from this Letter:
  - The O and B fluxes entering the fit are earlier published AMS results (the Letter's Refs. [8,9]), not measured here. The same is true of the He, C, O and Li, Be, B spectral indices used for comparison [Documented, C142].
  - The assumption that O is a purely primary shape and B a purely secondary shape is a modelling choice. The coefficients are conditional on it, and a good chi2 does not test it. This is my [Proposal]-level caveat, not a paper statement.
  - The paper says the fit allows the N/O abundance at the source to be determined without Galactic propagation. I have not evaluated that statement. The fit is descriptive, not proof of a mechanism.

**Do not mix in the Phys. Rept. review numbers** [Documented, C91]
- The review quotes a different summary: 3.9 million nitrogen nuclei over 2.2 GV to 3.3 TV, a 3.8% error at 100 GV, and the fit 0.092 +- 0.002 x Phi_O + 0.61 +- 0.02 x Phi_B with chi2/d.o.f. = 59/64.
- That is the review's own summary, with a larger event sample (which data period it covers was not checked). It is not the Letter's result.
