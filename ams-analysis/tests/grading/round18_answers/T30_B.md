**Verdict: No. Do not clip the eigenvalues and move on.** A correlation of -1.3 is impossible, because |ρ| ≤ 1 for any valid covariance. That makes it a construction error, not round-off. Clipping would hide the defect and leave a matrix that looks valid. [General method; skill rule in inference-and-unfolding, non-PSD diagnosis]

## Defects in priority order

**1. The covariance is invalid (blocking).**
- Clipping is only acceptable when the most negative eigenvalue is at round-off level, about machine precision times the largest eigenvalue. A -1.3 correlation is far outside that.
- Likely causes:
  - mixed absolute and relative conventions, or mixed units
  - bin ordering mismatched between components
  - rounded or hand-typed correlation tables
  - a subtracted term with an implausible correlation
  - a component added twice
  - an under-regularized unfolding, which gives strong neighbour anticorrelation (bins 4 and 5 are adjacent)
  - a variance computed for a different bin than the covariance
- Diagnosis steps:
  1. Check that the matrix is symmetric and finite, symmetrize it as (C+Cᵀ)/2, and compute the eigenvalues.
  2. Check that the diagonal is positive and that every |ρ| ≤ 1.
  3. Check whether stat plus syst blocks sum to the total.
  4. Check kind (absolute vs relative), units and bin order.
- `scripts/validate_covariance.py` does this; add `--demo-psd-clip` to see what a clip would change, as a labeled [Proposal] diagnostic only.
- Repair: rebuild from coherent variations so each block is PSD by construction. Propagate through the full unfolding with toys; the toy sample covariance is PSD. Never fix it by dropping correlations or by adding components in quadrature when their correlations are unknown.
- If a clip is eventually justified, record it and confirm that the fit or χ² result is unchanged within tolerance.

**2. Response-matrix normalization, which may double count efficiency.**
- A column sum of 0.85 while you also multiply the counts by a trigger efficiency is a double-count risk. The inefficiency must live in only one place.
- Two consistent conventions:
  - **Conditional response:** columns sum to 1, and the inefficiency lives in the acceptance and efficiency factors A_j and ε_j. Check the metadata flag `conditional_on_selected` in `scripts/validate_response.py`.
  - **Efficiency inside the matrix:** columns sum to ε ≤ 1, and no separate efficiency factor is applied. Check the metadata flag `includes_efficiency`.
- A 0.85 sum can mean inefficiency, but it can also mean migration to reconstructed bins outside the stored range (under/overflow not stored in R). Don't assume it is inefficiency. Check whether the matrix stores under/overflow rows, and check orientation: are columns the true bins and rows the reconstructed bins?
- Also check that the 0.85 does not already contain the trigger efficiency, and that exposure is not already A·T when you multiply by acceptance again.
- Run `validate_response.py` with the declared orientation and normalization. Specify what the 0.85 represents and where each efficiency (trigger, selection) is applied exactly once.

**3. The two problems may be linked.** A mis-normalized response, or an efficiency correction applied twice, changes the unfolded flux and its covariance. Fix the response first, then regenerate the covariance. Don't repair a covariance computed from a flawed response.

## Inputs that would change the verdict
- The eigenvalue spectrum. If the smallest eigenvalue is about 1e-16 times the largest, a clip is fine, but then the -1.3 would not appear. A -1.3 correlation points to a defect upstream.
- The metadata for the response matrix: orientation, normalization, and whether it includes under/overflow or efficiency.
- Whether the covariance is absolute or relative, its units, its bin order, and which stat and syst blocks were summed.
- The unfolding method and its regularization, and whether the toys were run through the full unfolding.
