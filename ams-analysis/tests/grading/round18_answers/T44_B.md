**Verdict: not acceptable as described. Two separate problems; the second is the more serious.**

**1. Automatic L-curve corner is a method choice, not a validated criterion.** [General method]
- The L-curve corner can be ill-defined, can be missing, or can sit at an under- or over-regularized point. It is also sensitive to binning and to the prior or truth spectrum.
- Declare the criterion in advance and check it on MC pseudo-data. For example, minimize expected bias² + variance on an ensemble that includes stress-test spectra (kinks, distorted shapes). Report the chosen strength and how the result moves when it changes.
- A regularization strength tuned on your own unfolded data, or copied from another analysis, has no meaning outside the response, bins and statistics it was tuned for.
- Required validation: nominal and reweighted closure, distorted-spectrum stress tests, response perturbations, pull and coverage toys, and stability versus binning. Add a prior-variation study and quote the spread as a systematic.
- For orientation, published AMS flux unfolding stops the iteration on a declared convergence criterion (successive steps agree within 0.1%) [Documented, C23]. That is a stated criterion, not an automatic one.

**2. Fitting the index with diagonal errors is wrong.** [General method]
- Unfolded bins are correlated. Tikhonov regularization adds smoothing correlations, and unfolding adds anticorrelations between neighbouring bins. Diagonal errors drop all of this.
- The χ² and the index uncertainty are then wrong, and the fit can show bias and spurious goodness of fit. Regularization also smooths real structure, so a power law fits more easily than it should.
- Per-bin errors alone do not describe the result. You need the full covariance of the unfolded points: statistical, finite-MC, systematic, and unfolding-induced.
- Check that the covariance is positive semidefinite. A singular matrix can signal over-regularization or an implicit constraint.
- Propagate it with toys through the full unfolding. Do not rely on linear propagation, and do not add components in quadrature when their correlations are unknown.

**Better options, depending on the estimand:**
- **Estimand is the index (a model parameter):** forward-fold a power law with `μ_i(θ, η) = Σ_j R_ij A_j T_j Φ_j(θ) Δx_j + b_i` and fit the raw counts with a Poisson likelihood. This needs no regularization, and the nuisances enter the fit directly.
- **A model-independent spectrum is also wanted:** unfold it, and fit the index only with the full covariance, using a generalized χ² `(Φ − Φ_model)ᵀ C⁻¹ (Φ − Φ_model)`. Also fit the response-folded model directly, and compare the two.
- **Check the fit itself:** show that toys give unbiased index pulls with correct coverage. Use a goodness-of-fit metric with a stated trigger for adding an uncertainty or changing the method, since "reasonable agreement" is not validation.
