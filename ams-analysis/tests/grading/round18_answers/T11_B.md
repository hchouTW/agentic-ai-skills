**Conversion of an elemental carbon flux from rigidity R to kinetic energy per nucleon T/A** [General method]

Your message gives no bins, flux values or isotope assumption, so I keep the conversion symbolic and use a few illustrative rigidities. The charge is Z = 6. The mass number is not fixed by "elemental carbon", so I assume A = 12.

**1. Variable conversion** (for an isotope with |Z|, A and rest energy mc²)
- pc = |Z|·R. R is in GV and pc in GeV. The charge sign of R plays no role here.
- E = sqrt((pc)² + (mc²)²)
- T = E − mc²
- T/A = T / A

For 12C, Z = 6, A = 12, mc² ≈ 11.178 GeV. This is the 12C nuclear mass, which is not exactly 12 times the nucleon mass. Check it against PDG before any precision use.

**3. The flux needs a Jacobian, not just new bin labels.**
Φ(T/A) = Φ(R) · dR/d(T/A). For a binned flux, convert the bin edges and divide by the new bin widths. Do not relabel the bin centres. For the illustrative bins, the factor dR/d(T/A) is:
- 2–10 GV: 2.151 as the exact bin average, versus 2.167 from the point Jacobian at the geometric centre. This is a 0.7% difference at low rigidity.
- 10–100 GV: 2.0034.
- 100–1000 GV: 2.00003.

The factor tends to A/|Z| = 2 at high R. The point Jacobian and the bin average differ most in the lowest bins, so use the bin-average factor there.

**4. The isotope assumption carries an uncertainty.**
- The conversion needs an assumed A, or an isotope-composition model. Carbon is mostly 12C, but that is an assumption.
- As a sensitivity check, take 13C with A = 13 and mc² = 12.112 GeV. At R = 10 GV this gives T/A = 3.777 GeV/n, against 4.155 GeV/n for 12C. This is a 9% shift at 10 GV, and it is an assumption sensitivity, not an AMS number.

**5. Practical points**
- State which variable the bins and the flux are in: R in GV, or T/A in GeV/n.
- The flux conversion Φ(R) to Φ(T/A) is deterministic once Z, A and the mass are fixed. It does not change the underlying measurement and adds no statistical information.
- If the flux is a ratio such as B/C, both species must use the same variable, so each species gets its own A in the conversion.
