**Short answer.** The RICH supplies the velocity, beta. The Tracker supplies the rigidity, R. Mass then follows from m = |Z| R / (gamma beta c), with R in GV and m in GeV/c^2. Above a few GV per nucleon, the beta resolution limits the mass measurement, not the statistics.

**How the RICH helps**
- [Documented, C13, C70] The RICH has two radiators. The central one is NaF (16 tiles, n = 1.33), and aerogel (92 tiles, n = 1.05) surrounds it. The Cherenkov thresholds are beta > 0.75 for NaF and beta > 0.953 for aerogel.
- [General method] A particle above threshold emits a Cherenkov cone with cos(theta_c) = 1/(n beta). Ring reconstruction, using the Tracker-extrapolated track, gives beta. The photoelectron count scales as Z^2 and gives charge.
- [Documented, C21, C70] Quoted velocity resolution is of order 0.1% (delta-beta/beta) near beta of about 1. C21 gives this for |Z| = 1 in the analysis papers, and C70 gives it as "better than 0.1%" for |Z| > 1 in the review. Treat 0.1% as a scale, and quote the Z range with it.
- [Documented, C27, C28] The TOF covers the low-beta range. The isotope papers use TOF, RICH-NaF and RICH-aerogel in separate velocity bins. They unfold the rates with rigidity and inverse-velocity resolution functions. These come from MC and are validated at beta of about 1 with data.
- [Documented, C28] The quoted widths are Gaussian cores. For the TOF, delta(1/beta) is 0.02. For RICH-aerogel, the core width is 7e-4 at beta = 1. Tails are not quoted in the main text.
- The method has been applied to the He isotopes (S12, C09), the deuteron (S13) and the Li isotopes (S14). Dedicated mass measurement works only in the range where a velocity detector has usable resolution.

**What limits it at high rigidity** [General method, from the mass-propagation formula in detector-and-observables]
- The mass error is (dm/m)^2 = (dR/R)^2 + (gamma^2 dbeta/beta)^2 - 2 rho (dR/R)(gamma^2 dbeta/beta) + (dZ/Z)^2. The correlation rho is usually assumed zero and must be checked.
- At fixed rigidity per nucleon, beta tends to 1 and gamma grows. The gamma^2 factor amplifies the beta error, so the velocity term dominates and the isotope mass peaks overlap.
- The upper limit in rigidity or T/A therefore comes from the beta resolution, not from event counts. The limit is harder for isotope pairs with a small relative mass difference. Rest-mass ratios are about 2 for D/p, 4/3 for He-3/He-4 and 7/6 for Li-6/Li-7.
- In the isotope papers, the analysed samples stop near 21 GV for D and He-4, 15 GV for He-3, and 25 GV for Li (S12-S14, C09). The ranges are quoted in rigidity, not T/A, and the papers do not state the reason for each endpoint. They are not a derived resolution limit.
- Aerogel is the only RICH radiator that works at high beta. Above beta of about 0.953 the NaF produces no ring, so NaF drops out as rigidity rises.
- Smaller effects:
  - rigidity resolution (dR/R grows with R in the Tracker spatial-resolution regime);
  - ring-quality, radiator-boundary and secondary-particle rings;
  - tracker-RICH extrapolation error;
  - possible rigidity-beta error correlation;
  - non-Gaussian beta tails.
- The tails matter for isotope templates and must be validated on a known-mass sample (for example abundant p or He-4). A Gaussian-core width alone is not enough.
- Fragmentation backgrounds are a separate limit for D and He-3 (C27).
