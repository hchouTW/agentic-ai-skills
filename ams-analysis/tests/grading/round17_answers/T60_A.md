**Short answer.** The per-interval numerical values of the spectral index are not available to me, so I will not give them. The AMS paper shows them only in figures and in Supplemental Material tables, and neither has been read in this skill's evidence base. I can give the interval boundaries, the qualitative behaviour, and the few numbers the main text does quote.

**What was measured** [Documented, C131, S54, PRL 120, 021101 (2018), main article only]
- Li, Be and B fluxes over 1.9 GV to 3.3 TV, from 5.4 x 10^6 nuclei: 1.9 x 10^6 Li, 0.9 x 10^6 Be and 2.6 x 10^6 B.
- Data period: May 19, 2011 to May 26, 2016.

**Spectral-index definition and intervals** [Documented, C136]
- gamma = d[log(Phi)]/d[log(R)], computed model-independently over rigidity intervals bounded by 7.09, 12.0, 16.6, 22.8, 41.9, 60.3, 192 and 3300 GV.
- The index is therefore evaluated in these seven intervals: 7.09-12.0, 12.0-16.6, 16.6-22.8, 22.8-41.9, 41.9-60.3, 60.3-192 and 192-3300 GV.
- Individual gamma values for Li, Be and B in each interval are shown only in Figs. 2 and 3 and in the Supplemental Material tables. I have not read or interpreted those, so I do not quote them. The tables are in the paper and the Supplemental Material; I did not read them.
- The Phys. Rept. review (S01) uses the same interval boundaries (C84). Its tables were not transcribed either.

**Qualitative behaviour of the indices** [Documented, C136]
- The magnitude and rigidity dependence of the Li, Be and B indices are nearly identical to each other.
- They are distinctly different from the He, C and O indices. Those He, C and O fluxes and indices come from an earlier AMS paper (Ref. 14, i.e. S11), not from this Letter.
- Above about 200 GV, Li, Be and B harden more than He, C and O.

**Flux-shape statements** [Documented, C135]
- The Li and B fluxes have an identical rigidity dependence above about 7 GV, and all three fluxes (Li, Be, B) above about 30 GV.
- Fits to a constant give Li/B = 0.72 +- 0.02 above about 7 GV and Be/B = 0.36 +- 0.01 above about 30 GV.
- Li/Be = 2.0 +- 0.1 above 30 GV.
- All three fluxes deviate from a single power law above 200 GV in an identical way. AMS also observed this in the primary He, C and O fluxes.
- The paper's own wording is that the different Be rigidity dependence is "most likely" due to the radioactive 10Be isotope (half-life 1.4 My). This is the paper's statement, not a measurement.

**Secondary-to-primary ratios** [Documented, C136]
- Ratios formed: Li/C, Be/C, B/C, Li/O, Be/O and B/O.
- Delta = d[log(Phi_S/Phi_P)]/d[log(R)] was determined in two intervals, 60.3-192 GV and 192-3300 GV.
- Quoted result: an average hardening of 0.13 +- 0.03 above about 200 GV.
- The Phys. Rept. review quotes 0.140 +- 0.025 for Delta[192-3300] - Delta[60.3-192], with a stated significance above 5 sigma [Documented, C84]. This is the review's figure on its own data set.
  - The two numbers are consistent within errors, but they come from different sources.
  - Do not merge them. I do not know how the 5 sigma was computed [Unknown/needs input].
- The review also quotes a high-rigidity ratio index of -0.266 +- 0.022 above 192 GV for these ratios [Documented, C82, review]. This is a different quantity from the 0.13 +- 0.03 hardening. I give no per-interval ratio indices.

**What this says about propagation** [Documented, C136, C84; the interpretation is the authors' reading, not a measurement of mechanism]
- The paper states the secondary-to-primary hardening is consistent with expectations if the hardening is due to propagation properties in the Galaxy.
- The review's reasoning: source-injection hardening would give similar hardening for secondaries and primaries. Secondaries harden more than primaries here, which favours a propagation origin.
- The review also states that no model predicted the observed behaviour of either the primaries He, C, O or the secondaries Li, Be, B (C84).
- Caveats:
  - A ratio's rigidity dependence is an empirical description. Mapping it to a diffusion-coefficient break or a specific propagation model is a model step I have not done.
  - The Be difference is an isotope-composition issue that complicates a clean secondary reading.
  - Ratios do not cancel all systematics automatically. The Li, Be and B background corrections are listed in C133 (5%, 8%, 5% at 2 GV; 2%, 13%, 8% at 3.3 TV, from simulation).
