**Source.** AMS, PRL 120, 021101 (2018), Li, Be and B fluxes, 1.9 GV to 3.3 TV, first 5 years (May 2011 to May 2016). The sample is 5.4 x 10^6 nuclei: 1.9 x 10^6 Li, 0.9 x 10^6 Be and 2.6 x 10^6 B. The main article was read; the Supplemental Material was not. [Documented, C131]

**Spectral-index definition and intervals.** gamma = d[log(Phi)]/d[log(R)], computed over rigidity intervals bounded by 7.09, 12.0, 16.6, 22.8, 41.9, 60.3, 192 and 3300 GV. [Documented, C136]

That gives seven intervals:
- 7.09-12.0 GV
- 12.0-16.6 GV
- 16.6-22.8 GV
- 22.8-41.9 GV
- 41.9-60.3 GV
- 60.3-192 GV
- 192-3300 GV

**What the spectral indices show** [Documented, C136, C135]
- The magnitude and rigidity dependence of the Li, Be and B indices are nearly identical to each other. They are distinctly different from the primary He, C and O indices. Those primary results come from an earlier AMS paper (S11/Ref. 14), not from this paper.
- Above about 200 GV, Li, Be and B harden more than He, C and O.
- The three fluxes deviate from a single power law above 200 GV in an identical way. This behavior is also seen in the AMS He, C and O fluxes.
- Li and B have an identical rigidity dependence above about 7 GV. All three (Li, Be, B) are identical above about 30 GV. [Documented, C135]
- The fitted ratios are [Documented, C135]:
  - Li/B = 0.72 +- 0.02 above 7 GV (chi2/ndf = 51/53).
  - Be/B = 0.36 +- 0.01 above 30 GV (chi2/ndf = 27/35).
  - Li/Be = 2.0 +- 0.1 above 30 GV.
- The different Be rigidity dependence at lower rigidity is attributed by the paper to the radioactive 10Be (half-life 1.4 My), described as "most likely". This is the paper's interpretation, not a measurement.

**Secondary-to-primary ratios.** Delta = d[log(Phi_S/Phi_P)]/d[log(R)] is evaluated over only two intervals, 60.3-192 GV and 192-3300 GV. It is computed for Li/C, Be/C, B/C, Li/O, Be/O and B/O. [Documented, C136]
- The only quoted number is an average hardening of 0.13 +- 0.03 above about 200 GV. The individual Delta values are in the figures and Supplemental Material and are not available here. [Documented, C136]
- The ratios are not constant, which a pure power-law secondary-to-primary relation would give.

**What this says about propagation.**
- The paper states that the 0.13 +- 0.03 hardening is consistent with expectations if the hardening is due to propagation properties in the Galaxy. [Documented, C136] This is the paper's interpretation, not a direct measurement of mechanism.
- Primary and secondary species both harden above about 200 GV. The secondaries harden more, so the ratio rises with rigidity. A rigidity-dependent propagation effect (diffusion or escape) therefore accounts for part of the secondary hardening, on top of any source-side change common to the primaries. [General method / interpretation, model-dependent; not quoted from the paper]
- Quantitative propagation parameters (diffusion index, halo size, reacceleration) need a transport model fit. They are not given by the data alone, and I am not quoting any here.

**Caveats.**
- The 0.13 +- 0.03 is an average over the two high-rigidity intervals, not a per-interval value.
- Ratio systematics (acceptance, efficiency, cross-section) do not cancel automatically; the paper's Li, Be and B systematic sizes are listed in C134.
- Hardening of the light ratios is also discussed in later AMS nuclei papers (for example F, C160). Those numbers belong to those papers, not this one.
