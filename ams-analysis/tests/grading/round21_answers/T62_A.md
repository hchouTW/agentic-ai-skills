**Short answer:** Layer-0 is not installed yet, and no public source gives a quantified gain for antiproton or helium measurements. Both parts of the question need a caveat.

**1. When (status and schedule)**

- [Documented, C102; Tier 6 CERN news item of 21 August 2026, page level] The planned schedule is:
  - Final acceptance test at INFN-University of Perugia until 30 August 2026.
  - A last series of measurements at CERN in October 2026.
  - Flight from Geneva to Kennedy Space Center in January 2027.
  - A dedicated SpaceX Cargo Dragon launch to the ISS planned for April 2027.
  - Preparatory spacewalks to install the receiving interface beforehand.
  - Mechanical and power connections expected to be complete during further spacewalks by May 2027.
- As of the ledger check on 2026-10-01, Layer-0 is not installed. These are planned dates, not events, and they may have slipped.
- The CERN item is institutional news, not an AMS Collaboration document. The AMS page (S48, ams02.space/tracker-layer-0-upgrade) gives no schedule.
- I could not browse here, so I cannot claim the schedule is current. Please confirm on the AMS site or with NASA.

**2. How much improvement for antiprotons and helium**

- [Documented, C101] The only public figures are promotional wording.
  - The AMS website says the upgrade increases AMS acceptance by 300%.
  - The CERN item says the particle acceptance rate rises by 300%.
  - Both say five years of data taking would equal fifteen years of additional data.
  - These come from page-level reading (S48, S49). They give no rigidity range, no selection, no species and no definition of acceptance.
  - Do not use them as an analysis acceptance or as a projected error reduction.
- I therefore cannot give a number for your antiproton or helium measurements. The sources give none, and I will not invent one. [Unknown/needs input]
- A single acceptance factor would not translate into a gain for either species. The gain depends on what the new layer does to each of these:
  - Geometric acceptance and the rigidity range reached for your selection. The index does not say how Layer-0 enters the track selection. The published AMS helium papers use L1 and L9 tracks (C59, C60, C62), so any selection that depends on those layers needs rework.
  - Rigidity resolution and the maximum detectable rigidity (MDR), if the new layer enters the fit. The nine-layer review values are only a reference for the old configuration (C63, C64). I do not know whether Layer-0 improves them.
  - For antiprotons, the dominant limits are background and charge confusion, both tail effects. Antiprotons are about 1e4 times rarer than protons (C74), and the published antiproton systematics are dominated by background and selection (C121, C23). More statistics reduces only the statistical part. The charge-confusion and template-fit systematics must be re-derived with the new hardware, and a Layer-0 hit changes the tail. Any claim about this needs tail validation rather than the Gaussian core of the resolution.
  - For helium, the statistical errors are already small at moderate rigidity. Gains would be mostly at the highest rigidities, where statistics and the rigidity scale and resolution matter. The size of any gain is unknown.
  - Livetime, trigger and exposure are separate from acceptance. A larger acceptance does not automatically scale the other quantities. If Layer-0 changes the geometry, the efficiency, acceptance, response matrix and systematics must be redone for the new era.

**3. What to do with this in an analysis plan**

- [Proposal] Treat pre- and post-Layer-0 data as separate detector eras, or justify combining them (C102). The era boundary must come from AMS sources. Pre-installation data would stay on the current configuration.
- The earliest sensible planning point is after installation and commissioning, which is after May 2027 if the schedule holds. Data from commissioning periods should not enter a flux until the collaboration defines good-run rules.
- Any expected improvement for your analysis would have to come from a MC study with the new geometry. That MC and the Layer-0 performance numbers are not public in the sources I can use, so use symbolic quantities (N, A, ε, T) until they are.
