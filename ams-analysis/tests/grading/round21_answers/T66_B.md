**Short answer.** AMS identified the 27-day period with a wavelet time-frequency analysis of the daily proton flux, and the significance quoted is a per-period 95% confidence level against red noise. The ledger does not show any trial-factor (look-elsewhere) correction for searching many periods. That is not proof that none was applied. Treat it as unknown.

**How the 27-day period was identified (daily proton flux, 1-100 GV, May 2011 to October 2019)**

- [Documented, C36, abstract level] AMS reported recurrent 27-day variations in the daily proton flux in 2014-2018, plus shorter 13.5-day and 9-day cycles in 2016. The strength of each varies with time and rigidity.
- [Documented, C45] The periodicity search used a wavelet time-frequency technique. It was meant to locate the time intervals in which periodic structures emerge. It reports normalized power and 95% confidence levels. A period near 27 days is above the 95% level from 2014 to 2018. Periods near 13.5 and 9 days are above it only in 2016.
- [Documented, C52, Supplemental Material text]
  - The method is a continuous wavelet transform with a Morlet wavelet.
  - Normalized power is the wavelet power divided by the variance of the series in the analyzed interval.
  - Significance comes from Monte Carlo simulations of a lag-1 autoregressive (red-noise) background, y_n = alpha*y_(n-1) + z_n.
  - The simulated background has the variance and lag-1 autocorrelation of the measured series.
  - The 95% level for each period is the power exceeded by 5% of the simulated background power values.
- [Documented, C43] The input series is a daily flux, Phi = N/(A eps T DeltaR). Migration is unfolded independently for each day.
- [Documented, C44] The systematic errors split into time-independent and time-dependent parts. The time-dependent part, the daily trigger and reconstruction efficiencies, is added in quadrature.

**Is it corrected for searching many periods?**

- [Documented, C45, C52] The significance is stated per period against the red-noise background, as a 95% confidence level. No trial-factor or look-elsewhere correction is described in the main article or in the extracted Supplemental Material text. The method also scans many periods and many time intervals, so a local 95% level does not by itself equal a global significance.
- [Unknown/needs input] Whether AMS applied any global correction is not stated in the text read. I cannot say from the ledger that none was applied. The numerical supplemental tables were not transcribed. A reader needing a definite answer should check the paper (PRL 127, 271102) directly.
- A 95% local level is a weak threshold for a scan over many periods and many time windows. About 1 in 20 periods would exceed it by chance in a red-noise series. This reading is my own and is not an AMS statement.
- The 27-day signal appears across several years and in several species. That recurrence strengthens the physical interpretation. It does not substitute for a stated global significance. The 27-day period is also the solar-rotation (Bartels) period, so it has a strong prior.

**What a trial-corrected version would need** [General method]

- Fix the period band, the test statistic, the observable and the threshold before the analysis.
- Report local and global significance using trial factors (Gross and Vitells, S37), calibrated with toys.
- The toys should reproduce the sampling, the exposure pattern and the correlated systematics, and use a red-noise null with matched variance and autocorrelation.
- Do not use Wilks or a single-frequency p-value.
- Apply the same search to an exposure series, an efficiency series and a control species. A coincident signal there points to a systematic, not a discovery.
- The ledger also records no covariance model for the systematic errors across days. That matters for the null.

**Scope notes**

- These statements cover the daily proton flux (S43). Do not extend C45 and C52 to the helium, electron or positron papers as if they were the same analysis. The helium analysis (C55) describes the same wavelet construction. The ledger does not describe a trial factor there either.
- The hysteresis significances (C53, C55) are a separate procedure. They select the interval pair with the largest difference, and no trial correction for that selection is described in the text read.
- The number is [Documented, C36] at abstract level. Re-check against the paper before quoting.
