**Short answer.** AMS judged the 27-day periodicity in the daily proton flux with a wavelet time-frequency analysis, and called it significant when the wavelet power exceeded a 95% confidence level set by red-noise Monte Carlo. As far as the sources I could check show, that significance is a per-period confidence level. No correction for searching many periods (look-elsewhere or trial factor) is described. That is not proof that none was applied.

**How the decision was made** (daily proton flux, 1-100 GV, 20 May 2011 to 29 Oct 2019, PRL 127, 271102)

- [Documented, C43] Fluxes were built per day and per rigidity bin as Phi = N / (A eps T DeltaR). Unfolding for bin migration was done independently for each day. Acceptance, trigger efficiency and collection time were evaluated per day.
- [Documented, C45] A wavelet time-frequency technique located the time intervals in which periodic structures emerge. It reports normalized power with 95% confidence levels. A period near 27 days is above the 95% level from 2014 to 2018. Periods near 13.5 and 9 days are above it only in 2016. The strength of all three changes with time and rigidity (abstract-level statement, C36).
- [Documented, C52, Supplemental Material text] The method is a continuous wavelet transform with a Morlet wavelet. Normalized power is the wavelet power divided by the variance of the series in the analyzed interval. The significance comes from Monte Carlo simulations of a lag-1 autoregressive (red-noise) background, matched to the measured variance and lag-1 autocorrelation. The 95% level for each period is the power exceeded by 5% of the simulated background power values.

So the "decision" is that the measured power at about 27 days exceeds the red-noise surrogate threshold during 2014-2018. It is a local, per-period, red-noise-referenced significance.

**Is it corrected for searching many periods?**

- [Documented, C45, C52, C105] No trial-factor or look-elsewhere correction is described in the main article or in the extracted Supplemental Material text. The supplemental daily-flux tables (C105) tabulate no global significance. The significance is stated per period against red noise.
- This is not proof that none was applied. The ledger covers the main article and the Supplemental Material text. It does not cover every internal check.
- A 95% level per period means that, with many periods and many time-frequency cells tested, some false exceedances are expected by chance. The recurrent 27-day signal is plausible on physical grounds (it matches the solar rotation period, and it persists over several years). Even so, a global significance would need a trial factor.
- [General method] A proper treatment would define the period band, the test statistic and the observable before the analysis. It would report local and global significance (Gross and Vitells trial factors, S37). It would calibrate with toys that reproduce the sampling, the exposure pattern and the correlated systematics. It would also apply the same search to exposure, efficiency and control series, to rule out exposure, orbit or season cycles.
- [Unknown/needs input] How the systematic errors are correlated across days is also not described. The text read gives no covariance model across days (C44, C105). The red-noise surrogates match only the variance and lag-1 autocorrelation of the measured series.

**Caveats on scope.** These statements concern the daily proton flux (S43) only. The daily helium analysis follows the same wavelet construction (C55). The electron paper reports 27, 13.5 and 9-day periods (C40, abstract level). Do not read the proton wavelet significance as applying to those species or to the Bartels-rotation products. The hysteresis significances (C53, C55) likewise carry no described trial-factor correction for the selection of the interval pair.

If you need a global significance, you would have to compute it yourself from the published daily tables (statistical, time-dependent systematic and total systematic errors per day and bin, C105). That is a user-side analysis and not an AMS result.
