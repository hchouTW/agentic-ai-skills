**Expected improvement**
- [Documented, C101; page level, promotional wording] The AMS website says the upgrade adds a new silicon tracker layer on top of the detector. It says this increases AMS acceptance by 300% and that five years of data taking will equal fifteen years of additional data. The CERN item says "particle acceptance rate by 300%".

**What to expect, qualitatively [Proposal / General method]**
- An acceptance gain would mainly help statistics-limited bins. For antiprotons these are the high-rigidity bins, where counts are low. For helium the relevant bins are those at the highest rigidities.
- Many antiproton and helium uncertainties are not statistical. They include template and charge-confusion tails, unfolding and response, MC/data efficiency corrections, and the acceptance model. These do not scale with exposure and would need their own re-validation with the new layer. A larger statistics sample does not shrink them.
- Whether charge-confusion tails, which matter most for antiprotons, improve is something to measure. Tail behaviour would need validation on data (invariant 4), and I won't claim a gain.
- Once Layer-0 is installed, treat pre- and post-installation data as separate detector eras. If you combine them, justify it. The era boundary must come from AMS sources (C102 limitation; the reconstruction-and-data-quality reference's hardware-eras section).
- Data taken after installation also needs commissioning and calibration time before it can be used in a publishable measurement. The index gives no timeline for that.
