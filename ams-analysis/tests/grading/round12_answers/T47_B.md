# AMS antiproton flux over a solar cycle

Source: AMS Collaboration, "Antiprotons and Elementary Particles over a Solar Cycle", PRL 134, 051002 (2025) [Documented, C07]. My evidence is the main article only. I did not read the Supplemental Material, and I had no web access, so I did not check for errata or newer papers (this skill's index was last verified 2026-09-20).

## What AMS published

**Data and method** [Documented, C07, C120]
- The antiproton flux is measured in each of 139 Bartels rotations (27 days each), from May 2011 to June 2022. That covers most of solar cycle 24, including the 2013 polarity reversal, and the start of cycle 25 [Documented, C122].
- The measurement covers 11 absolute-rigidity bins from 1.00 to 41.9 GV. After selection and background subtraction there are 1.1x10^6 antiprotons.
- There are about 10^4 protons per antiproton. Selection combines the TRD, TOF, RICH, tracker and ECAL.
- Per-rotation flux = N / (A (1+delta) eps T DeltaR). N is background-subtracted and unfolded for bin migration. A is the Monte Carlo acceptance. delta is a data-minus-MC correction (8% at 1 GV, falling to 3% at 10 GV). eps is the trigger efficiency and T is the collection time.
- Several independent analyses of the same data were consistent.

**Systematics** [Documented, C121]
- The total is about 9% at 1 GV and about 4% above 5 GV for all rotations.
- The acceptance error is time-independent: 4% at 1 GV, falling to 2% above 20 GV.
- The time-dependent part comes mainly from the rotation-to-rotation change in antiproton spectral shape in the unfolding: about 4% at 1 GV, below 0.3% above 3 GV.
- Time-independent and time-dependent terms are added in quadrature.
- A bin-to-bin or rotation-to-rotation covariance matrix is not given in the main text.

**Results** [Documented, C122, C123, C124]
- The antiproton flux varies significantly in time below about 10 GV. The relative variation falls with rigidity and is not visible above about 10 GV (shown with 13-rotation moving averages).
- Time-averaged spectrum: the antiproton flux rises to a peak between 2 and 4 GV, with a turnover near 3 GV. The proton, electron and positron fluxes fall with rigidity.
- Antiproton, proton, electron and positron fluxes all show a minimum around 2014-2015 and a maximum around 2020.
- Below about 4 GV, the antiproton variation is significantly smaller than that of protons, electrons and positrons.
- Antiproton-proton hysteresis: there are two branches, before and after 2014-2015. It is seen in every rigidity bin below 11.0 GV and resembles the electron-positron hysteresis. The paper reads this as a clear charge-sign effect.
- Antiproton-electron relation: the fluxes are linearly related in all bins from 1.00 to 7.09 GV, and the antiproton flux is modulated less than the electron flux.
- Spectral index versus variation size: the relative variation M (ratio of maximum to minimum of the 13-rotation moving average) correlates with the spectral index. The flux with the larger index varies less. The relation is the same within errors for (e-, antiproton) and (e+, proton). The collaboration calls this a universal relation independent of charge sign. It takes it to mean the modulation differences are mainly due to spectral shape, and as input for the antiproton local interstellar spectrum.
- "Universal relation" and "charge-sign effect" are the collaboration's interpretation, not a transport-model result. They are not a demonstrated heliospheric model.

## What you can get without the supplement

- The method, the sample size, the rigidity range and the time span (above).
- The qualitative time behaviour: where the variation is significant, the 2014-2015 minimum, the 2020 maximum, and the antiproton versus proton/electron/positron comparison.
- The systematics budget as percentages at the quoted rigidities, and which parts are time-dependent.
- The correlation and hysteresis conclusions, and the spectral-index versus variation-size conclusion, at the level the text and figures state them.
- You can read approximate trends from the figures. I did not interpret the figures, so any such reading is yours and not a documented value.

## What you cannot get without the supplement

- **The flux values.** Each rotation's flux is tabulated in Tables S1-S139 and the 11-year average in Table S140, both in the Supplemental Material. Without them you cannot rebuild the time series, fit it, or compute your own ratios or correlations.
- **The 4-sigma comparison.** The statement that the antiproton-proton hysteresis differs from the electron-positron one by more than 4 sigma, below 4.88 GV, comes from a supplement table. You cannot reproduce or check it.
- **Per-bin index and M values.** The spectral-index figure and the per-bin values are in the supplement and figures, and the errors on the ratios appear only in a figure. I did not read these values.
- **Details of the selection, cutoff and background templates.** These are in the supplement and earlier papers; the unfolding method is only cited. I cannot supply selection cuts, cutoff values or template details.
- **Covariance.** The main text gives no covariance between rigidity bins or rotations, and the quoted systematics are percentages only. Any joint fit would need you to build correlations yourself [Proposal], or to find them in the supplement.
- **Other species' inputs.** The proton, electron and positron fluxes used for comparison are rebinned from earlier AMS papers. You need those papers, not this one.

## Cautions

- All values are in absolute rigidity, not energy. Do not convert them to kinetic energy per nucleon without stating the species and A [General method].
- Do not extend these claims to the daily-flux papers or to other rigidity ranges.
- I give no numbers beyond those in this skill's ledger. For anything quantitative, get the supplement tables.
