**Cite PRL 113, 121101 (2014), "High Statistics Measurement of the Positron Fraction in Primary Cosmic Rays of 0.5-500 GeV" (AMS Collaboration).** [Documented, C113; main article read, tables and Supplemental Material not transcribed]

**Sample (2014 paper)** [Documented, C113]
- Data from the first 30 months, 2011-05-19 to 2013-11-26. That is a factor 1.7 more statistics than the 2013 paper.
- The bin-by-bin fits give 10.9x10^6 primary positrons and electrons, of which 0.64x10^6 are positrons. They also give 3.50x10^6 protons in the fitted sample.
- Over 41x10^9 events were analysed in total [Documented, C114].

**Behaviour above ~200 GeV** [Documented, C113]
- The paper states that the positron fraction no longer increases with energy above about 200 GeV.
- The log-slope fit gives E0 = 275 +- 32 GeV (chi2/d.o.f. = 3.9/12).
- Quote it as "no longer increases" (the slope flattens). Do not write "decreases" or "turns over". The paper does not say that.
- The paper also states that the minimal model without an exponential source cutoff is excluded at 99.9% C.L. [Documented, C113]. That is a model statement and belongs to the collaboration's interpretation.

**Comparison with the 2013 first result, PRL 110, 141102** [Documented, C03, C110]
- Range was 0.5-350 GeV, from 6.8x10^6 positron and electron events (plus about 7x10^5 protons). Data ran from 2011-05-19 to 2012-12-10, about 8% of the expected AMS data sample.
- Below 10 GeV the fraction decreases with energy, as expected from secondary production on the interstellar medium.
- From 10 to 250 GeV it rises steadily. The paper says this is not consistent with only secondary production.
- From 20 to 250 GeV the slope falls by an order of magnitude, and no fine structure is seen.
- The paper said the behaviour above 250 GeV needed more statistics. The 2014 paper supplies that and finds the fraction flattens above about 200 GeV.
- The 2014 paper states its result is consistent with the 2013 one. The later paper extends the range and statistics and supersedes the first for citation as the current measurement.
- Method and selection follow the 2013 paper (C114). The dipole anisotropy limit tightens from delta <= 0.036 (16-350 GeV) to delta <= 0.030 above 16 GeV, both at 95% C.L. [Documented, C111, C113]

**How to phrase it as a secondary-production test**
- The AMS papers state that the 10-250 GeV rise is not consistent with only secondary production. That sentence is from the 2013 paper (C110). The 2014 paper confirms the rise and adds the flattening above about 200 GeV (C113).
- Cite the claim as the collaboration's reading of the fraction. The measurement itself is the fraction versus energy. It does not by itself identify the origin (pulsar, dark matter or other) [Documented, C113]. It also depends on the secondary-production and propagation model you compare with.
- If you compare with a model, treat the positron fraction and the separate fluxes as correlated, not independent. The separate fluxes are PRL 113, 121102 [General method].
- These energies are in total lepton energy (ECAL), not rigidity.
