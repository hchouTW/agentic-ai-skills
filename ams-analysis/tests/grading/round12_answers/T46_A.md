**Paper to cite for 0.5-500 GeV:** the AMS high-statistics positron fraction, PRL 113, 121101 (2014), one of the three companion PRLs of 2014 (the other two are the separate e+/e- fluxes, PRL 113, 121102, and the combined (e+ + e-) flux, PRL 113, 221102). Cite 121101 for the fraction, not the flux papers. [Documented, C113; main article read, tables/Supplemental not transcribed]

**Sample.** All data from the first 30 months, 2011-05-19 to 2013-11-26, which is a factor 1.7 more statistics than the 2013 paper. The bin fits give 10.9x10^6 primary positrons and electrons (of which 0.64x10^6 are positrons) plus 3.50x10^6 protons in the fit. The analysis starts from over 41x10^9 events. Energies are ECAL energies, not rigidities. [Documented, C113, C114]

**Behaviour above ~200 GeV.** The paper states that the positron fraction no longer increases with energy above about 200 GeV. Supporting numbers from the paper: a fit of slope = c log(E/E0) above 30 GeV gives E0 = 275 +- 32 GeV (chi2/d.o.f. = 3.9/12 with correlations). Its minimal model (diffuse power laws plus a common source with exponential cutoff) fitted over 1-500 GeV gives chi2/d.o.f. = 36.4/58 and 1/Es = 1.84 +- 0.58 TeV^-1, with the no-cutoff version excluded at 99.9% C.L. [Documented, C113] The wording is "no longer increases", not "falls" or "flat": do not paraphrase it more strongly.

**Comparison with the 2013 first result (PRL 110, 141102).**
- Range and sample: 0.5-350 GeV, 6.8x10^6 positron and electron events (plus about 7x10^5 protons), data 2011-05-19 to 2012-12-10 (about 8% of the expected AMS sample). So the 2014 paper extends the upper edge from 350 to 500 GeV and has 1.7x the statistics (10.9x10^6 vs 6.8x10^6 e+/e-). [Documented, C03]
- Method: the same basic analysis (TRD and tracker track, ECAL cluster, beta about 1 downward Z = 1, energy-dependent ECAL-estimator cut, ECAL energy above 1.2x the maximum Stoermer cutoff, fit in the [TRD estimator - log(E/p)] plane per ECAL energy bin for the positive and negative samples). Systematics are treated the same way, with the selection systematic from 1000+ repeated analyses and charge confusion from tracker resolution and secondary tracks. [Documented, C107, C108, C114]
- Consistency: the 2014 paper states that the earlier result is consistent with it. The 2014 paper supersedes the 2013 one in range and statistics, so cite the 2013 paper only as the first result, not as the current measurement. [Documented, C113, C03]
- Model fits are not directly comparable. The 2013 minimal model (fit 1-350 GeV) gives a cutoff of 760 (+1000, -280) GeV, while the 2014 fit (1-500 GeV) is quoted as 1/Es = 1.84 +- 0.58 TeV^-1. The parameter forms differ and both are model-dependent, so do not quote them side by side as a changing measurement. [Documented, C109, C113]
- The statement about the behaviour above ~200 GeV belongs to the 2014 paper only. The 2013 data stop at 350 GeV, and I have no indexed claim giving the 2013 paper's own wording about the high-energy trend, so I do not attribute that statement to it. [Unknown / not in the claim ledger]

**Caveat for your use as a "pure-secondary-production test".** [General method / Proposal]
- The AMS paper reports the measurement and a model-dependent minimal-model description. It lists pulsar and dark-matter classes of interpretation as the collaboration's qualitative reading. Whether the rise above the secondary expectation excludes pure secondary production is your model step, not an AMS-stated result in the claims I checked.
- The fraction depends on the electron spectrum as well as on the positron spectrum, so a secondary-production model must describe both leptons. State the propagation and secondary-production model and treat it as a nuisance, and do not extrapolate beyond 0.5-500 GeV. The fraction and the separate fluxes (121102) come from the same events and share systematics, so do not fit them as independent data.
