# AMS antiproton flux over the solar cycle: what the paper gives and what needs the supplement

The paper is "Antiprotons and Elementary Particles over a Solar Cycle", Phys. Rev. Lett. 134, 051002 (2025) (source S09). My index reads only the main article, not the Supplemental Material. I had no web access, so I could not check for newer papers or errata. The skill's index was last verified 2026-09-20.

## What AMS published

**Data set** [Documented, C07, C120]
- The flux is measured per Bartels rotation (27 days). There are 139 rotations, from May 2011 to June 2022.
- There are 11 absolute-rigidity bins from 1.00 to 41.9 GV.
- After selection and background subtraction the sample is 1.1x10^6 antiprotons. The data set has over 2.0x10^11 cosmic-ray events, with about 10^4 protons per antiproton.
- The selection combines TRD, TOF, RICH, Tracker and ECAL.
- Several independent analyses by different groups were consistent.
- This sample, period and binning differ from the 2016 antiproton paper (S08: 3.49x10^5 antiprotons, 1-450 GV). Do not mix numbers between the two papers.

**Method** [Documented, C120]
- Flux per bin and rotation is Phi = N / (A (1+delta) eps T DeltaR).
- N is corrected for background and bin-to-bin migration by unfolding. The unfolding is cited from an earlier AMS paper, not described in detail.
- A is the Monte Carlo effective acceptance, including selection efficiencies and antiproton interactions in the detector material.
- delta is a small data-minus-MC correction, 8% at 1 GV falling to 3% at 10 GV.
- eps is the trigger efficiency and T is the collection time.

**Systematics** [Documented, C121]
- The total is about 9% at 1 GV and about 4% above 5 GV, for all rotations.
- Components quoted in the paper:
  - Event selection: 3.5% at 1 GV, 0.5% at 10 GV.
  - Background template statistics: 1.5% at 1 GV, below 1% above 3 GV.
  - Trigger: below 1%.
  - Geomagnetic cutoff: about 2% at 1 GV, below 0.4% above 2 GV.
  - Acceptance (mainly interaction cross sections): time-independent, 4% at 1 GV down to 2% above 20 GV.
  - Unfolding: a time-independent part from the resolution function, 2.5% at 1 GV, and a time-dependent part from spectral-shape variation, about 4% at 1 GV.
  - Rigidity scale: below 0.5%.
- Time-independent and time-dependent terms add in quadrature.

**Rigidity and time dependence** [Documented, C122]
- The time-averaged antiproton flux rises from 1 to 2 GV, peaks between 2 and 4 GV, turns over at about 3 GV and falls above 4 GV. The proton, electron and positron time-averaged fluxes all decrease with rigidity.
- The antiproton flux varies significantly in time below about 10 GV. The relative variation shrinks with rigidity and is not visible above about 10 GV. The paper uses 13-rotation moving averages.
- Antiproton, proton, electron and positron fluxes all have a minimum around 2014-2015 and a maximum around 2020.
- Below about 4 GV the antiproton variation is significantly smaller than that of the other three.
- The data cover most of solar cycle 24 (including the 2013 polarity reversal) and the start of cycle 25.

**Correlations** [Documented, C123]
- Antiproton and proton fluxes show hysteresis. This is the same qualitative behaviour as electron vs positron. It appears in every rigidity bin below 11.0 GV.
- Antiproton and electron fluxes are linearly related from 1.00 to 7.09 GV, with the antiproton modulated significantly less than the electron.
- The paper reads this as a clear charge-sign effect in the hysteresis, with spectral shape also important. These are the collaboration's interpretations, not a transport-model result.

**Model-independent analysis** [Documented, C124]
- The relative variation magnitude M is the max/min ratio of the 13-rotation moving average. The spectral index is gamma = dlogPhi/dlogR.
- M(antiproton) < M(e-) and M(p) < M(e+). The flux with the larger index varies less.
- Delta M / Delta gamma is the same within errors for the (e-, antiproton) pair and the (e+, p) pair. AMS calls this a "universal relation" independent of charge sign. It attributes the modulation differences mainly to spectral shape, and offers it as input for the antiproton local interstellar spectrum.
- "Universal" is the collaboration's wording for a finding in this data set. Do not extend it beyond the stated rigidity range (up to 11 GV for the trend).

## What you can get from the paper alone

- The headline results: reduced antiproton variability relative to p, e- and e+, the 2014-2015 minimum and 2020 maximum, the hysteresis and linear relations, and the shape-versus-variation relation.
- The analysis structure: flux formula, correction categories, systematic budget with the quoted percentages, time range, binning and sample size.
- Qualitative statements read from the text, such as the turnover near 3 GV and the rigidity above which variation disappears.

## What you cannot get without the supplement

All of the following are [Unknown/needs input] from my index, because the Supplemental Material was not read.
- **The flux numbers.** Per-rotation fluxes are in Tables S1-S139 and the 11-year average in Table S140. Without them you cannot get per-bin or per-rotation values, absolute magnitudes, or your own refits.
- **The hysteresis details.** The method follows an earlier AMS paper, and the table behind the claim that the antiproton-proton hysteresis differs from electron-positron by more than 4 sigma is in the supplement. I can cite the main-text statement of the 4 sigma difference below 4.88 GV, but I cannot reproduce or check it.
- **Per-bin spectral indices and M values.** The sliding-window (5-7 bins) index figure is in the supplement. Errors on the ratios are shown only in figures.
- **Selection and analysis details.** The selection cuts, geomagnetic-cutoff treatment, background templates and collection-time handling are described in the supplement and earlier papers (S08). I will not invent cuts, template shapes or livetimes.
- **Covariance.** No bin-to-bin or rotation-to-rotation covariance matrix was in the text I read. The 13-rotation averaging is said to handle correlated systematics, so treat any chi-square or correlation fit with care. The systematic percentages apply at the quoted rigidities only. Do not extrapolate them to other bins.
- **Unfolding.** The unfolding procedure is cited, not described, so the response matrix is not reproducible from the paper.

## Caveats

- The proton, electron and positron fluxes in the comparisons come from earlier AMS papers, rebinned (S43, S45, S46). The antiproton claims do not restate their numbers.
- The result is in rigidity, not kinetic energy, and per Bartels rotation. Do not transfer it to the daily-flux papers or to energy variables.
- No newer paper or supplement was checked.
