All four points come from one paper: AMS, "Properties of a New Group of Cosmic Nuclei: Results from the Alpha Magnetic Spectrometer on Sodium, Aluminum, and Nitrogen", PRL 127, 021101 (2021) (source S59). The ledger records that I read the main article only. The Supplemental Material was not read, and the spectral-index values and flux shapes appear only in figures, which I did not read. I did not check for newer papers or supplements (no browsing here; the index was last verified 2026-09-20). Read the figures and Supplemental Material for anything finer than the numbers below.

**1. Data sample and rigidity range** [Documented, C162]
- AMS collected 0.46 million Na and 0.51 million Al nuclei, in the rigidity range 2.15 GV to 3.0 TV.
- The period is the first 8.5 years on the ISS, 19 May 2011 to 30 October 2019. In that time AMS recorded 1.50 x 10^11 cosmic-ray events in total.
- Each flux is measured in 49 rigidity bins. The total flux error at 100 GV is 5.0% for Na and 4.8% for Al.

**2. Background from interactions between L1 and L2** [Documented, C163]
- This is the background from heavier nuclei interacting between tracker layers L1 and L2 (the TRD and upper TOF region).
- It is evaluated by fitting the L1 charge distribution with templates of Ne, Na, Mg, Al and Si, followed by cuts on the L1 charge.
- It varies smoothly with rigidity:
  - Na: 8% below 10 GV, rising to 25% at 3 TV.
  - Al: 9% below 10 GV, rising to 16% at 3 TV.
- This is separate from two other backgrounds the paper treats:
  - Interactions above L1 (carbon fiber and aluminum honeycomb support structures) are estimated from simulation validated with AMS charge-changing cross sections.
  - Charge confusion from non-interacting Ne, Mg and Si is below 0.5%.
- The overall background-subtraction uncertainty for Na is 1.5% at 2 GV, 1.5% at 100 GV and 6% at 3.0 TV. For Al it is 1%, 1.5% and 5% at the same points.
- Do not mix up the 8-25% background size with its uncertainty.

**3. Unfolding corrections for Na** [Documented, C164]
- Bin-to-bin migration is corrected by unfolding. The paper refers to its Ref. [25] for the procedure, and I have not read the details.
- The correction is (N_i - aleph_i)/aleph_i. Here N_i is the number of events corrected for migration, as used in the flux, and aleph_i (the paper's symbol) is the observed number of events in bin i.
- For Na it is +20% at 3 GV, falling smoothly to +6% at 10 GV, -1% at 100 GV, -10% at 300 GV and -20% at 3.0 TV. The paper says the Al corrections are similar.
- The sign changes from positive at low rigidity to negative at high rigidity.
- The total systematic budget per bin is not quoted in the text.

**4. Which flux the Na and Al spectral indices track** [Documented, C166]
- Below about 100 GV, the Na flux and spectral index follow the N flux and spectral index.
- Above about 100 GV, the Al flux and spectral index follow the N flux and spectral index.
- The paper's own statement therefore pairs Na with N at low rigidity and Al with N at high rigidity. The Na and Al indices are not compared directly with each other.
- The spectral index is gamma = d[log Phi]/d[log R], obtained in a model-independent way over non-overlapping rigidity intervals. The interval boundaries are 7.09, 12.0, 16.6, 28.8, 45.1, 80.5, 211.0 and 3000.0 GV.
- The N comparison is the AMS N flux from Phys. Rep. 894 (Ref. [3]), re-analysed over the same collection time. It is not a new N measurement in this Letter.
- Index values are shown only in the figure and I did not read numbers from it. Quantitative statements about the index shape need the figure.
