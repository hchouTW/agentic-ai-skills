**Scope.** Both papers are AMS Tier 1 papers read at main-article level only. Their Supplemental Material was not read. The index was last verified 2026-09-20 and I could not browse, so I make no claim about newer or superseding papers.

## Data sample, rigidity range and period

| | Iron (S57, PRL 126, 041104, 2021) | Fluorine (S58, PRL 126, 081102, 2021) |
|---|---|---|
| Sample | 0.62 x 10^6 Fe nuclei after background subtraction [Documented, C149, C151] | 0.29 x 10^6 F nuclei after background subtraction [Documented, C156] |
| Rigidity range | 2.65 GV to 3.0 TV, 49 bins [Documented, C149] | 2.15 GV to 2.9 TV, 49 bins [Documented, C156] |
| Data-taking period | First 8.5 years, 2011-05-19 to 2019-10-30 [Documented, C149] | Same: 2011-05-19 to 2019-10-30 [Documented, C156] |
| Publication | 2021 (paper date, separate from the data period) | 2021 (paper date, separate from the data period) |

- Both papers draw on the same 1.50 x 10^11 total cosmic-ray events. That number is the whole event sample, not the Fe or F sample.
- Both papers state that no earlier rigidity measurements of the Fe or F flux existed.
- Quoted total flux errors are 4.8% at 100 GV for Fe [C149] and 5.9% at 100 GV for F [C156]. These are single-point values, not a range.
- Fe purity is above 97% over the whole range. The main impurity is Mn from finite charge resolution, below 3% [C151].
- F backgrounds are mainly Ne, Mg and Si interacting above tracker L2 [C157]. The part from the L1-L2 material is 4-15% depending on rigidity. The part above L1 is 14% at 2 GV, 18% at 100 GV and 15% at 2.9 TV, estimated from simulation.

## What the F/Si versus B/O comparison implies (S58, C160, C161)

**Fits.**
- Both ratios were fitted with a broken power law with the break at 175 GV [C160]. F/Si was fitted over 28.8-2900 GV. The paper's B/O fit interval is not stated explicitly in the text next to the B/O values.
- F/Si: Delta1 = -0.34 +- 0.02 below 175 GV and Delta2 = -0.19 +- 0.07 above. The hardening is Delta2 - Delta1 = 0.15 +- 0.07.
- B/O: Delta1 = -0.405 +- 0.005 and Delta2 = -0.26 +- 0.03.
- The paper compares the F/Si hardening with the earlier AMS hardening of the light ratios (Li/C, Be/C, B/C, Li/O, Be/O, B/O) of 0.140 +- 0.025. That value comes from earlier AMS papers, not from the fluorine paper itself.
- The B/O data are the earlier AMS measurement (the paper's Ref. [11]). Only the fit to them is new here.

**Ratio of ratios.**
- The paper fitted [(F/Si)/(B/O)] with a broken power law [C161]: k = 0.39 +- 0.01 and R0 = 9.8 +- 0.9 GV. The slope is deltal = -0.055 +- 0.013 below R0 and delta = 0.052 +- 0.007 above.
- Above 10 GV a single power law R^delta describes it with delta = 0.052 +- 0.007. The paper states this differs from zero by 7 sigma. I have no trial factor or significance details beyond the paper's statement.

**Implication, as the paper concludes it [C161].**
- The F/Si rigidity dependence is distinctly different from that of B/O (or B/C).
- The propagation properties of heavy cosmic rays from F to Si therefore differ from those of light cosmic rays from He to O.
- Secondary cosmic rays also fall into two classes. This is the secondary counterpart of the primary He-C-O versus Ne-Mg-Si split.

## Limits on the interpretation

- [General method, caution] This is a statement about empirical rigidity dependence plus the paper's own interpretation. Per source policy, a fit shows description and consistency, not a proven mechanism. The index records no mechanism (for example, a specific energy-dependent or species-dependent grammage) that the paper establishes.
- The two-class conclusion rests on the differing ratio slopes. The paper also says the F/B ratio is compatible with a constant above 150 +- 60 GV [C159]. It expects F/B proportional to R^(0.012 +- 0.013) above 86.5 GV, using the Si/O ratio from the earlier Ne-Mg-Si paper.
- The ratio-of-ratios and the F/Si-B/O comparison with GALPROP and HELMOD are shown in figures that I did not read. The index records only the fit numbers.
- The F/Si and B/O ratios share only partial systematics (inelastic cross sections and rigidity scale differ by species). The (F/Si)/(B/O) slope should not be assumed to cancel acceptance or efficiency effects.
- For Fe, the paper's source-term result is that Fe/Si at the source is not constant at the 3 sigma level in its slab model [C155]. That is a model result, separate from the F paper's propagation conclusion.
- I give no other AMS numbers, and none beyond these claims. Quote the paper's own errors with the stated rigidity and context.
