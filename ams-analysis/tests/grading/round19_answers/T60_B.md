**What is documented**

1. **Which interval edges were used** [Documented, C136, S54: AMS PRL 120, 021101, main article only]. The spectral index is gamma = d[log Phi]/d[log R]. It was computed model-independently over rigidity intervals bounded by 7.09, 12.0, 16.6, 22.8, 41.9, 60.3, 192 and 3300 GV. The same edges appear in the Phys. Rept. review [Documented, C84, S01, review level]. That gives seven intervals: 7.09-12.0, 12.0-16.6, 16.6-22.8, 22.8-41.9, 41.9-60.3, 60.3-192 and 192-3300 GV.

2. **Why there are no per-interval values.** The ledger says the individual gamma values for Li, Be and B appear only in Figs. 2-3 and in the Supplemental Material tables of S54 [C136]. Those were not read. The review's values are likewise in figures and tables I did not transcribe. I am not reading values off a plot or quoting them from memory. Please take the numbers from the Supplemental Material tables of PRL 120, 021101, or from the Phys. Rept. 894 tables. I can then help interpret them.

3. **Behaviour of the three indices** [Documented, C136, C135, S54].
   - The Li, Be and B indices are nearly identical to each other in magnitude and rigidity dependence. They are distinctly different from He, C and O.
   - Above about 200 GV, Li, Be and B harden more than He, C and O.
   - The He, C, O comparison comes from an earlier AMS paper (S11, the "Ref. 14" cited in S54), not from the Li/Be/B paper.
   - The Li and B fluxes have an identical rigidity dependence above about 7 GV. All three fluxes are identical above about 30 GV. The three fluxes deviate from a single power law above 200 GV in an identical way [C135].
   - Li/B = 0.72 +- 0.02 above 7 GV (chi2/d.o.f. = 51/53), and Be/B = 0.36 +- 0.01 above 30 GV (27/35) [C135]. Li/Be = 2.0 +- 0.1 above 30 GV [C135, C83].
   - The paper says the different Be rigidity dependence at lower rigidity is most likely due to radioactive 10Be (half-life 1.4 My). This is the paper's own wording and not a measurement [C135]. The review makes the same attribution below 30 GV [C83].

4. **Secondary-to-primary ratios** [Documented, C136, S54; C84, S01]. The ratios are Li/C, Be/C, B/C, Li/O, Be/O and B/O. A power law Phi_S/Phi_P proportional to R^Delta was fitted in two non-overlapping intervals, 60.3-192 GV and 192-3300 GV.
   - S54 gives an average hardening of Delta[192-3300] - Delta[60.3-192] = 0.13 +- 0.03 above about 200 GV [C136].
   - The review gives 0.140 +- 0.025, stated to exceed 5 sigma [C84].
   - Do not mix the two values. They come from different data sets: S54 uses the first 5 years, and the review uses a later, larger sample. The ledger does not say how the 5 sigma was computed, including correlations and any interval-choice effect. Treat that detail as [Unknown/needs input].
   - The per-ratio Delta values are in figures and tables that are not in the ledger. The review's quoted -0.266 +- 0.022 above 192 GV is for these ratios [C82 context], but I did not verify it against the review's own table. Check it before reuse.

**What this says about propagation**

- [Documented, C136, C84] Both papers state that the secondary-to-primary hardening above about 200 GV is consistent with expectations if it is due to propagation properties in the Galaxy. The review adds that source-injection hardening would give a similar hardening in secondaries and primaries. It also states that no model predicted the observed behaviour of the primary He, C, O or the secondary Li, Be, B.
- [Proposal / my reading, not a result] The logic is as follows.
  - Secondaries are produced by fragmentation of primaries during propagation. Their flux scales roughly as source spectrum times escape (diffusion) time, so a secondary/primary ratio is mainly sensitive to the rigidity dependence of the grammage traversed, that is, to the diffusion coefficient.
  - A ratio that hardens at about 200 GV therefore points to a change in propagation, for example the diffusion coefficient flattening. Injection hardening would largely cancel in the ratio.
  - Li, Be and B having nearly the same indices, and differing from He, C and O, fits the picture that the secondaries share a common origin in propagation.
  - This is an empirical description plus a model-level interpretation. The data do not prove a specific mechanism. Alternatives such as reacceleration, nested leaky-box models, local sources or a halo-size effect would need a model fit. Those model papers were not read here.
- Caveats on rigidity-dependent behaviour:
  - Below about 30 GV, the Be and Li difference is attributed to 10Be and not to rigidity-dependent diffusion. Radioactive decay and the decay-versus-escape timescale also matter there.
  - The ratios do not cancel acceptance, efficiency or systematics automatically. The review's tables treat cross-section, unfolding and rigidity-scale systematics as correlated between numerator and denominator, and combine the others in quadrature [C85]. The correlation coefficients are not given.

**Useful measurement context** [Documented, C131, S54]. The S54 sample is 5.4 x 10^6 nuclei (1.9 x 10^6 Li, 0.9 x 10^6 Be, 2.6 x 10^6 B) over 1.9 GV-3.3 TV, from May 2011 to May 2016. The total flux error is 3%-4% in the 100-108 GV bin. The review's sample is larger: 3.0 million Li, 1.7 million Be, 4.2 million B [C83].
