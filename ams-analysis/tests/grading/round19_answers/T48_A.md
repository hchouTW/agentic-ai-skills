**1. The AMS proton flux (the thing you are cross-checking against)**
- [Documented, C05, C31; S06, PRL 114, 171103, 2015, main article read in full text] The proton flux covers 1 GV to 1.8 TV, in 72 rigidity bins, on about 300 million events. The data period is 19 May 2011 to 26 Nov 2013 (the first 30 months). Rigidity is the native variable.
- The index records no flux values (per-bin numbers or uncertainties) for it. The numbers are in the paper's tables and supplemental material, which are not transcribed in the index. The paper is the primary source and wins over any database.
- Later AMS proton results exist, for example daily proton fluxes (S43, 2021) and the solar-cycle papers. They have different periods and binning, so say which dataset you are checking.

**2. CRDB (LPSC Grenoble), https://lpsc.in2p3.fr/crdb/ [Documented, S50, C125-C128; Tier 6 secondary]**
- It is a compilation of published data, 10^6 to 10^21 eV. On 2026-10-02 the page stated V4.2 (September 2026), 143 experiments, 614 publications. These counts change with each release.
- The AMS proton values it holds are [Unknown/needs input]. One test query on 2026-10-02 (proton flux, native rigidity axis, exp_dates=AMS, conversions and combinations off) returned two AMS02 sub-experiments: 2011/05/19-2013/11/26 and 2011/05/19-2018/05/26, 72 rows each. The release was not recorded and no value was compared with a paper. No ledger claim rests on it.
- To get the values, use `scripts/crdb_query.py`. The REST parameters are in C128:
  - `fetch --num H --energy-type R --out FILE`
  - add `exp_dates=AMS&combo_level=0&energy_convert_level=0`
  - the script writes a sidecar with the request, retrieval time and export date.
  - Record the CRDB version and the retrieval date yourself. `--crdb-version` is only set if you pass it.
- How to use the returned rows:
  - Do not merge sub-experiments (C126). Map each `SUBEXP_NAME` to its paper by name and data-taking range. The 2011-2013 one should correspond to S06 (C31); the period must match.
  - A systematic of 0 means "not separately quoted" (C126). Take the systematics from the paper.
  - Check `PUBLI-DATAORIGIN` (table or plot). Compare value, bins and uncertainty with the paper. Where they differ, the paper wins.
  - Time series are excluded by default (`time_series=no`, C128).
  - Keep the native rigidity axis. If you convert to kinetic energy, state |Z|=1, A=1 and the proton mass. Use `scripts/ams_kinematics.py`.

**3. ASI SSDC Cosmic Ray Database, https://tools.ssdc.asi.it/CosmicRays/sf_search.jsp [Tier 6; page not read, S52; description only from S53]**
- What I can say, from a 2017 proceedings abstract (Di Felice, Pizzolotto et al., PoS ICRC2017, 1073) [Documented, C130, abstract level]:
  - The database was "under development" at the ASI Space Science Data Center.
  - It was originally built to support retrieval of PAMELA and AMS-02 results and was expanding to other charged-cosmic-ray missions, with new retrieval, visualisation and download tools.
  - Per the abstract it offers differential energy fluxes and flux ratios per species, as functions of energy, rigidity, time and magnetic parameters, for galactic and solar particles and for particles trapped in the magnetosphere.

**4. How to run the cross-check**
1. Fix the estimand: proton flux vs rigidity (native), which AMS period (S06 is 2011-05-19 to 2013-11-26), and time-averaged vs time-resolved.
2. Take the AMS values from the S06 paper tables (or supplement).
3. Pull the CRDB export, native axis, combinations and conversions off. Label each row `[Secondary: CRDB, version V, retrieved date]`.
4. From the SSDC page, record the dataset name, its rigidity or energy axis, and the access date, once you can read it.
5. Compare bin by bin. Use a stated metric and a trigger, for example the relative difference in each bin against the paper's quoted total uncertainty, with a trigger to investigate when it exceeds that. "Looks consistent" is not validation.
6. Report differences (plot transcription, rebinning, different period) to the database maintainers. A database value is a transcription, never an AMS result.
