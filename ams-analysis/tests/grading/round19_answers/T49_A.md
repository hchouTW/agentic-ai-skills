## What the F/Si versus B/O comparison shows

All fit values below are [Documented, C160, C161], taken from the fluorine paper.

- **F/Si fit.** Si is used as the characteristic heavy primary. The fit is a broken power law with a break at 175 GV, over 28.8-2900 GV.
  - C = 0.044 +- 0.001, Delta1 = -0.34 +- 0.02, Delta2 = -0.19 +- 0.07, chi2/d.o.f. = 13/16.
  - The F/Si ratio hardens above 175 GV by Delta2 - Delta1 = 0.15 +- 0.07.
- **B/O fit.** Same functional form, applied to the earlier AMS B/O data.
  - C = 0.097 +- 0.003, Delta1 = -0.405 +- 0.005, Delta2 = -0.26 +- 0.03, chi2/d.o.f. = 24/36.
  - The B/O data come from an earlier AMS paper, not from this Letter; only the fit is reported here.
- **Comparison with the light-secondary hardening.** The paper compares the F/Si hardening with its quoted AMS result of 0.140 +- 0.025 for the Li/C, Be/C, B/C, Li/O, Be/O and B/O ratios. That 0.140 +- 0.025 comes from earlier AMS papers, not from this Letter [Documented, C160].
- **Double ratio.** [(F/Si)/(B/O)] is fitted with a broken power law with a break at R0 = 9.8 +- 0.9 GV, giving chi2/d.o.f. = 24/45 [Documented, C161].
  - Above about 10 GV it is described by a single power law R^delta with delta = 0.052 +- 0.007, a 7 sigma difference from zero.
  - The paper says it does not change with time below 20 GV, but that figure is in the Supplemental Material, which was not read.
- **F/B.** The F/B ratio above 7 GV is compatible with a constant above R0 = 150 +- 60 GV [Documented, C159]. The paper expects F/B proportional to R^(0.012 +- 0.013) above 86.5 GV, using the Si/O ratio from the earlier Ne, Mg, Si paper [Documented, C161].

### Implication for propagation

[Documented, C161, the paper's own conclusions] The F/Si rigidity dependence is distinctly different from that of B/O (or B/C). The paper concludes that the propagation properties of heavy cosmic rays from F to Si differ from those of light cosmic rays from He to O, and that secondary cosmic rays also have two classes, analogous to the primary classes.

Caveats on this implication:
- The 7 sigma significance and the two-class statement are the paper's own statements. The text gives no trial factor or significance calculation.
- The B/O and F/Si fits use different parent species, different rigidity intervals and different break rigidities. B/O is fitted with a 175 GV break, but the B/O fit interval is not stated explicitly in the main text. The double ratio is therefore a comparison of two fitted shapes, not a model-independent propagation measurement.
- The F/Si fit has a large Delta2 uncertainty. The hardening 0.15 +- 0.07 is about 2 sigma by itself. The stronger statement rests on the double ratio at 7 sigma.
- [General method / not shown in the index] Choosing Si as the F parent assumes that the heavy primaries Ne, Mg and Si share a rigidity dependence. The papers support this only above 80.5 GV (C160) or 86.5 GV (C147, C148). The F parent mix (Ne, Mg, Si and others) is not separated here. A result that depends on this should be checked with a different parent choice.
- A fitted functional form is a description, not proof of mechanism. Any statement about grammage, escape, a break in the diffusion coefficient or a source effect is a model step and is outside the direct measurement [General method; see source-policy "Result interpretation"].
- The Fe paper (C155, in the slab-model fit) reports that the Fe/Si ratio at the source is not constant at the 3 sigma level in that model. It is a separate point and does not by itself explain the F/Si result.
