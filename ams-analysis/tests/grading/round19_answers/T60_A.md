- What can be said qualitatively about the indices [Documented, C136]:
  - The magnitude and rigidity dependence of the Li, Be and B spectral indices are nearly identical.

**Flux and ratio facts that go with this**

- Rigidity dependence [Documented, C135]:
  - Li and B have an identical rigidity dependence above about 7 GV. All three fluxes (Li, Be, B) are identical above about 30 GV. The 7 and 30 GV boundaries are approximate ("about") in the text.
  - Constant fits give Li/B = 0.72 +- 0.02 above 7 GV (chi2/d.o.f. = 51/53), Be/B = 0.36 +- 0.01 above 30 GV (27/35), and Li/Be = 2.0 +- 0.1 above 30 GV.
  - All three fluxes deviate from a single power law above 200 GV in an identical way. The same behavior is seen in the AMS primary He, C, O fluxes.
- Be below 30 GV [Documented, C135, C83]: the Be flux has a different rigidity dependence there. The paper says this is most likely due to the radioactive 10Be isotope (half-life 1.4 My). This is the paper's own statement, not a measurement. AMS has not measured 10Be here, and the isotope measurements in S12-S14 concern He, D and Li, not Be.
- Secondary-to-primary ratios [Documented, C136]: the ratio index is Delta = d[log(Phi_S/Phi_P)]/d[log(R)], taken over the two intervals 60.3-192 GV and 192-3300 GV. It was computed for Li/C, Be/C, B/C, Li/O, Be/O and B/O. The average hardening above about 200 GV is 0.13 +- 0.03. Only this average is quoted; the per-ratio, per-interval Delta values are in the figures and the Supplemental Material and are not in the ledger.

**What this says about propagation**

1. Interpretation as stated by AMS [Documented, C136]: the paper states that the secondary-to-primary hardening is consistent with expectations if the hardening is due to propagation properties in the Galaxy.
2. Why the secondary-to-primary hardening points to propagation [General method]:
   - Li, Be and B are produced mainly by fragmentation of primaries (C, O and heavier) on the interstellar medium, so a secondary flux scales roughly as (primary flux) x (escape time) x (cross section).
   - The secondary spectrum is therefore steeper than the primary by about the diffusion index delta.
   - A secondary-to-primary index difference Delta that turns positive at high rigidity means the secondary spectrum hardens faster than the primary spectrum. That is the signature of a rigidity-dependent change in the escape or diffusion term, not of a source change.
   - Because Li, Be and B hardening exceeds that of He, C and O, the hardening cannot be explained by a source-spectrum feature shared by all species. A propagation-type change is the natural reading.
3. Caveats, which are labeled so and are not AMS conclusions:
   - Mapping Delta onto a diffusion index requires a propagation model (leaky-box or a diffusion code), cross-section inputs and an assumed injection spectrum. All of those are model choices.
   - The data alone show an empirical hardening, not the mechanism.
   - The Be difference below 30 GV and the 10Be decay add a time-scale dependence that a pure power-law reading of Delta would miss.
   - The slightly different survival and cross-section corrections per species mean ratio uncertainties do not cancel automatically. The paper's systematics are quoted per source, not as a total.
4. Related primary-and-secondary context from other AMS papers (not the Li, Be, B paper):
   - The nitrogen spectral index lies between the primary (He, C, O) and secondary (Li, Be, B) indices [Documented, C142]. Nitrogen is fitted as a sum of 0.090 +- 0.002 x Phi_O and 0.62 +- 0.02 x Phi_B. This is a descriptive two-component fit and not proof of mechanism.
   - Ne, Mg, Si show an index offset from He, C, O of -0.045 +- 0.008 above 86.5 GV [Documented, C148]. That result is about primary classes and is not part of the secondary argument.
