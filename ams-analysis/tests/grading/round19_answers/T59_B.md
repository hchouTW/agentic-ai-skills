**Scope.** The Ne, Mg, Si paper is S56 (PRL 124, 211102, 2020). The Li, Be, B paper is S54 (PRL 120, 021101, 2018). Both are Tier 1. Both were read as full-text main articles only, on 2026-10-02. The Supplemental Material was not opened, so per-bin tables and combined systematic totals are not available here. The values below are the per-source sizes quoted in the main text. They are not totals.

**Comparison** (all [Documented])

| Item | Li, Be, B (S54, C132, C134) | Ne, Mg, Si (S56, C144, C146) |
|---|---|---|
| Data period and range | 5 years, 19 May 2011 to 26 May 2016; 1.9 GV to 3.3 TV | 7 years, 19 May 2011 to 26 May 2018; 2.15 GV to 3.0 TV |
| Trigger efficiency (selection) | > 98% for 3 <= Z <= 5, over the whole rigidity range | > 94% for the three nuclei, over the whole rigidity range |
| Trigger-efficiency systematic | < 0.5% | < 1% |
| Inelastic cross-section systematic | < 2%-3% up to 100 GV; < 3%-4% at 3.3 TV | < 3.5% up to 100 GV; < 4% from 100 GV to 3 TV |
| Rigidity-scale systematic | < 1% up to 200 GV; rising to 5%-7% at 3.3 TV | < 1% up to 300 GV; 6% at 3 TV |

- The trigger-efficiency row mixes two different quantities. The efficiency is stated in the event selection. The systematic is the size of the resulting flux error.
- The Ne, Mg, Si paper quotes the rigidity-scale error as 6% at 3 TV. The Li, Be, B paper quotes a 5%-7% spread at 3.3 TV.
- Both papers keep the cross-section systematic separate from the rigidity-scale systematic.

**How to read the differences.**
- The Ne, Mg, Si cross-section systematic is larger than the Li, Be, B one by about 1 percentage point. The ledger gives no reason for this, so I do not attribute one. The nuclei differ, and so does the quoted range. Do not infer a cause from the ledger.
- The trigger-efficiency selection and systematic figures are each quoted for a different Z group, so they are not directly comparable beyond what the table states.
- The rigidity-scale growth at the top of the range is quoted at different end points: 3.3 TV for Li, Be, B and 3 TV for Ne, Mg, Si. Compare them as the papers state them, without extrapolating one to the other's end point.
- Do not combine these sizes into a total. The papers' totals are in the Supplemental Material, which was not read.

**Where the 1/30 TV^-1 comes from.**
- In the Ne, Mg, Si paper (C146), it is the residual tracker-misalignment error on the rigidity scale. It is estimated by comparing the E/p ratio for electrons and positrons. The paper quotes it from its Ref. [26], Berdugo et al., NIM A 869, 10 (2017). That is a cited value, not something measured in the Letter.
- A second rigidity-scale contribution comes from the magnetic field map measurement and its temperature corrections.
- The Li, Be, B paper (C134) gives the same number, 1/30 TV^-1, and says it is from the E/p of e+/e-. The ledger records no reference attached to it in that paper. So the shared origin with the Ne, Mg, Si Ref. [26] is an inference from the identical number and method. It is not documented.
- The Berdugo NIM A 869, 10 paper itself is not in the source index and was not opened. I cannot confirm its content from here. Treat the attribution as "as cited by S56". The index also lists two different 2017 NIM A 869 papers (S19, pages 29 and 110), which are not that one.
- Other AMS papers quote different values. For example, the sulfur paper has 1/34 TV^-1. Do not assume 1/30 applies to every AMS nuclei paper.
