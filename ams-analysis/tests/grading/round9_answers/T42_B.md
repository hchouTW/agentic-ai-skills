**Short answer.** Quote the CLs 95% upper limit **s_up = 4.9 signal events** (n = 3, b = 4.2, background treated as exactly known), and always state next to it the expected (median) limit under background only, **5.8 events**, with its bands. Your observed limit is better than the median, which is a mild downward fluctuation (n = 3 against 4.2 expected) and not a sign of extra sensitivity. All numbers below are [General method], computed with `scripts/poisson_diagnostics.py`. Nothing here is AMS-specific; n = 3 and b = 4.2 are your inputs.

**Why a construction must be named.** With n below b, the answer depends on the construction, so say which one you used:

| Construction (95%, n=3, b=4.2) | Result on signal s | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| Classical Poisson (P(N<=3 | s+b) = 0.05) | s < 3.55 | Exact, but tighter than the sensitivity because n fell below b. It is not protected against downward fluctuations. |
| Feldman-Cousins (unified, s >= 0) | [0, 4.18] | A proper frequentist interval. The lower end of 0 is expected behaviour, so there is no evidence for a signal. |
| **CLs (CLs+b / CLb)** | **s < 4.90** | Conservative by design, so it over-covers. It is not a frequentist interval. It is the usual choice for a limit and comes with an expected band. |

CLs expected limit under background only: median 5.79, 1 sigma band [4.14, 7.90], 2 sigma band [3.51, 11.53]. Your observed 4.90 sits inside the 1 sigma band, so it is unremarkable.

Do not use S/sqrt(B), Gaussian errors or a Wilks-type interval here. The counts are tiny and the signal is bounded at zero. Do not quote the bare `-ln(0.05) = 3.0` bound either. That is the zero-count result and does not apply to n = 3 with b = 4.2.

**How to show it.**
1. State the inputs: n_obs = 3, b = 4.2, CL = 95%, one-sided, and the construction (CLs, known background).
2. Show the observed limit together with the expected limit (median, 1 and 2 sigma bands, as a "Brazil band" if you scan a parameter such as mass or lifetime). A single number without the expected band is incomplete.
3. Say in one line that CLs is conservative and is not a frequentist interval. Give the FC interval [0, 4.18] or the classical 3.55 only as a cross-check, labelled as such. Do not mix the labels.
4. Convert to the physical quantity, such as a flux or rate limit: s_up / (ε · exposure), where exposure already contains acceptance and livetime. Propagate the uncertainties of ε and the exposure into the limit. Do not multiply conditional efficiencies as if they were independent.

**What the number assumes, and what to check.**
- b = 4.2 is treated as exact. If it has an uncertainty (a control region, a template, MC statistics), then use a profile-likelihood or toy-based limit, or marginalize the background. Vary sigma_b and show that the limit moves little. The script's `--sigma-b` option and `likelihood_limits.py` support this. A difference between the two treatments is a method systematic. Do not call a profile limit a CLs limit.
- If the search has several bins or a shape, use a multi-bin likelihood and calibrate it with toys. Check the coverage with seeded toys.
- If you looked in many places (mass, bins, selections), the look-elsewhere or trials factor matters for any excess claim. For a limit, state the signal region that was fixed before looking.
- A signal-efficiency uncertainty can be folded in the same way as the background uncertainty.

**Unknown, needs input.** Is the background estimated with an uncertainty, and from what? Is this a counting experiment or a binned/shape fit? Is the target a count limit, or a flux/rate limit, and what are the efficiency and exposure? Would a different CL convention (for example a power-constrained limit) be required by your collaboration or venue? If the work is for AMS, the required limit construction is set by collaboration practice, which is not available here. Whatever you choose, label it as your proposal, not as AMS practice.

**Suggested wording.** "Observing 3 events with an expected background of 4.2 (assumed known), the 95% CL CLs upper limit on the number of signal events is 4.9 (expected 5.8, 1 sigma range 4.1 to 7.9)."
