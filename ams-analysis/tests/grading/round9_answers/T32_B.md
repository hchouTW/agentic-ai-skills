# Design: 27-day positron/electron ratio, 5-50 GV, with a one-year periodicity search

Labels: [Documented, C##] means a claim in the skill's source-index. [General method] and [Proposal] are my recommendations. [Unknown/needs input] marks things only the analysis team can supply. I quote no AMS livetime, efficiency, or rejection numbers, and none are invented here.

## 1. Estimand (fix this first)

- Target: R_k(R_j) = Phi_e+(k, j) / Phi_e-(k, j), for 27-day time bins k and rigidity bins j. The range 5-50 GV is in rigidity |R| (Tracker), with charge sign from the Tracker only.
- Report two products: (a) a per-bin ratio, and (b) a single integrated 5-50 GV ratio per bin for the periodicity search. The integrated version has far more counts per bin, which matters because positron counts at tens of GV in 27 days are small.
- Use the ratio of fluxes (equivalently the ratio of the same-bin counts after corrections), not the positron fraction. Do not mix the two definitions.
- Variable: AMS's daily lepton papers bin in rigidity, 1.00-41.9 GV [Documented, C40, C41]. The Bartels-rotation lepton paper and the time-integrated lepton fluxes bin in energy [Documented, C39, C48, C24, C25]. Your 5-50 GV range extends past the 41.9 GV upper edge of the daily-lepton rigidity bins, so high-rigidity bins will be statistically thin. State the bin variable explicitly, and if you go to energy, state the conversion (electron mass is negligible at these rigidities, |Z| = 1).
- AMS has published Bartels-rotation (27-day) lepton products, so 27-day bins are a known granularity. They are not a requirement [Documented, C39, C48; the deuteron and Li-isotope analyses used 108-day bins instead, C27, C29].
- Ratio R_k is the time-dependent quantity; the 830 +- 30 day e+/e- transition across the 2013 polarity reversal is the review's logistic fit and the S42 abstract's observation [Documented, C90, C39, C89]. It is a low-frequency solar-cycle feature that your periodicity search must remove before looking at one year.

## 2. Time binning

- [General method] 27 days is a fixed Bartels-rotation count. In one year there are 365.25/27 = 13.5 bins, so a one-year signal is sampled at 13.5 points per cycle. That is well above the Nyquist limit (shortest resolvable period is 54 days), so a one-year search is supported.
- Consequences:
  - Periods shorter than 54 days are aliased. The solar-rotation recurrence (about 27 d) sits at the bin width and averages away or aliases to near-zero frequency. So these bins cannot be used to study 27-day or shorter structure, and the 27-day bins neither help nor hurt the annual search as long as the 27-day signal is not strong. If shorter periods matter, use daily products as AMS did [Documented, C40, C41, abstract level].
  - The fractional offset of 13.5 bins per year means the annual phase drifts through the bin grid, which is fine; just fit the phase.
- [General method] Bin edges must not straddle hardware-era, reconstruction-version, or calibration boundaries. Fix bins, rigidity range and the stability metric before looking at the time dependence [Proposal].
- Span: May 2011 to the latest processed data (about 15 years, about 200 bins). One-year frequency resolution is about 1/T, so it is resolvable from solar-cycle trend components only if you model the trend. Short spans (fewer than about 3 cycles) give a weak annual test.

## 3. Per-bin measurement chain (workflow)

1. Selection: Tracker charge sign and |Z| = 1; TRD and ECAL lepton-hadron separation; E/p compatibility for electrons. TRD and ECAL do not measure charge sign [General method; roles in antimatter-and-leptons, C65, C67, C72 for review-level numbers; do not quote rejection numbers without scope].
2. Yield extraction per (k, j): template fit in the TRD/charge-confusion plane in the pattern AMS used for positrons (templates: correct-sign lepton, proton, charge-confused lepton) [Documented, C24, S04 scope]. Needs separate fits in each time bin, with templates that either follow the data period or are shown stable. Finite-template statistics must be included (Barlow-Beeston) [General method].
3. Charge confusion e-→e+ is a tail effect and must be validated in the tail and by time period, not from the Gaussian core [General method]. Protons are the dominant e+ background and their time variation (solar modulation) differs from leptons, so the proton-template contamination varies with k.
4. Per-bin exposure E_k(j) = sum of A_eff x T_live x cutoff transmission inside the bin, computed per charge sign. Quality-time conditions (normal conditions, zenith angle, outside the SAA) and cutoff factors follow your analysis choice; AMS's daily and rotation products used 40 degrees or 30 degrees pointing cuts and different cutoff treatments, so neither is a rule [Documented, C48, C51, C54-C58; scope: those analyses].
5. Unfolding or forward folding per time bin; migration is small at 5-50 GV but charge confusion is a sign migration (matrix M(e+<-e-), M(e-<-e+)) [General method]. Unfold independently per bin as AMS did, or fit jointly with shared response [Documented, C43, C46, C49].
6. Ratio R_k = Phi+/Phi-: do not assume acceptance and efficiency cancel. Classify each nuisance as fully correlated (cancels), partially correlated (reduce), or independent per sign (does not cancel). Items that differ by sign: geomagnetic cutoff transmission (differs for the two signs in direction/time), charge confusion, the proton background, and ECAL/TRD efficiencies for e+ and e-. Verify rather than assert cancellation [General method; invariant 5].

Efficiencies are conditional, not independent. Each correction effect is applied once (veto, calibration, efficiency, response) [General method; invariant 3].

## 4. Covariance model (needed for the periodicity test)

- Statistical errors: independent between bins (disjoint events), but e+ and e- in the same bin share nothing statistically except the shared template/control samples.
- Systematics: classify each as persistent (template shape, cross-section model), slowly varying (calibration drift, neighbouring bins correlated), or per-period independent. Build one covariance over (time, rigidity) and check it with `scripts/validate_covariance.py` [General method].
- Time-dependent systematics (efficiencies evaluated per period) are added in quadrature in AMS's daily proton paper [Documented, C44]; how AMS correlated systematics across days or rotations is **[Unknown/needs input]** (not described in the text read), so you must supply your own model.
- Do not normalize all periods to a reference period without carrying the induced correlation [General method].

## 5. Periodicity search for one year

Predefine (before looking): observable, trend model, test statistic, period (or band), and threshold.

1. Remove the solar-cycle trend jointly with the periodic term, not before. Fit
   R_k = T(t_k; trend params) + a cos(2 pi t_k / P) + b sin(2 pi t_k / P),
   where T is a smooth trend (low-order spline, or a polarity-reversal model such as the review's four-parameter logistic fit [Documented, C90]) with a stated flexibility. Detrending by hand first leaks power and biases the annual amplitude. Amplitude = sqrt(a^2 + b^2), not a^2 + b^2.
2. Use a weighted least-squares fit with the full covariance (generalized least squares), or a generalized Lomb-Scargle with uncertainty weights, since exposure and uncertainties differ by bin and some bins are missing.
3. Single frequency versus search: if the period is fixed a priori at P = 1 year (365.25 d), there is one trial and the local p-value is the global one. If you scan a band of periods (for example to identify the period), then a look-elsewhere effect applies; report local and global significance (trial factors, Gross and Vitells [S37]) and calibrate with toys that reproduce the sampling, exposure pattern and correlated systematics. Do not use Wilks or a single-frequency p-value for a scan [General method].
4. Null model for toys: smooth trend plus red noise (AR(1), like AMS's wavelet confidence levels from red-noise surrogates [Documented, C45, C52, daily proton flux only]) plus correlated systematics. Red noise matters because the solar cycle and its short-term structure put power at low frequency, which inflates apparent significance at 1 year if the noise is assumed white.
5. Cross-check method: wavelet time-frequency power with 95% red-noise confidence levels (as AMS used for periodicities in the daily proton flux [Documented, C45, C52]); this is another method, not a validation of this one.
6. Compare the e+ and e- series separately and jointly. Because the ratio can hide structure in the individual fluxes (the S42 abstract reports e+ and e- structures that are not visible in the ratio [Documented, C39]), search e+ and e- fluxes and the ratio. A signal in the ratio requires that e+ and e- differ in modulation, which is plausible via charge-sign-dependent drifts [General method; AMS reports positron variations similar to protons and unlike electrons, with positrons modulated more than protons below about 7 GV and a hysteresis below about 8.5 GV, C40, C41, abstract level]. Do not merge those two thresholds.

## 6. Non-physical annual sources (the dominant risk)

A one-year period is exactly where instrumental and orbital effects land. Run the identical search on:
- the livetime/exposure series per bin and per rigidity bin, and the geomagnetic-cutoff-weighted exposure,
- efficiency and acceptance series, the ISS orbit-geometry (beta angle, seasonal thermal) and quality-time fractions,
- a control species with no expected annual term (for example protons or helium, which AMS shows have their own time structure, C36, C37, C41; so use a species/range chosen in advance and state its expected modulation), and a non-contiguous control rigidity bin.
A coincident annual signal in any of these is a systematic, not a discovery. Also check the 27-day-bin alignment: the ISS orbital precession and beta-angle cycle (of order two months) can alias into the annual frequency in a 13.5-points-per-year series; test for it explicitly with the exposure series.

Physical sources to keep distinct, as interpretation steps (not part of the measurement): heliospheric seasonal effects (Earth's heliolatitude excursion, orbital distance), and solar-cycle low-frequency leakage. A modulation or drift model is a separate step with its own assumptions [General method].

## 7. Statistics at low counts

At 30-50 GV a single 27-day bin may have few positrons. Use Poisson-exact or toy-validated errors, not Gaussian ones; do not use S/sqrt(B). Options: merge rigidity bins (integrate 5-50 GV, or use two or three wide bands), or lengthen time bins in the high band and state the supported rigidity range for each time resolution. Check the ratio's error with toys (`scripts/statistical_toys.py` ratio-spread options; `scripts/poisson_diagnostics.py` for exact intervals) [General method].

## 8. Validation, with metrics and triggers

- Closure: inject a known annual modulation (a few chosen amplitudes, a few phases) into per-bin counts or the ratio and check that the full chain (templates, unfolding, trend fit, covariance) recovers amplitude and phase within the stated uncertainty. Trigger: bias beyond one standard error of the amplitude, or coverage below nominal in toys, means the trend model or covariance must be changed.
- Null calibration: toys under the null (trend + red noise + systematics) give the distribution of the annual-amplitude statistic; the observed value is compared with it. Trigger: if the toy and analytic p-values disagree materially, use the toy one.
- Stability: the abundant control rate versus time must be flat after livetime and cutoff correction within a pre-set tolerance; if not, add a period split, a scale factor, or a systematic.
- Robustness: vary trend flexibility, rigidity band (5-50 versus 5-20 versus 20-50 GV), cutoff safety factor (AMS varied 1.0-1.4 in the deuteron analysis [Documented, C27]; here it is a nuisance to vary, not a rule), template choice, tight versus loose ECAL cut. The annual amplitude should be stable; if it changes more than its statistical error, report the spread as a systematic.
- Split tests: odd versus even years, first versus second half of the data, early (positive polarity) versus late (negative polarity) epochs; the annual amplitude should be consistent, or the change itself is a result to report.
- "Reasonable agreement" is not validation; state the metric and the trigger as above.

## 9. Reporting

- Per-bin R_k with the full covariance, exposures and counts, so others can refit. A single combined number hides the time dependence [Proposal].
- Annual amplitude and phase with uncertainty, local and global p-value (global only if period scanned), and the null/control-series results. State the data period, rigidity range, bin variable, trend model and covariance model.
- Do not interpret the annual term as heliospheric physics, dark matter, or anything else without a separate model step.
