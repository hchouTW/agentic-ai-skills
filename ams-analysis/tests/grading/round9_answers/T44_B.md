**Verdict: not acceptable as described.** There are two separate problems: the automatic L-curve choice of regularization, and fitting the unfolded points with diagonal errors only. Both can be fixed.

## 1. Automatic L-curve corner

- [General method] The L-curve corner (maximum curvature of the residual-norm versus solution-norm curve) is a heuristic. It has no guarantee of minimizing bias² + variance, and its corner can be poorly defined or unstable for noisy or low-count data.
- [General method] Declare the criterion in advance and determine it without tuning on the final unfolded spectrum. For example, minimize the total expected error (bias² + variance) on MC pseudo-experiments that include stress-test spectra, then document the chosen strength and its sensitivity. A strength tuned on the data themselves, or copied from another analysis, has no meaning outside its own response, prior, bins and statistics.
- [General method] Before trusting any automatic criterion, compare it with generalized cross-validation and leave-one-out cross-validation, and with the best fixed setting, on seeded toys. `scripts/unfolding_diagnostics.py choose-regularization` does this. A criterion whose toy error is well above the best fixed setting (the L-curve corner often is) is not a safe automatic rule.
- Regularization biases the result toward smoothness. That is the same direction as the spectral shape you want to fit, so any bias is partly circular. Quote the bias with a prior and reweighting variation, and unfold with different priors as a systematic.
- Required validation:
  - nominal closure
  - reweighted-spectrum closure
  - distorted-spectrum stress tests
  - response perturbations
  - pull and coverage toys
  - stability versus binning
  - bias and variance as a function of regularization strength
  - toys through the full unfolding for the covariance, not only linear error propagation
- [Documented, C23] Published AMS flux analyses (S06, S08) use iterative unfolding with a declared convergence criterion (successive fluxes agree within 0.1%), together with closure-type validation and independent analyses. That is a pre-declared criterion, not a data-driven automatic rule. Details of validation beyond the main article are in the Supplemental Material, which was not read.

## 2. Fitting with diagonal errors

- [General method] Regularized unfolded bins are strongly correlated, and the correlations are induced by the regularization itself. A fit using only diagonal errors gives wrong chi2 values, wrong parameter uncertainties, and a possibly biased index, because the penalty smooths neighbouring bins.
- Use the full covariance of the unfolded result. That means data statistics plus response-matrix statistics and systematics, which is a full matrix, not a diagonal.
  - Check that the covariance is symmetric and positive semi-definite with `scripts/validate_covariance.py`.
  - `response-covariance` in `scripts/unfolding_diagnostics.py` gives the response-statistics part.
  - Do not quote per-bin percentage errors as if they were independent.
- Note that regularized unfolded points are biased, with the bias correlated across bins, so even the full statistical covariance does not cover the regularization bias. Add that bias as a systematic.
- Better option: if the estimand is the spectral index, forward-fold. Fit the parametric model (a power law, or a power law with a break) through acceptance, efficiency and response directly to the reconstructed counts, with a Poisson likelihood and nuisance parameters. This avoids regularization and covariance issues entirely.
  - The result is conditional on the parametrization, and goodness of fit tests the family. Say that explicitly.
  - Use `nonparam-fold` or the parametric fold comparison if you want to check unfolding against the fold on the same toys.
  - If you use a smoothness prior in a non-parametric fold, it favors falling spectra. State that, and repeat with a different penalty.
- If the unfolded spectrum is required, fit it with the full covariance, then validate the index with toys (generate pseudo-data with a known index, unfold, fit, and check bias and pulls). Report the sensitivity of the index to the regularization strength and prior.
- Also check the diagonal-response assumption if you are using any bin-by-bin step. Steep spectra with finite resolution have appreciable migration.
