**Verdict: not publication-grade as described. The skeleton is right, but the chain is under-specified and a bin-by-bin MC factor is unsafe for a proton flux unless you show migration is negligible. Several terms are probably missing or double-counted.**

Assumptions: proton flux at the top of the instrument in rigidity, public-level reasoning, no internal numbers. I use symbols (`n_i`, `A_j`, `ε_j`, `T_j`) throughout.

## Defects in priority order

1. **Bin-by-bin MC correction versus unfolding [General method].** The rigidity spectrum is steeply falling and the Tracker response has non-Gaussian tails. Events migrate from abundant low-R bins into higher reconstructed bins, so a diagonal factor biases the flux in a rigidity-dependent way. A bin-by-bin factor is also tied to the MC spectral shape: it is only valid if the MC spectrum matches the data. Purity or a good resolution core does not show that migration is negligible. Required:
   - Build the response `R_ij = P(R_meas in bin i | R_true in bin j, selected)`, tails included.
   - Either unfold (iterative Bayesian, regularized or SVD) with a full covariance, or forward-fold with `μ_i = Σ_j R_ij A_j ε_j T_j Φ_j ΔR_j + b_i`.
   - Keep bin-by-bin only if you demonstrate negligible migration at your target precision.
   - Public AMS flux papers use unfolding for the migration correction. For phosphorus the correction runs from about +25% at 3 GV to -21% at 1.2 TV [Documented, C30]. That is one paper and one nucleus, not a proton number. It does show the correction is not small.

2. **Double counting [General method].** "Acceptance times livetime" and a "MC correction factor" can overlap. If the bin-by-bin factor is the full MC `N_true/N_reco`, it already contains acceptance, efficiency and migration. Multiplying it again by an acceptance from the same MC counts those effects twice. Assign each effect to exactly one category: acceptance, efficiency, veto, calibration or response.

3. **Missing background subtraction.** The formula starts from "count events after cuts", but the proton sample needs `n_i - b_i`. Contaminations to consider:
   - charge confusion;
   - He and Z=1 neighbours, and fragmentation from heavier nuclei;
   - deuterons;
   - electrons and positrons (TRD or other-subsystem controls);
   - albedo and upward-going particles at low R.
   Estimate `b_i` with a template fit that includes finite-template statistics (Barlow-Beeston) and a covariance. Backgrounds are tail effects, so they cannot be estimated from the Gaussian core.

4. **Efficiency not shown in your formula.** Efficiency is distinct from acceptance. Trigger and selection efficiencies are conditional, so their product is not valid if they are correlated. Data/MC differences should be measured on data, for example with control samples or a tag-and-probe style method [Proposal]. Apply the data/MC scale factors as a correction rather than relying on raw MC efficiency [Documented, C25, C27; AMS applies data/MC efficiency corrections to the acceptance].

5. **Livetime is not the same as exposure.** Define `T_j` as livetime times the fraction of time each rigidity is above the local geomagnetic cutoff. Select `|R| > SF · R_c`. AMS uses SF = 1.2 and scans 1.0-1.4 as a systematic [Documented, C20, C31, C27]. Not every AMS paper uses the same scheme: the daily analysis uses a data-derived cutoff [Documented, C51]. Also state:
   - quality-time selection and the zenith pointing and SAA exclusions [Documented, C31];
   - the cutoff model and its version.
   If your livetime is the raw DAQ livetime, the cutoff transmission is missing and the flux is wrong at low R.

6. **Acceptance and interaction corrections.** `A_j` must come from the generation record, with survival probability through upstream material and in-detector interactions. The cross-section model needs its own systematic. AMS has measured inelastic cross sections in flight and adjusted the Glauber-Gribov model to them [Documented, C80]. That is a hint for method, not a number to reuse.

7. **Bin width and bin-center convention.** Dividing by `ΔR` and quoting at the bin center is biased for a steep spectrum. State the convention. Use a bin-average or flux-weighted position, not the arithmetic center, and note the Jacobian if you convert to kinetic energy or `T/A`. Check conversions with `scripts/ams_kinematics.py`. Convert with explicit `Z`, `A` and mass (`p = |Z|R`).

8. **No uncertainty model.** You need statistical and systematic uncertainties with bin-to-bin covariance. Adjacent fluxes are correlated through the unfolding and shared systematics. Systematic sources, each with a size and a correlation:
   - rigidity scale;
   - resolution and tails;
   - cutoff factor;
   - acceptance and cross section;
   - background;
   - efficiency (trigger, track, charge, data/MC);
   - unfolding prior and regularization.
   Never combine sources of unknown correlation in quadrature as if independent.

9. **Validation is not stated.** Needed, each with a metric and a trigger for changing the method:
   - MC closure;
   - closure with a reweighted spectrum;
   - a distorted-spectrum stress test;
   - response perturbations (resolution, tails, scale);
   - stability versus time, geometry, detector region and track configuration;
   - cut variations;
   - bias and variance versus iterations or regularization.
   "Reasonable agreement with a published spectrum" is only a plausibility check, not validation.

## Corrected chain [Proposal]

`Φ_i = (n_i - b_i, unfolded with R_ij) / (ΔR_i · A_i ε_i T_i)`, with:
- `T` including cutoff transmission;
- `ε` carrying data/MC scale factors;
- no separate bin-by-bin factor.

Or forward-fold using the response model above.

## Inputs that would change the verdict

- Is the response matrix off-diagonal fraction small? If it is negligible in your bins and spectrum shape, bin-by-bin can be defended after a closure test.
- What exactly is in the MC factor: only migration, or also acceptance and efficiency?
- Is the target a model-independent spectrum or a model parameter fit? A parameter fit is better served by forward folding.
- Rigidity range, data period, track configuration, cutoff model and safety factor.
- Whether this is a public-data or internal analysis. Internal AMS cuts, bins and systematic sizes are not available to me, so I did not invent any.
