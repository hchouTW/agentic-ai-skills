## What to quote

1. State the construction explicitly. For example: "CLs 95% CL upper limit s < 4.9 events (n = 3, B = 4.2 assumed exactly known); median expected limit 5.8 (+2.1/-1.6 at 1 sigma)." Do not call a CLs limit a frequentist interval. Do not call the FC interval a CLs limit.
2. Report the expected sensitivity next to the observed limit.
3. Quote the FC interval [0, 4.18] as a cross-check if you want a frequentist statement. Do not read the lower bound of 0 as evidence for zero signal.
4. Convert counts to a flux or rate limit only through the exposure: `Phi_UL = s_UL / (E * DeltaX)`, with E being the exposure (acceptance x efficiency x livetime). Propagate the uncertainty on E as a nuisance.

## How to show it

- A single figure: a horizontal axis of s (events, or the flux or rate after conversion) with the observed limit as a marker. Draw the median expected limit with green (1 sigma) and yellow (2 sigma) bands from the table above. The observed limit should be drawn against the band, not alone.
- Alternatively, plot CLs(s) against s. Mark the 0.05 line and the crossing at s = 4.9.
- Include a small table giving n, B, CL, construction (CLs), observed limit, median expected limit with +/-1 and 2 sigma limits, and a note on whether B was treated as known.
- Caption it with the construction, the confidence level, the assumed B and its treatment, and the conversion to flux or rate if applicable.

## Caveats and checks

- **The background is treated as exactly known.** B = 4.2 is certainly estimated, so it has an uncertainty. If sigma_b is non-negligible, re-run with the uncertainty. Options: `cls-limit --sigma-b` (a marginalized approximation, so vary sigma_b and confirm the limit moves little), or better a profile construction (`likelihood_limits.py profile-limit` or `profile-cls`). A wider background uncertainty lengthens the limit, and a difference between the marginalized and profiled results is itself a method systematic. For guaranteed coverage with an uncertain background, use `neyman-limit` (Berger-Boos).
- **Signal efficiency/systematics.** If the signal acceptance or efficiency are uncertain, they should also enter as nuisances before converting to a flux limit.
- **Seed/reproducibility.** The numbers above are deterministic (no toys). If you move to toy-based constructions, record the seed, toy count and confidence level.
- **Do not use S/sqrt(B) or Gaussian/Wilks errors.** At n = 3 these are invalid; use exact Poisson constructions as above.
- If you tell me the species or search (for example an antihelium or antideuteron search), the exposure, and the background uncertainty, I can go on to a flux or rate limit and a fuller treatment. Do not use unpublished candidate counts or invented efficiencies in that step.
