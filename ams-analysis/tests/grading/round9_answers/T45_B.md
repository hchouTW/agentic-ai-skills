**Short verdict: the assumption is not safe as stated.** Acceptance and efficiency partly cancel in a positron/electron ratio, but not fully. A constant fit that uses only the quoted bin errors is likely to underestimate the uncertainty on the constant, and its chi2 may be misleading.

**1. Cancellation has to be shown effect by effect.** [General method]

Ratios do not cancel acceptance, efficiency or systematics automatically. Classify each effect as correlated, partial or independent, and verify it.

| Effect | Expected behaviour | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Geometric acceptance, livetime/exposure | Largely common if the same fiducial region and the same 27-day intervals are used | Same selection geometry and identical intervals. At low energy the geomagnetic transmission differs between charge signs, so use per-sign cutoff handling. |
| Trigger efficiency, selection efficiency | Partial. The TRD/ECAL/Lambda_CC selection efficiencies are measured per species and need not be equal for e+ and e-. | Efficiency versus species and energy |
| Charge confusion (e- reconstructed as e+) | Species-specific. It does not cancel and affects the positron sample asymmetrically. It is a Tracker-tail effect, so the Gaussian core is not enough. | Charge-sign migration validation |
| Proton contamination | Much larger in the positive sample than the negative one, so it does not cancel. | Template fit and control regions |
| Energy scale and unfolding | Correlated only if the same response and the same energy scale are used for both. | Shared versus species-specific response |
| Material/interaction effects | Species-dependent in general | Variation per species |

[Documented, C24, C25] In the AMS lepton analyses, the positron and electron templates, charge-confusion modelling and data/MC efficiency corrections are species-specific. This supports treating the cancellation as partial.

**2. Quoted bin errors are the wrong error model for a constant fit.** [General method]

- Statistical errors are independent between 27-day bins built from disjoint events.
- Systematics are not independent. Persistent ones (calibration, template shape, charge-confusion modelling) are correlated across all bins. Slowly varying ones are correlated between neighbouring bins. Some are independent per bin.
- If you combine stat and sys in quadrature and fit with a diagonal chi2, you treat the persistent systematics as independent between bins.
  - This understates the uncertainty on the fitted constant, because the persistent part does not average down as 1/sqrt(N).
  - It gives a chi2 that is not calibrated, so "chi2/ndf ~ 1" is not evidence of constancy.
- Build one covariance across the time bins, with a persistent part, a slowly varying part and an independent part. Fit with the full-covariance chi2, and check the matrix with `scripts/validate_covariance.py`.
- Reference-period normalization cancels only the persistent part of the systematics. It also correlates the statistical errors of all bins with the reference, so carry that covariance.
- Do not take the persistent part as zero because the quantity is a ratio. Quantify it with `ratio-toys` in `scripts/statistical_toys.py`, using an explicit numerator-denominator correlation rho per systematic. The script cannot determine rho for you.
- With low counts (high energy, or a small denominator), the ratio of counts is biased and non-Gaussian. Use toys, not Gaussian errors. The delta method Var(N/D) ~ f^2[(sigma_N/N)^2 + (sigma_D/D)^2 - 2 rho sigma_N sigma_D/(N D)] is valid only for small relative errors.

**3. "Constant" is a physics hypothesis, not a given.** [General method]

Solar modulation depends on charge sign through drifts in the heliospheric field, whose polarity reverses over the solar cycle. So e+/e- can vary with time even if acceptance cancels perfectly.

[Documented, C89, review-level, S01] In the AMS lepton time-dependent analysis, short-term structures appear in both fluxes with about the same relative amplitude and largely cancel in R_e = Phi(e+)/Phi(e-). The long-term evolution differs between electrons and positrons.

- A constant fit over a long span can therefore be wrong because of real physics, not only because of systematics.
- A poor fit should not automatically be absorbed into inflated errors.
- Use the same variable, bins and period for e+ and e-. The ratio is energy-dependent, so a constant is only meaningful within a fixed energy bin or a narrow range. Fit it per energy bin or in a range where the ratio is flat.

**4. What to do** [Proposal]

1. Define the estimand: Phi(e+)/Phi(e-) per energy bin and per 27-day bin, in energy.
2. List each nuisance with a classification (correlated, partial or independent), a rho and how you verified it.
3. Build the time-by-bin covariance and fit with the full-covariance chi2.
4. Test constancy against alternatives. Compare the constant against a linear or charge-sign-modulation trend and against splits by solar-polarity period, with a stated metric and a trigger for adding an uncertainty or changing the model.
5. Run stability checks. The efficiency or control-species rate versus time should be flat after livetime and cutoff correction. Do not correct a drift twice.
6. State the result as the fitted constant with its covariance-based uncertainty, and keep any modulation interpretation as a separate model step.

**Inputs that would change this verdict:**
- the energy range;
- whether your bin errors are stat-only or stat+sys, and how the systematics are correlated between bins;
- your charge-confusion and proton-contamination treatment;
- whether the e+ and e- selections and exposures are identical.

Each is **[Unknown/needs input]**. If the quoted errors are statistical only and the efficiencies are measured per species, the assumption fails outright.
