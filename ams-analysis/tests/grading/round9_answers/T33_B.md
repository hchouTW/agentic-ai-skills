## 1. The 2013 positron-fraction paper is not the latest measurement

The 2013 paper is Aguilar et al. (AMS), "First Result from AMS on the ISS: Precision Measurement of the Positron Fraction in Primary Cosmic Rays of 0.5-350 GeV", PRL 110, 141102 (2013), DOI 10.1103/PhysRevLett.110.141102. It is the first AMS result and is correct to cite as such. [Documented, C03; the source index records abstract-plus-metadata verification only, so I have not read the paper body.]

- **Scope:** the positron fraction over 0.5-350 GeV, from 6.8x10^6 positron and electron events. [Documented, C03]
- **Superseded:** the source index records it as superseded in energy range and statistics by the 2014 high-statistics positron-fraction paper, PRL 113, 121101 (0.5-500 GeV). The 2014 paper is verified at metadata level only. [Documented, C03; S03]
- **Later work:** the index lists later AMS lepton results.
  - The 2019 positron-flux paper, PRL 122, 041102, reports 1.9 million positrons up to 1 TeV. It reports an excess starting near 25 GeV, a dropoff above about 284 GeV, and a source-term cutoff at more than 4 sigma. The article body gives 4.07 sigma, so a 5 sigma claim is not supported. [Documented, C04, C35]
  - Time-resolved positron and electron analyses followed. [Documented, S42, S45, S46]
  - The Phys. Rept. 894 review (2021) covers the first seven years. [Documented, S01]
