**Short answer:** your colleague is right about one number and wrong about the claim. 3.0 events is the 95% limit on the total Poisson mean when you see zero events. It is not the limit on the signal "whatever the background is". Your numbers are user-supplied: N = 0, B = 0.5 ± 30% (σ_B ≈ 0.15).

**Where the 3.0 comes from** [General method]
- For N = 0 the classical upper limit on the total mean is -ln(1 - CL) = -ln 0.05 = 3.00.
- The classical limit on the signal is s_up = -ln(1 - CL) - B. For B = 0.5 that gives 2.50 events (script output: 2.4957), not 3.0.
- It shrinks as B grows and is no longer positive once B reaches about 3.0. Calling it "independent of the background" is wrong for the classical construction.
- The statement is only true for a flat-prior Bayesian limit, which answers a different question.

**Why you should not quote the shrunken number as your result:**
- A limit that gets tighter because the expected background is larger rewards a downward fluctuation. With a known B = 0.5 it is a small effect, but the principle matters.
- Report a construction that protects against it, and say which one you used. The 30% uncertainty on B adds a further reason for care (see below).

**What I computed** (all [General method], 95% CL, N = 0, B = 0.5; run with `poisson_diagnostics.py` and `likelihood_limits.py`):

| Construction | Upper limit on s |
|---|---|
| Classical, B known exactly | 2.50 |
| Feldman-Cousins, B known (interval [0, 2.63]) | 2.63 |
| Feldman-Cousins, B = 0 (for comparison) | 3.09 |
| CLs, B known | 3.00 (equals -ln 0.05) |
| CLs, B marginalized with σ_B = 0.15 | 3.00 |
| Profile likelihood, toy-calibrated (σ_B = 0.15, seed 1) | 2.67 |
| Berger-Boos guaranteed coverage (σ_B = 0.15, β = 0.01, seed 1) | 3.23 |

- The profile-likelihood asymptotic value (1.35) is not usable at N = 0, because the asymptotic formula fails at small counts. Use the toy-calibrated value.
- The CLs limit equals 3.00 because CLs = CLs+b/CLb, and with N = 0 the background cancels in that ratio. So the 3.0 is the CLs limit regardless of B. That is probably what your colleague has in mind.
- The CLs expected (background-only) band has a median of 3.0 and +1σ and +2σ values of about 4.4 and 5.8. Report this expected sensitivity next to the observed limit.

**What to do about the uncertain background:**
1. A 30% uncertainty on B = 0.5 (σ_B = 0.15) barely moves the result. Across all the constructions above the limit stays between about 2.5 and 3.2 events. The choice of construction matters more than the uncertainty on B.
2. Do not use the known-B formula as if B had no uncertainty. Treat B as a nuisance parameter. Your 30% should be an actual constraint, from a control sample or a background-estimation systematic, not a guess.
3. Prefer a profile-likelihood construction, toy-calibrated, with the Gaussian (or log-normal, since B must be positive) constraint on B. Compare it with a marginalized (Cousins-Highland) result. Vary σ_B and check that the answer is stable. A difference between the two is a method systematic.
4. If you need guaranteed coverage for every true background, use the Berger-Boos construction (3.2 events here). The extra length over the plug-in value is the price of that guarantee.
5. Quote a CLs limit (3.0) as the conservative headline if you want protection against downward fluctuations. Do not describe it as a frequentist interval with nominal coverage. State the construction, the CL, the B and σ_B you used, and the expected limit.
6. Convert the count limit to a flux or rate limit only with an exposure, acceptance and selection efficiency. Those carry their own uncertainties, and the signal efficiency, tail-validated, is usually a larger systematic than the 30% on B. I cannot supply any of these numbers for you.

For a rare antinucleus search the background tail (charge confusion, misidentification) is the hard part. A Gaussian-core estimate of it is not acceptable.

**Verdict:** the colleague's 3.0 holds as a bound on the total mean for zero events, and as the CLs limit when you report CLs. It does not hold as a background-independent limit on signal in general. For your case, quote a profile or Berger-Boos limit with the B uncertainty included, plus the CLs/expected sensitivity, and state which construction you used.
