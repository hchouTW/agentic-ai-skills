**1. Why the ratio does not cancel automatically**

Classify each effect as correlated, partial or independent. For a positron-to-electron ratio:

| Effect | Cancels? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Geometric acceptance | Largely, if the fiducial region is identical | Verify with MC that the selection geometry is the same for both signs. |
| Trigger efficiency | Partial | It depends on species and rigidity. |
| Selection and identification efficiency | Not fully | e+ and e- are selected with the same ECAL/TRD/Tracker cuts, but the efficiencies are measured per sign and carry their own errors. |
| Charge confusion | No | It is sign-specific and a tail effect. It affects e+ and e- differently, and the proton background that feeds the positron sample has no electron counterpart. |

**2. Why quoted bin errors alone are wrong for a constant fit across 27-day bins**

- **Statistical errors** are independent between bins built from disjoint events. They are fine to use. [General method]
- **Systematic errors are not independent between bins.** They fall into three groups:
  - persistent: template shape, cross-section model, charge-confusion model. These are correlated across all periods.
  - slowly varying: calibration drift. These are correlated between neighbouring bins.
  - independent per period: for example, efficiencies evaluated each period.
- **A diagonal-only chi2** for a constant fit therefore gets the wrong uncertainty on the constant and the wrong goodness-of-fit. Persistent systematics are shared across bins. They do not average down with the number of bins, so the error on the fitted constant is underestimated. [General method]
- The fix is a full covariance over time, and over rigidity if you also have rigidity bins. It needs the numerator-denominator cross-covariance, and it should be validated with `scripts/validate_covariance.py`. A Kronecker (time x rigidity) structure is valid only if the correlation actually factorizes. Test that.
- With a measured joint covariance, run `statistical_toys.py ratio-measured`. It compares the constant-ratio fit with the full covariance against the diagonal-only fit, giving chi2 and toy p-values and the sigma ratio. Without a measured covariance, `ratio-cov` with declared structures is the fallback. [General method]
- If you normalize bins to a reference period, only the persistent part of the systematics cancels. The statistical errors of all periods then become correlated with the reference, and you must carry that covariance too. [General method]

**3. Physics reasons a constant may be the wrong model**

- Solar modulation is charge-sign dependent through heliospheric drifts, and the polarity reverses over the solar cycle. Opposite-sign particles need not follow the same time variation, so e+/e- need not be constant in time. [General method]
- AMS has reported lepton time structure: positron variations similar to protons and unlike electrons, hysteresis between electron and positron fluxes below about 8.5 GV, and a smooth e+/e- transition across the 2013 polarity reversal over 830 +- 30 days.
