**1. The 2013 paper is not the latest measurement**

- The 2013 paper is Aguilar et al. (AMS), PRL 110, 141102, "First Result from AMS on the ISS: Precision Measurement of the Positron Fraction in Primary Cosmic Rays of 0.5-350 GeV" [Documented, C03]. It reports the positron fraction from 0.5 to 350 GeV, using 6.8x10^6 positron and electron events.
- It is the first AMS result, and the source ledger marks it as superseded in range and statistics by the 2014 papers [C03].
- The 2014 papers are PRL 113, 121101 (positron fraction, 0.5-500 GeV), PRL 113, 121102 (electron and positron fluxes) and PRL 113, 221102 (e+ + e- flux) [Documented, S03]. The ledger holds only the metadata of these papers, not their full text.
- Later AMS positron papers include:
  - PRL 122, 041102 (2019), "Towards Understanding the Origin of Cosmic-Ray Positrons". It reports the positron flux up to 1 TeV, an excess starting near 25 GeV and a dropoff above about 284 GeV. It also reports a source-term energy cutoff at more than 4 sigma, quoted as 4.07 sigma in the article body. A claim of 5 sigma is not supported [Documented, C04, C35].
  - PRL 121, 051102 (2018), on time structures in the electron and positron fluxes [Documented, C39].
  - PRL 131, 151002 (2023), on temporal structures in positron spectra and charge-sign effects, using daily positron fluxes over 11 years from 1.00 to 41.9 GV [Documented, C41].
- If you cite the 2013 paper, call it "the first AMS positron-fraction result" and not "the latest".
- I have no web access here. My source index was last verified on 2026-09-20, so I cannot confirm that nothing newer exists. Check INSPIRE-HEP, the journal and ams02.space for papers or supplements after that date.
