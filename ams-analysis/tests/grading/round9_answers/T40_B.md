No. A constant positron-to-antiproton ratio is consistent with a common origin but does not prove one.

**What AMS reported** [Documented, C77, Phys. Rept. review, S01]: above 60 GeV the positron-to-antiproton flux ratio is consistent with a constant. A constant fit over 60-525 GeV gives 2.00 +- 0.035 (stat.) +- 0.06 (syst.), chi2/d.o.f. = 7.2/12. The review reads this as nearly identical energy dependence of positrons and antiprotons at high energy, and it only "suggests a possible common source". It adds that continued data taking is needed to confirm the origin. Scope: this is a fit in energy over 60-525 GeV. The earlier antiproton paper (C06) reports a similar rigidity dependence at roughly 60-500 GV, which is a different variable and range.

**Why it is not proof**

1. **Consistency is not discrimination.** chi2/d.o.f. = 7.2/12 only says the data do not reject a constant within the uncertainties. A shallow slope or a gentle curvature smaller than the errors would also pass. Constant-ratio results are only as strong as the sensitivity to a slope. [General method] To quantify this, fit a power-law slope in the ratio, report its confidence interval, and state which slopes are excluded.
2. **Equal shape does not mean equal origin.** Different mechanisms can give similar spectral shapes over a limited range. Examples are secondary production in the interstellar medium with propagation-dominated shaping, or a shared propagation or acceleration effect acting on both species. Positrons and antiprotons also suffer different energy losses: positrons lose energy by synchrotron and inverse-Compton radiation, which antiprotons do not. A constant ratio over only a decade in energy is a narrow test. [General method / Proposal]
3. **The two measurements are not independent.** They share Tracker charge-sign and acceptance systematics and the same data set. In a ratio, acceptance and efficiency cancel only partially, so each nuisance must be classified as correlated, partial or independent before the 0.06 systematic is trusted. [General method]
4. **The interpretation is conditional.** The review states that antiprotons are not produced by pulsars. It also states that a cutoff in the antiproton spectrum is expected if high-energy antiprotons come from dark-matter annihilation. No such cutoff is claimed as observed. These statements are the collaboration's qualitative reading, not measurements (C77, C75).
5. **Charge-confusion and background tails matter.** Both the positron and antiproton samples depend on tail-validated charge-confusion and background estimates (positrons: proton background and charge confusion; antiprotons: electron and charge-confusion backgrounds). Errors in these tails would propagate into the ratio. [General method]
6. **Other AMS data point the other way.** The positron-to-proton ratio is not consistent with a constant (3.4 sigma against a constant, C76). Positrons and antiprotons therefore do not both track protons, which is worth stating, though it does not by itself establish a common origin for positrons and antiprotons.

**What would strengthen the case.** More statistics and a wider energy reach, to test the constant at higher energy where models diverge. A quantitative slope limit on the ratio. A joint model fit of positron, antiproton, electron and proton data, as the review itself recommends (C75). A search for the dark-matter-like cutoff or a departure from constancy at higher energy.

**Bottom line.** The measurement is a suggestive observation of similar energy dependence (the review's own wording is "suggests a possible common source"), not evidence of a shared origin. Model-independent proof would need discriminating predictions that differ between the common-origin and separate-origin hypotheses, which this constant-ratio result alone does not provide.
