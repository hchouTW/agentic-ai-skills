## Verdict

The workflow is the right skeleton, but as described it is not defensible for a publication-level AMS-02 proton flux. Three problems matter most: a likely double count of acceptance and efficiency through the "MC bin-by-bin factor", an unvalidated bin-by-bin treatment of migration, and missing backgrounds, exposure and bin-centering definitions. I only have your one-sentence description, so the points below are risks to check, not confirmed bugs.

Assumptions: the target is the top-of-instrument differential proton flux in rigidity, in m^-2 sr^-1 s^-1 GV^-1. [General method]

## Defects in priority order

### 1. Double counting between "acceptance" and "bin-by-bin MC factor" (highest priority)

You divide by acceptance x livetime x bin width and then also apply an MC bin-by-bin factor. Check what each one contains. [General method]

- If the bin-by-bin factor is `N_true / N_reco` from MC, it corrects migration. It must not also contain efficiency or acceptance.
- If the factor is "MC truth over reconstructed counts in the fiducial region", it already contains selection efficiency and acceptance. Then dividing by `A` as well counts the same effect twice. One effect belongs to exactly one correction category: veto, calibration, efficiency or response.
- Write the chain explicitly. Per bin, state whether `A` is geometric or effective. State whether efficiency is inside `A` or separate, and whether the bin-by-bin factor includes inefficiency. If a response matrix "includes inefficiency", then `A` and `ε` are already in it and must not be applied again. [General method; efficiency-acceptance-backgrounds]
- A useful check is the audit-spec checklist: one `effect_id` per effect, with `response.includes` and `estimator.applies` stating what is applied where. Exposure already contains acceptance and livetime. [General method]

### 2. Bin-by-bin correction is only valid if migration is shown to be negligible

The diagonal estimator `Φ_i = (n_i - b_i) / (ΔR_i · A_i ε_i T_i)` is valid only when off-diagonal migration is negligible at your precision and spectral steepness. [General method; charged-cosmic-rays, inference-and-unfolding]

- A steep spectrum with finite rigidity resolution has appreciable migration even when the resolution core looks small. Abundant low-rigidity events feed upward into higher reconstructed bins. The bias is rigidity dependent and grows toward the high-rigidity end, where the resolution tails matter most.
- Purity is not a migration study. Build the response matrix `R_ij = P(R_meas in bin i | R_true in bin j, selected)`, including tails, and test the diagonal approximation against it.
- The bin-by-bin factor also depends on the MC input spectrum. If you derive it from a spectrum that differs from the measured one, the result inherits that prior (circularity). Iterate or reweight to the measured spectrum, check convergence, and quote the prior spread as a systematic.
- Public AMS flux analyses correct for migration by unfolding, not by bin-by-bin factors. For example, the phosphorus migration correction ranges from about +25% at 3 GV to -21% at 1.2 TV, so it is not small [Documented, C30, scope: phosphorus only; do not transfer to protons]. The iteration criterion stated for the antiproton paper is agreement within 0.1% between successive steps [Documented, C23, antiproton analysis only].
- Recommendation [Proposal]: use iterative Bayesian, SVD/Tikhonov or a forward-folded fit. Fix the iteration count or regularization criterion in advance on a pseudo-data ensemble, not on the final unfolded spectrum. Run MC closure, a prior-variation study and stress-test spectra. Propagate stat and sys covariance with toys through the full unfolding. If you keep the bin-by-bin factor, show quantitatively that it agrees with the unfolded result within a stated tolerance, and name what happens if it does not.

### 3. Background subtraction is missing

Your description has no `n_i - b_i`. For protons, the contaminations depend on rigidity. [General method; charged-cosmic-rays]

- Charge-migration background from adjacent |Z|, deuteron and He contamination, electron contamination (relevant at low rigidity and for the TRD), and, at high rigidity, charge-sign confusion.
- Charge confusion is a tail phenomenon. Do not estimate it from the Gaussian core of the rigidity resolution. Validate the tails, for example with control samples or data-driven templates.
- Template fits need finite-template-statistics treatment (Barlow-Beeston or the Conway variant). Propagate the background covariance.

### 4. The "acceptance" and "livetime" definitions need to be exposure-aware

[General method; efficiency-acceptance-backgrounds]

- Acceptance, efficiency, exposure and livetime are different quantities. Exposure is `Σ_t A_eff(t) T_live(t) x geomagnetic transmission`, per rigidity bin. A single global livetime does not work if acceptance varies in time, for example after hardware or configuration changes.
- Livetime must be quality-approved live time, not calendar time. It must account for dead time and prescales.
- Geomagnetic transmission is rigidity dependent. The time fraction above the local cutoff differs per bin. State the cutoff model, the safety factor and whether you use a cut or a transmission function. Vary the factor as a systematic. Public AMS analyses require rigidity above 1.2 times the maximum geomagnetic cutoff in the field of view and then scan the factor, e.g. 1.0-1.4 in the deuteron analysis [Documented, C20/C31 for the 1.2 requirement; C27 for the scan]. These are what AMS reports, not a rule for you.
- Effective acceptance from MC needs a generation record: generation surface, solid angle, generated spectrum, weights, material and geometry model, and version. Generated-spectrum reweighting must not be circular.
- Do not multiply conditional efficiencies as if independent. Check the product against a direct joint efficiency and apply the chain in two orders. Two 99% selections give a joint between 98% and 99%, so 98% is a floor, not a measurement.
- Survival probability through upstream material and in-detector interactions is a separate correction that depends on the cross-section model. Vary it.

### 5. Data/MC efficiency corrections are not mentioned

[General method]

- MC-only efficiency is not enough. Measure data/MC scale factors with tag-and-probe or orthogonal-subsystem samples, as functions of rigidity, species and time. Binomial exact intervals near 0 or 1, not Wald.
- Validate MC against data for the variables you cut on (charge estimators, track quality, TOF/TRD/RICH variables). Check the MC provenance: the production period, geometry and physics list.
- Rigidity-scale and resolution mismatches between data and MC feed straight into migration, so the response itself needs data-based validation.

### 6. Bin-width division and bin centering

[General method]

- Dividing by `ΔR` gives a bin-averaged flux. For a steep spectrum, assigning it to the arithmetic bin center is biased. State the prescription (a spectral-shape-dependent position where the flux equals the bin average) and report which variable (rigidity) the bins and flux are in.
- If you convert to kinetic energy, quote `Z`, `A` and the Jacobian. For protons `A = 1`. Use `scripts/ams_kinematics.py` for the arithmetic. Do not relabel the axis.

### 7. Uncertainties and validation

[General method]

- Statistical: Poisson counts, plus MC-statistics uncertainty of the correction factor, which is often forgotten in the bin-by-bin method.
- Systematics ledger, with each source classified and propagated: trigger and selection efficiency, acceptance, cutoff factor, background, migration/unfolding with prior spread, rigidity scale and resolution, material cross-sections, time stability. Add independent sources in quadrature and treat correlated ones through a covariance. Give the full covariance between bins, since unfolding and shared systematics correlate neighbouring bins.
- "Reasonable agreement with the published spectrum" is not validation. Agreement with published AMS spectra is a plausibility check only. State a metric and a trigger for changing the method or adding an uncertainty for each of these checks:
  - MC closure on truth (including an injected spectrum with a different shape)
  - stability in time (e.g. Bartels-rotation blocks), detector region, geometry and track configuration
  - loose/tight cuts
  - alternative responses, priors and binning
  - upward/downward and two-charge-sign splits where relevant

## Inputs that would change the verdict

- What exactly the bin-by-bin factor is (`N_true/N_reco`, `A_eff` ratio, or both) and which spectrum was used to build it. This decides whether item 1 is a real double count.
- Rigidity range and binning relative to the resolution, and the MC response matrix. A purely low-rigidity measurement with wide bins may tolerate a bin-by-bin correction. A TV-range measurement will not.
- Whether acceptance `A` is geometric or effective, and whether efficiencies are data-corrected.
- Whether this is public data or MC-only, which data period and which track configuration, and which cutoff model and safety factor.
- Whether backgrounds were subtracted and how.

## Recommended structure [Proposal]

`Φ_j` from `μ_i = Σ_j R_ij · A_j ε_j T_j Φ_j ΔR_j + b_i`, with `R_ij`, `A_j`, `ε_j`, `T_j` each defined once, and the unfolding (or forward-folded fit) in place of the bin-by-bin factor. Keep the bin-by-bin result as a cross-check. State the estimand, the response and the exposure before choosing the algorithm.
