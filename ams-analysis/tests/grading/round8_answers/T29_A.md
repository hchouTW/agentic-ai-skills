# Measurement brief: helium-4 flux, 2 GV to 3 TV, AMS-02, public inputs only

Labels: [Documented, Cxx] = claim ID in source-index, at that claim's own verification level; [General method]; [Proposal]; [Unknown/needs input]. No AMS selection cut value, bin edge, efficiency, acceptance, livetime or systematic size is invented here; where a number appears it is cited to a claim or marked as user-supplied or arithmetic. This brief does not replace Collaboration review.

## 1. Estimand and level

- **Target:** differential flux of helium at the top of the instrument (not the local interstellar spectrum, not top of atmosphere) vs absolute rigidity, Phi(R) in m^-2 sr^-1 s^-1 GV^-1. [Proposal] Result level: flux, with covariance. [Documented, C59, C62] AMS has published He flux over 1.9 GV-3 TV (first 30 months, S07) and 2.2 GV-3.0 TV (first five years, S11), so this is a reproduction or re-measurement of a published quantity, not a new range.
- **Species:** He, |Z| = 2, charge sign +1 (sign from Tracker/magnet only). The abstract's elemental flux is He = 3He + 4He; "helium-4" is an isotope-level label. [Unknown/needs input] The user must decide: (a) elemental He flux in rigidity (the published form; no A assumption needed), or (b) a pure 4He flux, which needs the 3He/4He isotope separation (mass from R and beta, only up to about 21 GV in the published isotope work) and cannot be extended to 3 TV. [General method] Above that range velocity resolution cannot separate isotopes, so a 4He-only flux to 3 TV would rest on a composition assumption. Default assumed below: elemental He, with 4He dominance carried as a composition systematic. [Documented, C60] The published helium flux varied the 3He/4He ratio from 0 to 0.2, giving 0.3%; [Documented, C09/C86] 3He/4He is a time-independent power law above 4 GV with index -0.294 +- 0.004 (S12 and review S01 sec. 11).
- **Variable:** true variable R_true = pc/(|Z|e) in GV, p = |Z| R; reconstructed R_meas. [General method, scripts/ams_kinematics.py, |Z| = 2, A = 4, m = 3.7274 GeV] 2 GV corresponds to p = 4 GeV/c and T/A = 0.435 GeV/n; 3 TV corresponds to p = 6000 GeV/c (6 TeV/c) and T/A = 1499 GeV/n. Any conversion to kinetic energy per nucleon needs A and a Jacobian (2.73 at 2 GV, 2.00 at 3 TV from the script), not relabeling.
- **Range and bins:** 2 GV to 3 TV (about 3.2 decades). [Proposal] Choose bin edges with width compared to the resolution (below). [Documented, C59, C62] The published analyses used 68 bins (S07) and 67 bins from 2.2 GV to 3.0 TV (S11); copy those only if the user is deliberately reproducing them. Bin-position prescription must be stated (spectrally weighted abscissa, not the arithmetic centre; [Documented, C79] the review plots at the abscissa for a flux proportional to R^-2.7 for proton tables).
- **Sample:** public. [Unknown/needs input] The skill's source index contains no public event-level AMS data release (no ntuples, no pass names, no good-run list). "Public AMS data" is therefore read as the published flux tables and supplements (S07, S11, S01, plus the time-resolved supplements S41, S44, S47); an event-level re-measurement is an internal-Collaboration job. Please confirm which is meant; if tables only, Sections 4-8 become a re-analysis-from-tables / forward-folding exercise (Section 7), and no efficiency or acceptance can be derived from them.

## 4. Selection, backgrounds, controls (selection ledger and background registry to be filled)

Selections are [Proposal] unless cited. For every cut the ledger names a conditional denominator, an efficiency source (data, MC, hybrid), correlations, sculpting, and a validation as metric-threshold-action. Thresholds are user-supplied or [Unknown].

Selection skeleton (published AMS pattern, [Documented, C59], first 30 months; not a universal rule, [Documented, C54, C56] other He papers differ): normal-operating-conditions collection time, AMS pointing within 40 degrees of zenith, ISS outside the South Atlantic Anomaly; downward-going; inner-tracker track with Z compatible with 2; full-span L1-L9 track with chi^2 quality; consistent Z on L1, upper TOF, lower TOF and L9 to remove interacting candidates; rigidity above 1.2 times the maximum geomagnetic cutoff (IGRF backtracing).

Geomagnetic selection: state field model and version; vary the safety factor and the model as a systematic. [Documented, C60] The published He analysis varied the factor 1.0 to 1.4 with an effect below 0.1%. Residual counts below cutoff are secondary/albedo.

Backgrounds (each needs origin, entry mechanism, control region and its signal contamination, estimator, transfer model, closure test, nuisances):

| Background | Origin | Proposed control/estimator |
|---|---|---|
| Proton (and d) contamination into Z = 2 | Charge estimator tails; relevant at high rigidity where p dominates in count | Template fit in the charge estimator, per rigidity bin, with finite-template nuisances; [Documented, C59] first-30-month analysis found proton contamination below 1e-4, subtracted with its uncertainty |
| He produced by heavier nuclei interacting at the top of AMS (charge-changing) | Adjacent Z (Li, Be, B, C ...) fragmentation | Charge templates from L1 vs inner tracker; [Documented, C59] below 1e-3 in the first 30 months |
| He interacting inside the detector (lost or mis-charged) | Inelastic cross section in AMS material | Survival-probability correction, not a background subtraction; [Documented, C80] measured in flight with horizontal-pointing data and Glauber-Gribov scaling |
| Charge-sign confusion (He^-, e.g. wrong sign) | Tracker resolution tail | Tail estimate must be data-driven or validated MC; reject a Gaussian-core estimate (invariant 4). Relevant to the highest bins |
| Rigidity migration | Finite resolution with non-Gaussian tails | Treated in the response, not as a background |
| Upward/albedo, low-R secondaries | Geomagnetic region | Cutoff factor; check the rate vs cutoff |

Public numerical purities and contaminations from papers are context only: [Documented, C62] He purity above 99.9% in the five-year analysis (S11), first-30-month contamination values as above.

## 5. Efficiency, acceptance, livetime/exposure, response (kept distinct)

- **Efficiency** `epsilon`: conditional chain with named denominators (trigger, track reconstruction, charge, quality, ACC veto); tag-and-probe or orthogonal-subsystem controls, binomial exact intervals; check factorization bias, ordering closure, and the Frechet bound. [Documented, C59] Trigger efficiency is quoted as 95-99.5% in the first-30-month analysis, with MC agreement within 0.5%. That is context only; not reusable for another period or hardware.
- **Acceptance** `A`: effective acceptance from the MC generation record, per rigidity bin (m^2 sr). It depends on the generated spectrum; avoid circularity by iterating or reweighting to the unfolded result. [General method, 4 pi/hemisphere conventions must be checked against the actual generation surface.]
- **Livetime/exposure:** collection time T_j per rigidity bin = livetime-weighted seconds above the (safety-factor times) cutoff; exposure = integral of A_eff times T over intervals, with acceptance and livetime allowed to vary together. [Documented, C59] First-30-month collection time was constant at 6.29e7 s above 30 GV and the five-year one was 1.23e8 s above 30 GV (C62); the low-rigidity values depend on cutoff. Data-quality vetoes reduce exposure only and are not also efficiency corrections (four-category rule).
- **Interaction/survival:** a separate item, species-specific (He cross sections), kept out of the geometric acceptance; do not apply it twice with the efficiency.
- **Response:** `R_ij = P(R_meas in bin i | R_true in bin j, selected)`, conditional on selection, with tails, under/overflow, and an explicit statement of whether efficiency and acceptance are inside the matrix. Orientation rows = reco, columns = truth. [Documented, C61] The published resolution function has a Gaussian core sigma(1/R)/(1/R) of 9% at 10 GV, 14% at 100 GV, 20% at 400 GV, 64% at 2 TV, with non-Gaussian tails beyond 2.5 sigma of 3% at 10 GV, 6% at 100 GV, 11% at 400 GV, 8% at 2 TV (first 30 months, He, S07), from simulation, checked in four ways in data. [Documented, C61, C63] MDR 3.2 TV.
- **Consequence for the top edge:** a 3 TV edge is at about the MDR, where the core width is of order unity (64% at 2 TV) and the sign and the tail matter. Reconstructed counts at the last bins are dominated by migration and charge-sign confusion, not by rigidity-resolved events. [Proposal] Treat the last bin(s) as wide, quote the flux only with a full response model, and consider a forward-folded estimator, not the diagonal one, in the whole upper decade.

## 6. Estimator and inference

- **Estimator [Proposal]:** do not use the diagonal estimator `(n - b)/(A eps T dR)`; migration is not small for a steep spectrum. [Documented, C62] The published unfolding corrections in the helium/carbon/oxygen paper are of order +14% at 3 GV, +6% at 5 GV and -4% to -6% from 150 GV to 3 TV (carbon values quoted in C62; the He numbers are in the paper). Use the general model `mu_i = sum_j R_ij A_j eps_j T_j Phi_j dR_j + b_i`, then either iterative Bayesian or regularized unfolding (the published AMS pattern: two-successive-iteration convergence criterion, [Documented, C23] 0.1% in the antiproton analysis, not stated for He) or a forward-fold of a parametric Phi(R; theta) when the aim is a spectral model parameter.
- **Statistics:** binned Poisson likelihood with Barlow-Beeston/Conway finite-template nuisances for template fits; exact (Garwood) intervals for low-count bins (the top decade has few events per bin, whatever the total; counts per bin are [Unknown/needs input]). No Gaussian errors or Wilks near boundary or low counts without toys.
- **Covariance:** full statistical-plus-systematic covariance across bins (unfolding correlates adjacent bins); scale/resolution systematics enter through migration, not as a final-value rescale; correlations across bins and time are declared, never "added in quadrature with unknown correlations".

## 7. Systematics ledger (one row per effect; each with representation and `applied_via`)

[Documented, C60, first 30 months He, S07] The published He ledger families, with sizes as the paper gives them (context for what must be covered, not a prediction for another period): trigger efficiency (below 0.2% under 100 GV, 1% at 3 TV); geomagnetic cutoff factor 1.0-1.4 (below 0.1%); acceptance after data/MC correction (below 1% under 200 GV, 1.4% at 3 TV); inelastic interactions (1% under 100 GV, 2% at 3 TV); 3He/4He composition (0.3%); rigidity resolution function, core varied 5% and tail amplitude 10% (below 1% under 400 GV, 3.5% at 3 TV); two unfolding procedures (0.25% at 2 GV, 0.4% at 3 TV); rigidity scale from tracker misalignment and the field map (below 0.6% to 100 GV, 6% at 3 TV); bin-width changes by factors of 2 and 4.

Required families for the new analysis, each with a named variation basis: trigger and selection efficiency (including factorization bias), acceptance/material, interaction cross-section model, background and charge-confusion template and tail, rigidity scale and resolution, response/unfolding prior and iteration count, template statistics, cutoff model and factor, livetime and time dependence, MC statistics and transport model.

Rigidity scale: [Documented, C64] the review reports the scale as the largest systematic at the highest energies (a coherent inner-tracker shift under 0.5 micrometre can shift it by 10% at 1 TV), measured in flight from E/p of e+/- in 72 bins over 2-300 GeV, with a time-dependent correction over four intervals in the first seven years and an overall accuracy of +-0.034 TV^-1. [General method] The effect on the flux is the scale shift times the local spectral index, and it enters the top bins as a correlated, large systematic. Do not tune the scale to remove a spectral feature (hardening near 200 GV is a published feature, [Documented, C81], so scale tuning would be circular).

Anti-double-counting: a calibration (alignment, TRD/TOF gain) removes its effect; only its residual is a systematic. Each effect gets one `effect_id` and one category (veto/calibration/efficiency/acceptance/exposure/livetime/response); `response.includes` and `estimator.applies` must not overlap (exposure already holds acceptance and livetime).

## 8. Validation (metric, threshold, action)

| Check | Metric | Trigger/action [Proposal; thresholds to be set from the size of the effect on the flux] |
|---|---|---|
| MC closure of unfolding | Pull of unfolded vs truth, per bin | Exceeds statistical + declared bias budget: change regularization/binning or add an uncertainty |
| Reweighted and distorted-spectrum stress | As above, with a kink at several hundred GV | Bias above the budget: forward-fold instead |
| Resolution and tail validation | Data vs MC L1-L8 vs L2-L9 rigidity, tail amplitude in the high-R bins | Disagreement beyond the assigned 10% tail variation: enlarge tail systematic; no Gaussian-core shortcut |
| Rigidity scale | E/p shift (e+/-) and L1-L9 vs inner-tracker shift | Shift outside budget: re-derive per interval |
| Charge confusion at high R | Data-driven tail rate vs MC | Disagreement: data-driven estimate becomes nominal |
| Stability | Flux vs time period, detector region, geometry, track configuration (L1-L9 vs L2-L8 or L1-L8), loose/tight charge cut, cutoff factor | Drift beyond the stat error of the split: time-dependent systematic or period split |
| Cross-check to published | Ratio to S07/S11 flux in overlap | Plausibility only; agreement does not validate (invariant 10). Where the periods differ, differences are expected at low rigidity because of solar modulation |
| Combined-efficiency closure | MC truth efficiency vs product of measured factors, per bin | Product-vs-joint difference becomes a systematic |
| Template fits | Pulls, toy p-value, fit bias across signal strength, alternate templates | Failure: change template model or add nuisance |
