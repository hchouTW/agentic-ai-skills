# 27-day positron-to-electron ratio, 5-50 GV, solar-cycle span, with a one-year periodicity search

## 1. Estimand and what is already public

- **Estimand [Proposal]:** R_k(x) = Φ_{e+}(x,k) / Φ_{e-}(x,k), the ratio of the two fluxes in the same 27-day bin k and the same lepton-variable bin x, at the top of the instrument. It is not the positron fraction e+/(e+ + e-). The two are monotonic transforms of each other, but the errors and the cancellation arguments differ, so state which one is the published quantity. The target here is the time dependence of the ratio, and any modulation model or drift interpretation is a separate model step.
- **Variable [Unknown/needs input, decide first]:** 5-50 GV is a rigidity range. AMS published daily e+ and e- in rigidity over 1.00-41.9 GV [Documented, C40, C41, abstract level], so 50 GV is above the upper edge of that public daily range. The Bartels-rotation lepton paper bins in energy, 1-50 GeV [Documented, C39, C48]. Time-integrated e+/e- fluxes also bin in energy [C24, C25]. Choose either rigidity or energy for the bins and say so. I propose rigidity as the primary variable, because the charge sign and the cutoff are tied to |R|, and an ECAL-energy cross-check. For |Z| = 1 leptons, p c = |R| (GeV) up to the electron mass, but the response differs: energy has a bremsstrahlung tail and rigidity has a Tracker tail. Do not mix the two without converting through the response.
- **Prior public results [Documented, abstract level]:**
  - 27-day variations recur in the daily p, He, e- and e+ fluxes, with strength that depends on time and rigidity [C36, C37, C40].
  - Positron variations are similar to proton and unlike electron; positrons are modulated more than protons below about 7 GV [C41]. An electron/positron hysteresis appears below about 8.5 GV [C40, C41]. These two thresholds are different and must not be merged. Both fall inside the proposed range, so the low edge of 5 GV sits in the regime where the e+ and e- time dependences differ strongly.
  - The e+/e- ratio shows a smooth transition across the 2013 polarity reversal over 830 +- 30 days [C39, C89, C90; the logistic fit itself is the review's, C90].
  - Months-scale e+ and e- structures are not visible in the ratio [C39].
  - AMS used a wavelet analysis with 95% levels from red-noise surrogates for the daily proton flux [C45, C52]. I did not find an AMS statement of a trial-factor correction, so I do not claim one. No AMS e+/e- one-year periodicity result is in the ledger, so this is a design for a new search, not a reproduction.

## 2. Design

### 2.1 Time binning
- **27-day bins aligned to Bartels rotations [General method / Proposal].** This follows the Bartels-rotation convention in the AMS lepton and nuclei products [C48, C49]. It is not a rule for other analyses; the deuteron and Li analyses used 108-day blocks [C27, C29].
- Bin edges must not straddle hardware-era, calibration or reconstruction-version boundaries [General method]. Fix the binning, the rigidity bins and the periodicity band before looking at the time dependence, and log any later change [Proposal].
- **Sampling:** a one-year period spans about 13.5 bins, and the Nyquist period is 54 days. A 365-day signal is therefore resolvable in principle, but only with about 13 samples per cycle. The solar rotation (27 d) is also exactly the bin size. The 27-day signal is not a periodicity that survives 27-day binning; it is aliased or averaged. This search targets the annual band only. Daily bins would be needed to treat 27 and 13.5 days.
- Span: the whole data-taking period available to you, covering the cycle-24 minimum, the 2013 reversal, the cycle-25 rise and the maximum. Number of bins is about 14 yr x 13.5 = roughly 190, subject to data gaps [Unknown/needs input].

### 2.2 Rigidity bins
- The ratio needs enough positrons per bin per 27 days. Positrons are a small fraction of the lepton sample at the top of the range [General method], so the high-rigidity bins will have low counts. Rather than quote a count, I propose a rule: merge rigidity bins until the least-populated bin has enough positrons for a toy-validated Poisson-exact interval (invariant 6); if a bin cannot meet that, restrict the range where time resolution is supported and say so.
- **[Proposal]** A coarse set, for example three bands, 5-10, 10-20, 20-50 GV (my illustrative choice, not an AMS binning). These straddle the 7 and 8.5 GV behaviours, and the search is repeated per band. The full 5-50 GV band-integrated ratio is a fourth, high-statistics series. Bin widths should be at least a few times the rigidity resolution; resolution and tail values are [Unknown/needs input] and not quoted.
- Cutoff: at 5 GV the lowest bin is the most cutoff sensitive (see 2.4).

### 2.3 Flux and ratio construction per bin
For each bin (k,x) and sign s:
- Φ_s(k,x) = (N_s - b_s) / (E_s(k,x) ΔR), with E_s = Σ A_eff ε T_live × cutoff transmission accumulated inside the bin [General method]. This is a ratio of fluxes with different counts, never an average of fluxes of bins with unequal exposure.
- A_eff, ε and T_live are distinct and not multiplied as if independent (invariant 3). Whatever is corrected once (veto, calibration, efficiency or response) is not corrected again.
- N_s comes from a template fit per bin: positive and negative samples fitted simultaneously, with templates for correct-sign lepton, correct-sign proton (antiproton for the negative sample) and charge-confusion lepton, in the discriminant plane (for example TRD and a charge-confusion estimator, as in the published time-integrated analyses [C24, C26, S04/S05 scope]). The positron template origin (electron data at lower energy, MC above) is the published practice of those papers and a choice, not a rule.
- Charge confusion is a Tracker tail effect: a migration M(e+ <- e-), M(e- <- e+) vs rigidity, validated on the tail (invariant 4). It grows with rigidity, so it matters most in the 20-50 GV band. A time-dependent migration (alignment or gain drift) is a risk for the periodicity search.
- Migration across rigidity bins is unfolded, or forward-folded, independently in each time bin [as AMS did in daily/Bartels products, C43, C46, C49]. Efficiency, acceptance and collection time are evaluated per bin.
- The ratio is formed after the unfolded fluxes: R = Φ_{e+}/Φ_{e-}. Where migration is small, the alternative is a ratio of counts, but only after demonstrating that the response cancels.

### 2.4 Exposure and geomagnetic cutoff
- Exposure per bin from livetime-weighted seconds above the cutoff, normal detector conditions, within a stated zenith angle, outside the South Atlantic Anomaly [Documented, C48, C51; the daily and Bartels analyses differ in pointing cut and cutoff choice, C54, C56, so no single set is the standard].
- Compute transmission per bin, and per charge sign, because opposite-sign trajectories are not mirror images in a given geographic direction. Vary the cutoff model and safety factor as a systematic, per bin and per sign; the lowest bin (5 GV) is the most sensitive. The cutoff depends on orbit, direction and time, and the ISS orbit precession and the seasonal orientation introduce the periodicities listed in section 4.
- Common intervals and common cutoff treatment for numerator and denominator.

### 2.5 Systematics and covariance
- Classify each nuisance for the ratio per the ratio blueprint (charged-cosmic-rays reference): common acceptance and livetime largely cancel if the fiducial and exposure are identical, but this is to be verified per effect. Trigger and selection efficiency are partial, charge confusion and background templates are sign-specific, the rigidity scale and unfolding are correlated only if the same response is used, and cutoff transmission differs per sign.
- Split into time-independent (persistent: template shape, cross-section model, scale) and time-dependent (efficiencies evaluated per bin, added as independent per bin, as in the published daily proton practice, C44), plus slowly varying terms correlated between neighbouring bins (gain, alignment, temperature drift) [General method].
- Build one covariance over (time, rigidity) with a documented ordering; a Kronecker product is valid only if the correlation factorizes, which must be tested. Check with `scripts/validate_covariance.py`. Statistical errors are independent between bins of disjoint events. The numerator and denominator share events with proton background and shared systematics, so carry that covariance and do not combine unknown correlations in quadrature.
- AMS's correlation model across days or rotations is [Unknown/needs input]; it was not found in the text read.
- Normalizing to a reference period cancels only the persistent systematics and correlates all periods with the reference, so avoid it or carry that covariance.

## 3. Periodicity search for one year

### 3.1 Pre-registered definition [General method / Proposal]
- Observable: R_k (and, for the cross-checks, e+ and e- separately, and the controls below).
- Search band: set before unblinding. For a one-year question I propose a narrow band around 365 d for the targeted test, plus a wider global scan over periods from the 54-day Nyquist limit to roughly one third of the span, to get the global significance and to show where the annual bin sits among other periods. The look-elsewhere range is stated explicitly.
- Test statistic: a generalized (floating-mean, error-weighted) Lomb-Scargle power or an equivalent weighted sinusoid fit with fitted phase and amplitude, because bins have unequal exposure, unequal errors and gaps. A wavelet analysis like AMS's [C45, C52] is an alternative for time-localized power, but a one-year period over a 13-14 yr span has only a few independent cycles and a wide cone of influence, so wavelet significance would be weak at that scale.
- Significance threshold and wording (candidate, excess, evidence) fixed beforehand.

### 3.2 Removing the solar-cycle trend (the central difficulty)
- The ratio changes slowly, with the 11-year cycle and the 2013 polarity reversal (a transition of about 830 d [C90, C89]). That trend has red-noise power at all long periods, and leaks into one year. Remove or model it without absorbing a true annual component.
- **[Proposal]:** fit simultaneously a smooth trend and a sinusoid, not detrend-then-search. The trend is a low-order spline or Gaussian-process term with a knot spacing much longer than one year (knots at least about 2 yr) plus the logistic reversal form [C90] as an alternative model, and the annual term is a sinusoid with free amplitude and phase. Fit with the full covariance. Check that results are stable against the knot spacing; a trend flexible enough to follow a one-year wiggle makes the test insensitive.
- Alternative: use the ratio of two nearby rigidity bands, or the ratio to a control (for example p or He), to cancel the common trend, but a species ratio introduces a different cancellation assumption and is a cross-check only.
- Sensitivity: compute the minimum detectable annual amplitude by injecting sinusoids into the real series and into toys before any claim of a null. A non-detection is reported as an upper limit on the amplitude with the injection efficiency, not as "no periodicity".

### 3.3 Calibrating the significance
- Toys must reproduce: the real 27-day sampling and gaps, the per-bin exposure, the per-bin errors, the red-noise (lag-1 autoregressive) structure of the solar-cycle residuals (the surrogate approach AMS used for wavelets, C52), and the correlated systematics (draw from the full covariance). A Wilks or single-frequency p-value is not acceptable (invariant 6 and the time-dependent reference).
- Report local and global p-value over the declared band (look-elsewhere / trial factor, Gross and Vitells approach as a method reference) and the surrogate-noise model dependence. The choice of noise model (white vs red vs solar-activity-correlated) changes the significance and is carried as a systematic on the significance.
- With few independent annual cycles, the result is sensitive to a single anomalous year; do a leave-one-year-out (jackknife) check.

## 4. Why an annual periodicity can be spurious: required controls

An apparent one-year signal in e+/e- may come from exposure, orbit, season or detector state rather than the heliosphere or the sources [General method]. Controls:
- Run the identical search on: the exposure (collection-time) series per rigidity and per sign; the livetime and trigger-efficiency series; the TRD gain/temperature and ECAL stability series; the charge-confusion estimate; and the same ratio for a control pair that has no expected annual physical modulation (for example a pure matter pair, or the p and He fluxes, which AMS shows to have nearly identical time variation [C09, C10, C38]). A coincident peak in a control is a systematic, not a discovery.
- Sources of annual structure to consider: the ISS orbit precession (period near 60 days, not annual) and its beta-angle/solar-array seasonality, which change the geomagnetic exposure and any thermal effect on the detector; the seasonal variation of the Sun-Earth distance; and the Earth's heliographic latitude excursion, which is physical and has an annual period and could modulate the particles of opposite sign differently through the drift (charge-sign-dependent modulation, general method). The last item is a physical effect to be reported as such if the controls are clean, but it is a model interpretation, not part of the measurement.
- Rigidity and sign dependence: a real heliospheric effect should show a rigidity-dependent amplitude and phase consistent between e+ and e- with the sign of the drift effect; an instrumental effect would likely be rigidity-independent or appear in both signs the same way. Report amplitude and phase per band and the e+ versus e- series separately, since the ratio can hide structures in the individual fluxes [C39] and the common one-year component may cancel in the ratio.
- Check the sensitivity to different polarity epochs (before and after 2013 reversal) by searching each epoch separately; modulation drift effects change sign across the reversal, so a physical annual term may change sign and phase while an instrumental one would not.
- Time lag and hysteresis: e+ and e- respond with different time lags to solar activity below about 8.5 GV [C40, C41], which can produce a detrending artifact in the ratio; test with lagged versions of the trend.

## 5. Validation and closure
- MC closure of the unfolding and templates per time bin with an injected annual modulation of known amplitude through the full chain (including the response and cutoff).
- Time-stability metrics with triggers: an abundant control species rate or an efficiency flat vs time after livetime and cutoff correction; a drift beyond tolerance adds a period split, a scale factor with uncertainty or a systematic (the tolerance is derived from the effect on the amplitude, not "reasonable agreement").
- Alternatives: loose/tight lepton selection; alternative track configurations; data-driven vs MC templates; energy vs rigidity variable; different bin merges; ECAL-energy-based ratio; period splits.
- Each check names the failure it targets and the deviation that triggers a method change.

## 6. Reporting
- Per-bin R_k with the full covariance matrix, exposures, and response products, so others can refit; a single "amplitude" number hides the time dependence.
- Annual amplitude and phase per band with confidence interval (profile or toy-calibrated), local and global significance, injection sensitivity, and the control-series results. State the band, trial range, trend model and noise model.
- Separate measurement from interpretation (drift modulation, charge-sign-dependent transport, source models).
- Do not extend AMS public results beyond their scope: the daily results cover 1.00-41.9 GV in rigidity and the Bartels-rotation lepton results are in energy; the 41.9-50 GV part of the requested range has no public daily counterpart in the ledger.

## 7. Unknowns and inputs that change the design

- Rigidity or energy as the bin variable; number of positrons per bin per 27 days at 20-50 GV (decides whether a 3-band split is feasible).
- Per-period livetime, exposure, efficiency, calibration and charge-confusion records; hardware eras and reconstruction versions within the span (bin boundaries).
- The systematic correlation model across time bins (not in the public text read) and whether you need a published-style trial-factor treatment (not described by AMS in the text read).
- Whether the annual band and trial range were fixed before looking at the data.
