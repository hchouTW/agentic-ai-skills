# Limit setting for a rare antinucleus search in AMS-02 with zero observed events

Species is left open (antihelium, antideuteron or heavier). Everything below is a [Proposal] or [General method] unless tagged otherwise. Public-evidence boundary: no peer-reviewed AMS-02 antihelium paper was found in INSPIRE-HEP (checked 2026-09-20; C14, search-limited), and no AMS antideuteron search paper was found (C19). The only AMS-authored antihelium result in the index is the 1999 AMS-01 limit (S22). I therefore give no AMS candidate counts, acceptances, livetimes, cuts or systematic sizes. All such inputs are symbolic or [Unknown/needs input]. Any user-supplied numbers should be labeled as such.

## 1. Estimand first

Decide what the limit is on before choosing a construction. The options are:
- a flux upper limit `Φ_UL(R)` in rigidity bins;
- an integrated flux limit above a rigidity threshold;
- a ratio limit relative to the matter partner (`anti-X / X`). A ratio limit does not automatically cancel acceptance or efficiency. Classify each nuisance as correlated, partial or independent, and show it.

State the signal topology and region up front, with charge `|Z|` and sign, and the mass hypothesis in `(R, β, |Z|)`.
- Sign comes from the Tracker and magnet only.
- `|Z|` comes from several subsystems.
- Mass comes from `R` and `β`.

Fix the signal region and the reporting thresholds before looking at data. The limit is a statement about a flux model (a spectral shape assumption within each bin). State that assumption, because the acceptance reweighting depends on it.

## 2. Blinding and predefinition

1. Mask the signal region in plots, logs and optimization. Name who may unblind and when.
2. Predefine the selection, the rigidity bins or range (this fixes the trial factor), the confidence level (95% one-sided is conventional), the construction, and the candidate diagnostics.
3. Predefine the reporting ladder: candidate, excess, evidence, discovery. A zero-count result is the limit branch. If events are later seen, a candidate is not an excess and an excess is not a discovery. Never convert an unpublished candidate report into a discovery claim.
4. Compute the expected sensitivity and its bands from background-only toys before unblinding. The limit is then judged against its expectation.

## 3. Background model, which decides the construction

Zero observed events does not mean zero expected background. The relevant background `b` is:
- charge-sign confusion of the abundant same-`|Z|` matter (e.g. He → anti-He) through Tracker tails and bad hit assignment;
- `|Z|` confusion from fragmentation and charge-changing interactions;
- secondary tracks;
- wrong mass from `β` errors;
- interactions producing antiparticles.

Invariant: confusion is a tail phenomenon. Do not estimate it from the Gaussian core of the rigidity resolution. Require data-driven tail validation.
- Use abundant matter samples under looser cuts to measure the sign-flip probability versus rigidity.
- Extrapolate to the tight selection with the extrapolation assumptions tested.
- Cross-check with redundant signed-rigidity fits (different layer subsets, upper and lower segments) and hit-pattern checks.
- Run subsystem `|Z|` consistency checks.

Fill the background ledger for each component (origin, entry mechanism, control region and its signal contamination, estimator, transfer model, closure test, nuisances and correlations). Use a migration-matrix estimator for charge-sign confusion. Because the tail extrapolation is the weakest link, `b` is an estimate with an uncertainty, not an exactly known number.

## 4. Choose the construction, and say why

Facts for `n_obs = 0` [General method]:

| Case | Limit on signal counts at 95% CL |
|---|---|
| `b = 0` | `N_UL = -ln(0.05) ≈ 3.00` (exact Poisson) |
| Known `b > 0`, classical construction | `-ln(0.05) - b`. This shrinks with `b`, can reach zero, and is not a result once `b` reaches about 3. |
| Known `b > 0`, CLs | `-ln(0.05)`, independent of `b`. This is a different construction. CLs is a conservative convention, not a confidence-level statement. |
| Flat-prior Bayesian, `n = 0` | Also about 3.00, but it answers a different question. State the prior. |

Do not present `-ln α` as the classical limit for any `b`. Do not use `S/√B`, Wilks, Gaussian errors or asymptotic formulae at zero counts on a boundary.

Recommended design [Proposal]:
- **Primary:** a forward-folded binned or counting Poisson likelihood with nuisance parameters, and a limit constructed with toys (CLs, or Feldman-Cousins if a two-sided or unified approach is wanted). Report which one and why. Report the expected-sensitivity band, for example the median and ±1/±2σ of the limit under background-only toys. Also show the observed limit against it.
- **Nuisances** (profiled or marginalized, with the treatment stated): tail probability / background normalization `b`, efficiency, exposure, and the energy/rigidity scale. Use auxiliary measurements (control-region counts, calibration), not arbitrary priors, unless the analysis is explicitly Bayesian.
- **Cross-check:** `scripts/poisson_diagnostics.py upper-limit --n 0 --b <B> --cl 0.95` gives the classical limit for a known `B`. If it reports zero or negative, that is not a result. Fall back to CLs/FC with toys. The script handles known background only and does not compute expected sensitivity. Record the seed, toy count, CL and script version next to any toy-based number.
- **Coverage:** check coverage across a scan of the true mean (discrete-distribution coverage oscillates with the mean). A single-mean check is a diagnostic, not proof.
- **Search range:** if scanning several rigidity bins or mass hypotheses, give local and global significance with a trial factor. For a limit, the corresponding point is that the per-bin limits are not independent, and the combined one needs a declared combination rule.

## 5. Converting the count limit into a physical limit

`Φ_UL = N_UL / (E · Δx)`, where `E` is the exposure in m² sr s and `Δx` is the rigidity (or energy) bin width.

- Do not double count. Exposure already contains acceptance, livetime and geomagnetic transmission: `E_j = A_eff,j · T_j` per bin.
- `A_eff` is `A_geo` times the average selection efficiency for the signal species. It is built from a generation record (surface, solid angle, generated spectrum, truth definition of acceptance, weights, material model version) using signal-species MC (anti-X), with the tail behavior and hadronic-interaction model checked.
- Use conditional efficiencies. Do not multiply marginal efficiencies as if independent. Show factorization and ordering closure, and treat Fréchet bounds as floors, not estimates.
- Material interactions matter for antinuclei. The survival probability of an anti-nucleus through material, and its annihilation or interaction cross-section, is a species-specific correction. It has large model uncertainty and no measured data to validate it, so a separate nuisance is needed and the cross-section model is varied. Do not apply scale factors measured on matter species to the antiparticle without justification.
- Geomagnetic transmission and the cutoff safety factor must be defined for the signal rigidity range (a rigidity-dependent, rigidity-to-`Z/A`-aware exposure; antinuclei of different `A` and `Z` are different in rigidity-to-energy conversions).
- Bin-center prescription: state how the flux limit position in the bin is defined.
- Propagate exposure uncertainty as a nuisance, with its correlation to the background model. Because a larger efficiency uncertainty weakens the limit, say whether the quoted limit includes the exposure uncertainty. Quote whether it is a limit on flux, on integrated flux, or on a ratio.
- For a ratio, use `Φ_UL(anti-X)/Φ(X)` with the matter flux measured with its own acceptance and efficiency, and show which pieces cancel.

## 6. Flux-model dependence

A rigidity-dependent limit assumes a within-bin spectral shape. Two ways to handle it:
- (a) Quote the limit per bin with the assumed shape, and the sensitivity to that shape as a systematic.
- (b) Quote one integrated limit above a threshold using a stated reference spectrum, and say that the limit is conditional on it.

Do not compare directly with a model prediction unless the same rigidity variable (or `Z/A`-aware conversion) is used. State `Z` and `A` in every energy or kinetic-energy-per-nucleon conversion. `scripts/ams_kinematics.py` can do the arithmetic.

## 7. Validation and failure modes

- Expected-sensitivity and coverage studies before unblinding.
- Injection tests: inject signal-like events into data-like samples; verify recovery and that the limit is set at the intended CL.
- Closure of the combined efficiency chain in MC.
- Abundant-sample validation of the sign-flip tail, with a stated metric and trigger (a "reasonable agreement" is not validation). For example, state the discrepancy that would add an uncertainty or change the method.
- Common failure modes to avoid: Gaussian-core charge confusion; quoting `-ln α` as a classical limit with `b > 0`; negative classical limits presented as results; exposure applied twice; ignoring antiparticle-specific interaction cross-section uncertainty; no blinding; tuning cuts on the signal region; candidate reported as discovery.
