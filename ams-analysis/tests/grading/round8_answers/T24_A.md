# Limit setting for a rare antinucleus search in AMS-02 with zero observed events

## Status and boundary

- [Documented, C14, C19; search-limited, checked 2026-09-20] No peer-reviewed AMS-02 antihelium paper and no AMS antideuteron search paper was found in INSPIRE-HEP. The only AMS-authored antihelium result in the index is the 1999 AMS-01 limit (S22), which is historical. The design below is therefore a [Proposal], not a description of AMS practice. I do not quote AMS candidate counts, cuts, livetimes, acceptances or systematic sizes, because no source gives them. This index was last verified 2026-09-20. I did not browse, so I make no claim about currency.
- Inputs that change the answer: which species (antideuteron, antihelium-3 or -4), the rigidity range, whether the work is public or internal, and whether the signal region is blinded. I proceed symbolically. User-supplied numbers would be labeled as such.

## 1. Estimand (fixed before any algorithm)

- Primary target: a flux upper limit on the antinucleus in rigidity bins, `Φ_UL(R) = N_UL / (E(R) · ΔR)`. [General method]
- Optional second target: a ratio limit relative to the matter partner (e.g. anti-He/He). A ratio does not cancel acceptance, efficiency or systematics automatically. Classify each nuisance as correlated, partial or independent and verify. A flux limit is cleaner as the headline.
- Define the signal by observables: sign from the Tracker/magnet only; `|Z|` from multiple subsystems (Tracker, TOF, RICH); mass from `R` and `β`. TRD, TOF, ECAL and RICH do not give charge sign.
- Convert between rigidity, momentum and kinetic energy per nucleon with explicit `Z` and `A`, using `p = |Z| R / c`.

## 2. Zero-count statistics [General method]

- With `n_obs = 0` and `b = 0`, the one-sided 95% CL Poisson upper limit on signal counts is `N_UL = -ln(0.05) ≈ 3.00`. This is the exact classical bound, and `scripts/poisson_diagnostics.py upper-limit --n 0 --b 0 --cl 0.95` reproduces it.
- For `n_obs = 0` and known expected background `b > 0`, the two constructions differ:
  - The classical limit is `-ln(0.05) - b`. It shrinks as `b` grows, can reach zero or go negative, and is then not a result.
  - The CLs limit is `b`-independent at `3.00`. CLs is a conservative convention, not a confidence-level statement.
  - A flat-prior Bayesian limit also gives about 3.00 but answers a different question.
  - Pick one construction in advance and state it. Never call the classical zero-count limit "independent of background".
- Recommended primary construction: Feldman-Cousins or CLs, with toys, using the expected background from the tail estimate in section 3. Quote both observed and expected sensitivity, with bands. For zero observed events the expected sensitivity depends on `b`, so a "3.00" headline is only valid in the `b ≈ 0` case.
- Do not use `S/√B`, Wilks or asymptotic (Cowan) formulae. The problem is a boundary problem with a few counts. Validate coverage with toys, and scan the true mean because Poisson coverage oscillates.
- Trial factor: if the search spans several rigidity bins or several species hypotheses, predefine the range. Per-bin limits do not need a trial factor, but a combined significance claim would.
- A limit is not a discovery claim. The reporting levels (candidate, excess, evidence, discovery) and their thresholds are declared in advance. Zero events means no candidate.

## 3. What "zero events" actually means: background and tail estimate

Zero observed events is not zero expected background. The leading backgrounds are tail phenomena, so Gaussian-core resolution estimates are rejected.

- Charge-sign confusion of the abundant same-`|Z|` matter (e.g. He → anti-He) through Tracker tails and bad hit assignment.
- `|Z|` confusion from fragmentation and charge-changing interactions, and secondary tracks.
- Wrong mass from `β` errors.
- Interactions producing antiparticles.

Estimator [Proposal]:

- Measure the sign-flip tail probability as a function of rigidity on abundant matter samples (He and protons, under looser cuts). Extrapolate to the tight selection and test the extrapolation assumptions explicitly. Use redundant signed-rigidity fits (different layer subsets, upper/lower segments), hit-pattern checks, and subsystem charge consistency before and after the magnet.
- Express the result as a charge-sign migration matrix versus rigidity, together with its uncertainty. Put the expected background `b(R)` into the background ledger: origin, entry mechanism, control region and its signal contamination, transfer model, closure test, nuisances and correlations.
- If `b` is small but not negligible, the extrapolation uncertainty dominates the limit. Carry it as a nuisance, not a fixed number.

## 4. Blinding and predefinition

- Define the signal region (sign, `|Z|`, `R`, `β`/mass window) before looking at data, and mask it in plots, logs and optimization.
- Fix the diagnostic checklist (event display, hit pattern, residuals, partial-fit consistency, subsystem estimators) before unblinding.
- Fix the limit construction, CL, test statistic, nuisance treatment and bins before unblinding.
- Compute expected sensitivity and coverage before examining the signal region.
- State who may unblind and when.
- Do not tune cuts, templates or bins repeatedly on the same data. Record iterations.

## 5. Exposure, from counts to flux

`E(R) = Σ_t A_eff(t) · T_live(t) × geomagnetic transmission`, per rigidity bin and for the signal species.

- Acceptance, efficiency, exposure and livetime are distinct. Do not multiply `A` and `T` again if `E` already contains them. Do not correct the same effect twice (veto, calibration, efficiency, response). If the response includes inefficiency, do not also apply it in `A`.
- Efficiencies are conditional, `ε_1 · ε_2|1 · ...`, measured on populations that are not biased. Do not multiply marginals. Two selections only bound the joint efficiency (Fréchet bounds). Do an ordering closure and a combined-efficiency closure in MC.
- Signal efficiency cannot be measured on data for the rare species. Use MC for the antinucleus with a documented generation record: surface, solid angle, spectrum, species, physics list and geometry. Validate on the abundant partner with data/MC scale factors. Do not move scale factors across species or periods without justification.
- Hadronic interaction cross-sections for antinuclei are poorly constrained. The survival probability through upstream material and in-detector interactions is a separate correction with its own cross-section model variation. Treat it as a leading systematic.
- Geomagnetic transmission and the cutoff safety factor determine which rigidities count as primary. Livetime must be quality-approved. Check time dependence of acceptance, such as after hardware changes.
- Avoid circularity if the generated spectrum is reweighted to a hypothesized signal spectrum. The limit is spectrum-dependent. Quote the assumed shape.

## 6. Likelihood and nuisances [Proposal]

- Binned Poisson in reconstructed bins `i`: `μ_i = Σ_j R_ij A_j T_j Φ_j Δx_j + b_i`, with the signal strength as the parameter of interest. Nuisances: tail probability, efficiency, exposure and cross-section model, each entering through auxiliary measurements (control-region counts, calibrations), not arbitrary priors.
- Profiling versus marginalization versus hybrid: state which. Check that nuisance constraints are not derived from the fitted data.
- With zero observed events the dependence on the nuisances enters through `b` and `E`. Propagate the uncertainty on `E` (including correlation with the background model) into `Φ_UL`.
- Method choice by estimand: a forward-folded counting likelihood with a constructed interval, not unfolding. Migration is a nuisance here, not a result.

## 7. Cross-checks and validation, with triggers

- Signal injection into MC and into abundant-sample data at the level of the limit, to confirm recovery of the injected strength and the correct coverage.
- Coverage toys, scanning the true mean, with seed, toy count and CL recorded.
- Alternate track fits and hit patterns, loose versus tight selection, period and geometry splits, charge-sign splits, orthogonal identification chains.
- Metric-based triggers, not "reasonable agreement". For example, if tight versus loose selection tail extrapolations disagree beyond their stated uncertainty, or a data/MC tail ratio in the abundant sample exceeds a predefined tolerance, widen the tail-probability nuisance or change the method.

## 8. Reporting

- Per-bin and combined limits, with confidence level, one-sided versus two-sided, construction, observed and expected limits with bands, and the exposure and its uncertainty. State the signal spectrum assumption and the species and rigidity range.
- Qualify the headline: the limit is on the stated species, in the stated rigidity range, for the stated selection. It is not a statement about other species.
- Do not compare to the historical AMS-01 limit (S22) or to third-party theory without stating differences in species, range, data set and construction. Comparison is plausibility only.

## 9. Unknowns and required inputs

- [Unknown/needs input] Collaboration-internal tail measurements, signal efficiency, exposure, livetime, selection and run rules. Supply symbolic `N_UL`, `E`, `ε`, `A`, `b` until the user provides them.
- Questions that would change the design: which species and range; public versus internal; blinded or not; what control samples exist for the sign-flip tail; whether `b` is known or estimated with an uncertainty.
