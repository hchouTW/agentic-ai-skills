## Design: 27-day-bin e+/e- ratio, 5-50 GV, solar cycle, one-year periodicity search

Public-reasoning design only. Everything below is [General method] or [Proposal] unless it carries a claim ID. AMS internal cuts, livetimes, efficiencies, systematic sizes and any trial-factor practice are [Unknown/needs input]; none are supplied here.

### 0. Estimand and the two choices to fix first

- **Estimand [Proposal]:** R_k(ρ) = Φ_e+,k(ρ) / Φ_e-,k(ρ), the positron-to-electron flux ratio (not the positron fraction e+/(e+ + e-)) in rigidity bins over 5-50 GV, in each 27-day bin k across the solar cycle. The ratio is a different estimand from the fraction, so state which is reported. The fraction would be a monotonic transform but has different error propagation.
- **Bin variable [Proposal]:** use rigidity. The Tracker measures rigidity, and the sign comes only from Tracker and magnet (invariant 2). This also matches the daily e+ and e- papers, which bin in rigidity over 1.00-41.9 GV (C40, C41). The Bartels-rotation lepton paper and the time-integrated lepton fluxes bin in energy instead (C48, C24, C25). If energy is used, state it and give the E-to-R relation.
- **Range caveat:** the documented daily lepton rigidity range tops out at 41.9 GV (C40, C41), so 41.9-50 GV has no documented AMS time-resolved precedent in the ledger. Treat it as your own extension and check the counts there.
- **27-day bins:** a Bartels rotation is a fixed 27-day count, so bins aligned to rotations are comparable. AMS used rotation-length bins for its Bartels-rotation lepton and nuclei products (C48, C49), and used 108-day blocks for its deuteron and Li-isotope analyses (C27, C29). Those are choices of those analyses, not rules. Alignment does not remove exposure differences within a bin.
- **Fix before looking [Proposal]:** rigidity edges, bin length, the periodicity band and test statistic, the trend model and the control series. Record any later change.

### 1. Binning and statistics

- **Rigidity bins:** the number of bins is set by counts per 27-day bin in the least populated bin (the 50 GV end, where positrons are scarcest and the proton background is largest). If counts are low, merge rigidity bins or restrict the upper edge, and use Poisson-exact or toy-validated intervals rather than Gaussian errors (invariant 6). The ratio's error is dominated by the positron count.
- **Event counts:** I give no counts, because the ledger has no per-bin value. Counts per bin are [Unknown/needs input].
- **Time-bin edges:** they must not straddle a hardware-era or reconstruction-version boundary, since either breaks the response and templates.
- **Bin sampling and the year:** 365.25 d / 27 d is about 13.5 bins per cycle, which is enough to resolve a one-year period. The Nyquist period is 54 d. A 27-day recurrence (Sun rotation) sits at the sampling period and aliases. Periods shorter than 54 d, including the 27-day recurrence, are not resolvable with this binning, so state that the search is limited to periods above about 54 d. A search band of, say, 100-800 d is a [Proposal].

### 2. Per-bin measurement chain (positive and negative samples, same bins)

1. **Selection:** Tracker charge sign and |Z|=1, downward-going, quality time only. The cutoff condition is |R| above a safety factor times the geomagnetic cutoff. The documented practice is 1.2, varied 1.0-1.4 (C31, C27, C49), but it is one analysis's choice. At 5 GV the cutoff matters, and it is sign- and bin-dependent, so compute transmission per time bin and per sign, not once.
2. **Lepton identification:** TRD (Λ_TRD, proton-like vs lepton-like) and ECAL (shower shape, E/p), neither of which gives charge sign (invariant 2). Public AMS yield extraction used a template fit in the (Λ_TRD, Λ_CC) plane with correct-sign lepton, correct-sign proton and charge-confusion templates (C24 for positrons, C26 for electrons). The ECAL estimator was used to remove most hadronic background (C24, C26). A proposal for this analysis is to fit positive and negative samples simultaneously, sharing templates and charge-confusion parameters, in each rigidity and time bin.
3. **Backgrounds:** protons faking positrons (dominant, orders of magnitude above signal); antiprotons in the negative sample (small after ECAL selection per C26, but check it); charge confusion e- to e+ and e+ to e- as a Tracker tail (not the Gaussian core; invariant 4), modeled as a sign migration matrix per rigidity and validated on data tails. At 5-50 GV, validate the template shape per time bin or at least per period, because TRD gain and gas drift with time (see the calibration table).
4. **Unfolding or response:** the AMS time-resolved fluxes unfolded migration independently in each time bin (C43, C46, C49). Do the same, or show that migration is negligible at the chosen widths. Do not use the diagonal estimator without validation.
5. **Exposure per bin k:** Φ_k = (N_k - b_k) / (E_k ΔR), where E_k = Σ A_eff × T_live × transmission, accumulated in the bin. Never average fluxes of bins with different exposure. Livetime, acceptance, efficiency and exposure are kept distinct (invariant 3). The published rotation exposure uses livetime-weighted seconds above cutoff, in normal conditions, within 40 degrees of zenith and outside the South Atlantic Anomaly (C48). That is that analysis's definition.
6. **Efficiency drift:** correct each drift in one category only (veto, calibration, efficiency or response), never twice.

### 3. What cancels in the ratio (and what does not)

Do not assume cancellation (invariant 5). Classify per effect:

| Effect | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Livetime, time interval, quality-time and cutoff-scheme definitions | Common if identical bins and period; geomagnetic transmission differs by charge sign at low rigidity, so the 5 GV end needs per-sign cutoff handling |
| Geometric acceptance | Largely common with the same fiducial region; verify by MC |
| Trigger and selection efficiency | Partially correlated; measured per sign |
| Material interaction and survival | Differ for e+ and e- (annihilation, bremsstrahlung tails); vary per species |
| Rigidity scale and unfolding | Correlated only if the same response and scale are used |
| Charge confusion, proton contamination | Sign-specific; the proton background in the positron sample does not cancel |
| TRD/ECAL template shape | Partially shared; include per-bin template statistics |

Carry the numerator-denominator covariance, not independent errors: Var(R) ≈ R² [(σ_N/N)² + (σ_D/D)² − 2ρ σ_N σ_D/(N D)] to first order, with toys when counts are small. The positron and electron samples come from disjoint events, so the statistical error is independent, and the shared systematics are the correlated part.

### 4. Covariance across time and rigidity

- Statistical errors are independent between disjoint time bins and rigidity bins (apart from unfolding correlations).
- Classify each systematic as persistent (calibration, template shape model, cross-section model: correlated across all bins), slowly varying (neighbouring bins), or independent per bin. The ledger does not say how AMS correlates systematics across days or rotations (evidence gap in time-dependent-analysis), so the correlation model is [Unknown/needs input] and has to be built and justified by you.
- Build one covariance over (time, rigidity) with a documented ordering. Use a Kronecker product only if the correlation truly factorizes, and test that. Check positive semi-definiteness with `scripts/validate_covariance.py`. A pass shows self-consistency, not physical validity.
- If you normalize to a reference period, only the persistent part cancels, and the reference bin's statistical error then correlates all bins. Carry it.

### 5. Solar-cycle trend (the dominant signal)

Over a solar cycle the e+/e- ratio is expected to be time-dependent because charge-sign-dependent drift modulation changes with heliospheric polarity (a [General method] statement). AMS has reported a smooth e+/e- transition across the polarity reversal, 830 ± 30 days (C90 review fit; C39 and C89 for the S42 abstract and review restatement). Its midpoint delay depends on energy. This was an energy-binned result (C39), so do not reuse it for rigidity bins without saying so. It also reported positron variations similar to proton and unlike electron (C41, below about 7 GV positrons are modulated more than protons), and an electron-proton and electron-positron hysteresis below about 8.5 GV (C40, C41). These do not change your design, but they set expectations for the low end of 5-50 GV. Do not merge the 7 GV and 8.5 GV thresholds.

For the periodicity search, the long-term trend is a nuisance. Do not search the raw ratio, because unremoved red noise leaks into low frequencies and mimics annual power. Options:
- Fit a smooth trend (spline or low-order polynomial with a fixed knot spacing set in advance, well above one year, for example 2-3 years) and search the residuals. Fixing the knot spacing is a trade-off: a trend flexible enough to absorb real annual power removes signal. Quantify this by injecting a one-year sinusoid and recovering it.
- Or fit trend and sinusoid simultaneously and report the covariance between them.

A logistic form across the polarity reversal is the review's descriptive fit (C90) and a possible trend template, but it is one model choice, not a rule.

### 6. One-year periodicity search

**Definitions fixed in advance [Proposal]:**
- Observable: the detrended ratio (and, as separate series, the positron flux and the electron flux), per rigidity bin and for a merged 5-50 GV bin.
- Hypothesis A (single frequency, fixed 365.25 d): the pre-specified test. Fit R_k = trend + a cos(2πt_k/T) + b sin(2πt_k/T) by weighted least squares with the full covariance. The amplitude is a² + b², and the null is a = b = 0. The p-value has no look-elsewhere factor because the frequency is fixed.
- Hypothesis B (period band, for example 100-800 d): a weighted generalized Lomb-Scargle or equivalent periodogram. It needs a method that handles the exposure pattern, unequal errors and a floating mean. It carries a look-elsewhere effect: report local and global significance with trial factors (Gross and Vitells, S37), calibrated with toys.

**Significance calibration (toys, not Wilks):** do not use a single-frequency p-value for Hypothesis B and do not use Wilks. Generate surrogates that reproduce: the actual time sampling and exposures; the measured variance and lag-1 autocorrelation (the AMS wavelet analysis used lag-1 autoregressive red-noise surrogates for its 95% confidence level, C45 and C52, which is that analysis's choice); the correlated systematics; and the detrending step (apply the same trend removal to each surrogate, otherwise the null is wrong). Any trial-factor treatment AMS used is not described in the text read, so it is [Unknown/needs input].

**Alternative method:** a wavelet time-frequency analysis, as AMS used for the daily proton flux (C45, C52). Its edge effects are large at periods near a year in a 15-year series of this length. It is a cross-check, not a replacement.

### 7. Controls (a one-year signal is very likely a non-physical effect until shown otherwise)

Annual periodicity is where instrument, orbit and Earth-Sun effects accumulate: ISS orbit and beta-angle cycles, seasonal attitude and exposure, geomagnetic-cutoff transmission, Earth-Sun distance, and detector temperature. Apply the identical search to:
- the exposure time series and the quality-time fraction per bin;
- the efficiency and acceptance series;
- an abundant control species (proton flux over the same rigidity range, or electrons alone), which tests common-mode effects;
- the positron-only and electron-only fluxes, because a ratio can cancel a common annual term that is present in each flux. An annual term in the numerator and denominator that cancels is not evidence against a ratio signal either way, so look at each;
- the proton-contamination estimate and the charge-confusion estimate per bin.

A signal coincident with the exposure, efficiency or control series is a systematic, not a discovery. Also split by rigidity: a physical modulation effect should have a rigidity dependence (stronger at low rigidity), whereas an exposure artifact may not.

### 8. Stability, validation and triggers

- Stability metric: control-species rate or efficiency versus time, flat after livetime and cutoff correction, with a stated tolerance. A drift beyond tolerance triggers a period split, a scale factor with uncertainty, or an added systematic.
- Closure: inject a known sinusoid of one-year period and a chosen amplitude into the series (and, better, into the event-level template-fit input), and show it is recovered with the right amplitude and phase through the full chain, including detrending. Report the amplitude to which the search is sensitive (expected sensitivity before looking at data).
- Coverage: toy-validate any interval that uses low counts. `scripts/poisson_diagnostics.py` gives exact Poisson limits for known background only.
- "Reasonable agreement" is not validation: state a metric and a trigger (invariant 10).

### 9. Reporting

- Report per-bin ratios with the covariance and the exposures so others can refit; a single combined number hides the time dependence.
- Report the periodogram (or amplitude fit) with local and global significance, the band searched, the number of independent trials, the detrending choice and its effect on injected-signal recovery, and the control-series results.
- A modulation-model or drift interpretation is a separate model step with its own assumptions; the measurement is the time dependence of the ratio.
- Do not describe any result as agreeing with, or as confirming, an AMS periodicity result: the ledger has AMS 27-day, 13.5-day and 9-day variations for daily leptons (C36, C37, C40), but no annual periodicity claim for e+/e-, so there is nothing documented to compare to.

### 10. Failure modes to avoid

- Searching the raw trend-dominated series; using a single-frequency p-value across a band; no exposure or control check.
- Independent errors across bins while persistent systematics are shared.
- Averaging fluxes of bins with different exposure; one cutoff transmission for all bins; ignoring per-sign cutoff at 5 GV.
- Assuming the ratio cancels acceptance and efficiency without a per-effect check.
- Treating charge confusion from the Gaussian core only.
- Mixing rigidity and energy bins, or quoting an energy-binned AMS result for a rigidity analysis.
- Quoting the 108-day binning or the 40-degree and 1.2-cutoff conditions as AMS-wide rules.
- Filling the AMS evidence gap (systematic correlation across rotations, trial factors) with invented detail.

### Inputs that would change the design

Public or internal data? Counts per bin at 40-50 GV; hardware eras and reconstruction versions in the span; whether rigidity or energy bins are required; per-period efficiency, exposure and calibration records; the template provenance; whether the search period and band are fixed in advance.
