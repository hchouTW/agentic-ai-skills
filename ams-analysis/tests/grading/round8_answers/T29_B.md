## 1. Estimand, target, level
- Target: differential flux (not a ratio). Level: flux.
- Estimand: Phi_He4(R) = d^4N / (dA dOmega dt dR) of primary 4He at the top of the instrument (no solar-modulation or interstellar correction), in m^-2 sr^-1 s^-1 GV^-1, as a function of absolute rigidity R, for 2 GV <= R <= 3 TV, with a stated period.
- Species: |Z| = 2, charge sign +1, A = 4, rest energy 3.7274 GeV [General method, PDG]. The detector measures |Z|=2, not A, so "He" in practice is 3He + 4He. Claiming 4He requires a composition assumption f = 3He/(3He+4He) or the isotope separation of the helium-isotope paper (S12, C09, 2.1-21 GV for 4He only). Above that range no isotope separation exists, so the 2 GV to 3 TV result is a total-helium flux with a 3He/4He composition systematic. [Documented, C60] the published helium flux varied 3He/4He from 0 to 0.2, giving 0.3% in that analysis. Recommendation [Proposal]: name the target "helium (Z=2) flux" and report any 4He interpretation separately.
- Variable: rigidity R = pc/(|Z|e). Conversions with |Z|=2, A=4, m=3.7274 GeV (script output, [General method]): 2 GV is p = 4 GeV/c, T/A = 0.435 GeV/n; 3000 GV is p = 6000 GeV/c, T/A = 1499 GeV/n. Binned fluxes in T/A need the Jacobian, not relabeling, and the A assumption.
- Range note [Documented, C59, C62, C61, C63]: the first 30-month helium paper covers 1.9 GV to 3 TV; the five-year He, C, O paper uses 67 bins from 2.2 GV to 3 TV; the helium maximum detectable rigidity is 3.2 TV. So the top bin edge at 3 TV sits just below the MDR: the migration and rigidity-scale effects dominate there and the answer is not a counting problem. The review quotes the helium flux from 1.9 GV to 3 TV (C81).

## 2. Data scope
- Data or MC: data for the result; MC for response, acceptance, templates (a generation record is needed).
- Period: [Unknown/needs input]. Choose and state start and end. Published helium analyses used the first 30 months (S07, C59), the first five years (S11, C62), and Bartels-rotation or daily fluxes (C49, C54, C56). Do not mix them.
- Hardware era: [Documented, C102] as of the 2026-10-01 index date the Tracker Layer-0 upgrade is not installed (planned launch April 2027, Tier 6 news item, planned dates only). An analysis through a given date uses the pre-Layer-0 nine-layer detector. Any later data must be a separate era.
- Reconstruction and MC production version: user-supplied; version mismatch between data and MC is a defect.

## 3. Observables and subsystem roles
- Tracker: rigidity, charge sign (only the Tracker and magnet give sign), |Z| from layer ionization (C66).
- TOF: direction (downward-going), trigger, |Z|, beta.
- ACC: side-entry veto.
- RICH: |Z| and beta, used here only as a cross-check and for low-rigidity checks, not for the flux.
- TRD, ECAL: not charge or sign detectors; TRD is |Z| support only. ECAL is useful here only through the electron-positron E/p used for the absolute rigidity scale (C64).
- Rigidity resolution: [Documented, C61, C63] the response has a Gaussian core plus non-Gaussian tails, with MDR 3.2 TV for He in the L1-L9 configuration. Core width alone is not validation; tails need an independent check.

## 4. Selection (ledger; one row per cut, each with a named conditional denominator and a metric-threshold-action validation)
Documented AMS helium practice, for orientation only and not a rule: downward-going track, |Z|=2 compatible on L1, upper TOF, lower TOF, inner tracker and L9, chi^2/dof < 10, L1 to L9 track, R above 1.2 times the maximum geomagnetic cutoff by IGRF backtracing, quality time (live time over 50 percent, pointing within 40 degrees of zenith, outside the South Atlantic Anomaly) [Documented, C59]. Other helium analyses differ (L1-only track with a data-derived cutoff, C54; 30-degree pointing, C56), so there is no single AMS selection.
Rows to fill, with all numeric thresholds [Unknown/needs input] unless user-supplied:
1. Quality time and livetime (exposure veto, not an efficiency).
2. Downward-going single-track definition.
3. Tracker track quality and layer configuration (state L1-L9 or alternative; MDR depends on it).
4. |Z|=2 consistency across subsystems (targets adjacent-element leakage and interacting candidates).
5. Geomagnetic: R > SF x R_c(max in field of view), model and version stated, SF scanned as a systematic.
6. Trigger (efficiency, prescaled sample).
Each efficiency has a stated conditional denominator; do not multiply marginals; check ordering closure and Frechet bounds if independence is not shown.

## 5. Backgrounds and tails (registry; each with estimator, control region, closure)
- Proton contamination (p mis-identified or high-R tail): [Documented, C59] below 1e-4 and subtracted in the 30-month paper; scope: that paper only.
- Helium from fragmentation of heavier nuclei interacting at the top of AMS: [Documented, C59] below 1e-3 there; estimate from data with a control, with the charge-migration matrix to adjacent Z.
- Charge confusion and sign: positive-rigidity helium samples can be contaminated by negative-rigidity particles at high R. This is a tail effect; use a data-driven tail check, not the Gaussian core.
- Albedo and secondaries near the cutoff, mainly at 2 to about 5 GV.
- Interacting helium in the detector: a survival-probability efficiency, not a background.
- 3He: signal (Z=2 element flux) or a composition systematic depending on the target definition in item 1.

## 6. Efficiency, acceptance, exposure, response (kept distinct)
- Efficiency epsilon_j: conditional factors, data/MC scale factors per bin (do not borrow from another species or period).
- Effective acceptance A_j from the MC generation record (surface, solid angle, spectrum and reweighting without circularity), including survival against upstream material with a stated cross-section model. [Documented, C60, C80] AMS measured helium inelastic interactions in flight from survival probabilities with horizontal-pointing data and a Glauber-Gribov model adjusted to them; this is the model-dependence an independent analysis must replace or match.
- Exposure T_j: livetime x geomagnetic transmission per bin, including the acceptance-livetime integral if both vary in time.
- Response R_ij = P(R_meas in bin i | R_true in bin j, selected): tails included, a declared normalization, and a declared statement of whether efficiency and acceptance sit inside the matrix. Rule: response.includes and estimator.applies must not overlap, and each physical effect sits in exactly one category (veto, calibration, efficiency, acceptance, exposure, response).
- Migration is not small: [Documented, C59] the helium flux unfolding correction in the 30-month paper was +15% at 2 GV and -19% at 3 TV (this analysis only). Hence the diagonal estimator Phi_i = (n_i - b_i)/(DeltaR_i A_i eps_i T_i) is invalid here; use unfolding or forward folding (see 7).

## 7. Inference
- Estimator [Proposal]: forward-fold a parametrized Phi(R; theta), or unfold with a response matrix from MC with a measured resolution core and tail, with the data-validated tail amplitude as a nuisance. Alternate method (unfolding in a second procedure) as a systematic: [Documented, C60] AMS used two unfolding procedures and took their difference.
- Likelihood: binned Poisson; Poisson/exact intervals in bins with few counts (relevant in the top bins; 3 TV counts are small, so no Gaussian errors or Wilks). The binned-count requirement is a [General method] decision; state the minimum expected counts.
- Binning: [Unknown/needs input]; edges at 2 and 3000 GV. Make the bin width exceed the resolution where unfolding is not used. Bin abscissa uses a stated prescription (not the arithmetic centre), e.g. the point where the flux equals the bin average for a spectral shape R^-2.7 as in the review tables (C79).
- Output: flux in each bin with the full covariance (statistical, unfolding and shared systematic blocks), the response matrix and the exposure so that others can forward-fold.

## 8. Systematics (one row per effect, with applied_via and double-counting check)
Required families, with AMS helium sizes shown only as context [Documented, C60, 30-month helium paper; do not apply to another period]: trigger (limited at high R by the prescaled sample); cutoff factor scan 1.0 to 1.4 (small); acceptance and data/MC correction; inelastic cross sections (grows with R); 3He/4He composition; rigidity resolution function (core +/-5%, tail +/-10%, largest at the top bins); unfolding difference; absolute rigidity scale (the dominant systematic at the highest rigidity, which is the 3 TV end: [Documented, C64] a coherent shift below 0.5 micrometre corresponds to 10% rigidity-scale shift at 1 TV in the review). The rigidity scale and resolution systematics must enter through the migration (re-run the response), not as a final-value scaling. Correlations across bins are partial or full; never combine unknown correlations in quadrature. Table sizes are not reproduced here; read the paper for any value you intend to quote.

## 9. Validation (metric, threshold, action)
- MC closure: injected spectrum recovered per bin; trigger: any deviation outside statistical plus assigned systematic changes the unfolding prior or adds an uncertainty.
- Stability versus time (Bartels-rotation blocks, per-period flux of a high-rigidity bin), detector region, track configuration (L1-L9 versus alternative), cutoff factor, selection-set variation: pass when the variation is within the assigned uncertainty; otherwise period split or added systematic.
- Rigidity-scale and tail validation by an independent observable (redundant partial-rigidity fits, E/p of leptons), with the metric in TV^-1.
- Plausibility comparison with published helium fluxes (C59, C62, C81) is a plausibility check only; agreement is not validation.
