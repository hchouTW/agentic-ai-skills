**Verdict: no, do not clip and move on.** A correlation of -1.3 is not round-off. It means the covariance was built wrongly, and clipping would hide that. The response-matrix column sum and the trigger-efficiency factor are a separate issue that could also change your fluxes. Both need fixing before the fit.

All of this is [General method] (inference-and-unfolding, "Non-positive-semidefinite covariance: diagnosis and repair", and "Count and response model"). I have no AMS-specific numbers to quote. Everything below is based on what you described, and I have not seen your matrices.

## 1. The covariance with correlation -1.3

- Any valid covariance has |rho| <= 1, so -1.3 cannot come from a real covariance. Cauchy-Schwarz forces C45^2 <= C44 * C55, and your entry violates that.
- The skill's rule: compute the eigenvalues of the symmetrized matrix (C + C^T)/2 and compare the most negative to the largest.
  - If the most negative eigenvalue is at round-off level (about machine precision times the largest), it is numerical. You may clip, record the change, and confirm the fit or chi2 is unchanged within tolerance.
  - A -1.3 correlation is far above round-off. That is a construction error, and you should not clip.
- Likely causes to check:
  - Mixed absolute and relative conventions, or mixed bin ordering or units.
  - Rounded or guessed correlation tables.
  - A subtracted term with an implausible correlation.
  - A component added twice.
  - Unfolding-induced anticorrelation between neighbouring bins from an under-regularized inversion. Adjacent bins 4 and 5 with a strong negative correlation is the typical signature of this. Check the iteration count or regularization strength.
- Repair:
  - Rebuild from coherent variations, so each block is PSD by construction.
  - Propagate through the full unfolding with toys. The sample covariance of the toys is PSD.
  - Never repair by dropping correlations, or by combining components in quadrature when their correlations are unknown.
- Run `scripts/validate_covariance.py` first. It does the eigenvalue diagnosis and can show the effect of a clip as a labeled [Proposal] without changing your matrix.
- If it turns out that only a tiny negative eigenvalue exists, clipping is acceptable. A -1.3 entry suggests that is not your case.

## 2. Column sums of 0.85 and the trigger efficiency

- Orientation and normalization of R must be stated. Columns are true bins.
  - If R is conditional on the selected sample (`conditional_on_selected`), columns sum to 1 and inefficiency lives in the acceptance A_j.
  - If R includes efficiency (`includes_efficiency`), columns sum to the efficiency (at most 1).
  - Inefficiency must not be in both places.
- A column sum of 0.85 has two legitimate readings:
  - Inefficiency: true events not reconstructed in the selected sample.
  - Migration of true events into reconstructed bins outside the tabulated range, because under/overflow rows are not stored in R.
  - Do not diagnose 0.85 as inefficiency alone. Check whether the matrix stores under/overflow rows, and which reading the declared metadata implies. Run `scripts/validate_response.py` with the correct metadata.
- Double-counting risk: if the 0.85 is an efficiency that includes the trigger (so R "includes efficiency") and you also multiply the counts by a trigger efficiency, the trigger inefficiency is applied twice. Equivalently, if 0.85 is overflow migration, then multiplying by a trigger efficiency is fine, but then R should not be treated as normalized as if it were an efficiency.
- Also check whether the trigger efficiency is already inside the acceptance or exposure A_j T_j. Exposure already contains acceptance and livetime, so do not multiply those again.
- Use one `effect_id` per effect (trigger, selection, response, calibration) and list where each is applied. That is the checklist from `scripts/audit_analysis_spec.py`: `corrections`, `response.includes`, `estimator.applies`.
- Efficiencies must be conditional. Do not multiply marginal efficiencies as if they were independent. Show a combined-efficiency closure in MC.
- Does the trigger efficiency depend on rigidity? If so, it is a function of the true bin, not a single number. Applying it to reconstructed-bin counts rather than true-bin counts would be inconsistent with the model mu_i = sum_j R_ij A_j T_j Phi_j DeltaX_j.

## 3. Likely link between the two problems

An inconsistent response (normalization or double-counted efficiency) combined with an unregularized or poorly conditioned inversion can produce wild anticorrelations. Fix the response normalization first, then rebuild the covariance.

## Order of work

1. Run `validate_response.py` and `validate_covariance.py`.
2. Settle where the inefficiency lives (R or A) and where the trigger efficiency is applied. Remove duplicates.
3. Check the eigenvalue magnitude. If large, rebuild the covariance and run toys through the full unfolding. If at round-off level, clip, record it, and confirm the chi2 is unchanged.
4. Validate the unfolding: MC closure, a reweighted-spectrum closure, stress tests, regularization versus bias and variance, and prior variation.

## Inputs that would change this verdict

- The size of the most negative eigenvalue relative to the largest.
- How R is declared (conditional or including efficiency), and whether under/overflow rows are stored.
- Whether the trigger efficiency is already inside A_j, the exposure, or R.
- Whether the covariance is absolute or relative, and its bin ordering.
- The unfolding method, and its iteration count or regularization strength.
