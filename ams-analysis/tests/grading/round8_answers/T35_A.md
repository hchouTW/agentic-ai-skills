**Short answer.** There is no single TRD proton rejection you can assume for a 100 GeV positron analysis. The only public number is a lower bound for a stated selection, and your analysis has to measure its own rejection.

**What is documented**
- [Documented, C67; AMS Phys. Rept. review, S01, Sec. 1.3] The TRD proton rejection measured on orbit is **above 1000 at 90% e± efficiency, in 2-200 GeV/c**. It improves when the e± efficiency is tightened to 65%.
- 100 GeV lies inside that range, so ">1000 at 90% e± efficiency" applies to the review's selection. This is a **lower bound** from the review. It is not a measured rejection at 100 GeV, and it is not a value you can use as a point estimate.
- The review gives no numeric value for 65% efficiency. Do not interpolate one.
- The rejection depends on the efficiency working point and the energy range. It also depends on the Λ_TRD definition (log-likelihood ratio of the e± hypothesis to the proton hypothesis over 20 layers) and on the selection that defines the proton sample.

**What this means for the analysis**
- [Documented, C72] The ECAL gives a separate handle. Λ_ECAL together with E/p > 0.7 adds further proton rejection. Tightening from 90% to 65% e± efficiency lowers the proton background by a further factor of about 3. The review states this is independent of the TRD rejection. The ECAL rejection curve is a figure, and no value is transcribed. Independence is the review's statement for its own samples, so validate it in your analysis.
- [Documented, C24] AMS's published positron flux analysis (S04) does not assume a rejection number. It uses an energy-dependent Λ_ECAL cut, then a template fit in the 2D (Λ_TRD, Λ_CC) plane. The templates are positrons and protons with correct charge sign, plus charge-confusion electrons.
- Charge confusion is a separate background. It is a Tracker tail, and the TRD does not measure charge sign. The 8% electron charge-confusion bound (C65) applies only to electrons after that selection, and it is not a general rate.
- A TRD rejection figure for the proton background therefore cannot stand in for the positron background budget.

**Recommendation [Proposal]**
1. Treat "TRD rejection above about 1000 at 90% e± efficiency" only as an order-of-magnitude, lower-bound plausibility check (C67, that selection only). Do not use it as an input to a yield or contamination estimate.
2. Measure the rejection yourself at 100 GeV.
   - Use a proton-enriched control sample, for example negative-rigidity or ECAL-reversed selections.
   - Make a rejection-versus-efficiency curve on independent control data.
   - Check stability against time and rigidity (gas and gain drifts change the TRD response).
   - Check the tail, not only the core.
3. Do not multiply TRD and ECAL rejections as if independent without validating their correlation.
4. Prefer a template fit of the proton contamination over a cut-and-count estimate based on an assumed rejection factor.
5. If you need a different number, such as rejection at 100 GeV at a given efficiency, fetch it from the primary paper or review figure and record its context. Alternatively, treat it as **[Unknown/needs input]**.

**Caveats.** C67 comes from the Phys. Rept. review at full-text level, with the numbers checked against the rendered PDF.
