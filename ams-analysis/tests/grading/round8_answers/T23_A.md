The ACC (Anti-Coincidence Counter) is AMS-02's side-entry veto. It rejects particles that enter or leave the Tracker volume transversely, through the magnet sides or otherwise outside the nominal acceptance. It also rejects events with extra activity.

**Layout** [Documented, C69]: sixteen curved scintillator panels, 800 mm long, sit inside the magnet bore around the Tracker. Wavelength-shifting fibres carry the light to PMTs, and each PMT reads two adjacent panels. The vertical edges are tongue-and-groove, so a particle crossing a seam is seen in two panels.

**Role in the trigger** [Documented, C69, C73]: for Z = 1 the level-1 trigger requires four TOF signals and no ACC signal. For e+/e- the level-1 trigger uses four TOF signals plus the total ECAL energy trigger.

**Performance** [Documented, C21, C69]: the side-entry rejection efficiency is 0.99999. The analysis papers quote it, and the review traces it to a pre-flight long-duration test. Its inefficiency definition and per-condition validity are not given, so do not treat it as a universal in-flight figure.

**What the ACC does not do** (cross-subsystem matrix, [Documented, C13]): it gives no direction, no charge sign, no |Z| and no β. Its only particle-identification role is the veto. The Tracker, TOF, RICH and TRD are the sources for those quantities.

**Analysis implications** [General method]:
- A tighter veto removes off-acceptance and shower-contaminated events. It also loses valid events that have accidental or backsplash activity. That loss is an efficiency to be measured as a conditional efficiency.
- The veto's effect must not also be folded into the geometric acceptance, because that would double count.
- Failure modes are ECAL backsplash at high energy vetoing signal (an energy-dependent inefficiency), accidental activity that varies with orbit and rate over time, and hardware inefficiency that lets side-entering events through.
- Check the veto's dependence on rigidity or energy and on time.
