**Short answer: no to both.** The positron cutoff is a 4.07 sigma result, not 5 sigma, and the electron flux does not show the same cutoff.

**Positron cutoff (AMS-02 positron flux, 0.5 GeV-1 TeV)**

- [Documented, C04, C35, C97] AMS reports a source-term energy cutoff of E_s = 810 (+310, -180) GeV. The abstract says "more than 4 sigma", and the article body gives 4.07 sigma. That is the point where 1/E_s = 0 in the fit, equivalently a cutoff significance of 99.99% C.L. A claim of 5 sigma is not supported by the source. (The Phys. Rept. review, S01, gives the same 4.07 sigma.)
- Scope and caveats of the 4.07 sigma:
  - The fit is model-dependent: a diffuse term plus a source term with an exponential cutoff, and the cutoff is a parameter of that source term. It is not a model-independent spectral break in the data. [Documented, C97]
  - The significance comes from varying all six fit parameters to map 1 to 5 sigma regions and finding where 1/E_s = 0 lies. [Documented, C97]
  - There is no look-elsewhere (trial-factor) correction. [Documented, C97]
  - The measured 1/E_s = 1.23 +- 0.34 TeV^-1 is therefore about 3.6 sigma from zero on its quoted error alone. The 4.07 sigma is the 6-parameter region result, so quote it as that. [General method / arithmetic]
- The separate model-independent features are a rise starting near 25 GeV (25.2 +- 1.8 GeV in piecewise fits) and a fall above about 284 (+91, -64) GeV. These are different statements from the 810 GeV source-term cutoff. [Documented, C04, C96]
- For reporting, 4.07 sigma is "evidence-level", above the usual 3 sigma threshold and below 5 sigma. It should not be called a discovery. [General method; the reporting levels are in the skill's reporting policy.]

**Electron flux (0.5 GeV-1.4 TeV, 28.1 million electrons)**

- [Documented, C98, C99, S01 review restating S05] The electron flux does not show the positron cutoff. The two tests below are both fits within the stated models.
  - The electron flux was fitted over 41.61-1400 GeV with a power law plus the positron source term, with the positron parameters fixed, including E_s = 810 GeV. The data are consistent with the source term at its full strength (f fixed to 1), consistent with no source term (f = 0), and with f free (f = 0.5 (+1.2, -0.6)) with no chi2 improvement. The electron data neither require nor exclude a charge-symmetric positron source term. They do not show the positron cutoff, and they do not show that it is absent. [C99]
  - A single power law with an exponential cutoff, fitted over 41.61-1400 GeV, gives 1/E_s = 0.00 (+0.08, -0.00) TeV^-1. Cutoff energies E_s < 1.9 TeV are excluded at 5 sigma. This is an exclusion within that model and range, not of every cutoff shape. It is a limit, not a detection. [C99]
- The electron spectrum has a different shape from the positron one. Over 20.04-1400 GeV it is a power law with a transition (E0 = 42.1 (+5.4, -5.2) GeV, gamma = -3.280, Delta gamma = 0.094 +- 0.014). The spectral index rises and then becomes nearly energy independent at about -3.18 above about 55 GeV. [Documented, C98]
- The review states that the nature of the electron excess is different from that of the positron excess and that the two spectra have distinctly different magnitudes and energy dependences. This is the collaboration's qualitative reading, not a measurement. [C98]
- Corrections to the question's framing:
  - The "5 sigma" in the electron analysis belongs to the exclusion of E_s < 1.9 TeV in the electron-only power-law-plus-cutoff model.
  - The "7 sigma" in the review is for the electron spectral transition (E0 free), a different statement. Neither is a significance for the positron cutoff.

**Bottom line:** the positron cutoff at about 810 GeV is a 4.07 sigma, model-dependent result (not 5 sigma, not a discovery), and the electron flux shows no such cutoff. Its electron cutoff lower limit is E_s > 1.9 TeV at 5 sigma within the single power-law-plus-cutoff model.
