**What AMS reports** [Documented, C84; AMS Phys. Rept. 894 review, S01, full-text, review-level scope]

- The Li, Be and B fluxes (secondary) were divided by the C and O fluxes (primary), giving Li/C, Be/C, B/C, Li/O, Be/O and B/O.
- Model-independent spectral indices were computed over rigidity intervals bounded by 7.09, 12.0, 16.6, 22.8, 41.9, 60.3, 192 and 3300 GV. The secondary indices are nearly identical to each other and distinctly different from those of He, C and O.
- A single power law Phi_S/Phi_P ∝ R^Delta was fitted to the ratios in two non-overlapping intervals, 60.3-192 GV and 192-3300 GV.
- Above about 200 GV the secondary-to-primary index hardens on average by Delta[192-3300] - Delta[60.3-192] = 0.140 ± 0.025.
- Related context: the review's high-rigidity secondary-to-primary ratio index above 192 GV is -0.266 ± 0.022 (C82 context). The primary He, C and O fluxes also harden from about 200 GV (C81).

**How significant**

- The review states the average hardening exceeds 5 sigma [Documented, C84]. The ±0.025 is the quoted uncertainty on the 0.140 shift, so the shift is about 5.6 times its error. Treat that ratio as arithmetic on the quoted values, not as the collaboration's significance computation.
- Caveats [Unknown/needs input]:
  - I did not read how the 5 sigma was computed. That covers which systematic correlations were included, and whether any interval-choice or trial-factor correction was applied.
  - The two interval boundaries (60.3, 192, 3300 GV) are the review's choice, not a prescription.
  - Ratio systematics are not simply cancelled. Cross-section, unfolding and rigidity-scale systematics are treated as correlated between numerator and denominator, and the other sources are added in quadrature (C85).
  - The 5 sigma is the average over the six ratios. These share the same primary and secondary fluxes, so they are not independent.
- This is a measured change of the empirical power-law index of the ratio. The physical interpretation is a separate model step.
  - The review reads the hardening as consistent with propagation in the Galaxy. Source-injection hardening would give similar hardening for secondaries and primaries.
  - The review also states that no model predicted the observed behaviour of the primary or secondary fluxes. That is the review's reading, and I did not read third-party model papers.
- The result is review-level (Phys. Rept. 2021, first seven years of data). I did not check for a later superseding AMS paper. The skill's index was last verified 2026-09-20, and I had no web access.
