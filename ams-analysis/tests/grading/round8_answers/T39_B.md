**Method (survival-probability measurement) [Documented, C80; also C60 for the helium-flux use]**

AMS does not use a dedicated target. It measures the survival probability of a nucleus crossing its own detector material, then converts that to an inelastic cross section.

- **Material.** Averaged over path lengths in the acceptance, the material traversed is about 73% carbon and 17% aluminium by mass. Small amounts of Si, O, H, Na, Au and others make up the rest. These are path-length averages over the acceptance, not a material budget.
- **Geometry.** The ISS attitude is such that AMS points horizontally. Nuclei therefore enter from either end of the tracker, going from L8 to L1 or from L2 to L9. The seven inner layers L2-L8 define the particle (dE/dx charge and rigidity).
- **Survival probability.** The charge distributions in L2-L8 and in L1 (or L8 and L9) are compared. This gives the probability that the nucleus keeps its charge through the outer material (L1-L2 and L8-L9).
- **Interaction inside the tracker.** The interaction probability between L2 and L8 (below 3%) is obtained by comparing the upper- and lower-TOF charges. It is combined with the L1-L2 and L8-L9 survival probabilities to give the L1-L9 survival.
- **Monte Carlo comparison.** A dedicated Monte Carlo uses the Geant4 Glauber-Gribov inelastic cross sections, varied by +-10%. It is compared with the data and the best-agreeing value is chosen.
  - For helium, a few-percent discrepancy at low rigidity (below about 30 GV) led to a modified Glauber-Gribov model matched to the data over the full rigidity range.
  - Below 30 GV, the measured He rigidity dependence is used to modify the model for heavier nuclei.
  - The helium-flux paper used the same approach with Glauber-Gribov cross sections scaled by 1.05-1.20 (C60).

**What was obtained [Documented, C80, review-level]**

- Survival probabilities were measured for Li, Be, B, C, N, O, Ne, Mg and Si.
- Before AMS, inelastic cross sections had been measured only below 10 GV, and only for He and C projectiles. Apart from He-on-C and C-on-C, no other measurements existed.
- The inelastic cross sections on carbon, averaged over 5-100 GV, are constant within the accuracy.
- The use as a systematic is in C60: the helium inelastic-interaction uncertainty is 1% under 100 GV and 2% at 3 TV. For the daily proton flux, inelastic interactions are a time-independent systematic (C44).

- The method is only summarized at review level.
- The survival-probability approach is AMS-specific, not a general recipe.
