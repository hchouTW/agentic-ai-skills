**Method [Documented, C80; review-level summary of S01, Phys. Rept. 894, Section 7]**

AMS measures nuclear interaction (inelastic) cross sections in its own detector material by measuring in-flight survival probabilities with cosmic-ray data.

- Pointing: the ISS attitude is chosen so that AMS points horizontally. Nuclei then pass through the detector in both directions, from L8 to L1 and from L2 to L9.
- Defining the particle: the seven inner Tracker layers L2-L8 define the particle, through dE/dx charge and rigidity. The charge distributions in L2-L8 are compared with the charge distribution in L1 (or in L8 and L9). This shows whether the nucleus survived the material between them.
- Inner interaction probability: the interaction probability between L2 and L8 (below 3%) is obtained by comparing upper-TOF and lower-TOF charges. It is combined with the L1-L2 and L8-L9 survival probabilities to give the full L1-L9 survival.
- Material: averaged over path lengths in the acceptance, the traversed material is about 73% carbon and 17% aluminium by mass. It also contains small amounts of Si, O, H, Na, Au and others. These are path-length averages, not a material budget.
- Monte Carlo comparison: a dedicated MC uses the Geant4 Glauber-Gribov inelastic cross sections, varied by +-10%. The best-agreeing value is chosen.
- Helium correction: below about 30 GV, helium shows a discrepancy of a few percent. This led to a modified Glauber-Gribov model matched to the data over the full rigidity range. Below 30 GV, the measured He rigidity dependence is used to modify the model for heavier nuclei.

**What was obtained [Documented, C80]**

- Before AMS, inelastic cross sections had been measured only below 10 GV, and only for He and C projectiles.
- Survival probabilities were measured for Li, Be, B, C, N, O, Ne, Mg and Si.
- The inelastic cross sections on carbon, averaged over 5-100 GV, are constant within the accuracy.
- Apart from He-on-C and C-on-C, no other measurements exist.
