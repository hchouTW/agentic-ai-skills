**Short answer:** assume at least about 10^3 from the TRD alone, at 90% e± efficiency. Do not treat that as a fixed number for 100 GeV, and do not use it as your analysis's rejection.

**What the public record supports**
- [Documented, C67] The AMS review (Phys. Rept. 894) says the TRD proton rejection measured on orbit is above 1000 at 90% e± efficiency over 2-200 GeV/c. This is a lower bound, not a point value. 100 GeV lies inside that range.
- [Documented, C67] The rejection improves if you tighten the e± efficiency to 65%. The source gives no number for that case, so I will not supply one.
- The figure belongs to that review's estimator and selection. The estimator is Λ_TRD, the log-likelihood ratio of the e± hypothesis to the proton hypothesis over the 20 TRD layers.
- It is also tied to that efficiency working point and data set. It is not a universal TRD property.

**What to use it for**
- [Proposal] Use ">1000 at 90% e± efficiency" as a plausibility benchmark when you check your own TRD performance. It is not an input to the analysis.
- [General method] The TRD is only one part of lepton/proton separation. The proton background is orders of magnitude above the positron signal, so the TRD factor alone does not set your contamination.
- [Documented, C72] The ECAL adds an independent handle. Λ_ECAL (16 variables) combined with E/p > 0.7 gives a further factor of about 3 in proton rejection when the e± efficiency is tightened from 90% to 65%. That is the review's statement for its own samples. Re-validate the independence claim in your own analysis, since the ECAL shape and E/p share the ECAL energy.
- [Documented, C24, C26] Published AMS lepton analyses do not just apply a TRD cut and quote a rejection. They apply an energy-dependent ECAL cut, then extract the yield with a template fit in the (Λ_TRD, Λ_CC) plane. The proton template comes from proton data, and a charge-confusion template is included.
- Charge confusion is a separate background and a Tracker tail effect. The TRD does not touch it. The review reports an electron charge-confusion fraction below 8% up to 1 TeV, after TRD and ECAL selection (C65). That is for electrons after that selection, not a general rate.

**What I cannot give you**
- A single rejection factor at exactly 100 GeV, an energy-dependent curve, or the rejection at your efficiency. None of these is in the public sources I have indexed.
- AMS-internal cuts, efficiencies and calibrations are not available here, and I will not invent them.

**What to do for your analysis**
1. State your working point first: e± efficiency, the Λ_TRD cut, and the energy bin. The rejection only means something at a stated efficiency.
2. Measure the TRD proton rejection on a data-driven proton-rich sample, or on protons selected using other subsystems. Check the tail as well as the core.
3. Do not multiply the TRD and ECAL rejections as if they were independent without testing it. Treat residual proton contamination as a measured quantity, with a transfer-factor or template-fit uncertainty, not a derived one.
4. If you have internal numbers, use them and label them as user-supplied.

**What would change this answer:** your e± efficiency target, whether you use data or MC, which data period and TRD gas conditions apply, and whether the TRD is combined with ECAL.
