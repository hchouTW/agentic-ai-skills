## Verdict

Not acceptable as designed. All three elements have a fundamental defect, and the first and third are invariant violations rather than matters of tuning. The estimand (flux or p̄/p versus |R|, range, data versus MC) is also not stated, and the design appears to start from a selection recipe rather than from the estimand and response.

## Defects, in priority order

**1. Charge confusion estimated from the Gaussian rigidity resolution [General method; AMS invariant].**
- Charge confusion (p → p̄-like, and e⁺ → e⁻-like) is a tail phenomenon. It comes from the non-Gaussian Tracker response: bad hit assignment, multiple scattering and interactions, and the 1/R sign flip near zero curvature.
- The Gaussian core says almost nothing about the tail. The tail probability can be orders of magnitude different from the extrapolated Gaussian value. The abundant protons (about 10⁴ times the antiprotons) make this the dominant background at high |R|.
- Required instead:
  - A charge-sign migration matrix M(p̄←p) as a function of rigidity, from calibrated MC with the tail validated on data. Use abundant matter samples under looser cuts, test-beam protons, and redundant partial track fits (for example, layer subsets or upper/lower segments) to measure the sign-flip probability and its extrapolation to the tight selection.
  - Or, preferably, a charge-confusion template in the fit. This is [Documented, C22 (S08)]: the published antiproton analysis uses a template fit with charge-confused-proton templates, and the charge-confusion MC was checked with 400 GV proton test-beam data.
  - A stated metric and a trigger for adding an uncertainty. "Looks consistent with the Gaussian" is not validation.

**2. "Signal = negative-sign tracks passing a TRD cut" [Documented structure, C22; General method].**
- The TRD does not measure charge sign. Sign comes from the Tracker and magnet only. The TRD only discriminates e from hadron, through transition radiation, which is γ-dependent.
- A TRD cut therefore does not turn a negative-sign sample into antiprotons. The negative sample contains e⁻, π⁻/μ⁻ and secondaries, and charge-confused protons.
- The cut gives no signal definition, only a background reduction, and its efficiency is rigidity-dependent. The TRD response changes with rigidity, so the e/p separation at fixed cut is not uniform, and p̄ efficiency must be measured with a proton-like sample.
- A p̄ selection needs more than that:
  - Tracker sign and |Z| = 1.
  - β consistent with the rigidity.
  - TRD and ECAL hadron-like response.
  - Geomagnetic selection.
- Bins with a hard TRD cut still need a residual-electron estimate.
- A cut-and-count with a cut efficiency applied cannot separate the remaining electrons and charge-confused protons. The published approach is a template fit of the negative-rigidity sample, with the signal template from proton data and backgrounds from data and MC, in overlapping rigidity regions [Documented, C22 (S08)]. That is what the design should be compared with.
- Cut-flow efficiencies must be conditional, with closure. Do not multiply marginal efficiencies: the TRD cut and the rigidity-dependent selections are correlated.

**3. Electron background "subtracted by MC" [General method].**
- MC-only subtraction hides the dominant systematic. The e⁻ rate depends on the interstellar electron flux, on the geomagnetic and albedo components, and on the TRD and ECAL response. Each of these is imperfectly modeled in MC. Do not subtract a number from simulation without data constraints.
- Required for each background (ledger fields: origin, entry mechanism, estimator, control region with signal contamination, transfer model, closure, nuisances):
  - A data-derived or data-constrained estimate. Templates are fitted in data, in a control region that is orthogonal and signal-depleted: for example, reversed TRD/ECAL cuts for an e⁻-rich sample, and a loose-cut sample for the charge-confusion tail.
  - A statement of the signal contamination of that control region.
  - A transfer model across rigidity, with a test that it is not flat by assumption.
  - Finite-MC-statistics nuisances (Barlow-Beeston or Conway), as templates are not exact.
  - A closure test with injected signal and with alternative templates.
  - Template provenance that is not circular. The fitted data must not define the template.
- Subtraction also needs a correlation treatment. The e⁻ and charge-confusion estimates share the Tracker and TRD systematics. They must be propagated jointly, not added in quadrature.
- Other backgrounds are not listed in the design: charge-confused protons (above), π⁻ and secondaries, and interacting particles. The background ledger must be complete.

**4. Further gaps (missing inputs that change the result).**
- No estimand or response chain. State flux or p̄/p versus |R|. If it is the ratio, state the proton denominator's own acceptance and efficiency, and demonstrate any partial cancellation rather than assuming it.
- Migration: with a steeply falling spectrum, finite resolution moves abundant low-R events upward. The published analysis unfolds iteratively and stops when successive fluxes agree within 0.1% [Documented, C23 (S08)]. A diagonal bin-by-bin estimator is valid only after showing negligible migration with a response matrix.
- Acceptance, efficiency, exposure and livetime are distinct. Use a generation record for the MC acceptance. Include the geomagnetic transmission. Apply each correction once, with no double counting between veto, calibration, efficiency and response.
- Uncertainty treatment: low-count high-|R| bins need Poisson or toy-validated intervals, not Gaussian errors or S/√B. Give the statistical, template, systematic and unfolding-induced covariances, and the correlated, partial or independent classification of each nuisance.
- Blinding or predefinition of the selection and the fit, so the cut is not tuned on the fitted data.

## Inputs that would change the verdict
- Whether a signed-rigidity template (or other charge-sign-sensitive variable) is actually fitted. A template fit with validated tails would address most of items 1 to 3.
- Data versus MC for the tail: is there a validated charge-confusion tail on abundant protons or test-beam data, and over which rigidity range?
- Rigidity range: the tail and the e⁻ background change character across it, and the e⁻ is dominant at low |R| and charge confusion at high |R|.
- Whether the electron control region is signal-free, and its transfer to the signal region.
- Public or internal work. I cannot supply AMS cut values, rejection factors or the numbers of the published analysis beyond what the source index documents (S08: 1-450 GV, abstract-level; C22, C23).
