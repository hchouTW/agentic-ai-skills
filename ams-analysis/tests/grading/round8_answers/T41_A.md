**Short answer.** The positron-to-electron ratio R_e = Φ(e⁺)/Φ(e⁻) changes smoothly across the 2013 solar polarity reversal over **830 ± 30 days**. This is the 10%-90% transition time of a fitted logistic function, and it is independent of energy. The midpoint of the transition, however, is delayed in an energy-dependent way.

**What was measured [Documented, C39, S42; C89, C90, S01].** The data are electron and positron fluxes over 79 Bartels rotations (May 2011 to May 2017), in energy from 1 to 50 GeV. This is an energy variable, not rigidity. The S42 abstract (C39) reports the smooth transition over 830 ± 30 days with an energy-dependent midpoint delay. The Phys. Rept. review restates the lepton data (C89, 23.5 million events, 49 energy bins). The logistic fit that produces the 830-day number is in the review (C90).

**How AMS got the number [Documented, C90, S01].**
1. The fluxes are built per Bartels rotation, with the flux defined as counts over acceptance, efficiency and collection time, and with unfolding done in each time bin. This follows the time-resolved method in C48 and C49. The review's R_e uses 3871 independent measurements in energy and time. In the ratio the short-term, months-scale structures largely cancel, because they appear in e⁺ and e⁻ with about the same relative amplitude (C39, C89).
2. R_e(t, E) is fitted with a four-parameter logistic function, R_e = R0(E)[1 + C(E)/(exp(-(t - t_1/2(E))/(Δt/Δ_80)) + 1)], with Δ_80 = 4.39. Δ_80 is chosen so that Δt is the 10%-90% transition time. The four parameters per energy are R0 (baseline), C (transition amplitude), t_1/2 (midpoint time) and Δt (duration).
3. The fits cover 1-21 GeV with χ²/d.o.f. about 1. Δt comes out energy independent at 830 ± 30 days.
4. The midpoint delay follows t_1/2(E) - t_rev = τ (E/GeV)^ρ. Here t_rev = 1 July 2013, the centre of the period with no well-defined solar polarity. The fit gives ρ = -0.33 ± 0.04 (stat) (+0.08, -0.15) (syst) and τ = 580 ± 19 (stat) ± 136 (syst) days. The midpoint shifts by 260 ± 30 days between 1 and 6 GeV.
5. The amplitude C is close to 1 at 1 GeV, falls with energy, and is consistent with zero above 20 GeV, so there is no transition to measure there.

**Caveats.**
- The logistic form is a descriptive fit chosen by the collaboration, not a physical model. The 10%-90% definition (Δ_80 = 4.39) sets what "830 days" means.
- Δt and t_1/2 are only well determined at low energy, where C is large. Above 6 GeV the review fixes Δt to 830 days and uses the delay formula for t_1/2 (C90).
- t_rev is a stated choice, so the delays t_1/2 - t_rev depend on it. The large systematic on ρ and τ means both errors should be quoted.
- The text read describes no trial factor and no correlated-error model across bins. That is not proof of absence, so I treat it as [Unknown/needs input]. I do not supply AMS operational detail beyond what the claims give.
- When quoting the number, attribute it to S42 (C39) plus the review's fit (C90, C89). The abstract-level statement should be re-checked against the paper before it is quoted as final.
- The 830-day result is for leptons, 1-50 GeV in energy, May 2011 to May 2017. It should not be extended to other species, to rigidity binning or to other periods.
- The drift-based physical interpretation (charge-sign-dependent modulation across polarity reversal) is a model step separate from the measurement. The review describes the data as in qualitative agreement with drift-including modulation models.
