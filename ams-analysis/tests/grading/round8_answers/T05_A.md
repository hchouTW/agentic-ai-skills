## Defects in priority order

1. **The bin-by-bin MC factor can double count or bias the result, and it is not shown to be valid (highest priority).**
   - Your formula already divides by acceptance and livetime. A further MC "correction factor" is therefore one of two things.
     - It is an efficiency or acceptance correction (trigger, selection, survival). Then it duplicates the `A` and `ε` you already divided by.
     - It is a migration correction. Then it is only legitimate when migration is negligible. A bin-by-bin factor is valid only after you show negligible migration with the response matrix.
   - With a steep spectrum and a finite rigidity resolution with tails, upward migration from abundant low-R events dominates. The resolution core looking small does not demonstrate small migration. Purity is not a migration study. [Documented, C30: the AMS migration correction for phosphorus runs from about +25% at 3 GV to -21% at 1.2 TV, so it is not a small effect.]
   - A bin-by-bin factor from MC also bakes in the MC's generated spectrum and resolution. That is a prior dependence, and it is circular if the MC spectrum is tuned to your measured spectrum.
   - **What to do.**
     - Write the response model `μ_i = Σ_j R_ij A_j ε_j T_j Φ_j ΔR_j + b_i`.
     - Then either unfold or forward-fold. This means iterative Bayesian, SVD or Tikhonov, or a forward-folded likelihood fit, with a full covariance.
     - Keep the bin-by-bin factor only if you can show negligible migration on the response matrix. State the metric and the trigger for abandoning it.
   - **Required checks.** Run all of these, and report the unfolding-induced bin correlations.
     - MC closure.
     - A reweighted-spectrum closure.
     - Distorted-spectrum stress tests.
     - Response-perturbation tests.
     - Pseudo-experiment pulls.
     - Stability against binning.

2. **There is no background subtraction.** "Count events after cuts" gives `n_i`, not signal. The estimator should be `(n_i - b_i)`.
   - For protons, `b_i` includes:
     - helium and deuteron contamination, and charge-migration leakage from adjacent Z through the Tracker, TOF and RICH charge estimators;
     - electron and positron contamination, with the TRD used as a lepton-rejection discriminant, not as a charge-sign detector;
     - charge-sign confusion, which is a tail effect and must be validated on the tail, not from the Gaussian core of the resolution;
     - albedo and secondary particles at low rigidity.
   - Each background needs a ledger entry: origin, estimator (template fit, sideband, migration matrix), control region and its signal contamination, closure test, and covariance.
   - For template fits, check template provenance, finite-template statistics (Barlow-Beeston), and cross-contamination.

3. **"Acceptance times livetime" is not exposure, and it is underspecified.**
   - Acceptance, efficiency, exposure and livetime are distinct (invariant 3). Say which acceptance you mean. `A_geo` is geometric. `A_eff` is `A_geo` times the average selection efficiency. If `A` is already `A_eff`, you must not divide by an additional `ε`, and you must not apply the MC factor as another efficiency.
   - Exposure is the time integral `Σ_t A_eff(t) T_live(t) × geomagnetic transmission`, and it has to be done per rigidity bin. A single global livetime times a single acceptance is wrong whenever livetime, acceptance and cutoff vary together.
   - Livetime must be quality-approved live time. It is not calendar time, and it needs prescale and dead-time handling.
   - **Geomagnetic transmission.** Your formula has no cutoff treatment, so low-R bins would be contaminated by secondaries and albedo. A primary selection requires `|R| > SF · R_c` with a stated field model and safety factor, or an exposure-weighted transmission per bin.
     - [Documented, C20 (antiproton), C31 (proton)] AMS public analyses use 1.2 times the maximum geomagnetic cutoff in the field of view, and vary that factor as a systematic. The scan is 1.2-1.4 in the antiproton analysis (C23) and 1.0-1.4 in the deuteron (C27) and proton/He time-structure (C49) analyses. No single selection is the AMS standard.
     - Vary the model and the safety factor, and propagate both.
   - Quality time is defined by conditions such as normal detector operation, pointing within 40° of zenith, and the ISS outside the South Atlantic Anomaly. [Documented, C31]

4. **The efficiency chain is unspecified.** "After cuts" needs a cut-flow ledger.
   - Each cut needs a named conditional denominator and an efficiency method: tag-and-probe, orthogonal selectors, unbiased or prescaled triggers, or MC with data/MC scale factors.
   - Do not multiply marginal efficiencies. Cuts that share information are correlated, for example charge estimators or track quality with geometry. Check the factorization bias with a direct joint measurement. Check ordering closure by applying the chain in two orders in MC. Check combined-efficiency closure against MC truth.
   - Without independence, two efficiencies only bound the joint (Fréchet bounds). Quoting the product is not a measurement.
   - Use exact or Bayesian binomial intervals near 0 or 1.
   - A pure-MC efficiency is not enough. Use data/MC scale factors, and do not transfer a factor from one species or period to another.
   - Add the survival probability against upstream material and in-detector interactions, with a stated cross-section model. [Documented, C80: AMS measures inelastic cross sections in flight from survival probabilities.]
   - Treat each physical effect in exactly one category: veto, calibration, efficiency or response. For example, a drifting TRD gain must not be both a time veto and an efficiency correction. This is a double-counting hazard when an MC factor is stacked on `A`, `ε` and `T`.

5. **Bin width and bin-centre convention.**
   - `ΔR_i` is the right normalization for a differential flux, but the flux in a bin is a bin average. For a steeply falling spectrum, quoting it at the arithmetic centre is biased.
   - State the prescription: the position where the flux equals the bin average, or a spectrum-dependent correction.
   - The width must be the true-bin width inside the response model, not the reconstructed width after migration.
   - If you later convert to kinetic energy per nucleon, you need the Jacobian `dR/d(T/A)` and the stated `A` and `Z`. Relabelling bins is not enough. `scripts/ams_kinematics.py` does this arithmetic, and the results are [General method].

6. **The uncertainty model is missing.** Both the statistical and the systematic covariance are absent.
   - Statistical: Poisson on the counts. Add the background-fit covariance and the finite MC statistics.
   - Systematic ledger, one row per effect, each with a validation and a double-counting check. Do not combine unknown correlations in quadrature.
     - Trigger and selection efficiency, including factorization bias.
     - Acceptance, material and interaction cross sections.
     - Background and charge-confusion tails.
     - Rigidity scale and resolution. Apply the scale shift through the migration, not to the final spectrum, and note that it is amplified by the local spectral index.
     - Unfolding and prior.
     - Geomagnetic cutoff model and safety factor.
     - Time dependence.
     - MC generator, physics list and material.
   - Adjacent flux bins are correlated through unfolding and shared systematics. Provide the covariance, not only per-bin percentages.
   - [Documented, C23, C25, C33] AMS papers add independent systematic sources in quadrature, but each source there was assessed as independent. That is not a licence to do it for sources whose correlations are unknown.

7. **Data and MC consistency, and sample hygiene.**
   - The MC must be validated against data with a metric and an action trigger. "Reasonable agreement" is not validation. Tails matter more than cores for charge confusion and background.
   - The MC provenance must be recorded: generator, spectrum, generation surface and solid angle, transport engine and physics list, geometry and material version, digitization and dead channels, calibration constants, reconstruction version and production period. The reconstruction version and the hardware era must match the data. Split by era or justify combining.
   - The calibration must not be tuned on the measured observable, for example by tuning the rigidity scale to remove a spectral feature.
   - Training, tuning, control and measurement samples must be disjoint.
   - **Acceptance without circularity.** If the MC generated spectrum is reweighted to the measured one, iterate or use a response that does not depend on it.

## Validation and cross-checks to demand
- MC truth closure of the whole chain in each bin.
- Stability of the result versus time (an abundant control species after livetime and cutoff correction), geometry and detector region, and track configuration.
- Loose and tight charge cuts, the two charge-sign selections, and alternate binning and priors.
- Comparison with published spectra is a plausibility check only. Agreement does not prove correctness.
- Each check should name the failure mode it targets and the deviation that triggers a method change or an added uncertainty.

## Inputs that would change the verdict
- Is the "bin-by-bin factor" an efficiency, a migration correction, or both? Does it include `A` or `ε`?
- Is `A` geometric or effective? Is the response matrix conditional on selection, or does it include inefficiency?
- Is the rigidity range low or high? Migration is largest at high rigidity with a steep spectrum, and cutoff effects dominate at low rigidity.
- Are you using the same Tracker layer configuration throughout, and which hardware era and time interval?
- Do you have a generation record, a control sample that is free of signal, and a background estimate for each source?
- Is the target a model-independent spectrum, which favours unfolding, or model parameters such as an index or break, which favour forward folding?
