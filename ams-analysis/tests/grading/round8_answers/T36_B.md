**Short answer: no, and no.**

**1. Positron cutoff at about 810 GeV is not a 5 sigma result.**
- [Documented, C35] The AMS positron-flux paper (S04, PRL 122, 041102, 2019) states the source-term energy cutoff at "more than 4 sigma" in the abstract, and the article body gives 4.07 sigma. A claim of 5 sigma is not supported.
- [Documented, C97] The Phys. Rept. review (S01) gives the same fit result: source-term cutoff E_s = 810 (+310, -180) GeV at 4.07 sigma. Note the large asymmetric uncertainty, so "about 810 GeV" is a central value of a poorly constrained parameter.
- What the quantity is: E_s is the cutoff energy of a fitted source term (a model with a collision/secondary term plus a source term with an exponential cutoff), not a directly observed break in the data. The 4.07 sigma is the point where 1/E_s reaches 0 within that model. It is model-dependent, and I would not describe it as a model-independent observation of a cutoff.
- [General method] Why one should be careful about quoting it as "5 sigma": E_s is a boundary/non-regular parameter (no cutoff corresponds to 1/E_s = 0), so Wilks/asymptotic chi-square conversions need toy validation, and any search over energy range or model form carries a trial factor. I did not find a statement in the indexed claims that such a treatment gives 5 sigma. [Unknown/needs input] The supplemental material of S04 was not read, so I can say only what the main article and review claims state.
- Related but separate (C96, S01): the positron spectrum also shows a rise from 25.2 +/- 1.8 GeV and a fall above 284 (+91, -64) GeV in piecewise fits. These are different quantities from E_s and should not be conflated with the 810 GeV figure.

**2. The electron flux does not show the same cutoff.**
- [Documented, C99, S01] The electron data neither require nor exclude a charge-symmetric positron-type source term. In a single power law with exponential cutoff (Eq. 9 of the review) fitted over 41.61-1400 GeV, the electron data exclude E_s < 1.9 TeV at 5 sigma. This is the likely origin of any "5 sigma" memory, but it is a 5 sigma exclusion of a low cutoff energy for electrons, within that model and range. It is not a 5 sigma detection of a positron cutoff, and it is not an exclusion of every cutoff shape.
- [Documented, C98, S01] The electron flux is instead described by a transition (two-power-law) fit with E0 = 42.1 GeV, and a two-power-law form with a force-field potential fits 0.5-1400 GeV (C100). The electron paper (S05, claim C26) covers 0.5 GeV-1.4 TeV with 28.1 x 10^6 electrons.
- So the electron and positron spectra are different in shape: the positron flux has a source-term cutoff at E_s = 810 (+310, -180) GeV (4.07 sigma), whereas the electron flux shows no cutoff below 1.9 TeV in the exponential-cutoff model, and its high-energy behavior is described by a different functional form. The experimental reach for both is limited by statistics near 1 TeV (positron data extend to 1 TeV, electron to 1.4 TeV).
- Interpretation of the differences (a common source, dark-matter cutoffs, pulsars) is the collaboration's qualitative reading, not a measurement (C75, C77, C96), and I am not drawing a model conclusion here.

**Caveats.**
- Verification level: S04 and S05 are full-text (main article only); the review S01 claims C97-C100 are review-level statements. Numbers are as stated in those sources; the index was last verified 2026-09-20 and I did not check for newer or superseding AMS papers (no web access), so I cannot assert currency beyond that date.
- Any quote should specify "source-term cutoff, positron flux fit, 4.07 sigma (S04), model-dependent."
