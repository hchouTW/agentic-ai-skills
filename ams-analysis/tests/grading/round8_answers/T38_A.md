Short answer: only with qualification. You can quote it as the number AMS reports and calls the N/O abundance ratio at the source, but not as a model-independent, propagation-free measurement of the source abundance.

**What AMS reports** [Documented, C91; Phys. Rept. 894 review, review-level summary]: the nitrogen flux (3.9 million nuclei, 2.2 GV to 3.3 TV) is fitted over the whole rigidity range as a weighted sum of a primary and a secondary component, Phi_N = Phi_NP + Phi_NS, with Phi_NP = (0.092 +- 0.002) x Phi_O and Phi_NS = (0.61 +- 0.02) x Phi_B. The chi2/d.o.f. is 59/64. The review describes the fitted 0.092 +- 0.002 as the N/O abundance ratio at the source. It compares this with the Solar-System value 0.135 (+0.051, -0.047) and calls the two consistent.

**Why you should not present it as a plain source measurement:**

1. **It is a fit parameter, not a direct measurement.** The 0.092 is the coefficient of the primary term in a two-component decomposition of the nitrogen flux. It is not an abundance measured at the source.
2. **It is conditional on the template choice.** The decomposition assumes that oxygen has a purely primary shape and that boron has a purely secondary shape. The review itself notes that O has the lowest secondary content among He, C and O, not zero. It also notes that B is mostly made from primary C and O, so it is not an independent secondary template. The word "source abundance" rests on the same assumption.
3. **The fit quality does not test the assumption.** A chi2/d.o.f. of 59/64 shows the two-term form describes the data. It does not show that the O and B shapes are the true primary and secondary shapes. The 0.092 and 0.61 are therefore conditional on that choice.
4. **The source N/O is not propagation-free in the strict sense.** Interpreting the primary coefficient as a source ratio also assumes that N and O propagate similarly (similar fragmentation and interaction losses and similar rigidity dependence). Any difference would enter as a systematic on the interpretation. This is not quantified in the ledger. 5. **The comparison with the Solar System is weak.** The reference value 0.135 (+0.051, -0.047) has a large uncertainty. "Consistent" is not a precision test, and the quoted +-0.002 on 0.092 is the fit error only. Do not read the uncertainty as the total uncertainty on the source abundance.
6. **Scope and provenance.** The number comes from the review's summary. It overlaps the separate AMS nitrogen PRL (121, 051103, 2018; S20), which is indexed here only as metadata. Attribute the numerical details to the paper first. Also, the A = 14.5 used in the kinetic-energy-per-nucleon comparison is a plotting convention and has nothing to do with the 0.092.

**Suggested wording.** "AMS reports a primary-component nitrogen-to-oxygen ratio of 0.092 +- 0.002 from a fit of Phi_N to 0.092 x Phi_O + 0.61 x Phi_B. This is interpreted by the AMS review as the N/O abundance ratio at the source, under the assumptions that O is purely primary and B purely secondary [Documented, C91]."
