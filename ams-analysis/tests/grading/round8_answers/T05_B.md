**Verdict: not acceptable as written for a proton flux.** The skeleton (counts / (acceptance x livetime x bin width)) is the right shape, but the summary leaves out backgrounds, geomagnetic exposure and the efficiency definition. It also uses a bin-by-bin MC correction where a response-based unfolding is needed. The bin-by-bin factor is also the step most likely to double count. I can't tell from one sentence which of these you actually do, so I list the ones that would change the verdict at the end.

**Assumptions.** Proton, top-of-instrument differential flux in rigidity, public-style analysis. All numbers are yours, and I supply none. Notation: n_i is the count in reconstructed bin i, b_i the background, A the effective acceptance, ε the efficiency, T the livetime/exposure time, ΔR the bin width.

## Defects, in priority order

**1. Bin-by-bin correction is the diagonal estimator, and it is invalid under migration [General method; Documented AMS practice, C31, C30].**
- Your estimator is Φ_i = n_i / (ΔR_i · A ε T), with an MC factor absorbing migration. That is only valid if migration between rigidity bins is negligible for your precision and spectral steepness.
- The proton spectrum is steeply falling and the rigidity resolution has tails (finite resolution, and the MDR sets the high-R behaviour). Abundant low-R events migrate upward into higher reconstructed bins, so the bias grows with rigidity. A "purity" or "resolution core looks small" argument is not a migration study.
- Published AMS flux analyses correct for migration by unfolding, not by a bin-by-bin factor (C31 for the proton flux, C30 for phosphorus). In C30 the correction runs from about +25% at 3 GV to -21% at 1.2 TV, so it is large and changes sign. That is documented for those papers only.
- A bin-by-bin MC factor also bakes in the MC's assumed true spectrum. If the MC spectrum differs from the data, the factor is biased. This is circular unless you iterate or reweight, and then check convergence.
- **Fix [Proposal]:** define the true and reconstructed bins and build R_ij = P(R_meas in bin i | R_true in bin j, selected), tails included. Then either unfold (iterative Bayesian, regularised, or similar) with a declared stopping criterion, or forward-fold a parametrised flux. Show the bin-by-bin factor is within your precision only if the response says migration is negligible.
- Required validation: MC closure, a reweighted-spectrum closure, a distorted-spectrum stress test, prior variation as a systematic, and toys propagated through the whole unfolding. The unfolding-induced bin correlations belong in the covariance.

**2. Background subtraction is missing: your formula uses raw selected counts.**
- The estimator should use n_i - b_i, with b_i from a ledger. A proton sample has at least these contributions:
  - charge-migration contamination (adjacent |Z|, mainly He);
  - deuteron admixture, which depends on rigidity;
  - an electron/lepton contribution that grows with rigidity;
  - charge-sign confusion (antiproton/negative-sign migration), which is a tail effect;
  - secondaries and albedo at low R.
- For each background you need the origin, the estimator (template fit or similar), a control region with its signal contamination, a closure test and the covariance.
- Charge confusion and rare misidentification are tail phenomena. Do not estimate them from the Gaussian core of the rigidity resolution; validate the tails (invariant 4).

**3. "Acceptance times livetime" is not the exposure.**
- Exposure is the time integral of effective acceptance, times livetime, times geomagnetic transmission. It is not a product of a single A and a single T.
- Geomagnetic cutoff: a primary selection needs |R| above SF x R_c (the local cutoff for position, direction and time). T per bin must include the fraction of time each rigidity is above the cutoff. Without this, low-R bins are wrong.
- Published AMS flux analyses require R above 1.2 times the maximum geomagnetic cutoff and vary this factor as a systematic (documented for S08, C20 and S06, C31; the scan range varies by paper). That is not a rule for yours. State your field model, version and safety factor, and propagate the variation.
- Livetime is the time the trigger and DAQ were live and quality-approved, not calendar time. Check prescales and dead time.
- Vetoed periods (bad detector state, SAA, off-zenith pointing) reduce exposure. Do not also fold them into efficiency, or you count them twice (invariant 3).
- Acceptance and livetime vary with time together, so integrate them jointly; do not multiply their averages.

**4. Where do A and ε come from? Possible double counting.**
- Acceptance, efficiency and exposure are distinct. A_eff = A_geo x average selection efficiency, so if you quote an "acceptance from MC" and then also apply ε, the selection efficiency is counted twice.
- If a response matrix, or your bin-by-bin factor, "includes inefficiency", then A and ε are already inside it and must not be applied again. Your sentence has three corrections (A, ε-like factors, and the MC factor), and the MC factor may restate the first two.
- Check the efficiency chain itself:
  - Efficiencies are conditional: ε_total = ε_1 · ε_2|1 · ε_3|12 .... Do not multiply marginal efficiencies unless independence is shown. Charge, track quality and geometry share information.
  - Test closure with two different orderings and compare the product with the joint efficiency in MC.
  - Apply data/MC scale factors per bin, measured on a signal-free control (tag-and-probe or an orthogonal subsystem). Use exact binomial intervals near 0 or 1.
  - Include survival against upstream material and in-detector interactions, with a cross-section model variation, separate from geometric acceptance.
- Use the audit checklist: one `effect_id` per effect in `corrections`, `response.includes`, `estimator.applies`. Exposure already contains acceptance and livetime.

**5. Bin width and bin centre.**
- Dividing by ΔR gives the bin-average flux. For a steeply falling spectrum, quoting it at the arithmetic bin centre is a bias. State the prescription, for example the position where the flux equals the bin average, or the exact bin-average factor from `scripts/ams_kinematics.py` ([General method]).
- State the variable. If you later convert to kinetic energy per nucleon, you need Z and A (A = 1 for protons, with the deuteron fraction as an uncertainty) and the Jacobian, not a relabelling.

**6. Systematics and validation are not mentioned.**
- At minimum cover: trigger and selection efficiency; acceptance, material and interactions; backgrounds and charge confusion; rigidity scale and resolution (scale shift times the local spectral index, propagated through migration, not applied to the final spectrum); unfolding, prior and regularisation; cutoff model and safety factor; MC generator and transport; MC and template statistics.
- Combine covariances, not variances. Never combine unknown correlations in quadrature. Adjacent flux bins are correlated through unfolding and shared systematics, so quote the covariance, not per-bin percentages.
- "Reasonable agreement" with MC or with a published spectrum is not validation. Name a metric and the deviation that triggers a method change or an added uncertainty (invariant 10). Agreement with a published spectrum is only a plausibility check.
- Cross-checks: loose/tight cuts, alternate track configurations, time-period splits, detector-region splits, MC-based versus data-driven efficiency, and an injected-spectrum closure.

## Corrected outline [Proposal]

1. Estimand: the top-of-instrument proton differential flux Φ(R) in m^-2 sr^-1 s^-1 GV^-1, with true bins {R_j}, reconstructed bins {R_i} and the cutoff treatment declared.
2. Selection ledger (conditional denominators) and the background ledger giving b_i and its covariance.
3. Conditional efficiencies with data/MC scale factors, plus the survival correction.
4. Exposure E_j = ∫ A_eff · T_live · transmission, from the generation record (no circular spectrum).
5. Response R_ij, with orientation and normalisation stated and inefficiency in either R or A, not both.
6. Count model: μ_i = Σ_j R_ij E_j Φ_j ΔR_j + b_i. Unfold or forward-fold for Φ_j.
7. Full covariance, closure and stability checks, and publication of the response and exposure.

The diagonal formula Φ_i = (n_i - b_i)/(ΔR_i A_i ε_i T_i) survives only as a special case, after the response shows negligible migration.

## Inputs that would change the verdict

- Is the MC factor defined as (true signal in the bin) / (selected, reconstructed signal in the bin)? Does it already include A and ε?
- Do you have a response matrix, and what do the off-diagonal terms and tails look like at your highest rigidities?
- Which backgrounds do you subtract and how? Which cutoff model and safety factor, and is T per bin including transmission?
- Data or MC, which period, and which track configuration and hardware era? Public data or internal?
- Precision target and rigidity range: a bin-by-bin factor can be defensible in a narrow, low-rigidity, wide-bin region where migration is shown to be small, but not across the whole range.

If the answers are "factor excludes A and ε, migration shown negligible, backgrounds subtracted, exposure with transmission", the chain is internally consistent. Otherwise, treat it as a likely double count plus a rigidity-dependent bias.
