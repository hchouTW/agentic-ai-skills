**Verdict: not acceptable as designed. The selection is invalid, the dominant electron background is handled by MC alone, and the charge-confusion estimate is a core-based estimate that must be rejected. The three defects are listed below in priority order.**

Assumptions: this is a flux or p̄/p measurement in |R|, and "TRD cut" means a TRD lepton/hadron estimator cut. I have no counts, rigidity range or data-vs-MC status, so everything below is symbolic. The design is a [Proposal] review; it is not AMS practice.

## Defects, in priority order

**1. The signal definition is wrong: a TRD cut does not define "negative-sign tracks".** [General method, invariant 2]
- Charge sign comes only from the Tracker and magnet. The TRD does not measure charge sign and is never a standalone charge-sign detector.
- The TRD (Λ_TRD, a log-likelihood ratio of the e± hypothesis to the proton hypothesis) separates leptons from hadrons through γ-dependent transition radiation.
- The signal must be defined as: Tracker negative sign, |Z| = 1 (Tracker, TOF and others), β consistent with the rigidity, and hadron-like TRD/ECAL response.
- The estimand should also be stated: flux or p̄/p versus |R|, with a rigidity range, a data period and a signed-R convention.

**2. The electron background is subtracted by MC only.** [General method; Proposal]
- Electrons are the dominant negative-sign background at some rigidities. The residual after TRD and ECAL rejection is a tail quantity that depends on the simulated TRD/ECAL response in the extreme lepton-like tail.
- An MC-only subtraction has no control region, no signal contamination check and no closure test, and it carries an unquantified data/MC mismatch.
- Required changes:
  - Fill in the background ledger: origin, entry mechanism, estimator, control region, signal contamination of that control, transfer model, closure test, nuisances and correlations.
  - Use a data-constrained estimator, for example a template fit of a TRD or ECAL discriminant, or of the signed rigidity, with e⁻ templates that are data-derived or MC-corrected by a control. Alternatively use a reversed-cut, e⁻-enriched control with a validated transfer factor.
  - Give each template its provenance without circularity, finite-template statistics (Barlow-Beeston or Conway), per-rigidity-bin shapes, and a check that the p̄ signal template (from proton data) does not contaminate the e⁻ template.
  - Do not use the same ECAL or TRD variable both to define the sample and to derive the template that measures it.
  - Check identifiability and test the fit with signal injection, including zero signal.
- [Documented, C22 (S08 only)] The published AMS antiproton analysis used a template fit to the negative-rigidity sample. The p̄ signal template came from proton data. The electron, π⁻ and charge-confusion-proton templates came from data and MC. The fit used three overlapping rigidity regions.
- [Documented, C26 (S05)] In the electron paper, the antiproton background was below 10⁻³ of the negative sample after the electron cut.
- The recipe above is a proposal modelled on that practice, not a claim that AMS did exactly this.
- Pure MC subtraction conflicts with the documented practice of data-constrained templates.

**3. Charge confusion estimated from the Gaussian rigidity resolution is rejected.** [General method, invariant 4]
- Charge confusion (p → "p̄", the sign-flip tail of the Tracker response) is a non-Gaussian tail phenomenon. It comes from hit-assignment errors, scattering, interactions and large-angle deviations. A Gaussian core extrapolated to 1/R = 0 will underestimate it by orders of magnitude.
- It matters because protons outnumber antiprotons by about 10⁴ at the same |R|, so a tiny tail fraction is a leading background.
- Required changes:
  - Estimate it as a charge-sign migration probability M(p̄←p)(|R|), as a function of rigidity.
  - Derive it from calibrated MC, with the tail validated on data. Use abundant-matter samples (protons under looser cuts), redundant partial track fits and hit-pattern or layer-subset consistency, and test the extrapolation to the tight selection.
  - Use the high-rigidity limit as the stress test: in the MDR region the signed-R tail is largest, and a sign-flip estimate outside a validated tail is unsupported.
  - Include tail-shape alternatives as a systematic, with a stated metric and a trigger for adding an uncertainty or changing the method.
- [Documented, C22 (S08)] The published antiproton analysis checked the charge-confusion MC with 400 GV proton test-beam data. That is a data-driven tail check, not a Gaussian estimate. Quote it only at that claim's scope.
- Charge confusion enters the template fit as its own component, with the signed-rigidity or Tracker-tail information as the discriminating variable.

## Other issues to settle (secondary)

- **Efficiency chain.** TRD, ECAL, Tracker-quality and |Z| cuts are correlated (they share rigidity dependence and the Tracker track). Use conditional efficiencies with ordering closure and a combined-efficiency closure in MC, not a product of marginal efficiencies. Define acceptance, efficiency, exposure and livetime separately, and do not correct one effect twice.
- **Geomagnetic selection.** Specify the cutoff-based selection and its safety factor, since the safety factor affects secondary and albedo contamination at low rigidity.
- **Ratio versus flux.** If the target is p̄/p, acceptance and efficiency cancellation between numerator and denominator must be shown, not assumed. Classify each nuisance as correlated, partial or independent, and carry the joint covariance.
- **Migration.** Check the response matrix for migration, including p/p̄ sign migration as explicit rows and columns. Use an unfolding or forward-folding scheme with closure tests, prior variation and covariance propagation. Do not use a bin-by-bin diagonal correction on a steep spectrum without a migration study. [Documented, C23, S08] The published AMS antiproton analysis used iterative unfolding that stopped when successive fluxes agreed within 0.1%; do not copy that number as a criterion for another analysis.
- **Statistics.** At high rigidity the antiproton counts are low. Use Poisson/exact or toy-validated intervals, and do not use S/√B or Gaussian errors near the boundary.
- **Validation.** "Reasonable agreement" is not validation. State a metric and a trigger for each check.
- **Other backgrounds.** Add π⁻ and secondary or interacting particles, charge-confused protons (item 3), and any e⁻ with bremsstrahlung or conversion mismatches. Each needs a ledger row.
- **Sources.** AMS public antiproton results are S08 (flux and p̄/p, 1-450 GV) and S09 (time dependence). I have not quoted any other AMS number. Label the proposals above as proposals. If a "latest result" or status is relevant, check primary sources; the index was last verified 2026-09-20 and I did no web check here.

## Inputs that would change the verdict

- A data-driven e⁻ control or template fit, with its contamination check and closure, would resolve defect 2.
- A validated tail estimate of the Tracker sign-flip probability (test-beam or abundant-sample data, with extrapolation tests) would resolve defect 3.
- Confirmation that the Tracker sign is part of the signal definition, with the TRD used only as a hadron-like selector, would resolve defect 1.
- The rigidity range, whether the target is flux or ratio, the data period, and whether the response matrix includes efficiency.
- Whether the work is public or internal. Any user-supplied internal numbers will be labeled as such.
