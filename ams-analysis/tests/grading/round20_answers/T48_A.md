**What I can document**

1. **The primary AMS proton flux** [Documented, C05, C31; S06, PRL 114, 171103 (2015); full-text, main article only]
   - Range: 1 GV to 1.8 TV, in rigidity, about 300 million events.
   - Data period: 19 May 2011 to 26 Nov 2013 (the first 30 months), 72 rigidity bins.
   - Selection: live time above 50%, z axis within 40 degrees of zenith, outside the South Atlantic Anomaly, R > 1.2 x maximum cutoff, iterative unfolding for bin-to-bin migration.
   - The values themselves are in the paper's tables. They are not in the skill's index. Use the paper as your reference.
   - Later AMS proton results exist, for example daily and Bartels-rotation proton fluxes (S41, S43). They have different periods and ranges. Match the dataset to the paper before comparing.

2. **CRDB (LPSC Grenoble), https://lpsc.in2p3.fr/crdb/** [Documented, S50, C125-C128]. This is a secondary compilation of published data.
   - Version and size: on 2026-10-02 the page gave V4.2 (September 2026), 143 experiments from 614 publications.
   - Access: website, REST interface, `pip install crdb`.
   - A test query (proton flux, native rigidity axis, `exp_dates=AMS`, conversions and combinations off) returned two AMS02 sub-experiments with different data-taking ranges. Both start 2011/05/19. One ends 2013/11/26 and the other 2018/05/26. Each had 72 rows. The CRDB release was not recorded and no value was compared with a paper.
   - Which AMS proton datasets and values the current release holds is [Unknown/needs input] until you query it and record the version and date.
   - To query it yourself, use the native rigidity axis with `combo_level=0` and `energy_convert_level=0`, restricted to AMS: `curl -L 'http://lpsc.in2p3.fr/crdb/rest.php?num=H&den=&energy_type=R&exp_dates=AMS&combo_level=0&energy_convert_level=0'`. The helper `scripts/crdb_query.py` does this politely, with one request and a metadata sidecar, and `summarize` lists the sub-experiments. Check the exact `num`/`den` spelling against the REST page (C128).
   - Do not merge the sub-experiments. Time series are excluded by default (C126, C128).
   - A systematic of 0 in CRDB means "not separately quoted" (C126). Take AMS systematics from the paper.

3. **ASI SSDC Cosmic Ray Database, https://tools.ssdc.asi.it/CosmicRays/sf_search.jsp** [Documented, C183 (search-form screenshot only, 2026-10-02, no query run) and C130 (2017 proceedings abstract)].

   **What the page says about itself**
   - It is headed "COSMIC RAY Database" ("Database for charged Cosmic Ray measurements") and shows "Version 3.3". That is its own label and is not the LPSC CRDB version, although the page calls itself "CRDB".
   - It gives access to published data from missions dedicated to charged cosmic-ray measurements. The data-set is "not comprehensive but in expansion".
   - The data sit in a SQL database. You search by particle species, measurement and/or mission name, and a refined search is available.
   - Results come as a table to plot, export and download. They include the published points with uncertainties and some meta-data.
   - Manipulated data, such as an energy-rigidity conversion or a unit change, are flagged in the output file.
   - It tells you to consult the original publication.

   **What the search form shows**
   - A Particle selector.
   - A "Plot: - vs -" pair of selectors.
   - An Experiments selector reading "All selected (25)".
   - Special-dataset boxes "SEP events" and "trapped".
   - SEARCH and RESET buttons.
   - A button "Most recent time-dependent AMS data available here!".
   - Login and "Feedback and contacts" links.

   **Context from the 2017 proceedings (older, not the current holdings)**
   - The database was "under development" and built to support PAMELA and AMS-02 retrieval.

**How to do the cross-check**
1. Fix the estimand: proton flux, rigidity (GV), time-averaged (S06) versus time-resolved.
2. Pull the AMS rows from CRDB on the native rigidity axis. Map each `SUBEXP_NAME` and its data-taking range to the AMS paper (the 2011/05/19-2013/11/26 range matches S06). Keep `PUBLI-DATAORIGIN` (table or plot) and record the CRDB version and retrieval date.
3. Do the same in the SSDC page through a browser query. Record its version, the date and the exact selection. Check the output file's flag for manipulated data (conversions, unit changes).
4. Compare both against the paper's table, and your own flux against all three. The paper wins any disagreement. Transcription from a plot, rebinning or a different data period are the likely causes and are worth reporting to the database maintainers.
5. Use a stated metric and trigger rather than "consistent" (invariant 10). Example: per-bin pull of your flux against the paper value using statistical and systematic uncertainties added in quadrature, flagging bins where |pull| exceeds a threshold you set in advance. This is a [Proposal] and is not AMS practice. Do not treat agreement between the databases as validation, since both are transcriptions.

**Rules to keep**
- A database value is secondary. Label it `[Secondary: CRDB or SSDC, version, retrieval date]`. It is never an AMS result.
- Do not average or merge sub-experiments or datasets with different periods or analyses.
- Do not convert rigidity to kinetic energy per nucleon without stating |Z|=1, A=1 and the proton mass. Use `scripts/ams_kinematics.py`.
