# Limit setting for a rare antinucleus in AMS-02 with zero observed events

## Verdict and scope

Zero observed events is the easy part of the statistics. The hard part is turning "0 events" into a flux or ratio limit, which requires a defensible exposure, an efficiency chain, and a background/charge-confusion estimate for the tail. The design below is a **[Proposal]** built on **[General method]** statistics. I am assuming a public-style analysis of antihelium or antideuteron, a flux (or antinucleus/matter ratio) upper limit, and a frequentist 95% CL. The target species (anti-He vs anti-D) and the rigidity range change the details, but not the structure.

Source status (index last verified 2026-09-20; browsing not used here, so I make no claim of currency):
- **[Documented, C14, search-limited]** No peer-reviewed AMS-02 antihelium paper was found in INSPIRE-HEP. The only AMS-authored antihelium limit in the index is the 1999 AMS-01 result (S22, historical).
- **[Documented, S28/C19]** No AMS antideuteron measurement or search paper was found, only a 2008 sensitivity conference paper and third-party prospects.
- I therefore do not quote any AMS candidate counts, livetimes, acceptances, rejection factors or published limits. Everything below is symbolic. Any numbers you supply are treated as **[Unknown/needs input]** or user-supplied.

## 1. Estimand (define before the statistics)

Pick one and state it:
- Flux limit: `Φ_UL(R_i)` in a stated rigidity (or kinetic-energy-per-nucleon) range, for a stated charge `|Z|` and mass hypothesis.
- Ratio limit: antinucleus/nucleus, e.g. `N̄He/He`. This is only useful if acceptance and efficiency partly cancel, and cancellation must be shown, not assumed.
- Integrated limit over a predefined range, if the data are too sparse for per-bin limits.

State the variable: rigidity `R = pc/(Ze)` (GV) is not momentum; `p = |Z|R/c`. If the result is quoted in kinetic energy per nucleon, state `Z` and `A` for the conversion (use `scripts/ams_kinematics.py` for the arithmetic). State the sign convention. Charge sign comes only from the Tracker and magnet; TOF, TRD, ECAL and RICH give `|Z|`, `β`, and other information, but never charge sign.

## 2. Counting model and the zero-count bound

Observed `n = 0` in a predefined signal region. Expected counts:

`μ = s(Φ) + b`, with `s = Φ · E · Δx` (flux case), `E` = exposure (m² sr s).

**[General method] Zero-count Poisson bound.**
- If `b = 0` (or neglected): one-sided 95% CL upper limit on signal counts is `N_UL = -ln(0.05) ≈ 3.00`.
- For known `b > 0` and `n = 0`, the two common constructions differ, and you must say which you use:
  - Classical (frequentist) limit: `N_UL = -ln(0.05) - b`. It shrinks with `b` and can reach zero or go negative. That is a known pathology: a downward fluctuation gives a "better than sensitivity" limit that is not meaningful.
  - CLs: `N_UL = -ln(0.05)`, independent of `b`. It is a conservative convention, not a 95% confidence statement in the strict sense.
  - Feldman-Cousins gives a different, `b`-dependent result that handles the boundary without the negative-limit problem.
- Do not present `-ln α` as the classical limit for arbitrary `b`.
- Report the **expected (median) sensitivity** and its bands from toys of the background-only hypothesis, so the reader can see whether `n = 0` is a lucky or an expected outcome. If the expected background is non-negligible and you observed zero, the observed limit lies at or below the median expected limit, and that must be said explicitly.
- No `S/√B`, Wilks, or asymptotic formulae. Counts are at the boundary; validate by toys. `scripts/poisson_diagnostics.py` (see `statistical-diagnostics`) gives the exact classical numbers and a seeded coverage check, but only for known background.

## 3. Flux and ratio conversion

`Φ_UL = N_UL / (E · Δx)`, where `E = ∫ A_eff(R,t) · T_live(t) · (geomagnetic transmission) dt` is the exposure for the **signal species**. Do not multiply acceptance and livetime again if `E` already contains them.

Points specific to an antinucleus:
- `A_eff` and `ε` must be for the antinucleus, not copied from the matter analog. Hadronic interaction cross sections of antinuclei differ from nuclei (annihilation in material above and inside the detector), so the survival probability through upstream material is a separate, model-dependent factor. Its cross-section model is a systematic and must be varied.
- Geomagnetic transmission depends on `|Z|/A` rigidity cutoffs, so use the signal species' own cutoff and safety factor.
- Unknown spectral shape within a bin: acceptance and efficiency depend on the assumed signal spectrum. Either quote the limit for a stated spectrum (flat, power law with an index, or a model), or quote it at the least favorable assumption. Avoid circularity (target spectrum used to tune acceptance).
- Efficiency chain: `ε_total = ε_1 · ε_2|1 · ε_3|12 ...` with conditional factors. Do not multiply marginal efficiencies; correlated cuts (Tracker charge, TOF/Tracker `|Z|`, track quality) must be measured jointly or bounded. Without independence, Fréchet bounds only give a floor and ceiling, and the floor is not an estimate. Do an ordering-closure and combined-efficiency closure test in MC.
- Efficiency for a species that is essentially absent from flight data cannot be measured directly. It must come from MC with data/MC validation on abundant proxies (e.g. He or p for the `|Z|` and track-quality parts, with the caveat that antinucleus interactions differ). The proxy extrapolation is a systematic. Label this **[Proposal]**.

## 4. The part that actually sets the limit: background and the tail

With zero observed events you still must justify that the *expected* background is small, since the classical limit depends on it and a reader will ask "how many events would you have expected from matter confusion?"

Background ledger (see `efficiency-acceptance-backgrounds`), each with origin, control region, transfer, closure and nuisance:
1. **Charge-sign confusion of the abundant same-`|Z|` matter** (e.g. He → anti-He, d → anti-d, p → apparent antideuteron through wrong mass): a Tracker tail phenomenon via bad hit assignment, multiple scattering, and interactions. Estimating this from the Gaussian core of the rigidity resolution is wrong; the tail must be validated.
2. **`|Z|` confusion**, from fragmentation and charge-changing interactions (e.g. a `Z=2` track whose measured charge is misassigned).
3. **Wrong mass from `β` errors** (for antideuteron versus antiproton; mass from `R` and `β`).
4. Secondary tracks and interactions producing antiparticles; albedo.

Data-driven tail estimate: use abundant matter samples under looser cuts to measure the sign-flip probability as a function of rigidity, then extrapolate to the tight selection. The extrapolation assumptions must be tested (e.g. across selection loosening steps, partial-fit consistency, data periods). Redundant signed-rigidity fits (different layer subsets, upper/lower segments) and hit-pattern checks provide independent handles. Subsystem `|Z|` consistency (Tracker, TOF, RICH) within validated tails is an extra requirement.

The charge-confusion probability enters as a nuisance with its own uncertainty (typically a one-sided constraint from the abundant-sample measurement, since it is estimated from extrapolation). Expected background `b ≪ 1` over the whole sample is the desirable outcome, but the claim "b ≪ 1" needs its own evidence; I do not give a number.

## 5. Procedure (blinding and predefinition)

1. Define the signal region in `(R, β, |Z|, sign)`, selections, bins, and the statistical construction **before** looking at the signal region. Mask it in plots, logs, and optimization. State who unblinds and when.
2. Compute the expected sensitivity (median expected limit with bands) from background-only toys, with nuisance variation, before unblinding.
3. Define candidate-level diagnostics in advance (event display checklist, hit pattern, residuals, partial-fit consistency, subsystem estimators), so that if events appear the interpretation is not improvised.
4. Unblind; with `n = 0` apply the chosen construction. Quote both the observed and the expected limit.
5. Binning policy: per-rigidity-bin limits with `n = 0` in each bin are not independent "tests", so state whether there is a trial factor (it matters for any later excess, not for the limit itself). A single integrated limit is the robust headline; per-bin limits are secondary.

## 6. Nuisance treatment

Likelihood: `L = Poisson(0 | s·(1+ηE)… + b(η_cc, …)) × Π_a C_a(η_a)`, where the auxiliary measurements `C_a` are control-sample results (charge-confusion probability from abundant samples, efficiency scale factors, interaction cross-section variation). These are not arbitrary priors, unless the analysis is explicitly Bayesian (in which case state the prior on flux; a flat prior on counts gives the same `3.00` for `b = 0` but a flat prior on flux, or a log prior, would not).

- Uncertainty on `E` (acceptance, efficiency, livetime, cross-section model, geomagnetic cutoff) enters the signal normalization. Treat it by profiling or hybrid marginalization, and state which. For a limit this is typically a modest degradation of the limit; the effect should be shown, not assumed small.
- Correlations between the background model and exposure (both use the same MC and the same control samples) must be carried, not combined in quadrature when unknown.
- Check coverage in the low-count regime with toys, including `n = 0` and signal strength near zero.

## 7. What to report

- The limit definition: one-sided, CL, construction (classical / CLs / Feldman-Cousins / profile with toys), nuisance method, signal spectrum assumed.
- Observed and expected limits (median and bands), with the exposure `E` and its uncertainty per bin or for the integrated range.
- The conversion `Φ_UL = N_UL/(E Δx)` and how `N_UL` follows from the construction.
- The expected background and how it was estimated, plus the validation of the tail.
- Wording: zero events means "no candidate observed; upper limit X at 95% CL", not "evidence of absence". If one or more events passed, they are candidates (not an excess, not a discovery) and reporting would follow predefined thresholds.

## 8. Cross-checks and failure modes

Cross-checks: orthogonal identification chains; loose/tight cut variations; period and geometry splits; charge-sign splits; alternate track fits; signal injection into data to prove the selection can see an antinucleus (closure with injected events); comparison with published limits for plausibility only; independent analyses.

Failure modes to avoid: estimating charge confusion from the Gaussian core; applying matter efficiency to the antinucleus without the interaction-cross-section correction; multiplying marginal efficiencies; using `S/√B` or Wilks at `n = 0`; quoting only the classical limit with `b > 0` and a negative or implausibly small result; looking at the signal region before fixing the cuts; presenting another experiment's method as AMS practice (analogy or proposal only).

## 9. Inputs that would change the design

- Species (antihelium vs antideuteron) and rigidity range: sets the dominant confusion path (He→anti-He sign flips versus p/d mass-confusion).
- Whether you have an expected background `b` and its uncertainty: decides classical vs CLs vs Feldman-Cousins.
- Whether a blinded signal region and a signal-injection capability exist.
