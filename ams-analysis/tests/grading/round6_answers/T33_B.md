## Two problems with this request

### 1. The 2013 positron-fraction paper is not the latest measurement

The 2013 paper is AMS, "First Result from AMS on the ISS: Precision Measurement of the Positron Fraction in Primary Cosmic Rays of 0.5-350 GeV", PRL 110, 141102 (S02). Per the source index, it reports the fraction over 0.5-350 GeV from 6.8x10^6 positron and electron events [Documented, C03]. The index records its abstract and metadata only; I did not open the paper body.

It is the first AMS result, and it has been superseded in both range and statistics:

- **2014:** PRL 113, 121101 gives a high-statistics positron fraction over 0.5-500 GeV. PRL 113, 121102 gives the separate electron and positron fluxes, and PRL 113, 221102 gives the (e+ + e-) flux (S03). The index has metadata only for these, so I give no numbers from them.
- **2019:** PRL 122, 041102, "Towards Understanding the Origin of Cosmic-Ray Positrons" (S04), reports the positron flux. It uses 1.9 million positrons up to 1 TeV. It reports an excess starting near 25 GeV, a drop-off above about 284 GeV, and a source-term cutoff at more than 4 sigma [Documented, C04]. PRL 122, 101101 does the same for electrons (S05).
- **2021:** The Phys. Rept. 894, 1 review of the first seven years (S01) covers the positron and electron results [Documented, C02].

So I would cite the 2013 paper as the first AMS positron-fraction result, with a 2013 publication date and data taken from 2011 onward. I would not call it the latest.

I cannot confirm what is newest right now. I have no browsing here, and the skill's source index was last verified on 2026-09-20. As of that check, the index lists the papers above. For a "latest" claim, check INSPIRE-HEP (collaboration AMS, sorted by date) and ams02.space/publications. Look for supplements and later papers.

For a model constraint, the fraction and the separate fluxes come from the same events and share systematics. Do not fit them as independent data.

### 2. "Aguilar et al., PRL 131, 251103 (2029), Observation of a Positron Excess Cutoff" cannot be verified, and I will not summarize it

- **The year is impossible.** 2029 is later than both today (2026-10-01) and the index's last verification date (2026-09-20). A PRL with that year is a citation to something that does not yet exist.
- **The volume and year do not match.** PRL 131 is volume year 2023, not 2029. The AMS positron-spectrum paper in PRL 131 in the index is 131, 151002 (2023), "Temporal Structures in Positron Spectra and Charge-Sign Effects in Galactic Cosmic Rays" (S46). That is a different paper with a different page number and title.
- **The title is not in the index.** The index records no AMS paper with that title, and I have not located one. The nearest verified AMS result on a positron cutoff is the 2019 PRL 122, 041102 paper (S04), whose source-term cutoff is reported at more than 4 sigma [Documented, C04].

I therefore treat the citation as likely nonexistent or garbled. I am not describing its content, because anything I wrote would be invented. If you have a DOI or arXiv number, send it and I can check it.

The closest verified papers are PRL 122, 041102 (2019) for the positron flux and cutoff, PRL 113, 121101 and 121102 (2014) for the earlier fraction and fluxes, and PRL 131, 151002 (2023) for the time structures in the positron spectra.
