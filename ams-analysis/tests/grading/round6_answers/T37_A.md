**What AMS reports.** [Documented, C84; AMS Phys. Rept. 894 review, S01, Section 10, covering the first seven years of data.] The Li, Be and B fluxes were divided by the C and O fluxes (Li/C, Be/C, B/C, Li/O, Be/O, B/O). A single power law, Phi_S/Phi_P proportional to R^Delta, was fitted to each ratio in two non-overlapping rigidity intervals, 60.3-192 GV and 192-3300 GV. Above about 200 GV the secondary-to-primary spectral index hardens on average by

Delta[192-3300] - Delta[60.3-192] = 0.140 +- 0.025.

The review states this significance exceeds 5 sigma. In other words, the ratios fall less steeply with rigidity above ~200 GV than they do at 60-192 GV.

Related context, each with its own claim:
- [Documented, C81] The primary He, C and O fluxes harden progressively from about 200 GV, with identical rigidity dependence above 60 GV.
- [Documented, C83] The secondary Li, Be and B fluxes share their rigidity dependence above about 30 GV.
- [Documented, C84] The secondary spectral indices are nearly identical to each other but distinctly different from those of He, C and O.
- [Documented, C82] The review notes that the p/He ratio index above 3.5 GV (Delta = -0.30 +- 0.01) is close to the high-rigidity index of the secondary-to-primary ratios (-0.266 +- 0.022 above 192 GV). That comparison is the review's, not a measured equality.

**How significant is it?** The headline is "more than 5 sigma", and it is the review's own statement for the average hardening across the ratios. Here is what that figure does and does not establish.
- [Documented, C84] It covers the change in index between the two fixed intervals, averaged over the secondary/primary ratios. It does not cover a precise onset rigidity.
- [Unknown/needs input] I did not read how the significance was computed. That includes how systematic correlations between numerator and denominator were handled, whether the interval boundaries were chosen after seeing the data, and whether any trial-factor correction was applied. The 192 GV boundary is the review's choice, not a prescription. Beyond the quoted statement, treat the exact significance as unverified.
- [Documented, C85] The review's ratio tables treat the cross-section, unfolding and absolute-rigidity-scale systematics as correlated between numerator and denominator. Other systematics are added in quadrature. The correlation coefficients themselves are not given.
- Li/C, Be/C, B/C and the O-normalized ratios share the same events and the same C and O fluxes. The "average" hardening is therefore not five independent confirmations.
- Using the same 0.140 +- 0.025 as a rough check, the central value is 5.6 times its quoted error. That is consistent with the ">5 sigma" statement but is only my back-of-envelope arithmetic [General method]. Do not quote it as AMS's own calculation.

**Interpretation is separate from the measurement.** The review reads the hardening as consistent with expectations if it arises from propagation in the Galaxy. If it came from source-injection hardening, secondaries and primaries would harden similarly. This is the review's interpretation, and I did not read the third-party model papers. The review also states that no model predicted the observed behaviour of the primary He, C, O or the secondary Li, Be, B (C84). That statement is the review's opinion, not a test of any model.

- All numbers come from the Phys. Rept. review's data set (C84, S01). They should not be attached to the earlier separate B/C paper (S10, C08). Its data period is shorter and it reported a power-law index above 65 GV.
