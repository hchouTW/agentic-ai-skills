**Verdict: not publishable as described. The structure is the right skeleton, but as written it has a likely bias (migration handled by a bin-by-bin factor), a likely double-count (the MC factor against acceptance and efficiency), and several missing pieces (background subtraction, cutoff and exposure, systematics, closure). Several of these cannot be settled from your one-sentence description; the inputs that would change the verdict are listed at the end.**

Your description, as I read it: `Φ_i = N_i / (A · T_live · ΔR_i) × C_i^MC`, where `C_i` is a bin-by-bin MC correction. A "proton flux" here means a differential flux in rigidity at the top of the instrument.

## Defects, in priority order

1. **A bin-by-bin MC factor is not an unfolding [General method].**
   - The diagonal estimator is only valid when migration between rigidity bins is negligible. With a steeply falling spectrum, lower true bins feed higher reconstructed bins, so the bias grows with rigidity.
   - Tracker resolution has non-Gaussian tails, and these matter most at high rigidity.
   - A bin-by-bin factor `C_i = N_true,i / N_reco,i` from MC absorbs the migration only for the spectrum that MC was generated with. If the true spectrum differs from the MC spectrum, for example a spectral hardening, the factor is biased. It is also circular if you tune the MC spectrum to your own result.
   - Published AMS flux analyses correct for migration by unfolding. For the proton flux, the unfolding correction is documented as part of `N/(A ε ΔR T)` [Documented, C31]. For phosphorus, the migration correction ranges from about +25% at 3 GV to -21% at 1.2 TV [Documented, C30]. The size depends on species and range, so it is not a small effect. Do not carry that number over to protons.
   - Fix: build the response matrix `R_ij = P(R_meas in bin i | R_true in bin j, selected)`, tails included. Then either unfold (with a stated prior or regularization, iteration, and stopping criterion) or forward-fold a parametrized `Φ(R;θ)`. If you keep a factor, show a closure test with a different input spectrum and add a prior-dependence systematic.

2. **Double counting between "acceptance" and the MC correction.** This is the most likely silent error.
   - If `A` is the effective acceptance, it already contains the selection and reconstruction efficiency, and any response matrix that "includes inefficiency" already contains `A` and `ε`. Applying a further MC factor that is also efficiency-like corrects the same effect twice.
   - If `A` is geometric only, then the trigger and selection efficiencies, and survival against upstream material and interactions, are missing from your formula.
   - Exposure is the time-integrated effective acceptance, so it already includes livetime. Check that the factor in your formula is not applied on top of it a second time.
   - Fix: write out one line per effect (geometry, trigger, each selection, survival and interaction, migration, livetime, cutoff, veto) and assign each to exactly one category: veto, calibration, efficiency, or response. Do not apply a single effect in two places.

3. **Efficiencies come only from MC.**
   - Efficiencies should be conditional factors with named denominators, measured with tag-and-probe, orthogonal subsystems or unbiased triggers. The data/MC scale factor carries its own uncertainty. A scale factor measured on one species or period should not be reused for another without justification (AMS practice: S04, C25; S13, C27).
   - Do not multiply marginal efficiencies as if the cuts were independent. The Tracker charge estimate, track quality and rigidity cuts are correlated. Check factorization bias, ordering closure, and the combined efficiency against MC truth.
   - Whether your bin-by-bin factor is an efficiency, a migration correction, or both is the question your description leaves open. It must be one or the other, or it must be split.

4. **Livetime and geomagnetic transmission are missing or unspecified.**
   - `T` should be quality-approved livetime (trigger prescale and dead time, bad-run vetoes), not calendar time.
   - The "primary" selection needs a cutoff treatment: rigidity above a safety factor times the maximum geomagnetic cutoff in the field of view. State the model and the factor, and vary it as a systematic. Published AMS analyses use 1.2 times the cutoff in the antiproton and proton analyses and scan about 1.0 to 1.4 [Documented, C20, C23, C31]. Other papers differ, so this is not a single AMS standard.
   - Below the cutoff, secondary and albedo populations appear. The low-rigidity bins need exposure that depends on rigidity.

5. **"Events after cuts" has no background subtraction.**
   - Proton backgrounds to model per rigidity bin: He and deuteron contamination, charge confusion in the Tracker `|Z|` and sign, electron contamination at low rigidity, and interactions in upstream material.
   - Use a ledger entry per background: origin, estimator (template fit with finite-template statistics), control region and its signal contamination, and closure test.
   - The estimator should be `(n_i − b_i)`, not `n_i`.

6. **Charge confusion is a tail effect.** Validate it on the tails of the rigidity resolution, not on the Gaussian core. This matters most at high rigidity, where sign misidentification and tail migration grow.

7. **Bin convention.** Dividing by bin width gives a bin-average flux. For a steep spectrum, quoting it at the arithmetic bin centre is biased, so state a prescription for where the flux is quoted. If you later convert to kinetic energy per nucleon, include the Jacobian and state `Z` and `A` (for protons `A = 1`, and the conversion uses `scripts/ams_kinematics.py`).

8. **Systematics and covariance.** At minimum:
   - trigger and selection efficiency, and acceptance and material (cross-section model variation);
   - background and template, and charge confusion;
   - rigidity-scale and resolution uncertainty, propagated through migration (shift the reconstructed variable and re-run the response, not only rescale the final spectrum);
   - unfolding prior and regularization, and the cutoff safety factor and model;
   - MC statistics and generator or hadronic-model choice.
   - Combine as a covariance. Do not add in quadrature when correlations are unknown. Fluxes in adjacent bins are correlated through unfolding and shared systematics, so report the full covariance.

9. **MC provenance and data/MC validation.** Record generator, spectrum, generation surface, physics list, geometry and reconstruction version. A version mismatch between MC and data is a defect. "Agreement" needs a metric and a trigger for adding an uncertainty or changing the method.

10. **Validation.** Closure on MC with a truth recovery test using a different input spectrum, stability versus time, detector region, geometry, and track configuration (for example different layer sets), and loose-versus-tight cut variations. Comparison with published AMS spectra is a plausibility check only; agreement does not prove correctness.

## What I would propose [Proposal]

Replace your estimator with a response-model one:

`μ_i = Σ_j R_ij A_j ε_j T_j Φ_j ΔR_j + b_i`

Then unfold, or forward-fold, for `Φ_j`, with covariance and the full systematic ledger. The diagonal estimator `Φ_i = (n_i − b_i) / (ΔR_i A_i ε_i T_i)` is acceptable only with a response-based validation that migration is negligible in that range.

## Inputs that would change this verdict (all [Unknown/needs input])

- **What the bin-by-bin factor contains.** Whether it is efficiency, migration, or both, and whether `A` is geometric or effective. This decides the double-count question in defect 2.
- **Rigidity range.** The bias is small at low rigidity and large at high rigidity, where the resolution tails matter.
- **How the factor is derived.** Which MC spectrum, whether it was tuned to your result, and what closure test exists with a different input spectrum.
- **Public or internal data.** I cannot supply AMS cuts, acceptance, livetime or systematic sizes, so all numbers stay yours (user-supplied).
- **Cutoff model and safety factor, and how backgrounds are handled** (defects 4 and 5).
