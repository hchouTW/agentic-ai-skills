**Verdict: no, don't clip and move on.** There are two separate problems, and they probably interact. Fix the response and efficiency bookkeeping first, then rebuild the covariance.

## 1. A correlation of -1.3 is a construction error, not round-off

[General method] For any valid covariance, |ρ_ij| = |C_ij| / sqrt(C_ii C_jj) <= 1. A value of -1.3 between bins 4 and 5 means the 2x2 sub-block has a negative determinant. So the matrix is not positive semidefinite by a wide margin, not by machine precision.

The repair rule in the skill's covariance procedure:
- **Clipping is only for numerical round-off.** That means the most negative eigenvalue is at about machine precision times the largest eigenvalue, and the chi2 or fit is unchanged after the clip.
- **A -1.3 correlation is a construction error.** Clipping would hide it. It would also change the very bin 4-5 anticorrelation that the unfolded-flux fit depends on, and the result would look plausible.

Check these in order:
1. Symmetrize (C + C^T)/2 and compute the eigenvalues. Compare the most negative to the largest. Run `scripts/validate_covariance.py` on the matrix with its declared metadata. It can show the effect of a clip as a labeled [Proposal] without changing your matrix.
2. Look for mixed conventions: absolute vs relative entries, bin ordering, units, or a covariance assembled from per-bin errors and a rounded or guessed correlation table.
3. Look for components added twice, or a subtracted term whose implied correlation is implausible.
4. Look for unfolding-induced anticorrelation between neighbouring bins. An under-regularized or near-inverted unfolding produces strong negative neighbour correlations. A singular or near-singular matrix can mean over-regularization or an implicit constraint.
5. Rebuild from coherent variations (a shift of one nuisance moving many bins, plus statistical terms). Each block is then PSD by construction.
6. Propagate through the full unfolding with toys. The sample covariance of the toys is PSD and includes the unfolding-induced correlations.
7. Never "fix" it by dropping correlations or by quadrature-combining components whose correlations are unknown.

## 2. Column sums of 0.85, combined with a separate trigger efficiency, is a likely double count

The response model is mu_i = sum_j R_ij A_j T_j Phi_j DeltaX_j + backgrounds. Where inefficiency lives must be stated once:
- **R conditional on the selected sample:** columns sum to 1 (to within stored under/overflow), and inefficiency lives in A_j or in the explicit efficiency factor.
- **R includes efficiency:** columns sum to the efficiency, which is at most 1, and you must not apply A or eps again.

A column sum of 0.85 has two legitimate readings:
- **(a) Inefficiency is inside R.** Then your extra trigger-efficiency factor is probably applied a second time, if the trigger loss is part of that 15%.
- **(b) Migration outside the tabulated range.** True events landed in reconstructed bins that are not stored (under/overflow). Then 0.85 says nothing about efficiency. The column sums would not be a reason to skip the trigger efficiency. Feed-in from outside the true range can distort the row totals too.

Do not call 0.85 "inefficiency" until you have checked whether the matrix stores under/overflow rows, and what its declared orientation and normalization are. Run `scripts/validate_response.py` with the declared metadata (`conditional_on_selected` vs `includes_efficiency`).

Then list each correction (trigger efficiency, selection efficiency, acceptance, livetime, geomagnetic transmission) with where it is applied. Each effect should have exactly one `effect_id`, and exposure already contains acceptance and livetime. If the trigger efficiency is in the MC that built R and also multiplied on the counts, you have corrected it twice. That biases the flux by a constant or energy-dependent factor, and it feeds into the covariance as well.

The two problems can interact. If R is normalized wrongly, the unfolding is ill-conditioned or biased. That can produce the strong negative neighbour correlations and the non-PSD result. Fixing the response is therefore the first thing to try, before rebuilding the covariance.

## 3. What to do, in order

1. Define the estimand and the response: orientation, normalization, under/overflow, and where each inefficiency lives.
2. Run `validate_response.py` and `validate_covariance.py`. A pass only means self-consistency under the declared metadata, not physical validity.
3. Remove any double-applied efficiency.
4. Rebuild the covariance from coherent components. Propagate with toys through the full unfolding (statistical, MC/template statistical, systematic and unfolding-induced pieces carried separately).
5. Validate the unfolding: nominal and reweighted-spectrum closure, stress tests, response perturbations, pulls/coverage, and the iteration count or regularization chosen without tuning on the final spectrum.
6. Only if the remaining negative eigenvalue is at round-off level, clip, record the change, and confirm the result is unchanged within tolerance.

## 4. Inputs that would change this verdict

- The most negative eigenvalue relative to the largest, and whether anything beyond bins 4-5 is bad. If only a hand-edited correlation entry is wrong, the repair is local.
- How the covariance was built (toys, analytic propagation, or hand-assembled), and its convention (absolute or relative, bin order).
- The declared response convention, and whether under/overflow rows and columns are stored.
- Whether the trigger efficiency is already in the MC that produced R and the 0.85 sums.
- The unfolding method and its regularization strength.
