**Short answer: no and no.** The positron cutoff is not a 5 sigma result, and the electron flux does not show the same cutoff.

**Positron cutoff significance**

- [Documented, C35, C97, C04] AMS reports a source-term energy cutoff for positrons of E_s = 810 (+310, -180) GeV. The significance is 4.07 sigma. The abstract of the 2019 PRL (S04) says "more than 4 sigma". A claim of 5 sigma is not supported.
- The cutoff belongs to a parametrized model, not to a feature seen directly in the data. The fit is a diffuse term plus a source term with an exponential cutoff, fitted over 0.5-1000 GeV with a force-field solar-modulation term. The cutoff is the point where 1/E_s = 0.
- 1/E_s = 1.23 +- 0.34 TeV^-1, and 1/E_s = 0 lies at 4.07 sigma. The significance came from varying all six fit parameters to map the 1 to 5 sigma regions. It has no look-elsewhere correction.
- 810 GeV is therefore a fit parameter with large asymmetric errors. Do not call it a measured peak or a sharp break.
- [Documented, C96, S01 review] The model-independent piecewise power-law fit gives a different quantity. It finds a spectral fall-off with a transition at E0 = 284 (+91, -64) GeV, and the decrease is stated at more than 3 sigma. These are chi2-difference statements for a fixed model pair with the transition energy free, and they are not look-elsewhere corrected.
- Do not mix the 284 GeV transition (more than 3 sigma) with the 810 GeV source-term cutoff (4.07 sigma). They are different parametrizations.
- The AMS Collaboration reads the positron behaviour qualitatively as a new high-energy source, astrophysical or dark matter. That is an interpretation, not a measurement.
- A projection to more statistics is a plan, not a result.

**Does the electron flux show the same cutoff?**

- [Documented, C99, S01 review] No. The electron data neither require nor exclude a charge-symmetric positron-like source term. Over 41.61-1400 GeV:
  - Fixing the source normalization at f = 1 gives chi2/d.o.f. = 15.5/24.
  - Fixing f = 0 gives 15.2/24.
  - Leaving f free gives f = 0.5 (+1.2, -0.6) with no chi2 improvement.
  - So the data do not discriminate between the two cases. The source parameters were held fixed from the positron fit, so their uncertainty is not propagated.
- A single power law with an exponential cutoff, fitted over 41.61-1400 GeV, excludes cutoff energies E_s < 1.9 TeV at 5 sigma. This is a limit within that model and energy range, not a claim about every cutoff shape. It is not a detection.
- So the 5 sigma number in the electron analysis is an exclusion of a cutoff below 1.9 TeV. It is not a 5 sigma positron cutoff.
- [Documented, C98] The electron flux (28.1 million events up to 1.4 TeV) is described by a power law with a smooth transition at E0 = 42.1 (+5.4, -5.2) GeV. The review states the nature of the electron excess is different from that of the positron excess. The electron and positron spectra differ in magnitude and in energy dependence.
- Both lepton species harden above about 20 GeV, but they behave distinctly differently. The electron index becomes nearly energy independent above 55.58 GeV.

**Caveats**

- The numbers come from the 2019 positron and electron PRLs (S04, S05, main articles only) and the Phys. Rept. review (S01). The skill's source index was last verified 2026-09-20.
- I did not browse for newer papers. I cannot claim currency beyond that date. For "latest" status, check INSPIRE-HEP and ams02.space for any updated significance with more data.
