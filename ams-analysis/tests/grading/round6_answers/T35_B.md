**Short answer:** You can assume a TRD proton rejection of more than about 1000 at 90% e± efficiency. This is a lower bound, not a fixed number. I can't give a single universal value for 100 GeV.

**What is documented**
- [Documented, C67; AMS Phys. Rept. 894 review, S01] The TRD estimator Λ_TRD is the log-likelihood ratio of the e± hypothesis to the proton hypothesis, built over the 20 TRD layers. The proton rejection measured on orbit is above 1000 at 90% e± efficiency, over 2-200 GeV/c. It improves when the e± efficiency is tightened to 65%.
- 100 GeV lies inside that 2-200 GeV/c range. The review figure was quoted as a flat lower bound, so I don't give a more precise value at exactly 100 GeV, and I don't give a number for the 65% working point either.
- The figure applies only to that selection (Λ_TRD at 90% efficiency, in that energy range). It is not a universal rejection factor, and it was not necessarily the cut used in any particular published positron paper.

**Caveats for a positron analysis**
- The TRD alone does not set the full proton suppression. The proton background is orders of magnitude above the positron signal. The published practice combines several tools:
  - an energy-dependent ECAL estimator Λ_ECAL cut, together with E/p;
  - a template fit in the 2D (Λ_TRD, Λ_CC) plane, where Λ_CC is the charge-confusion estimator [Documented, S04, C24].
- The review says ECAL gives a further factor of about 3 when the e± efficiency is tightened from 90% to 65%, independent of the TRD rejection [Documented, C72]. That is the review's statement for its own samples, and you should re-validate the independence yourself. ECAL shape and E/p share the ECAL energy.
- Rejection and efficiency trade off. Quote the rejection together with the e± efficiency at which it was measured. Any rejection factor you assume must be tied to your own Λ_TRD cut, and efficiency must be measured on a lepton control sample such as electrons. Do not multiply TRD and ECAL rejections as if independent without checking correlations. Check TRD conditions (gas, gain) and time stability as well.
- Treat tails carefully. Proton contamination of the positron sample is dominated by tails of the Λ_TRD distribution, not by its core. Validate the tail with a proton-enriched control sample, reversed cuts, and an orthogonal ECAL-selected sample.
- The TRD does not measure charge sign. Charge confusion (electrons reconstructed as positrons) is a Tracker/magnet tail effect and is not reduced by TRD rejection. The review quotes an electron charge-confusion fraction below 8% up to 1 TeV after TRD and ECAL selection, which applies to electrons only (C65).

**[Proposal]:** For planning, use "rejection > 10^3 at 90% e± efficiency" as a baseline for the TRD alone. Then measure your own rejection versus efficiency curve at about 100 GeV from data (proton-enriched sample, flight data) and MC, propagate its uncertainty, and size the residual proton contamination through a template fit rather than relying on a quoted rejection number.
