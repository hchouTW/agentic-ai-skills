# Review: antiproton analysis (TRD cut, MC electron subtraction, Gaussian charge confusion)

**Verdict: not acceptable as designed.** Three defects are blocking: (1) the signal definition treats the TRD as if it supplied charge sign, (2) the charge-confusion estimate rests on the Gaussian core, and (3) the electron subtraction is MC-only with no data validation. Several further gaps (estimand, efficiency chain, inference, unfolding, validation) are listed below. All AMS-specific practice statements are labeled; no internal AMS numbers are assumed.

## Defects in priority order

### 1. Signal definition: "negative-sign tracks passing a TRD cut" [General method / invariant]
- Charge sign comes only from the Tracker/magnet. The TRD is never a charge-sign detector. Its role here is hadron-vs-lepton separation (a gamma-dependent transition-radiation response). So the TRD cut is an e-/p-bar rejection tool, not a sign selector. Sign must come from the Tracker's signed rigidity.
- The signal selection is under-specified. A p-bar candidate needs the following:
  - negative Tracker sign;
  - |Z| = 1 from Tracker/TOF;
  - a velocity beta consistent with the rigidity and a proton mass, from TOF/RICH;
  - hadron-like TRD and ECAL response;
  - a primary-selection geomagnetic cut, with rigidity above the cutoff times a safety factor.
- A bare "negative sign and TRD cut" leaves e-, pi-, mu- and sign-flipped protons in the sample, with no stated way to separate them.
- A hard TRD cut also creates a correlated-efficiency problem. TRD response depends on gamma and so on rigidity, and it is correlated with the rigidity and with the ECAL variables. Its efficiency must be measured conditionally (see item 4).
- Define the estimand before the method: the p-bar flux or the p-bar/p ratio versus |R|, including the variable (rigidity), the bins, and the p denominator. [Proposal]

### 2. Charge confusion from the Gaussian rigidity resolution (blocking) [General method, invariant 4]
- Charge confusion is a tail phenomenon. It is driven by non-Gaussian effects: large-angle multiple scattering, hard delta rays and bremsstrahlung, interactions in material, bad hit assignment, and noise hits. It is also driven by the maximum-detectable-rigidity (MDR) behavior at high |R|.
- The Gaussian core gives a probability that is exponentially small and wrong by orders of magnitude. It must be rejected, and the tail must be validated.
- The effect is large because the sign-flip background is protons, which outnumber antiprotons by a large factor. The reference quotes 2.42e9 protons against 3.49e5 antiprotons for the published AMS sample [Documented, S08]. A tail fraction that looks negligible per proton can still be comparable to the signal, especially at high rigidity.
- Required instead [Proposal, consistent with the documented approach in S08, claim C22]:
  - Treat charge-confused protons as an explicit background component, modeled as a migration probability M(p -> p-bar) versus |R|.
  - Take the tail from calibrated MC. The calibration needs data: a high-rigidity proton test-beam sample, or flight protons with signed-rigidity variables from independent partial fits (for example upper versus lower tracker segments, or inner versus full span).
  - Use a charge-confusion-sensitive estimator, such as one built from track-quality and hit-pattern variables, as a fit dimension. Then fit the confusion yield directly instead of computing it.
  - Validate extrapolation from loose to tight selections on abundant protons. Test it as a function of rigidity, time, and geometry.
  - Quote a systematic from the tail-shape and the cut-dependence of that extrapolation.
- Trigger: if the data/MC tail ratio in the control sample deviates beyond its statistical error, add an uncertainty or change the model. "Reasonable agreement" is not validation.

### 3. Electron background subtracted by MC alone (blocking)
- Electrons are a dominant negative-sign background at some rigidities. Subtracting an MC prediction rests on the MC reproducing the TRD/ECAL tails of the lepton response. It also rests on the MC reproducing the e- flux, which is itself an AMS measurement and not an input of the simulation. Both are unvalidated assumptions.
- Required [Proposal; the published antiproton analysis fits a template of data-derived and MC-derived components, S08, C22]:
  - Fit templates rather than subtracting. Use a discriminant such as a TRD estimator (the log-likelihood ratio between the e and p hypotheses), an ECAL estimator, or signed rigidity. Fit n(p-bar), n(e-) and n(charge-confused p) simultaneously per rigidity bin.
  - Derive the e- template from a data control, for example a sample selected by ECAL with inverted or relaxed TRD. Measure its contamination from p-bar signal, and correct for it.
  - Use MC shapes only after data/MC validation of the tails, and carry finite-template statistics (Barlow-Beeston or Conway) as nuisances.
  - Add pi- and secondary or interacting particles to the background ledger; they are not covered by an e- subtraction.
  - Do not use ECAL variables both to define the lepton-rejected sample and to build the template that measures it (circularity). The TRD and ECAL chains are independent in physics, but they share track and energy information. Keep a correlation record.
- Failure mode to avoid: tuning the template choice, binning or discriminant repeatedly on the fitted data. Predefine them, or blind the signal-sensitive region.

### 4. Efficiency, acceptance and exposure [General method]
- Name the denominator and numerator for each efficiency: trigger, track reconstruction, |Z| selection, TRD cut, ECAL cut, beta-consistency, and the geomagnetic cut.
- The product of marginal efficiencies is invalid where cuts share information. The TRD cut and the ECAL cut share rigidity dependence and ECAL energy. Measure the efficiencies conditionally, and compare the product with a joint estimate. Check ordering closure.
- p-bar efficiencies cannot be measured on p-bar data, so they must come from proton or other proxies with a justified transfer. This needs a stated transfer model and a closure test in MC. Do not copy scale factors between species without justification.
- Keep acceptance, exposure, livetime, and geomagnetic transmission distinct. Exposure already contains acceptance and livetime, so do not apply them twice. Do not correct the same effect in two categories (veto, calibration, efficiency, response). The p-bar-specific interaction (absorption and annihilation) correction in upstream material is a separate item. Its cross-section model dependence needs its own systematic.

### 5. Inference and unfolding [General method]
- Write the symbolic likelihood: mu_i = sum_j R_ij A_j T_j Phi_j Dx_j + sum_k b_ik, with Poisson counts and auxiliary-measurement constraints.
- Do not use a diagonal bin-by-bin correction without a migration study. The spectrum is steep and the resolution has tails, so migration is not negligible. Published AMS practice uses unfolding with a declared stopping criterion [Documented, S08, C23].
- The response needs three parts: rigidity migration, charge-sign migration (the confusion), and the species change. State the orientation and normalization, and run `scripts/validate_response.py` if a matrix is supplied.
- Validation set: MC closure, reweighted-spectrum closure, a distorted-spectrum stress test, response perturbation, and toy coverage, including the signal-strength-near-zero fit-bias test.
- The p-bar/p ratio does not cancel acceptance, efficiency, or systematics automatically. Classify each nuisance as correlated, partial, or independent, and carry the numerator-denominator covariance.

### 6. Other points
- Low counts at the highest rigidities: use Poisson or toy-validated intervals, not S/sqrt(B), Wilks, or Gaussian errors near boundaries.
- Time and solar modulation: check the stability across periods and the geomagnetic effect at low rigidity. Splits by period and by charge sign are useful cross-checks.
- Data quality and run selection, and the data and MC version and period, are unspecified.
- Do not quote AMS rejection factors, rates, or counts as inputs unless they come from the source ledger. The numbers above (3.49e5 p-bar, 2.42e9 p, 1-450 GV) are [Documented, S08] and apply only to that paper's selection and period.
- Do not present this blueprint as AMS practice beyond the claims labeled Documented.

## Inputs that would change the verdict
1. Does the Tracker sign requirement exist in the actual selection (it is not in the description)? If yes and the TRD cut is only e- rejection, defect 1 becomes a wording and completeness issue.
2. A data-validated tail for sign flips (test beam or abundant flight protons), and whether the charge-confusion estimator is a fit dimension.
3. Is the electron subtraction cross-checked against a data-driven template? Is the MC e- spectrum normalized to a measured flux?
4. Public or internal work, data versus MC, rigidity range, data period, and whether the signal region is blinded.
5. The efficiency measurement method and a joint-efficiency closure.

## Required outputs for a re-review
A background ledger (e-, pi-, charge-confused p, secondaries), each with its control region, signal contamination, transfer model, and closure test; the conditional-efficiency table; the response card (rigidity and sign migration); a systematic ledger with correlation class; and the validation metrics with the triggers for adding an uncertainty.
