# Review: antiproton analysis (TRD-cut signal, MC electron subtraction, Gaussian charge confusion)

## Verdict

Not acceptable as designed. All three elements have a defect that changes the result, and the first and third are structural errors rather than refinements. I would not proceed to a flux until they are fixed.

1. The signal definition treats the TRD as if it were a charge-sign detector.
2. The electron background is subtracted from MC alone, with no data-driven control.
3. The charge-confusion estimate comes from the Gaussian core of the rigidity resolution, but charge confusion is a tail phenomenon.

## Defects in priority order

### 1. Signal definition: "negative-sign tracks passing a TRD cut"

- [General method / AMS invariant] Charge sign comes only from the Tracker and magnet. The TRD, TOF, ECAL and RICH do not measure charge sign. The TRD is a lepton/hadron discriminator (transition radiation depends on gamma). The sign has to come from the Tracker, and the TRD cut only rejects electrons. The write-up should say that explicitly. As worded, it risks implying the TRD identifies the charge.
- The signal is an antiproton: negative sign, |Z| = 1, hadron-like TRD and ECAL response, and beta consistent with the rigidity. A TRD cut alone is not enough. The following are needed:
  - |Z| = 1 from the Tracker and TOF.
  - Beta/rigidity consistency, i.e. a mass or velocity check.
  - A hadron-like ECAL response where ECAL is used.
  - Geomagnetic and primary selection.
- Cut-and-count on a single TRD cut leaves the electron residual and the charge-confused proton as unquantified contamination. A template fit is the more defensible design. [Documented, S08, C22] The published AMS antiproton analysis is a template fit to the negative-rigidity sample. The signal template comes from proton data. The backgrounds are electron, pi-minus and charge-confusion proton templates, from data and MC. It is done in overlapping rigidity regions, with the charge-confusion MC checked against 400 GV proton test-beam data. Describing your design as "AMS practice" would be wrong. It is at most an analogy to that paper, and I do not know its internal details.
- Efficiency is not defined. State the TRD-cut efficiency as a conditional factor, measured per rigidity bin on an unbiased sample. Do not multiply marginal efficiencies of cuts that share the Tracker track or rigidity (Frechet bounds apply). The TRD cut efficiency depends on rigidity (gamma) and on time and detector conditions. Check these.

### 2. Electron background subtracted by MC

Electrons are the dominant negative-sign background at some rigidities, and a pure-MC subtraction is the weakest way to handle it.

- [General method] The electron yield in the TRD-passing sample depends on the residual TRD and ECAL rejection tail, which is exactly what MC models worst. TRD-estimator tails, bremsstrahlung and shower tails, and data/MC mismatch all matter. Normalization and shape both need data constraints.
- Require the following:
  - Background ledger entry: origin, entry mechanism, control region, transfer model, signal contamination of the control, closure test, nuisances.
  - A signal-depleted, data-based control, for example reversed or loosened TRD/ECAL cuts or a lepton-enriched sample, with the antiproton signal leaking into it accounted for.
  - A transfer factor from control to signal region that is validated with orthogonal cuts. Do not assume it is flat in rigidity or time.
  - Better: fit the electron yield rather than subtract it. Use a simultaneous template fit with data-derived or data-corrected templates, finite-template-statistics nuisances (Barlow-Beeston or Conway), and electron normalization floating.
  - Other negative-sign components must be in the ledger too: pi-minus and other secondaries or interacting particles, and charge-confused protons (see item 3). "Electrons only" is incomplete.
- Subtracting a background with a systematic uncertainty means the uncertainty must be propagated as a nuisance, with correlations to the signal shape. A subtraction with only a statistical error is not acceptable.
- Check that the data and MC TRD estimator distributions agree in the tail, with a stated metric and a trigger for adding an uncertainty or changing the method ("reasonable agreement" is not validation).
- Do not use the same events or variable both to define the lepton-rejecting selection and to derive the template that measures the residual (circularity).

### 3. Charge confusion from the Gaussian rigidity resolution

- This is the most serious error. [AMS analysis invariant / General method] Charge confusion and rare misidentification are tail phenomena. The probability that a proton is reconstructed with negative rigidity is not given by the Gaussian core of the rigidity resolution, which would give essentially zero at any rigidity where the core is narrow. It is driven by non-Gaussian effects: hard scattering, bad hit assignment or hit-pattern effects, delta rays and material interactions, and for the highest rigidities the finite spatial resolution against a nearly straight track (|1/R| near zero). The Gaussian estimate will badly underestimate it, and the error grows with rigidity.
- Since protons outnumber antiprotons by orders of magnitude (published sample: 3.49e5 antiprotons versus 2.42e9 protons, S08, 1-450 GV), even a small sign-flip probability is a large background fraction at high |R|. The signal-to-confusion ratio is the quantity at risk.
- Replace it with a tail-validated estimate:
  - Model charge confusion as a charge-sign migration matrix (p to p-bar-like), as a function of rigidity.
  - Derive its shape and rate from MC that has been validated against data tails, for example high-rigidity proton test-beam data (the published analysis uses 400 GV proton test-beam data, S08, C22) and flight-data checks using redundant partial track fits (for example upper versus lower tracker segments or different layer subsets) and hit-pattern/track-quality variables.
  - Use abundant matter samples under looser cuts to measure the sign-flip tail versus rigidity, and test the extrapolation to the tight selection.
  - Include it as a template (charge-confused proton) in the fit, with a nuisance for its normalization and shape, rather than a fixed subtraction.
  - Add track-quality or charge-confusion estimator cuts and measure their efficiency on signal.
- Required evidence: tail closure on data (not just MC), a stated metric (for example agreement of the sign-flip fraction within a stated uncertainty in each rigidity range), and a trigger for assigning an extra systematic.

## Other issues that follow from the description

- **Estimand not stated.** Is the target the antiproton flux or the antiproton-to-proton ratio? If a ratio, acceptance and efficiency do not cancel automatically. The TRD cut, the Tracker quality cuts and the rigidity response differ between signs and between species. Classify each nuisance as correlated, partial or independent, and verify (partial cancellation is to be shown, not assumed).
- **Rigidity response and migration.** Steeply falling spectra mean upward migration of abundant low-rigidity events. Use a response matrix and unfold or forward-fold; a bin-by-bin correction needs a migration study (purity is not one). [Documented, S08, C23] The published analysis uses iterative unfolding stopped when successive fluxes agree within 0.1%. A copied iteration count means nothing for your response. Validate with closure, reweighted-spectrum and stress tests, pseudo-experiments, and prior variation.
- **Double counting.** Keep each effect in one place: if the response matrix includes inefficiency, do not apply acceptance or efficiency again. Electron subtraction and TRD efficiency should not both absorb the same leakage.
- **Exposure.** Acceptance, efficiency, livetime and geomagnetic transmission are separate; state the generation record (surface, solid angle, spectrum, species) for the MC acceptance and avoid circular reweighting.
- **Statistics.** At the highest rigidities counts are low. Use Poisson-exact or toy-validated intervals, not Gaussian or S/sqrt(B) errors. Correlations across bins from unfolding must be carried in the covariance.
- **Time dependence and period/geometry splits.** Check stability of the TRD efficiency, the electron template and the charge-confusion tail with time and detector conditions.
- **Blinding and tuning.** Do not repeatedly tune cuts or templates on the fit sample; predefine or record iterations.

## Inputs that would change the verdict

- Rigidity range of interest (the Gaussian-core error and the electron/proton balance both change strongly with R).
- Whether the target is flux or ratio.
- Whether a data-based electron control and a charge-confusion control (test-beam or abundant-sample tail) exist.
- Whether the work is public-data-based or internal (internal selections, constants and rejection factors are not available to me, and I have not supplied any).
- Whether the TRD cut efficiency is measured on data and how.

If those exist and the fixes above are made (Tracker-only sign, |Z| and beta checks, data-constrained electron treatment in a template fit, tail-validated charge-confusion migration, unfolding with closure and full covariance), the design becomes reviewable. Until then, the electron and charge-confusion systematics are unquantified, and the result should not be reported as a flux.
