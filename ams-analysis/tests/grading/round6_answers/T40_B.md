**No. A constant ratio is consistent with a common origin, but it does not prove one.** The collaboration itself words it only as a suggestion.

**What AMS reported** [Documented, C77; Phys. Rept. review S01, Section 6, Fig. 69]
- Above 60 GeV the positron-to-antiproton flux ratio is consistent with a constant.
- A constant fit over 60-525 GeV gives 2.00 ± 0.035 (stat.) ± 0.06 (syst.), with chi2/d.o.f. = 7.2/12.
- The review reads this as positrons and antiprotons having nearly identical energy dependence at high energies. It says this "suggests a possible common source", and that continued data taking is needed to confirm the origin.
- The first antiproton paper reports a related observation in a different variable and range: the p̄, p and e⁺ fluxes have nearly identical rigidity dependence at about 60-500 GV [Documented, C06; S08].
- The review's antiproton sample is 5.6e5 events over 1-525 GV [Documented, C74]. The constant-ratio statement applies only to 60-525 GeV.

**Why it is not proof**
1. **A fit is not a test of the hypothesis.** The constant fit shows the data are compatible with a flat ratio over that range. It does not show that flatness requires one origin, because other scenarios can produce a similar shape, for example different sources with similar spectral shapes. Whether any such alternative is excluded is a separate step. [General method]
2. **Collaboration interpretation is not measurement.** The "possible common source" language is the review's qualitative reading, not a measured quantity. The review also says the interpretation is not yet definitive. Modelling uncertainties must improve first, and all AMS data (electrons, positrons, protons, nuclei) should be used to constrain models [Documented, C75, C77].
3. **The review ties the interpretation to conditional statements.** It says antiprotons are not produced by pulsars. It also says a cutoff in the antiproton spectrum is expected if high-energy antiprotons come from dark-matter annihilation, and no such cutoff is claimed as observed in this range [Documented, C77]. So the comparison discriminates between scenarios only through those conditional statements, not through the constant ratio alone.
4. **The two species are different.** Positron and antiproton fluxes are separate estimands. They come from different analyses: for positrons, a Tracker charge sign plus TRD and ECAL separation and a template fit in (Λ_TRD, Λ_CC). For antiprotons, a template fit of the negative-rigidity sample with different backgrounds. They also have different acceptance, efficiency, and charge-confusion systematics [Documented, C24-C26, C22]. A ratio does not cancel these automatically, so each nuisance should be classed as correlated, partial, or independent and checked [General method].
5. **A ratio hides the energy and propagation dependence.** Positrons lose energy to synchrotron and inverse-Compton radiation, while antiprotons do not, so a flat ratio implies that propagation and energy-loss effects cancel or are subdominant over the range. That is itself a model assumption. The measurement does not test it independently. [General method]
6. **The finite range and precision limit what can be excluded.** The result is a constant over 60-525 GeV at the quoted precision. A model-dependent constant would not rule out a slow trend or a cutoff outside the range or below the sensitivity. Positron data show a source-term cutoff at 810 (+310, -180) GeV in a fit (C97, same as C35, 4.07 sigma), which is a model-dependent result and separate from the ratio constancy.

**How to state it**
- Defensible: "The positron-to-antiproton flux ratio is consistent with a constant of 2.00 ± 0.035 ± 0.06 over 60-525 GeV, which AMS says suggests a possible common source." [Documented, C77]
- Not defensible: "AMS proved positrons and antiprotons share a common origin."

**What would strengthen or change the conclusion** [Proposal]
- A joint fit to e⁺, e⁻, p̄ and p with a shared covariance of systematics, rather than treating them as independent data.
- Extension of the range and statistics, including whether a cutoff or trend appears in either spectrum.
- Specific model comparisons using the full AMS data set, with the propagation and source-term assumptions varied as nuisances.
