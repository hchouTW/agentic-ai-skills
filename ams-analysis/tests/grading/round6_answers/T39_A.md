**Method (in flight, from survival probabilities).** [Documented, C80; review-level summary, Phys. Rept. 894 (S01), Section 7]

- Material: path-length averaged over the acceptance, the material traversed is about 73% carbon and 17% aluminium by mass, with small amounts of Si, O, H, Na, Au and others. This is a path-length average, not a material budget.
- Geometry: the ISS attitude lets AMS point horizontally. Nuclei therefore pass both from L8 to L1 and from L2 to L9.
- Particle definition: the seven inner layers L2-L8 define the particle, using dE/dx charge and rigidity. The charge distributions in L2-L8 are compared with those in L1 (or in L8 and L9).
- Survival probability: the interaction probability between L2 and L8 (below 3%) is obtained by comparing the upper- and lower-TOF charges. It is combined with the L1-L2 and L8-L9 survival probabilities to give the L1-L9 survival.
- Model tuning: a dedicated Monte Carlo with Geant4 Glauber-Gribov inelastic cross sections is varied by +-10%. The best-agreeing value is chosen.
- Helium: a discrepancy of a few percent below about 30 GV led to a modified Glauber-Gribov model, matched to the data over the full rigidity range.
- Heavier nuclei: below 30 GV, the measured He rigidity dependence is used to modify the model for heavier nuclei.
- Earlier helium flux paper: Glauber-Gribov cross sections were scaled by 1.05-1.20 (C60, S07).

**What was obtained.** [Documented, C80]

- Survival probabilities were measured for Li, Be, B, C, N, O, Ne, Mg and Si.
- The inelastic cross sections on carbon, averaged over 5-100 GV, are constant within the accuracy.
- Before AMS, inelastic cross sections had been measured only below 10 GV, and only for He and C projectiles. Apart from He-on-C and C-on-C, no other measurements exist.
- I do not have tabulated numerical cross-section values (mb). In the source ledger they are in figures and are not transcribed, so I will not quote numbers.
- The method follows a dedicated AMS paper that the ledger notes was not read. This answer is therefore only a review-level summary of that paper.

**Related quoted effects (systematic sizes, not cross sections).**

- Helium flux: the inelastic-interaction systematic is 1% below 100 GV and 2% at 3 TV (C60).
- C and O fluxes: the systematic is below 2.2% (C) and 2.7% (O) up to 100 GV, and 3% and 3.5% at 3 TV (C62).
- The Li survival probability was also measured with AMS data (C29).
- In flux ratios, cross-section systematics are treated as correlated between numerator and denominator (C85).
