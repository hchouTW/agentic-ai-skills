# 27-day positron-to-electron ratio, 5-50 GV, and a one-year periodicity search

Labels: [Documented, C##] means the claim is in the ams-analysis source-index at the stated scope. The rest is [General method] or [Proposal]. AMS internal inputs (livetime, efficiencies, calibration, cuts, per-bin counts) are [Unknown/needs input]. I give no AMS numbers beyond the documented ones below.

## 0. Estimand and what is already published

- Target: R_k(R_bin) = Phi_e+(k, R_bin) / Phi_e-(k, R_bin), the positron-to-electron flux ratio. Index k runs over 27-day time bins from the start of data taking to the end of the usable sample. R_bin are rigidity bins inside 5-50 GV. Two choices should be fixed before looking at the data: the rigidity bins, and whether to also form one wide 5-50 GV bin. A single wide bin is the best choice for the periodicity search, where statistics matter most.
- Two further choices are made explicit here. The fraction e+/(e+ + e-) is the published quantity, and the ratio e+/e- is a monotonic transform of it. The ratio has a different error propagation, so I fix one and state it. The ratio can also be formed in energy rather than rigidity.
- [Documented, C36-C41, S41-S47; abstract level] AMS has reported daily and Bartels-rotation proton, helium, electron and positron fluxes with recurrent 27-day variations. The daily lepton fluxes are binned in rigidity over 1.00-41.9 GV (C40, C41). Positron variations are similar to protons and unlike electrons, and positrons are modulated more than protons below about 7 GV (C41). A hysteresis between electron and positron fluxes appears below about 8.5 GV (C40, C41). The Bartels-rotation lepton paper bins in energy, 1-50 GeV (C39, C48).
- Consequence for the stated range: the daily rigidity-binned lepton paper stops at 41.9 GV, so 41.9-50 GV is not covered by that result. The 27-day lepton product is in energy, not rigidity. State which variable your bins use. Do not claim your 5-50 GV result duplicates a published one.
- [Documented, C90, S01; review-level] The Phys. Rept. review describes a four-parameter logistic fit of the positron-to-electron ratio across the 2013 solar polarity reversal, with Delta t = 830 +- 30 days, energy independent, and t_rev = 1 July 2013 as a stated choice. That is the collaboration's descriptive fit, not a rule.
- [Documented, C07, S09; abstract level] Over an 11-year solar cycle (1.00-41.9 GV), antiprotons vary less than protons, electrons and positrons. This is context only. It does not bear on the e+/e- ratio.
- Before building this, check INSPIRE-HEP, the journal and ams02.space for the current status of the lepton time-structure papers. This skill's index was last verified 2026-09-20, and I have not browsed.

## 1. Design, in analytical dependency order

### 1.1 Observable space and binning

- **Variable.** Use absolute rigidity |R| (Tracker), with charge sign from the Tracker/magnet only. TRD and ECAL do not measure charge sign. R = pc/(Ze) is not momentum, and for |Z| = 1, p = R in GeV/c per GV. Fix the convention that e- has R < 0.
- **Rigidity bins.** Pick bins at least twice the rigidity resolution, with the response matrix carried. The lowest bins sit near the geomagnetic cutoff, and the highest are limited by positron counts. The tail of the rigidity response matters for charge confusion.
- **Statistics versus bins.** The positron sample is the limiting factor. With 27-day bins, the least populated (time, rigidity) cell is the high-rigidity positron bin. Check it against the inference you intend to use (Poisson-exact or toy-validated at low counts, not Gaussian errors). If counts are too low, merge rigidity bins. A wide 5-50 GV bin, or two or three bins, is better for the periodicity search than ten narrow ones. State the rigidity range where 27-day resolution is supported. [Unknown/needs input: the per-bin counts.]
- **Time bins.**
  - Use the Bartels rotation: a fixed 27-day count, giving aligned, comparable bins (C27 and C29 use four-rotation, 108-day blocks, but that is those analyses' choice, not a rule). 27 days is also the Nyquist-safe choice for a one-year search: there are about 13.5 bins per year, so the 365-day period is resolved with more than two bins per cycle, and the Nyquist period is 54 days.
  - Do not let a bin straddle a hardware-era or reconstruction-version boundary. Fix the binning before looking at the time dependence, and log any later change.
  - Aligning to rotations makes bins comparable but does not remove exposure differences within them.
  - Aliasing check: because the bin width equals the solar-rotation period, the sampling does not resolve the 27-day modulation. It averages it. An exact 27-day sampling can also alias any residual 27-day component onto a long period. Check that the residual power at the sampling frequency does not leak into the annual band.

### 1.2 Data and MC scope

- Data: the full flight-data span, in time-ordered bins. MC is needed for the efficiency, response and charge-confusion templates. Record the MC provenance: generator, spectrum, transport and physics list, reconstruction version, production period. Version or hardware-era mismatches invalidate the response and templates.
- Public vs internal: with public information only, I can specify the design but not supply efficiencies, acceptance, livetime or cuts. Use symbolic inputs N, epsilon, A, T.

### 1.3 Selection

- Tracker charge sign and |Z| = 1; TRD and ECAL lepton/hadron separation; E/p consistency; ACC veto; downward-going tracks.
- Geomagnetic: |R| > SF x R_c with the safety factor and field model stated. [Documented, C31, S06; C27, S13] Public analyses used a factor of 1.2 (varied 1.0-1.4 in the deuteron analysis). That is scope-limited practice, not a universal value. Compute the transmission per time bin, not once for the whole period. The 5 GV lower edge is where the cutoff matters most, and the sensitivity can differ between charge signs. Propagate the cutoff-model variation per bin and per sign.
- Quality time: [Documented, C31, S06; C48, S42; C51, S43] Public analyses used normal-condition time, within 40 degrees of zenith, outside the South Atlantic Anomaly, and C48 (Bartels-rotation leptons) states these conditions for exposure per rotation. The Bartels-rotation nuclei analysis uses a different pointing cut (30 degrees, C56). These are those analyses' choices, not rules.

### 1.4 Signal and background model, per time bin

- [Documented, S04 and S05; claims C24, C25, C26] AMS lepton analyses used a template fit in the (Lambda_TRD, Lambda_CC) plane for the positron signal, with templates for correct-sign leptons, correct-sign protons and charge-confusion leptons. This is the lepton analysis practice for the time-integrated flux, not a rule for yours.
- Proposal for this design: fit the positive and negative charge samples in each (time bin, rigidity bin) with shared templates and charge-confusion parameters, and a proton-rich control (reversed cuts).
- Backgrounds. Protons misidentified as positrons are the dominant background. Charge confusion e- to e+ is a Tracker tail that contaminates the positron sample, and it needs a charge-sign migration matrix versus rigidity, validated in the tail and not from the Gaussian core. Also consider secondary and albedo leptons, and bremsstrahlung and photon-conversion matching losses. Antiproton contamination of the negative sample is small.
- Template stability. Templates built from the full data set and applied per bin are persistent systematics. Templates re-derived per bin have finite-statistics nuisances. Pick one and carry it explicitly. TRD gain drift and ECAL scale drift change the template shapes in time, so validate the discriminants of an abundant control species (protons, or electrons) against time.

### 1.5 Efficiency, acceptance, exposure, response

- Per time bin k: Phi_k(i) = (N_k - b_k) / (E_k DeltaR), with E_k = sum of A_eff x T_live x cutoff transmission accumulated within bin k. Never average fluxes of bins with different exposure. Keep acceptance, efficiency, livetime and exposure distinct, and correct each drift in one category only.
- [Documented, C43, C46, C49] AMS time-resolved results use Phi = N / (A epsilon T DeltaR) with the migration unfolded independently in each time bin and acceptance, trigger efficiency and collection time per bin.
- For the ratio, the response needs a charge-sign migration matrix for each sign and the rigidity migration, with the same response conventions in numerator and denominator.
- Do not assume cancellation. Classify each effect per the ratio blueprint: trigger efficiency (partial, species/sign-dependent), geometric acceptance (largely common if the fiducial region is the same), selection efficiency and charge confusion (sign-specific, do not cancel), material survival (species/sign dependent), livetime and exposure (common if the intervals are identical, but the geomagnetic transmission differs with charge sign at low rigidity, so treat per-sign cutoff separately), unfolding (cancels only for a shared response and rigidity scale). Verify each by MC and control samples.

### 1.6 Inference: the ratio and its uncertainty

- R_k = N+_k-corrected / N-_k-corrected, or equivalently the flux ratio per bin. First-order propagation: Var(R) ~ R^2 [(sigma_+/N+)^2 + (sigma_-/N-)^2 - 2 rho ...]. This delta method is valid only for small, Gaussian relative errors. At low positron counts use toys or a Poisson-exact approach.
- Statistical errors are independent between time bins (disjoint events). Systematics are not. Classify each as persistent (template shape, cross-section model, rigidity scale; correlated across all periods), slowly varying (calibration drift; correlated between neighbouring bins), or independent per period. Also classify them across rigidity (normalization, smooth shape, independent). Build one covariance over (time, rigidity), with a documented ordering. A Kronecker product is valid only if the correlation truly factorizes, and you need to test that. Check the matrix with `scripts/validate_covariance.py`.
- [Documented, C44, S43] AMS split systematics into time-independent and time-dependent parts, the latter evaluated each day and added in quadrature. [Unknown/needs input] The ledger does not say how AMS correlated systematics across days or rotations, so that gap is yours to fill, not to assume.
- Normalizing every period to a reference period cancels only the persistent part, and correlates the statistical errors of all periods with the reference. Carry that covariance, or avoid the normalization.

### 1.7 Solar-cycle interpretation (separate model step)

- Solar modulation is charge-sign dependent through drifts in the heliospheric magnetic field, whose polarity reverses over the cycle, so e+ and e- need not follow the same time variation (hence the 2013 reversal fit in C90 and the hysteresis in C40, C41). The ratio therefore has a long-term trend, possibly with a step or transition near the reversal, plus the modulation signals.
- A force-field parameter, a drift model or an interpretation is a separate model step with its own assumptions. The measurement is the time dependence of the ratio.

## 2. The one-year periodicity search

### 2.1 What you are really testing

A one-year periodicity in the e+/e- ratio, on top of a large solar-cycle trend and possible polarity-reversal structure. The solar cycle is a red, non-stationary component that will dominate low frequencies. A period near 1 year will pick up leakage from the trend unless you handle the trend explicitly.

### 2.2 Define before looking [Proposal]

- Period or frequency band: either one pre-specified period (1 year, 365.25 d) or a pre-defined band (for example 300-430 d) scanned. The first has no look-elsewhere factor, but it must truly be fixed in advance. If you also scan other periods (27 d, 13.5 d, 9 d, 180 d), the search band includes them, and you need a trial factor.
- Observable: the ratio in the wide 5-50 GV bin as the primary, with the rigidity-binned ratios as secondary. Fix the choice.
- Test statistic: a weighted (floating-mean) Lomb-Scargle power, or a sinusoid fit with free amplitude and phase on the detrended series, with weights from the full bin covariance. Equal-width, nearly regular 27-day bins, with some missing or low-exposure bins, still need a method that handles the sampling and uses the bin uncertainties.
- Significance threshold, set in advance, and the amplitude you are sensitive to.

### 2.3 Detrending, the critical step

- Fit and remove the solar-cycle trend before testing, or fit trend and sinusoid jointly. Options: a smooth spline or low-order polynomial with a fixed knot spacing much longer than a year (for example at least 2-3 years), or a model with the 2013 reversal (logistic transition, as in C90) plus a smooth term. A trend with a knot spacing near a year absorbs the signal, and one that is too stiff leaks power into it.
- The detrending is part of the test statistic: repeat it in every toy. Show that the result is stable against the knot spacing (sensitivity as a function of the detrending choice is a systematic).
- Alternative that avoids detrending: test the ratio against a smoothed-in-time version of itself, or analyze per solar polarity epoch (before and after the reversal), with a transition window excluded and declared in advance. Do not combine epochs that have different definitions.

### 2.4 Significance and look-elsewhere

- Calibrate with toys that reproduce: the sampling and exposure pattern; the statistical errors (Poisson-exact, not Gaussian, at low counts); the correlated systematics (the full covariance, including persistent and slowly varying parts); and the red-noise character of the residual after detrending.
- Do not use Wilks or a single-frequency p-value (S37, Gross and Vitells trial factors). Report local and global significance. If the period is fixed a priori, the local p-value is the global one, and you say so.
- Noise model: [Documented, C45, C52, S43 only] AMS used a wavelet analysis with normalized power and 95% confidence levels from Monte Carlo lag-1 autoregressive (red-noise) surrogates, in the daily proton flux paper. That is one analysis's choice. [Unknown/needs input] Whether any trial-factor correction was applied is not described in the text read, which is not proof of absence. A red-noise null suits this problem, but with 27-day bins and about 13.5 bins per year, a series of roughly 14 years has only about 14 cycles of one year. Low-frequency sensitivity is poor, and an annual signal is only a handful of bins from the trend knots, so report the expected sensitivity (injected-signal recovery) before unblinding.

### 2.5 The dominant concern: non-physical annual effects

An apparent annual period can come from the instrument, orbit or analysis, not the heliosphere. Apply the identical search to each of these control series, and treat a coincident signal there as a systematic, not a discovery:
- Exposure and livetime per bin, and the geomagnetic transmission at 5 GV in particular (seasonal cycle of ISS orbit and beta angle, annual cycle of thermal conditions, the precession of the orbit).
- Efficiency series: trigger, TRD, ECAL, tracker, and the charge-confusion probability versus time.
- Control species with the same dependence on time-varying detector state but no expected charge-sign effect, such as the proton or helium flux, and a lepton quantity that should not depend on the sign (for example the e+ + e- sum). Because the ratio divides out common exposure effects, check the sign-dependent pieces that do not cancel: cutoff transmission per sign, charge confusion, and template stability.
- A genuine heliospheric annual effect has a physical origin to test: Earth's heliographic latitude varies annually within about +-7 degrees. In a charge-sign-dependent drift regime, that is a conceivable physical source, but one that needs a model and is separate from the measurement.
- Also test: splitting by detector region, and by data periods (a signal should persist in each half, with the phase fixed), and the stability of phase across years. A true annual signal keeps its phase. A solar-cycle artifact does not.

### 2.6 Cross-checks and failure triggers

- Alternate rigidity bins and ranges (5-10, 10-20, 20-50 GV; check the 41.9 GV boundary of the published daily result), alternate time-bin alignment (shift bin edges by half a rotation), alternate detrending, tight/loose lepton selection, data-driven vs MC charge-confusion, alternate templates, signal injection with a known annual amplitude and phase on the full covariance, and closure on MC.
- State a metric and a trigger for each. "Reasonable agreement" is not a validation. For example: recovered injected amplitude within a stated fraction, or power in the exposure series below a stated fraction of the ratio's power. A failure adds a period-split, a scale factor with uncertainty, or a systematic, or changes the method.

## 3. Reporting

- Per-bin results (R_k with rigidity bin, exposures, counts) and the full covariance, so others can refit. A single combined number hides the time dependence.
- Report the fitted annual amplitude and phase with uncertainty, the local and global significance, the detrending sensitivity and the control-series results. Use "candidate", "excess", "evidence" and "discovery" only against pre-defined thresholds. A modulation-model or drift interpretation is separate and labelled as a model.
- A null result is also a result: give the amplitude upper limit as a function of period.

## 4. Failure modes to avoid

- Averaging fluxes of bins with different exposure, or one cutoff transmission for all bins.
- Treating drift as both a veto and an efficiency correction.
- Independent errors assumed across time while persistent systematics are shared.
- A periodicity claimed from a single-frequency p-value, without detrending in the toys, without an exposure and control-series check, or on a fixed band chosen after seeing the data.
- Reading charge-sign differences in modulation as detector-independent physics without sign-specific handling.
- Quoting the 108-day deuteron/Li binning, or the proton quality-time conditions, as AMS-wide rules.
- Filling the evidence gap (covariance across days, trial factor) with invented AMS detail.

Does the sample span the 2013 polarity reversal (then the trend needs the transition term), and is the transition window to be excluded? Per-period livetime, exposure and calibration records, hardware eras and reconstruction versions inside the span? Is the search band and trial range fixed in advance? Is the target the ratio e+/e- or the fraction e+/(e+ + e-)?
