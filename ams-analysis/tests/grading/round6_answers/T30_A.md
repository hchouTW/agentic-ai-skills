**Verdict: no, do not clip and move on.** Both symptoms point to a construction error. Clipping would hide one of them and could bake the other into the flux.

I have not seen your matrices, so everything below is [General method] or [Proposal] and rests on the numbers you quoted. I used no AMS-specific figures.

## 1. Correlation of -1.3 between bins 4 and 5

A correlation coefficient must satisfy |rho| <= 1. A value of -1.3 means the 2x2 sub-block has a negative determinant, so the covariance is not positive semidefinite. That is far larger than round-off.

The repair rule is: clip only if the most negative eigenvalue is at round-off level, about machine precision times the largest eigenvalue. A -1.3 correlation is O(1) in relative terms, so this is a construction error. Clipping would silently change the variances and correlations of the neighbouring bins, and the cause would remain.

Likely causes, in the order I would check them:
- **Mixed conventions.** Absolute and relative covariance combined, bin ordering permuted between components, or units that differ.
- **Rounded or hand-typed correlation tables**, or a subtracted term with an implausible correlation.
- **A component added twice**, or components with unknown correlations combined in quadrature.
- **Under-regularized unfolding.** Near-inversion produces strong neighbouring-bin anticorrelation. A true value near -1 is plausible there, and a -1.3 may be a numerically bad estimate of it.

What to do:
1. Symmetrize, (C + C^T)/2, and compute the eigenvalues. Compare the most negative to the largest. Running `scripts/validate_covariance.py` does this and can show the effect of a clip as a labeled [Proposal] without changing your matrix.
2. If the negative eigenvalue is only round-off level, clip, record the change, and confirm the chi2 or fit result is unchanged within tolerance.
3. If it is large, as here, do not clip. Rebuild the covariance from coherent variations, so that each block is PSD by construction. Propagate statistical and systematic variations through the full unfolding with toys, because the sample covariance of the toys is PSD.
4. Never "fix" it by dropping correlations or by combining components in quadrature when their correlations are unknown.

Also record the bin ordering, the units, absolute versus relative, and whether the components are additive.

## 2. Column sums of 0.85 plus a separate trigger efficiency

The risk here is double counting. The 0.85 can mean different things, and the metadata you declare decides which.

- **R conditional on the selected sample.** Columns (true bins, including stored under/overflow) should sum to 1, and the inefficiency lives in A_j or epsilon_j. A 0.85 sum then needs another explanation, such as migration outside the tabulated range.
- **R that includes efficiency.** Columns sum to the efficiency, at most 1. Then a 0.85 may be the efficiency, which includes the trigger. Multiplying the counts by a trigger efficiency again applies the same effect twice.
- **Truncated matrix.** True events migrate into reconstructed bins outside the tabulated range, or you did not store under/overflow rows. The sum is below 1 without any real inefficiency. Do not diagnose 0.85 as inefficiency alone.

Decision rule: apply each effect once.
- If 0.85 is meant as the efficiency (selection, reconstruction and possibly trigger) and sits in R, remove the separate trigger factor or take it out of R.
- If R is conditional (columns sum to 1), the trigger efficiency goes in A_j or epsilon_j once, and the 0.85 must be explained as feed-out to unstored bins.

Also check that the trigger efficiency is not already part of an exposure or livetime number. If exposure is already A_j T_j, do not multiply by A or T again.

Run `scripts/validate_response.py` with the declared normalization (`conditional_on_selected` or `includes_efficiency`). Check whether under/overflow rows are stored. A pass means the matrix is self-consistent, not physically valid.

The two problems may also be related. An unfolding built from a mis-normalized response, or one that uses an inversion with a wrongly scaled R, can produce spurious large anticorrelations in the covariance.

## 3. What I would ask for, because these inputs change the verdict

- The eigenvalue spectrum of the symmetrized covariance, especially the most negative value relative to the largest.
- How the covariance was built: absolute or relative, which components, ordering, and whether it came from toys.
- The declared response normalization, whether under/overflow are stored, and whether the 0.85 is the same in all columns.
- Where the trigger efficiency is applied: inside R, in A, in the exposure, or at the count level.
- The regularization or iteration setting, and whether the unfolding was validated (MC closure, coverage with toys, bias versus variance).

Until these are fixed, do not fit or quote the flux with this covariance.
