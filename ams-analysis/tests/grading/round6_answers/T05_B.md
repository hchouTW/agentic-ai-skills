# Review: proton flux from "N after cuts / (A x T x ΔR), times a bin-by-bin MC factor"

## Verdict

**Not publication-ready as described.** The skeleton is the right one. It is the diagonal estimator `Φ_i = (n_i - b_i) / (ΔR_i · A_i ε_i T_i)`. But as written it has no background subtraction, an ambiguous efficiency/acceptance split, no rigidity-migration treatment, and a "bin-by-bin factor from MC" that is probably double counting. The last two are the most serious. I have assumed this is a top-of-instrument differential proton flux in rigidity, from data. Please correct me if not. [Proposal]

## Defects, in priority order

**1. The bin-by-bin MC factor is an unfolding substitute, and it is only valid under conditions you have not shown.** [General method]
- A bin-by-bin factor `Φ_i^true / Φ_i^reco` taken from MC is only valid if migration is small. Here the spectrum is steeply falling and the rigidity-resolution tails are not Gaussian. Events from lower true bins feed higher reconstructed bins, so the bias is rigidity dependent.
- The factor also inherits the MC input spectrum. If the generated spectrum is not the measured one, the factor is biased. If you tuned it to the measured spectrum, it is circular.
- The migration correction is not small in public AMS analyses. For phosphorus it runs from about +25% at 3 GV to -21% at 1.2 TV [Documented, C30]. That is a heavy nucleus, not a proton, so treat it only as evidence that the correction is large and rigidity dependent. Public AMS flux analyses correct migration by unfolding [Documented, C31].
- Fix: build the response `R_ij = P(R_meas in bin i | R_true in bin j, selected)`, tails included. Then either unfold or forward-fold with `μ_i = Σ_j R_ij A_j ε_j T_j Φ_j ΔR_j + b_i`. If you keep bin-by-bin, you must show with a response study that migration is negligible in each bin. State a metric and a trigger for switching method. "Looks fine" is not validation.

**2. Double counting between the MC factor and acceptance and efficiency.** [General method]
- Is the MC factor a pure migration correction, or does it also contain acceptance, efficiency, trigger and survival effects? If `A` already comes from MC selected/generated, and the factor again carries efficiency and acceptance, one effect is corrected twice.
- Also check that the factor does not absorb a data/MC efficiency difference that you separately corrected with a scale factor. Each physical effect belongs in exactly one of four categories: veto, calibration, efficiency, response. Record that category.
- If the response matrix includes inefficiency, then `A` and `ε` are already inside it and must not be applied again.
- Fix: write one correction ledger with one entry per effect and the category it lives in. This is also the checklist `scripts/audit_analysis_spec.py` uses (`effect_id`, `response.includes`, `estimator.applies`). Exposure already contains acceptance and livetime.

**3. "Acceptance times livetime" is not complete exposure.** [General method]
- Acceptance, efficiency, exposure and livetime are distinct. "A x T" is the right shape only if `A` is effective acceptance and `T` is the quality-approved livetime weighted by geomagnetic transmission per rigidity bin.
- Below or near the cutoff, the transmitted fraction depends on rigidity, so `T` is bin dependent. Use a stated cutoff model and safety factor. Public AMS flux analyses require rigidity above 1.2 times the maximum geomagnetic cutoff and vary this factor as a systematic [Documented, C31]. Note that other analyses use different factors and ranges, so this is not a universal rule.
- Livetime is not calendar time. Check prescales, dead time, SAA exclusion, pointing and detector-condition quality.
- Efficiency must be a product of conditional factors with named denominators, not a product of marginals. If you multiply two 99% selections you get a joint efficiency between 98% and 99%, not 98%. Measure the joint efficiency or do ordering and combined-efficiency closure in MC.
- Survival probability through upstream material and in-detector interactions is a separate correction, with a cross-section model variation as its systematic.

**4. No background term.** [General method]
- "Count events after cuts" gives `n_i`, not signal. Proton-sample contamination includes deuterons and helium (charge confusion or fragmentation), electrons and positrons at some rigidities, and the charge-sign tail.
- Charge confusion is a tail phenomenon. It cannot be estimated from the Gaussian core of the rigidity resolution, and it needs tail validation.
- Fix: a background ledger (origin, control region, signal contamination of the control region, estimator, closure). Use a template fit or migration matrix where appropriate, with finite-template statistics included.

**5. Bin width and bin centering.** [General method]
- Dividing by `ΔR` gives a bin-averaged flux. For a steep spectrum, quoting that at the arithmetic bin center is biased. Give the centering prescription (the rigidity where the flux equals the bin average), or quote the bin-averaged value.
- If you later convert to kinetic energy, remember `Φ(T) = Φ(R) · dR/dT` needs the Jacobian and bin edges, not just relabeling. Use `scripts/ams_kinematics.py` for the arithmetic.

**6. Systematics and correlations are not mentioned.** [General method]
- The required families are trigger and selection efficiency, acceptance and material, background and charge confusion, rigidity scale and resolution (scale shift times local spectral index, propagated through migration, not applied to the final spectrum), unfolding/response and prior dependence, template statistics, cutoff and time dependence, and MC statistics and physics-list alternatives.
- Adjacent flux bins are correlated through unfolding and shared systematics. Combine covariances, not variances. Do not add unknown correlations in quadrature.
- Public AMS flux analyses add independent systematic sources in quadrature after treating them separately [Documented, C31]. That is a reported practice, not a license to assume independence in your analysis.

**7. Data/MC validation.**
- The MC factor is only as good as the MC. Check the MC provenance (generated spectrum, physics list, material model, reconstruction version matching data), and compare tails, not only cores. Data/MC agreement needs a metric and an action threshold.

## Cross-checks to include

- Closure: inject a known spectrum into MC and recover it, including with a different input spectrum from the one used to build the factor.
- Stability versus time, geometry and detector region, upward/downward splits, and loose/tight charge and track-quality cuts.
- Cutoff-factor scan, alternate track configurations, and alternate response or prior.
- Comparison with published spectra is a plausibility check only, not proof of correctness.

## Inputs that would change this verdict

- What exactly the MC factor contains (migration only, or also efficiency and acceptance).
- A response matrix or migration study per bin showing whether bin-by-bin is acceptable.
- Rigidity range, data period or MC, and track configuration.
- Cutoff model and safety factor; how livetime is computed.
- How backgrounds were estimated.
- Whether this is public-data/proxy work or internal. I cannot see internal cuts, efficiencies or acceptances, and I have not supplied any. Any numbers you give me are treated as user-supplied.
