**Short answer: only with qualifiers.** You can say that AMS's review reports 0.092 +- 0.002 and describes it as the N/O abundance ratio at the source. You should not present it as a model-free measurement of the source N/O. It is a fit coefficient, and it depends on assumptions about the shapes of other fluxes.

**What the number is** [Documented, C91; S01, the AMS Phys. Rept. 894 review, Section 12, Cosmic nitrogen flux; review-level summary]
- AMS measured the nitrogen flux over 2.2 GV to 3.3 TV, using 3.9 million nitrogen nuclei.
- The flux was fitted over the whole rigidity range as Phi_N = Phi_NP + Phi_NS, with Phi_NP = (0.092 +- 0.002) x Phi_O and Phi_NS = (0.61 +- 0.02) x Phi_B. The fit gave chi2/d.o.f. = 59/64.
- The review describes the 0.092 +- 0.002 as the N/O abundance ratio at the source. It compares this with the Solar-System value of 0.135 (+0.051, -0.047) and calls the two consistent.

So the number is a fit coefficient: the weight of a "primary" component, scaled to the oxygen flux, in a primary-plus-secondary decomposition of nitrogen.

**Why it cannot be quoted as a plain source abundance** [Documented, C91 limitation; General method for the interpretation]
1. **The result depends on the assumed shapes.** The decomposition treats oxygen as a purely primary shape and boron as a purely secondary shape. The review itself notes that O has the lowest secondary content among He, C and O, not zero. It also notes that B is mostly made from primary C and O. The 0.092 and 0.61 are therefore conditional on those two choices. The review's phrase "source abundance" rests on the same assumption.
2. **A good fit does not test the assumption.** chi2/d.o.f. = 59/64 shows the model describes the data. It does not show that O is purely primary or that B is purely secondary.
3. **It is not a measurement of the source in the strict sense.** It is a model-dependent inference that goes through the propagation picture. The AMS flux measurement is at the top of the instrument, and the step to the source is an interpretation step. See `source-policy` on result interpretation, and the ratio guidance in `charged-cosmic-rays`.
4. **It is a ratio of fit weights, not a ratio of fluxes at fixed rigidity.** It is a normalization between two spectral shapes. Converting it to a number-abundance ratio is a model step. The review's A = 14.5 is only a plotting convention for the kinetic-energy-per-nucleon comparison, and it is not an input to the fit.
5. **The uncertainty is the fit's own.** The +-0.002 is the uncertainty from this decomposition. It does not include the systematic uncertainty from the choice of reference shapes (O as primary, B as secondary). I did not find that uncertainty quantified in the indexed claim, so treat it as unknown.
6. **Scope.** The source is a review-level summary (S01), and the claim ledger notes that it overlaps a separate nitrogen paper (Tier 1) that is not indexed here. If you need to cite this formally, check the primary nitrogen PRL (listed in S20 as PRL 121, 051103, 2018) against the review. I have not read that paper.

**Suggested wording**
> "AMS reports Phi_N = (0.092 +- 0.002) Phi_O + (0.61 +- 0.02) Phi_B, where the first coefficient is interpreted by the AMS Collaboration as the N/O abundance ratio at the source [Aguilar et al., Phys. Rept. 894 (2021)]. This interpretation assumes O is purely primary and B purely secondary."

**Do not write** "AMS measured N/O at the source = 0.092." Also do not set 0.092 against the Solar-System value 0.135 as a precision comparison without noting the +0.051/-0.047 uncertainty on the Solar-System value. The review calls the two consistent, and that is all the comparison supports.

**What would change this answer:** a systematic-uncertainty budget for the reference-shape choice, for example fits with different primary and secondary templates, or a propagation-model fit that returns the source abundance. Either would let you attach a source-level uncertainty to the number.
