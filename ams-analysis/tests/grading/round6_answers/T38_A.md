Short answer: only with qualifiers. You can quote it as what the AMS review calls the N/O abundance ratio at the source, with its uncertainty and the assumptions behind it. You should not present it as a direct, assumption-free measurement of the source N/O.

What the number is [Documented, C91]: In the AMS Phys. Rept. review (S01, Sec. 12, nitrogen flux; 3.9 million N nuclei, 2.2 GV to 3.3 TV), the nitrogen flux is fitted over the whole rigidity range as a weighted sum of a primary reference and a secondary reference:

Phi_N = Phi_NP + Phi_NS, with Phi_NP = (0.092 +- 0.002) x Phi_O and Phi_NS = (0.61 +- 0.02) x Phi_B.

Here chi2/d.o.f. = 59/64. The review describes the fitted 0.092 +- 0.002 as the N/O abundance ratio at the source. It compares this with the Solar-System value 0.135 (+0.051, -0.047) and calls the two consistent.

Why you should not quote it bare:

1. **It is a fit coefficient, not a counted ratio.** 0.092 is the coefficient of the primary component in a two-template decomposition. It is not an N/O flux ratio at any rigidity. The observed N/O ratio includes the secondary part and is larger.
2. **It is conditional on the template choice [Documented, C91 limitation].** The fit treats oxygen as a purely primary shape and boron as a purely secondary shape. The review itself notes that O has the lowest secondary content among He, C and O, and that B is mostly made from primary C and O. Neither template is exact, so 0.092 and 0.61 depend on that choice. The source N/O is therefore not a propagation-free measurement in a strict sense. The review's wording "source abundance" rests on the same assumption.
3. **The fit quality does not test the assumption.** chi2/d.o.f. = 59/64 shows the two-component form describes the data. It does not show that O is purely primary or that B is purely secondary.
4. **The +-0.002 is the fit uncertainty.** It is not a full systematic budget for the template assumption, and I have not seen one in the source. [Unknown/needs input]: a systematic from varying the reference shapes is not given here.
5. **"At the source" is an interpretation.** Relating the primary-component coefficient to a source abundance is a model step, as in the source-policy rule on result interpretation. It assumes the primary component propagates like O, with no rigidity-dependent difference between the two species. Your comparison to a solar abundance also inherits the cited 0.135 +0.051/-0.047 and the review's judgment of "consistent".
6. **Scope.** The number belongs to the review's nitrogen section, reported with the review-level caveat that the main-paper nitrogen result (PRL 121, 051103, 2018, S20) overlaps but was not read here. For a precise citation, check the original PRL. The claim has full-text status only for the review.

Suggested wording: "In the AMS review (Phys. Rept. 894, 1), fitting the nitrogen flux as a sum of a primary component proportional to the O flux and a secondary component proportional to the B flux gives a primary coefficient N/O = 0.092 +- 0.002, interpreted there as the N/O abundance ratio at the source. This is conditional on taking O as purely primary and B as purely secondary."

If you are writing this up, state the template assumption explicitly, give the uncertainty as "fit uncertainty", and do not quote 0.092 as a measured source abundance without the "as interpreted in the fit" qualifier. A check of how much 0.092 moves when the reference shapes change would be needed to claim more.
