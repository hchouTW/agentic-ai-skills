**Short answer: No to both.** The positron cutoff is not a 5 sigma result, and the electron flux does not show the same cutoff.

**1. Positron cutoff significance**

- [Documented, C35, C97] AMS reports a positron source-term cutoff of E_s = 810 (+310, -180) GeV. The significance is **4.07 sigma**. The abstract of the 2019 PRL (S04) says "more than 4 sigma". The Phys. Rept. review (S01) gives the same 4.07 sigma. A claim of 5 sigma is not supported.
- [Documented, C97] The 4.07 sigma is the point where 1/E_s = 0 lies in a six-parameter confidence-region scan. In other words, it is the significance for rejecting "no cutoff" within that model.
- [Documented, C97, limitation] It is model-dependent. The fit is a diffuse term plus a source term with an exponential cutoff, and a force-field solar-modulation potential is included. E_s = 810 GeV is the cutoff of a parametrized source term. It is not a spectral break measured directly in the data. The significance has no look-elsewhere correction.
- [Documented, C96] The positron flux also shows a fall-off in a separate piecewise fit. The fit parameter is 284 (+91, -64) GeV, and the review states the decrease as "more than 3 sigma". This is a different parametrization with a different number. Do not mix the 284 GeV and 810 GeV values.

**2. Does the electron flux show the same cutoff?**

- [Documented, C98] No. The electron and positron spectra have distinctly different magnitudes and energy dependences. The review states that the nature of the electron excess differs from that of the positron excess. The electron flux (28.1 million events, up to 1.4 TeV) is described by a power law with a gentle transition, E0 = 42.1 GeV. Above about 55 GeV its spectral index is nearly energy independent, averaging -3.180 +- 0.008 over 55.58-1400 GeV.
- [Documented, C99] The review tested the positron source term in the electron flux (41.61-1400 GeV, with E_s = 810 GeV fixed from the positron fit). The electron data are consistent both with a charge-symmetric source term (f = 1) and with its absence (f = 0). With f free, f = 0.5 (+1.2, -0.6) and there is no chi2 improvement. So the data neither require nor exclude a shared source term. This is not evidence of the same cutoff in electrons.
- [Documented, C99] In a single power law with an exponential cutoff over 41.61-1400 GeV, cutoff energies E_s < 1.9 TeV are excluded at 5 sigma. This is the only 5 sigma number in this context, and it is an exclusion of a low cutoff in the electron flux. It does not apply to the positron cutoff. It is also model- and range-specific (the Eq. 9 form, 41.61-1400 GeV), and it does not exclude every cutoff shape. It is a limit, not a detection.
- [Documented, C99] The review says that further data taking, expected through 2028, should test whether the source term contributes to electrons.

**Caveats**

- The numbers above come from the 2019 positron and electron PRLs (S04, S05) and the Phys. Rept. review (S01, covering the first seven years of data). For a "latest result" check against newer AMS papers, current primary sources (INSPIRE-HEP, journal, ams02.space) would need to be consulted. This skill's index was last verified 2026-09-20, and I did no web lookup. A later paper could supersede these numbers.
- The review mentions a projected data set of 5 million positrons and 2 TeV by 2028. That is a plan, not a result.
