**Short answer.** The AMS Physics Reports review (S01, 2021) reports that above about 200 GV the secondary-to-primary flux ratios harden. The index of the ratio increases by 0.140 ± 0.025, and the review states a significance above 5 sigma. I could not check how that significance was computed. I also could not browse, so I cannot say whether a later AMS paper supersedes this (the index was last verified 2026-09-20).

**What was measured** [Documented, C84; S01, Phys. Rept. 894, review-level scope]
- The secondary Li, Be and B fluxes were divided by the primary C and O fluxes. This gives six ratios: Li/C, Be/C, B/C, Li/O, Be/O and B/O.
- A single power law Phi_S/Phi_P ∝ R^Delta was fitted in two non-overlapping rigidity intervals, 60.3-192 GV and 192-3300 GV.
- On average, the index hardens by Delta[192-3300] − Delta[60.3-192] = 0.140 ± 0.025. The hardening starts at roughly 200 GV, and the 192 GV boundary is the review's choice.
- In the high-rigidity interval the ratios follow a power law with index −0.266 ± 0.022 above 192 GV [Documented, C82, which cites this value].
- Context [Documented, C81, C84]: the Li, Be and B spectral indices are nearly identical to each other but differ distinctly from those of He, C and O. The primary He, C and O fluxes harden progressively from about 200 GV.

**Significance** [Documented, C84, at the claim's own level]
- The review states that the hardening exceeds 5 sigma.
- Treat this as the review's statement, not something I verified. The handling of systematic correlations, any trial or interval-choice correction, and how the sigma was computed were not read, so they are [Unknown/needs input].
- The numbers are from the review's latest data set, not from the earlier separate B/C paper (S10, C08, 1.9 GV-2.6 TV, data to May 2016).
- The numerator-denominator correlations (cross sections, unfolding, rigidity scale) are handled in the review's ratio tables (C85). The correlation coefficients are not given.
- The error budget in the ratio tables combines the other systematic sources in quadrature.
- The 0.140 ± 0.025 is a fit-parameter difference between two intervals. It is not by itself a global trial-corrected significance.

**Interpretation (a model step, separate from the measurement)** [Documented, C84, review's reading]
- The review says the result is consistent with expectations if the hardening is due to propagation in the Galaxy.
- If the hardening came from the sources (injection), secondaries and primaries would harden similarly, so the ratio would not change much.
- The review also states that no model predicted the observed behaviour of the primary He, C, O or the secondary Li, Be, B.
- I did not read third-party model papers. Do not read this as a demonstration of a specific propagation mechanism.

**Caveats**
- The review's proton-to-helium ratio and fit (C82) are a different quantity. The −0.266 index belongs to the secondary-to-primary ratios, not to p/He.
- Isotope effects (for example 10Be) are not part of this result. The 10Be remark in C83 concerns the Be and Li shapes below 30 GV, is only "most likely", and is not a measurement.
