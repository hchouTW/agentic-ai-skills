**Verdict: the estimator as described is not correct. It corrects at least three effects twice, and it has a normalization problem in the bin-width and exposure terms.** All of this is [General method] or [Proposal]. No AMS-specific numbers are involved, and I have no access to your internal definitions, so each point says what to check.

## Defects, in priority order

**1. Efficiency applied twice: response matrix plus trigger efficiency.**
- If the response matrix "already includes efficiency", its columns (true bins) sum to the efficiency, which is at most 1. This is the `includes_efficiency` convention.
- Applying a trigger efficiency on top counts the trigger loss twice. Inefficiency may live in `R` or in `A`/`ε`, never both.
- Check first whether the trigger efficiency is a factor in the numerator of the matrix. That is, was the matrix built from MC events that passed a trigger model or a trigger-efficiency weight?
  - If yes, drop the separate trigger factor.
  - If the matrix is conditional on the selected and triggered sample, so columns sum to 1 (`conditional_on_selected`), then efficiency and acceptance must be applied outside it, once each.
- Run `scripts/validate_response.py` with the declared metadata to see which convention your matrix actually follows.
- A column sum below 1 can also mean migration to bins outside the tabulated range (under/overflow), not only inefficiency. Check this before attributing the deficit to efficiency.
- If the trigger efficiency is measured on data and the matrix efficiency comes from MC, you have two overlapping estimates of the same loss. Choose one, or apply the data/MC scale factor once, as a correction to the matrix efficiency.

**2. TRD gain drift corrected twice: run veto and efficiency.**
- A single physical effect must sit in exactly one category: data-quality veto, calibration, efficiency, or response.
- A veto removes exposure. You then reduce livetime and exposure for the vetoed period and do not also correct the efficiency for that period.
- If the veto removes the bad-gain periods, an efficiency that includes a TRD-gain-dependent loss over the same periods double-corrects. The efficiency correction should cover only what the veto leaves behind, and that remainder should be measured over the retained periods.
- Preferably, handle the drift as a time-dependent calibration of the TRD discriminant (a template or threshold per period). The veto then covers only periods where calibration fails, and any residual becomes one systematic.
- Record the category in the systematic ledger, and in `double_counting_checks` answer the question "is a time-dependent drift counted as both a veto and a systematic or efficiency?"
- The veto must also be reflected in the livetime and exposure, or in the time-resolved acceptance, so that the vetoed time is not counted in the denominator.

**3. Efficiency, acceptance, exposure and livetime are being mixed.**
- Your denominator, `livetime × exposure × bin width`, is suspect. Exposure (m² sr s) already contains acceptance and livetime: `Σ_t A_eff(t) T_live(t) × geomagnetic transmission`.
  - If your "exposure" is that quantity, multiplying by livetime counts time twice.
  - If "exposure" means something else, such as geometric acceptance, name it as such. Then the livetime factor is fine, but efficiency and geomagnetic transmission must also appear exactly once.
- Do not integrate the denominator as a product of separate time-averages when acceptance and livetime vary together. Integrate per time interval.
- The inputs, acceptance, exposure and efficiency, are distinct quantities. State for each one where it enters, once, in the chain `μ_i = Σ_j R_ij A_j T_j Φ_j Δx_j + Σ_k b_ik`. If exposure is already a single number `E_j = A_j T_j`, do not multiply by `A` or `T` again.

**4. Bin-width and bin-centre handling.**
- `Δx` must be the true-variable bin width, in the same variable as the flux: rigidity or kinetic energy per nucleon, with `Z` and `A` stated.
- The flux is then quoted at a bin position that needs an explicit prescription, not the arithmetic centre by default.

**5. Background subtraction `(N − b)`.**
- This is reasonable only if `b` is estimated in reconstructed bins with a stated estimator and control region. Its uncertainty, and any signal contamination of that control region, must be propagated.
- Subtracting `b` in reconstructed space and then dividing by a true-bin quantity is also wrong when migration is non-negligible.
- Bin-by-bin division `Φ_i = N_i / (Δx_i T_i A_i ε_i)` is valid only when off-diagonal migration is negligible. For a steep spectrum with finite resolution it is not. That is why you have a response matrix at all. Either unfold or forward-fold with the full `R`. Do not also divide by a diagonal estimator and apply the matrix.
- With the matrix in hand, unfold `N − b` (or forward-fold including `b`), then divide by `Δx · E`.
- Check that the matrix itself carries no efficiency twice, per defect 1.

## Further checks
- Conditional efficiencies must be conditional on the preceding selection. Multiplying marginal efficiencies (trigger, selection, TRD) is valid only if independence is shown. Otherwise measure them jointly. Run the ordering closure and the combined-efficiency closure in MC.
- If a scale factor and an independent MC-model variation both count the same discrepancy, you have another double count.
- Propagate uncertainties as covariance, not per-bin percentages combined in quadrature.

## Corrected structure [Proposal]
1. One calibration, applied before selection and response: the TRD gain, with a residual systematic.
2. A veto only for periods where calibration fails, with exposure and livetime reduced accordingly.
3. Trigger and selection efficiency: measured as conditional efficiencies, each with a named denominator. They live either inside `R` or in the efficiency factor, once.
4. Response matrix: state orientation and normalization.
5. Exposure: one number per true bin, integrated over time intervals. It contains acceptance and livetime.
6. `Φ_j = [unfold(N − b)]_j / (E_j · Δx_j)`, or a forward-folded fit.

## Inputs that would change the verdict
- The column normalization of your response matrix (does it sum to the efficiency or to 1?).
- Whether the trigger efficiency was in the matrix generation or is a separate measurement.
- What your "exposure" contains.
- The veto period list, and whether the efficiency was measured on vetoed or on retained periods.
- The size of the migration.
