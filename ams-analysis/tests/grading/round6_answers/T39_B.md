**Short answer:** AMS measures inelastic cross sections in its own detector material in flight, from survival probabilities of cosmic-ray nuclei. It then tunes a Geant4 Glauber-Gribov model to those data. The numerical cross-section values are only in figures of the sources. I cannot quote them, but I can give what is documented about the method and the precision.

**Method [Documented, C80, review-level (Phys. Rept. 894); the method follows a dedicated AMS paper that I did not read, so this is a summary]**

- Material: path-length averaged over the acceptance, the traversed material is about 73% carbon and 17% aluminium by mass, with small amounts of Si, O, H, Na, Au and others. This is a path-length average, not a material budget.
- Geometry: the ISS attitude lets AMS point horizontally. Nuclei therefore pass both from L8 to L1 and from L2 to L9.
- Particle definition: the seven inner layers L2-L8 define the particle (dE/dx charge and rigidity). The charge distributions in L2-L8 and in L1 (or L8 and L9) are compared to see whether the nucleus survived.
- Interaction probability: the L2-L8 interaction probability (below 3%) comes from comparing the upper- and lower-TOF charges. It is combined with the L1-L2 and L8-L9 survival probabilities to give L1-L9.
- Model comparison: dedicated Monte Carlo with the Geant4 Glauber-Gribov inelastic cross sections varied by +-10% is compared with the data. The best-agreeing value is chosen.
- Model adjustment: for helium there was a discrepancy of a few percent at low rigidity (below about 30 GV). This led to a modified Glauber-Gribov model matched to the data over the full rigidity range. Below 30 GV, the measured He rigidity dependence is used to modify the model for heavier nuclei.

**What was measured and found [Documented, C80, S01]**

- Survival probabilities were measured for Li, Be, B, C, N, O, Ne, Mg and Si.
- The inelastic cross sections on carbon, averaged over 5-100 GV, are constant within the accuracy.
- Before AMS, inelastic cross sections had been measured only below 10 GV, and only for He and C projectiles. Apart from He-on-C and C-on-C, no other measurements exist.
- The actual cross-section values are in figures of the review and are not transcribed in the skill's ledger. I am not giving mb numbers.

**Related documented uses [Documented]**

- Li: the survival probability of Li in AMS material was measured with AMS cosmic-ray data in the Li isotope analysis (C29, S14).
- He, first 30 months: the He inelastic-interaction systematic used the survival-probability method with a horizontal-pointing sample. The Glauber-Gribov cross sections were scaled by 1.05-1.20, giving 1% under 100 GV and 2% at 3 TV (C60, S07). These numbers are for that He analysis only.
- C and O, first five years: the inelastic-cross-section systematic is below 2.2% for C and 2.7% for O to 100 GV, and 3% and 3.5% at 3 TV (C62, S11). These are systematic uncertainties on the fluxes, not the cross sections themselves.

**Caveats:** I did not read the dedicated cross-section paper, so check it for the numerical values and the exact procedure. The survival-probability approach is AMS-specific, not a general recipe.
