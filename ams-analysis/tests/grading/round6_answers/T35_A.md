**Short answer:** there is no single TRD rejection you can assume for a 100 GeV positron analysis. The published figure is conditional on the selection efficiency and the energy range, so you should either quote it with that context or measure it yourself.

**What is documented.** [Documented, C67, Phys. Rept. 894 review, S01] The proton rejection of the TRD, measured on orbit at 90% e± efficiency, is above 1000 in 2-200 GeV/c. It improves when the e± efficiency is tightened to 65%. Your 100 GeV falls inside that range, so "above about 1000 at 90% e± efficiency" is a defensible lower bound if you use a similar TRD selection. Three caveats apply:
- The value 1000 is a lower bound for that energy range. It is not a point value at 100 GeV.
- It holds for the review's selection (the Lambda_TRD estimator, a log-likelihood ratio of the e± to the proton hypothesis over 20 layers) and its efficiency definition. It is not a universal figure.
- The index does not give a rejection value at 65% efficiency, so I do not quote one.

**Combining with the ECAL.** [Documented, C72, S01] Lambda_ECAL (16 variables) together with E/p > 0.7 adds proton rejection. Tightening from 90% to 65% e± efficiency lowers the proton background by a further factor of about 3. The review states this is independent of the TRD rejection. The rejection curve itself is a figure, so no ECAL value is transcribed here, and the independence should be re-validated in your own sample.

**What you should not do.**
- Do not multiply the TRD and ECAL rejections as if independent without checking. ECAL shape and E/p share the ECAL energy, and the Tracker charge and E/p share the track [General method].
- Do not treat the rejection as a single number. In a positron analysis the dominant background is the proton tail, with protons far more abundant than positrons. What matters is the residual contamination after your full selection, validated in the tail.
- Do not quote the TRD rejection as the whole story for the positron sample. Charge confusion (e⁻ reconstructed as e⁺) is a separate background from the Tracker tail. The review gives an electron charge-confusion fraction below 8% up to 1 TeV after TRD and ECAL selection (C65). That bound applies to electrons with that selection and is not a general rate.

**How AMS handles it.** [Documented, S04, C24] The positron paper does not rely on a rejection factor alone. It cuts on the ECAL estimator, then extracts the yield with a template fit in the 2D (Lambda_TRD, Lambda_CC) plane. The templates are correct-sign lepton, correct-sign proton, and charge-confusion lepton. A [Proposal] for your analysis is to do the same: use the rejection only to choose the selection, and measure the residual proton contamination from data-driven templates or controls.
