# Limit setting for a rare antinucleus in AMS-02 with zero observed events

Assumptions: the species is unspecified (antihelium, antideuteron, or another antinucleus), the work is public-domain with no internal AMS inputs, and the target is a flux or ratio upper limit. Every quantity below is symbolic. I do not supply AMS counts, efficiencies, acceptances, livetimes, or tail rates. Where a number is needed it is [Unknown/needs input] or user-supplied.

**Status boundary [Documented, C14, C19, S22, S28].** No peer-reviewed AMS-02 antihelium paper and no AMS antideuteron search paper were found in INSPIRE (checked 2026-09-20, search-limited). The only AMS-authored antihelium result in the index is the 1999 AMS-01 limit. Nothing here is AMS practice. It is a proposal, and any comparison with earlier limits is plausibility only. If "latest status" matters, re-check INSPIRE and ams02.space, because this index may be out of date.

## 1. Estimand (fix this first)

Choose before any algorithm:
- A flux limit `Φ_UL(R)` or `Φ_UL(E_k/n)` for the antinucleus, in a stated variable with stated `|Z|`, `A`, and mass. Rigidity, kinetic energy per nucleon, and energy are not interchangeable.
- Or a ratio limit relative to matter, for example anti-He/He. A ratio does not cancel acceptance, efficiency, or systematics automatically. Classify each nuisance as correlated, partial, or independent, and verify.

Also fix the interval type: a one-sided upper limit, at a stated CL, over a predefined rigidity range. Define whether the limit is per bin or integrated. If the spectrum is assumed, say that it is a model-dependent limit. Otherwise give bin-wise limits.

## 2. Observable space and signal region (blinded)

Discriminating power comes from different subsystems (see detector-and-observables):
- Charge sign comes from the Tracker/magnet only. The TRD, TOF, ECAL, and RICH do not measure charge sign.
- `|Z|` comes from several subsystems: Tracker, TOF, and RICH (and TRD, depending on species).
- Mass comes from `R` and `β`.

Define the signal region in `(signed R, β, |Z|)` before looking at data, and mask it in plots, logs, and optimization. Say who may unblind, and when.

## 3. Background model: the limit is only as good as the tail estimate

Zero observed events does not mean zero expected background. The expected background `b` must be estimated and validated before the limit is quoted.

Misidentification paths:
- Charge-sign confusion of the abundant same-`|Z|` matter (for example He to anti-He). This is a Tracker-tail effect from hit assignment, multiple scattering, and interactions.
- `|Z|` confusion from fragmentation and charge-changing interactions.
- Secondary tracks and interaction-produced antiparticles.
- Wrong mass from `β` errors.

Validation rules:
- Charge confusion and misidentification are tail phenomena. Reject any estimate built on the Gaussian core of the rigidity resolution.
- Measure the sign-flip probability `P_flip(R)` on abundant matter samples under loosened cuts. Extrapolate to the tight selection, and test the extrapolation assumptions.
- Compare redundant signed-rigidity fits (different layer subsets, upper versus lower track segments).
- Check subsystem `|Z|` consistency before and after the magnet and material.

Background ledger (per entry: origin, entry mechanism, control region and its signal contamination, transfer model, closure test, nuisances, correlations; schema in efficiency-acceptance-backgrounds):
- `b = N_matter_sel × P_flip(R) × (tail-selection efficiency)`, with its uncertainty from control-sample statistics and extrapolation.
- Control regions must be signal-free. Do not use the signal region as a control.

## 4. Efficiency, acceptance, exposure

The exposure for the signal species is `E(R) = Σ_t A_eff(t) · T_live(t) · (geomagnetic transmission)`, with units m² sr s. Keep acceptance, efficiency, livetime, and exposure distinct (invariant 3).

- Conditional efficiencies `ε_1 · ε_2|1 · ε_3|12 …`. Do not multiply marginal efficiencies. Check the joint efficiency directly and show ordering closure. Without independence, only the Fréchet bounds apply, and a bound is not an estimate.
- Signal efficiency must be computed for the signal species, not for matter. Use MC for the antinucleus, which needs the right interaction cross-sections. These are poorly known for antinuclei and are a major systematic. Include the survival probability through upstream material.
- Provide the MC generation record: surface, solid angle, spectrum, species, physics list, geometry version, weights.
- Reweight the generation spectrum without circularity. A flux limit needs an assumed signal spectral shape inside each bin. Quote the shape dependence as a systematic.
- If the response matrix includes inefficiency, do not also put it in `A`. Apply each correction once.
- Resolution tails and migration: for a steep spectrum, upward migration from lower-`R` abundant events can dominate. Test the response matrix before using a diagonal estimator.
- Corrections to include once each: geomagnetic transmission (cutoff and safety factor), dead time, and time-dependent acceptance after hardware changes.

## 5. Statistical construction

The expected-count model is `μ = s + b`, with `s = E(R) · Φ · Δx · ε_eff`, so that `N_UL → Φ_UL = N_UL / (E · Δx)`. All of the following is [General method]:

- Case `n_obs = 0`, `b = 0`, no nuisances: the exact one-sided 95% CL Poisson upper limit on signal counts is `-ln(0.05) ≈ 3.00`. In general `-ln α`. This is an exact classical statement, not S/√B or a Gaussian approximation.
- Case `n_obs = 0`, known `b > 0`:
  - The classical limit is `-ln α - b`, which shrinks with `b` and can reach zero or go negative. That is a known pathology: a downward fluctuation of the background would give a stronger limit than the experiment's sensitivity justifies.
  - The CLs limit is `b`-independent, `-ln α`. It is a conservative convention, not a CL statement.
  - Feldman-Cousins gives a different, unified construction, and with n=0 its upper limit moves with `b` (a known behavior to explain, not a bug).
  - These are different constructions. Pick one in advance, name it, and do not present `-ln α` as the classical limit for any `b`.
- Recommended primary result: CLs (or an equivalent stated construction) together with the expected sensitivity and its bands, computed from background-only toys before unblinding. Also quote the classical zero-background bound as a reference.
- Nuisances (efficiency, exposure, `P_flip` tail, background normalization): use a binned Poisson likelihood with auxiliary constraints from real control or calibration measurements, not arbitrary priors. Choose profiling, marginalization, or a hybrid, and state which. Do not use asymptotic formulae. The count is on the boundary with few events, so use toys and check coverage, including at `s = 0`.
- Limits are not significances. If the search covers many rigidity bins or a mass or sign range, a trial factor applies to any excess claim. A limit across predefined bins needs no look-elsewhere correction, but a combination across bins does need a stated rule.
- Combine bins or periods with a joint likelihood, with correlated systematics (shared exposure, shared tail model) carried through, not quadrature-combined.
- `scripts/poisson_diagnostics.py` (exact upper limit, Garwood interval, seeded coverage; classical limit, known background only) can produce the exact numbers and a coverage check. Use statistical-diagnostics if exact values are needed.

## 6. Systematics ledger (each classified, each with a trigger)

- Signal efficiency: antinucleus interaction cross-sections, MC mismodeling, tail of the selection efficiency.
- Exposure: acceptance, livetime, geomagnetic transmission.
- Background tail `P_flip`: control-sample statistics, extrapolation to the tight selection, dependence on rigidity, time, and track topology.
- Assumed signal spectrum shape and migration.
- Rigidity-scale uncertainty (as an uncertainty on bin boundaries).

For "agreement" claims, state a metric and the trigger for changing the method or adding an uncertainty. "Reasonable agreement" is not validation (invariant 10).

## 7. Validation before unblinding

- Closure and signal-injection tests. Inject a known number of signal-like events and recover the limit behavior, including `s = 0`.
- Coverage of the construction at low counts, with toys.
- Pulls of nuisances. Stability under loose and tight variations, alternate track fits, period and geometry splits, and charge-sign splits.
- Candidate-level diagnostics fixed in advance: event display checklist, hit pattern, residuals, partial-fit consistency, subsystem estimators.
- Expected sensitivity at the predefined `b`. If the expected background is non-negligible, a zero-observed result may be an under-fluctuation, so quote it with the expected-limit band.

## 8. Reporting

- Say what was blinded, and what changed after unblinding (nothing, ideally).
- Report `n_obs`, the expected `b` with its uncertainty, `E`, the efficiency curve, and the limit with its CL and construction. Give the expected limit and bands.
- Use the four-level wording: candidate, excess, evidence, discovery. Zero events means none of these. If events later pass, a candidate is not an excess and not a discovery.
- Do not describe other experiments' or the AMS-01 limit as the AMS-02 method. An AMS-01 or other result may be quoted as a historical comparison only, with its context.

## 9. What would change this design

Needed from the user, with the effect on the answer:
- Species and `|Z|`, `A`: sets the topology and misidentification paths.
- Rigidity range, and whether data or MC only: sets `P_flip` extrapolation and whether the limit is data-based or a sensitivity study.
- Whether a blinded region exists, and the expected `b`: decides classical versus CLs and how seriously the pathology matters.
- Flux versus ratio target: sets which systematics cancel.
- Public or internal work: if internal, these are user-supplied inputs and must be labeled as such.

Failure modes to avoid: a tail estimate from the Gaussian core; unblinded optimization of cuts; efficiency taken from matter instead of the signal species; double correction of the same effect; asymptotic or S/√B limits at n = 0; presenting `-ln α` as the classical limit when `b > 0`; fabricated counts, sensitivities, or candidate claims.
