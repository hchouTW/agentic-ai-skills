# Design: 27-day positron-to-electron ratio, 5-50 GV, solar cycle, one-year periodicity search

Labels: [Documented, C##] = AMS public source in the skill's source-index (abstract or main-article level only, as stated); [General method]; [Proposal]; [Unknown/needs input]. Scope is public-domain reasoning; I give no AMS internal cuts, livetimes, efficiencies or event counts, and no AMS count or systematic size is quoted. This is a design, not a result.

## 0. Verdict up front

- The measurement is feasible as a design, but two things must be settled before any periodicity claim: (1) 5-50 GV is not a range AMS has published for this ratio in time. [Documented, C40/C41] AMS's daily electron and positron fluxes cover 1.00-41.9 GV in rigidity, and [Documented, C39] its Bartels-rotation lepton paper (27-day bins) covers 1-50 GeV in energy, not rigidity. Your 5-50 GV in rigidity therefore extends past the published rigidity range at the top (41.9 GV) and mixes a variable choice. Treat 41.9-50 GV as a new analysis needing its own validation, not as a reproduction.
- [Documented, C39, abstract level] AMS reported that short-term structures of months appear coincidently in the electron and positron fluxes but are not visible in the positron-to-electron ratio, and that across the solar polarity reversal the ratio shows a smooth transition over 830 ± 30 days. So the ratio is expected to be dominated by a slow, step-like polarity-reversal trend at low energy and to be featureless at short timescales. A null result for a one-year period is a plausible outcome, and the design must be able to say so with a stated sensitivity.
- Any annual signal is much more likely to be an exposure, orbit, season or detector-state effect than physics, so the control series (section 6) are part of the primary design, not an afterthought.

## 1. Estimand and observable space (fix first)

- Target: R_e(t_k, R_b) = Φ(e⁺)/Φ(e⁻) at top of instrument, 27-day bin k, rigidity bin b, for |Z| = 1 leptons. The e⁺ and e⁻ are separated by Tracker/magnet charge sign only (invariant 2); TRD and ECAL provide lepton/hadron separation, never charge sign.
- Variable: rigidity |R| in GV, per your request (p = |Z|R/c, so for leptons E ≈ pc to within the electron mass; state that energy and rigidity bins then differ only through bremsstrahlung/energy response). [Documented, C39 vs C40/C41] AMS used energy for the Bartels lepton paper and rigidity for the daily lepton papers; state which defines your bins and use the same one for numerator and denominator. [Proposal] Use rigidity, since the Tracker measures it and the sign comes from it.
- 27-day bins: [General method] a Bartels rotation is a fixed 27-day count; align bins to rotations, which makes bins comparable but does not remove within-bin exposure differences. [Documented, C48/C49] AMS built its Bartels-rotation lepton and nuclei fluxes per rotation, with migration unfolded independently per rotation; this is one analysis's choice, not a rule. (The deuteron and Li isotope analyses used four-rotation 108-day bins instead, C27, C29; that is not a rule either.)
- Period of validity: data from May 2011 through your chosen end date; I do not assert an AMS end date. Bin edges must not straddle hardware-era or reconstruction-version boundaries (a change breaks response and templates).
- Rigidity bins: [Proposal] merge to a few coarse bins (for example 5-10, 10-20, 20-50 GV; edges are a proposal to be set by counts, before looking at time dependence). The positron count in a 27-day, high-rigidity bin is the limiting statistic; the lower-count bins need Poisson-exact or toy-validated errors, not Gaussian errors (invariant 6). [Unknown/needs input] the actual per-bin positron counts; I do not supply them.
- Binning is fixed before looking at the time dependence, with later changes recorded.

## 2. Response chain for the ratio

Per time bin k and rigidity bin b:

R_e = (N⁺ - b⁺)/(N⁻ - b⁻) × [A⁻ ε⁻ T⁻ ΔR] / [A⁺ ε⁺ T⁺ ΔR] after unfolding/forward-folding for migration.

- Do not assume the ratio cancels acceptance, efficiency, or exposure. Classify each effect as correlated, partial, or independent and verify it (invariant 5).
  - Exposure/livetime T and acceptance A: largely common for the same events' time and geometry, but [General method] the geomagnetic transmission can differ with charge sign at low rigidity (east-west effect). At 5-~15 GV the cutoff safety factor matters; verify by computing transmission per bin and per sign. Check that numerator and denominator use the same cutoff treatment in rigidity.
  - Trigger/selection efficiency: species-dependent (e⁺ and e⁻ have different charge-confusion and TRD/ECAL selection response); measure per sign. [Documented, C46] AMS found its data/MC selection-efficiency correction δ by comparing each cut with detectors unrelated to that cut, for electrons and positrons; this is one analysis.
  - Material/interaction: lepton-specific, small difference between signs; verify, do not assume zero.
  - Charge confusion: e⁻ to e⁺ and e⁺ to e⁻ are different, sign-specific tail effects, not a Gaussian-core number (invariant 4). The migration matrix M(e⁺←e⁻), M(e⁻←e⁺) vs rigidity is a tail quantity validated on data-driven samples. It matters most at the top of your range (20-50 GV) and is not constant in time if the Tracker response drifts.
  - Proton background: dominant for positrons (the positive sample contains protons, the negative one antiprotons, which are far fewer). [Documented, C24 positron paper; C26 electron paper] AMS's approach in the time-integrated energy analyses was an ECAL cut plus a 2D template fit in (Λ_TRD, Λ_CC); the antiproton background in the negative sample was below 10⁻³ after the electron cut (C26). [Documented, C46] The daily papers estimated the electron background with a TRD template fit and the positron proton background with the TRD estimator. These are shown as AMS practice for those analyses, not as the recipe here. [Proposal] Fit templates per time bin or per block of bins, with finite-template statistics nuisances, and check that the template shapes are stable in time.
- Rigidity response: unfold or forward-fold per time bin independently (the AMS approach for its daily and rotation products, C43, C46, C49); the migration correction is not small for steep spectra, so the diagonal estimator is not allowed without response validation.
- One category per correction (veto, calibration, efficiency, response); a time-dependent drift is corrected in one only.

## 3. Handling the solar-cycle structure before the periodicity search

The question has two time scales; keep them separate.

1. Solar-cycle and polarity-reversal trend. [Documented, C39; C90 (review, S01)] The e⁺/e⁻ ratio has a smooth transition across the polarity reversal, 830 ± 30 days independent of energy in the review's logistic fit, with an energy-dependent midpoint delay; t_rev = 1 July 2013 is a stated choice. [General method] Solar modulation depends on charge sign through drifts in the heliospheric field, so e⁺ and e⁻ need not follow the same time variation. This transition is the largest time structure in the ratio and sits at low frequency (multi-year). It has large power near the annual frequency if not removed, through spectral leakage.
2. Annual periodicity: the target.

[Proposal] Model the slow trend as a free parametric or smooth component, and test for annual power in the residual:
- Option A: fit a smooth trend per rigidity bin (logistic with the four parameters as in C90, or a spline whose knot spacing is at least two to three times the period of interest) and search the residual. Report results for several trend choices; if the annual conclusion depends on the trend model, it is not robust.
- Option B: include the periodic term in the same likelihood: R_k = trend(t_k; θ) × [1 + a cos(2π t_k/P + φ)], fit a and φ at P = 1 year, with trend parameters as nuisances. Then the estimand is the amplitude a (with its upper limit if consistent with zero), not a p-value.
- The logistic form is the collaboration's descriptive fit (C90), not a physical model; its parameters Δt and t_1/2 are determined only at low energy, and above ~20 GeV the transition amplitude is consistent with zero. So at the 20-50 GV end the trend is nearly flat and the ratio is mostly a count-limited constant; the sensitivity to an annual term there is statistical, not physical.

## 4. Periodicity search design (define before looking)

[General method]/[Proposal]; all choices below are fixed before unblinding the time series.

- Band and test: one-year period P = 365.25 d is a single hypothesis. With 27-day sampling, 365.25 d = 13.5 samples per cycle, comfortably above the Nyquist limit (54 days), so one-year is well resolved. With about 14 years of data the frequency resolution is about 1/T ≈ 0.07 per year, so nearby periods (for example 330-400 d) are not distinguishable from one-year; do not claim "exactly annual" beyond that width.
- If the claim is "a period at exactly one year" (pre-specified): single trial; local = global. If you search a band (for example 100-800 d) for any period: it is a look-elsewhere search. [General method] Report local and global significance with trial factors (Gross and Vitells, S37) calibrated with toys that reproduce the sampling, exposure pattern, correlated systematics and the red-noise structure. Do not use Wilks or a single-frequency p-value.
- Statistic: [Proposal] a weighted (generalized) Lomb-Scargle periodogram, or equivalently the likelihood-ratio fit of the amplitude a in Option B, using the per-bin uncertainties as weights (bins with different exposures and counts must not be treated as equal). Gaps (SAA, downtime, quality-time cuts) make the sampling uneven, so a method that handles uneven sampling is required.
- Null model: [Documented, C52 for the method AMS used in the daily proton analysis; S43] the AMS wavelet analysis assessed significance against Monte Carlo lag-1 autoregressive (red-noise) surrogates matched to the measured variance and lag-1 autocorrelation (95% level per period, no trial-factor correction described in the text read, which is not proof none was applied; C45, C52). [Proposal] use the same style of surrogate (AR(1) with the fitted trend retained) but generate toys from the full correlated model, including the shared systematics, and give the global p-value. A wavelet is appropriate for a transient or time-varying period (AMS saw its 27-day structure only in 2014-2018, C36), but for a stationary one-year test the periodogram or amplitude fit has more power. Run both and say which was pre-specified.
- Window function: compute the spectral window of your sampling and mask; report the periodogram of a unit-amplitude one-year sine injected in the same sampling to show leakage and the expected peak width.
- Sensitivity: before looking, inject sinusoids of amplitude a at P = 1 yr into toys at the observed counts and uncertainties and give the minimum detectable amplitude and the coverage of the upper limit. Report a (with an interval) and its limit even if there is no signal, because "no one-year periodicity found" with unknown sensitivity is not a result.
- Do not interpret a peak at ~13.5 or ~27 days as annual: [Documented, C36, C40; abstract level] AMS's recurrent 27-day, 13.5-day and 9-day variations in daily proton and electron fluxes are on different timescales and the 27-day bin averages them out (the 27-day sample is at the fundamental, so aliasing of 27-day solar rotation structure into your series is possible; check it).
- [Unknown/needs input] How AMS treated trial factors for its periodicity or hysteresis significances is not described in the text read (C45, C52, C53); I do not claim AMS applied or omitted one.

## 5. Covariance and correlations over (time, rigidity)

- Statistical errors are independent between time bins built from disjoint events; the statistical error of the ratio's numerator and denominator is also independent (different events), but a shared-event background (proton template) induces correlation.
- Systematics are not independent across bins: [General method] classify each as persistent (calibration, template shape, cross-section model; correlated across all 27-day bins), slowly varying (neighbouring bins), or independent per bin; and across rigidity as normalization, smooth shape, or independent. [Documented, C44, S43 for the daily proton flux; C49 for the proton/helium time-structure analysis] AMS split systematics into time-independent and time-dependent parts and (in the proton daily analysis) added the time-dependent efficiency part in quadrature day by day; [Documented, C44] no covariance model across days appears in the text read. Do not copy that as a covariance model; build one: [Proposal] a single covariance over (time, rigidity) with documented ordering, check with scripts/validate_covariance.py (a pass is only self-consistency, not physical validity), and a Kronecker-product (time ⊗ rigidity) form only after testing that the correlation factorizes.
- A persistent normalization systematic shifts all bins together and cannot create a periodic signal, but a slowly time-varying one (for example a slow Tracker or TRD drift) can create low-frequency structure, so it is exactly the type that interacts with detrending.
- A periodic signal in the statistic must be tested against toys that include these correlated components; independent-error toys would overstate significance.

## 6. Controls: where an annual signal can come from other than physics

[General method] Apparent periodicities arise from exposure, orbit, season or detector-state cycles. Apply the same search (same trend model, same method, same toys) to each of:

1. The exposure time series per bin and per sign: livetime, collection time above the cutoff (for the ISS, collection time depends strongly on rigidity through the geomagnetic field, C51), SAA passage time, fraction of time within 40 degrees of zenith (the 40-degree and SAA conditions are those of one set of AMS analyses, C48, C51, not a rule).
2. ISS orbital and attitude cycles: the orbit precesses, with a beta-angle cycle that drives thermal conditions and detector state; a seasonal variation in thermal state can change efficiencies, TRD gain or ECAL response. [General method] Include a beta-angle and temperature series if available. [Unknown/needs input] no such AMS series is supplied here.
3. A control species in the same bins: proton flux (or the electron flux alone, and the positron flux alone). [Documented, C39] AMS reported short-term structures coincident in electron and positron fluxes but absent in the ratio; so an annual structure present in e⁺ and e⁻ individually but not in the ratio is the expected common-mode case and does not indicate a ratio signal. Searching both the ratio and the separate fluxes tells you which it is.
4. The efficiency series (trigger, ECAL/TRD selection, charge-confusion fraction) and the TRD/ECAL template-fit parameters versus time.
5. The geomagnetic-cutoff model variation (for example the safety factor 1.0-1.4 used as a systematic in some AMS analyses, C27, C49, one analysis's choice) per bin and per sign: a coincident annual term in the lower rigidity bins points to a cutoff/exposure systematic.

A coincident signal in any of these is a systematic, not a discovery. The deliverable should state, for each control, the metric (amplitude and its uncertainty in the same units) and the threshold that would trigger a period-split, a scale factor with uncertainty, or an added systematic.

Also check for rigidity dependence: a real heliospheric modulation effect would be expected to decrease with rigidity [Documented, C38 for proton and helium fine structures vanishing above 40 GV], so an annual amplitude that is rigidity-independent or grows with rigidity is more consistent with a detector or exposure origin.

## 7. Time stability and closure

- Show stability: an abundant control species' rate (for example protons or the positive-sample proton fraction) flat versus time after livetime and cutoff correction; a drift beyond the stated tolerance adds a period-split, scale factor or systematic. Per-period efficiency, acceptance and calibration values are [Unknown/needs input]; I do not provide them.
- MC closure of the whole chain per bin: inject a known ratio and a known annual modulation into MC at the observed counts and recover it.
- Signal injection in data: multiply the positron counts by [1 + a cos(2πt/P)] in toys and recover a with the full pipeline (including detrending), to measure bias introduced by the trend subtraction.
- Cross-check: independent analyses with different templates, alternative discriminants, loose/tight selection variations, and a charge-sign split of the cutoff transmission.
- Validate "reasonable agreement" with a metric: for example a pull distribution of the control-series amplitudes with a stated tolerance (the tolerance chosen as the fraction of the expected signal amplitude), and a trigger for method change.

## 8. Joint-likelihood option (preferred if the data support it)

[General method] L = Π_k Π_b Poisson(n_kb | μ_kb(θ_shared, φ_k, η)) × constraints, with shared parameters (for example the trend shape), per-bin parameters (normalizations or the sinusoid in Option B), and nuisances η shared or per bin by the classification in section 5. Each bin uses its own exposure and, if the response changed, its own response. Test the joint model against separate fits with toys. Report per-bin results with the covariance and exposures so that others can refit; a single combined amplitude hides the time dependence.

## 9. What would change the conclusion

- Counts per bin: if the 27-day, 20-50 GV positron counts are too low for the target amplitude, lengthen the time bin (loses nothing for a one-year period; two bins per year cannot resolve it, but 13.5 do) or merge rigidity bins, and state the rigidity range where the time resolution is supported.
- The trend model: if the annual amplitude changes materially between trend models.
- Control coincidence: any annual term in exposure, efficiency, beta angle, or the proton control.
- Charge-confusion tail drift in time at high rigidity.
- The definition: whether the target is the ratio or the separate fluxes; whether bins are in rigidity or energy.

## 10. Reporting

Report: the pre-specified band and statistic, the periodogram (or amplitude fit) with the sampling window, the local and global significance with the trial treatment, the amplitude a and its interval or upper limit for P = 1 yr at each rigidity bin, results for the control series, results for each trend model, and the covariance. Use the words "no evidence of" or "limit on" unless the global significance and controls support more; a modulation or drift interpretation is a separate model step. Compare with AMS results only as plausibility (agreement is not validation); AMS reported [Documented, C39] the ratio was smooth over solar-polarity reversal without visible short-term structure in 1-50 GeV in energy, and [Documented, C41] that positron variations resemble protons (modulated more than protons below about 7 GV) and differ from electrons at short and long timescales, in 1.00-41.9 GV in rigidity. These are abstract-level statements; the supplemental numerical tables were not transcribed (evidence gap), and the skill's index was last verified 2026-09-20, so I make no claim on whether newer AMS time-structure papers on the ratio exist; check INSPIRE-HEP and ams02.space for "latest".
