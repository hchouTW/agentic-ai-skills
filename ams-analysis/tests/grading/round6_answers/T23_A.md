The ACC (Anti-Coincidence Counters) is AMS-02's side-entry veto. [Documented, C13, C69]

**What it does.** It surrounds the Tracker inside the magnet bore and rejects particles that enter or leave the tracker volume transversely, i.e. through the magnet sides, outside the nominal acceptance. It also flags events with extra activity (for example backsplash from ECAL showers). Such events would otherwise have badly measured rigidity or be off-acceptance. [Documented, C69 for the transverse-rejection purpose; the extra-activity role is a [General method] description of a veto]

**Hardware.** Per the Phys. Rept. review, it consists of sixteen curved scintillator panels, 800 mm long, with wavelength-shifting fibres routed to PMTs from two adjacent panels. The vertical edges are tongue-and-groove, so a particle crossing a seam is seen in two panels. [Documented, C69]

**Trigger role.** In the level-1 trigger, a Z = 1 particle requires four TOF signals and no ACC signal. [Documented, C69, C73]

**Performance.** Side-entry rejection efficiency is quoted as 0.99999. This is a pre-flight long-duration test value in the review (C69), and it is the same figure quoted in the analysis papers (C21) for |Z| = 1. It applies only in that context, and the inefficiency definition is not given in the source. [Documented, C21, C69]

**What it does not do.** The ACC gives no direction, charge sign, |Z|, beta, or lepton/hadron ID. It is a veto only. Charge sign comes from the Tracker/magnet alone. [Documented, subsystem matrix in the skill's detector reference]

**Analysis caveats.**
- The veto's cost is an efficiency, to be measured as a conditional efficiency. It is lost for valid events with accidental or backsplash activity, which is energy-dependent at high energy and orbit/rate-dependent in time.
- Do not also fold the veto into the geometric acceptance, or the effect is double counted.
- A hardware inefficiency would let false side-entering events through, so its time stability should be checked.
