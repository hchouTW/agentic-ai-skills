**[Documented, C80, S01 — AMS Phys. Rept. review, "AMS on the ISS Part II," Section 7, full text]**

**Method.** AMS measures in-flight nuclear survival probabilities (and from them, inelastic cross sections on its own material) using periods when the ISS attitude points AMS horizontally, so cosmic-ray nuclei pass through the detector both "forward" (Layer 8 → Layer 1) and "backward" (Layer 2 → Layer 9). The seven inner tracker layers (L2–L8) define the particle's charge and rigidity via dE/dx and tracking, and the charge measured in the outer layers before and after (L1 vs. L8, or L8 vs. L9) is compared to the L2–L8 charge to see whether the nucleus survived intact or interacted. Specifically:
- The interaction probability between L2 and L8 itself (found to be below 3%) is obtained by comparing upper- and lower-TOF charges.
- That is combined with the separately measured L1–L2 and L8–L9 survival probabilities to build the full L1–L9 survival probability.
- A dedicated Monte Carlo using the Geant4 Glauber-Gribov inelastic cross-section model, varied by ±10%, is compared against the data, and the best-agreeing cross-section value is chosen.
- Below about 30 GV, a few-percent discrepancy showed up for helium, so AMS built a modified Glauber-Gribov model tuned to match the helium data over the full rigidity range, and used that measured helium rigidity dependence to also correct the model for heavier nuclei at low rigidity.

**Material traversed.** Averaged over the path lengths in the AMS acceptance, the material is about 73% carbon and 17% aluminium by mass, with smaller amounts of Si, O, H, Na, Au and others. (The review is explicit that this is a path-length average over the acceptance, not a full material budget.)

**What values it got.** Survival probabilities were measured this way for Li, Be, B, C, N, O, Ne, Mg and Si. The review states that the resulting inelastic cross sections on carbon, averaged over 5–100 GV, are constant within the achieved accuracy — but it does **not** give the numeric cross-section values in the text; they are shown only in figures, which were not transcribed into this skill's source ledger. Before AMS, inelastic cross sections for cosmic-ray nuclei had only been measured below 10 GV, and only for helium and carbon projectiles (He-on-C, C-on-C); AMS extended this to Li through Si and to much higher rigidity.
