**No, the ~810 GeV positron cutoff is not a 5σ result — it's 4.07σ.** [Documented] AMS's positron flux paper (PRL 122, 041102, 2019) and the Phys. Rept. 894 review fit the positron flux to a diffuse term plus a source term with an exponential cutoff, `1/E_s`. The fitted cutoff energy is `E_s = 810 (+310, −180) GeV`, and the significance of `1/E_s ≠ 0` (i.e., that a cutoff exists at all) is **4.07σ**, reported in the papers as "more than 4σ" (99.99% C.L.) — not 5σ. That's a six-parameter fit-region scan, not a look-elsewhere-corrected significance.

**No, the electron flux does not show the same cutoff.** [Documented] AMS tested whether the electron flux contains the same charge-symmetric source term found in positrons (same shape, same fixed cutoff `E_s = 810 GeV`) and found the data **cannot distinguish** the two cases: fitting with the source-term normalization fixed to 1 (present) versus fixed to 0 (absent) gives essentially the same fit quality, so electrons are consistent with either. Separately, fitting the electron flux directly for its own exponential cutoff excludes cutoff energies below 1.9 TeV at 5σ — i.e., if the electron spectrum has a cutoff at all, it must be above ~1.9 TeV, more than double the positron value. So there is no observed ~810 GeV cutoff in the electron flux; the electron and positron spectra are explicitly described as behaving differently in this respect.

Both results come from the same source (Phys. Rept. 894 review, restating PRL 122, 041102 for positrons and PRL 122, 101101 for electrons); scope is the AMS lepton flux measurements as published through that review.
