**[Documented]** The only public number is from the AMS Phys. Rept. review (Aguilar et al., "AMS on the ISS, Part II," Phys. Rept. 894, 1, 2021, §1.3): the TRD's measured on-orbit proton rejection is **above 1000 at 90% e± efficiency, over 2–200 GeV/c**, and improves further if the e± efficiency working point is tightened to 65%. A 100 GeV positron sits inside that 2–200 GeV/c momentum range (positron mass is negligible, so momentum ≈ energy), so "rejection > 1000 at 90% e± efficiency" is the documented figure you can assume — provided you adopt the same working point (90% e± efficiency) and the same TRD-alone selection the review describes.

Caveats that matter if you use this number:
- It's a **lower bound at a specific efficiency**, not a universal constant. It is TRD-only; AMS's actual positron analyses (e.g. PRL 122, 041102, 2019) don't use a simple TRD cut-and-count — they run a 2D template fit in `(Λ_TRD, Λ_CC)` space, so the effective background rejection of a full analysis is not just this number.
- ECAL adds further, largely independent rejection (a further factor of about 3 at 65% vs. 90% e± efficiency, per the same review), but combining TRD and ECAL rejection multiplicatively as if fully independent should be checked, not assumed — they exploit different physics but any shared cuts/efficiencies need factorization-bias verification.
- The TRD is never a charge-sign detector; it separates electrons from protons (or antiprotons), not positrons from electrons. The Tracker/magnet is what fixes the sign.
- This value comes from the review's summary of the first seven years; if your analysis uses a different track/detector configuration, era, or efficiency working point, treat this as a starting assumption to be validated on your own control sample, not as a guaranteed figure — rejection depends on selection and efficiency context (source-index claim C67).

If you need a number outside 2–200 GeV/c, at a different e± efficiency, or a rejection curve rather than a single working point, that's not in the public record at that granularity and would need to be estimated from your own MC/data validation.
