## Verdict

No — don't clip and move on. You have (at least) two separate problems, and the −1.3 correlation is not the kind of defect eigenvalue-clipping is for.

## 1. Correlation of −1.3 between bins 4 and 5

A correlation coefficient is bounded to `[-1, 1]` by construction (Cauchy–Schwarz) for *any* valid covariance matrix, regardless of its eigenvalue spectrum. So this isn't something a PSD check discovers as a borderline numerical issue — the matrix is already known to be wrong before you even compute eigenvalues.

To show what the skill's own checker reports here, I built a small illustrative 5-bin matrix reproducing your stated ρ(bin4,bin5) = −1.3 (not your real data) and ran `scripts/validate_covariance.py` on it:

```
errors:
  correlation_bound: 1 correlation(s) outside [-1, 1]; rho[3,4] = -1.300000
  not_psd: smallest eigenvalue -1.881 is negative beyond tolerance
           (1.0e-08 x largest 14.69); suspect a construction error, not numerics
```

That second line is the key distinction the reference draws: round-off-level negative eigenvalues (magnitude ~ 1e-8 × the largest eigenvalue) are numerical and safe to clip; a negative eigenvalue orders of magnitude above that — which is guaranteed here, since |ρ|>1 forces it — is a **construction error**. Per `inference-and-unfolding.md`: *"Construction error: do not clip. Check mixed conventions (absolute vs relative, bin ordering, units), rounded or guessed correlation tables, subtracted terms with an implausible correlation, components added twice, and unfolding-induced anticorrelation from an under-regularized inversion. Rebuild from coherent variations... propagate through the full unfolding with toys."*

Likely causes to check, roughly in order of how common they are for an *unfolded-flux* covariance specifically:
- **Unfolding-induced anticorrelation from an under-regularized (or directly inverted) response.** Bin 4–5 are adjacent, which is exactly where matrix-inversion-type unfolding produces spurious large anticorrelations if the response is ill-conditioned or not regularized. Given you separately report a response matrix problem (below), this is a strong candidate — the two issues may share a root cause.
- Mixed absolute/relative or mixed statistical+systematic blocks added with an inconsistent convention (e.g. one block already includes what the other double-adds).
- A hand-built or interpolated correlation table (guessed/rounded values) rather than a covariance propagated from actual variations.
- Bin-ordering mismatch between two matrices you added together.

Run `scripts/validate_covariance.py` on your *actual* matrix — it also reports `numerical_rank` and `condition_number`, which will tell you if this is an over-regularized/degenerate unfolding rather than a bookkeeping slip.

## 2. Response columns summing to 0.85, plus a separately applied trigger efficiency

This is the exact scenario the reference warns about: *"Do not diagnose a sum of, say, 0.85 as inefficiency alone: check whether the matrix stores under/overflow rows, and state which reading the declared metadata implies."* There are two legitimate readings, and they lead to opposite verdicts on your trigger-efficiency multiplication. I ran both through `scripts/validate_response.py` on an illustrative 3-bin matrix with column sums of 0.85 to show what each looks like:

**Reading A — inefficiency declared inside the matrix** (`normalization: includes_efficiency`, `inefficiency: inside_matrix`) with `efficiency_applied_separately: true`:
```
error: double_counting.inefficiency — inefficiency is declared inside the matrix
       and also applied separately: it would be counted twice
```
If this is your case, multiplying by the trigger efficiency **is** a real double count (your invariant-3 violation: `A` and `ε` already inside the response, applied again).

**Reading B — inefficiency declared outside the matrix** (`normalization: conditional_on_selected`, `inefficiency: outside_matrix`), same 0.85 column sums:
```
error: normalization.truth_sum — truth-bin sums [0.85,...] are not 1 under
       'conditional_on_selected' (probability lost or duplicated)
error: overflow.lost_probability — probability leaves the reco axes and
       underflow/overflow is 'not_represented'
```
If this is your declared convention, 0.85 is *not* a legitimate inefficiency reading at all — it means either the matrix wasn't actually built to the declared convention, or true events are migrating into reconstructed bins outside your tabulated range and you aren't storing under/overflow. Your separate trigger-efficiency multiplication would then be conceptually correct, but the matrix itself needs fixing before you trust anything downstream of it.

**You cannot tell which case you're in from the column sum alone.** Check your response card's declared `inefficiency` and `underflow_overflow` fields (or write them down if you never declared them), and run `scripts/validate_response.py` on your real matrix + metadata.

## Bottom line

- Don't clip: the −1.3 correlation already proves this isn't a round-off-level PSD issue: fix the construction (most likely candidate is the response/unfolding regularization, given problem 2), rebuild the covariance from coherent variations or toys, and re-check with `validate_covariance.py`.
- Resolve the response-matrix reading first (inefficiency inside vs. outside, under/overflow explicit or not) — that determines whether your trigger-efficiency multiplication is a bug or correct, and it likely also explains the anticorrelation.
- Both problems point the same way: silently repairing symptoms (clip eigenvalues, renormalize columns) without finding the construction error will hide a real bias rather than fix it.
