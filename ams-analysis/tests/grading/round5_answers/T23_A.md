The **Anti-Coincidence Counters (ACC)** are scintillator panels surrounding the Tracker whose job is **vetoing**, not measuring a physics observable. [Documented — public detector description]

- **Purpose.** Reject particles that enter through the sides of the magnet/Tracker (i.e., outside the nominal geometric acceptance defined by the top-to-bottom track), and reject events with extra activity that would corrupt reconstruction.
- **Mechanism.** A charged particle crossing a panel produces a scintillation signal; thresholded panel signals feed an event-level veto decision (fire the ACC → veto the event), rather than a track or energy reconstruction.
- **What it is *not*.** It doesn't measure rigidity, charge sign, `|Z|`, or velocity — it has no role in the cross-subsystem matrix for any of those quantities except as a "side-entry veto," where it's the primary source (Tracker/TOF/ECAL/RICH only give *geometry support* for that same veto, not an independent measurement).
- **Trade-off to keep in mind.** A tighter veto removes more off-acceptance and shower-contaminated events but also loses some valid events to accidental or "backsplash" activity (e.g., particles scattering back from an ECAL shower at high energy). That loss is an **efficiency** to be measured on its own — it must not also be folded into the geometric acceptance, or the two corrections double-count the same effect.

In short: the ACC is a veto/quality system, not a spectrometer subsystem — it decides whether an event is *kept* for analysis, not what its kinematics are.
