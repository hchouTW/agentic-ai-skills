**Verdict: not yet sound — the described procedure needs revision before it can be trusted as a proton flux measurement.**

Your recipe — count events after cuts, divide by acceptance × livetime × bin width, apply a bin-by-bin factor from MC — is a **diagonal/bin-by-bin-correction estimator**. That form is only valid under specific conditions that your description doesn't establish. In priority order:

1. **No background subtraction is mentioned.** The flux estimator is `Φ_i = (n_i − b_i) / (ΔR_i · A_i · ε_i · T_i)`, not `n_i / (...)`. For a proton sample this matters at the level of charge confusion (opposite-sign leakage), electron contamination, and D/He cross-contamination depending on rigidity. If your "count events after cuts" already nets these out, say how (sideband, template fit, MC-corrected) and where the background ledger lives; if it doesn't, this is the largest gap. [General method — this is the standard flux blueprint, not an AMS-specific claim]

2. **"Bin-by-bin factor from MC" as the whole migration treatment is invalid under appreciable migration, and this must be demonstrated, not assumed.** A steeply falling spectrum (proton flux falls roughly as a power law) has non-negligible feed-up from lower true bins into higher reconstructed bins even when the resolution core looks small. The diagonal/bin-by-bin correction is only acceptable after showing migration is negligible at your required precision using the actual response matrix — otherwise you need a response-matrix unfolding or forward-folded fit. Before trusting the correction factor, validate it against `R_ij` (nominal MC closure at minimum; ideally reweighted-spectrum and distorted-spectrum closure tests too).

3. **"Acceptance" is ambiguous between geometric acceptance and effective acceptance.** These are not interchangeable: geometric acceptance is a pure geometry integral, effective acceptance already folds in the average selection/reconstruction efficiency for the species/bin. State which one you're using. If efficiency is *not* already inside your "acceptance" term, it's simply missing from the formula as written, and if selection efficiencies are combined as a product of several cut efficiencies, that's only valid if the cuts are independent for this population — a claim that needs a factorization-bias check, not an assumption.

4. **Livetime alone is not exposure.** Exposure requires livetime *and* the fraction of time each rigidity bin is above the geomagnetic cutoff (with whatever safety factor you use), integrated correctly over the interval when acceptance and livetime vary together. Your description doesn't mention a geomagnetic selection or transmission factor at all — this biases low-rigidity bins specifically if omitted.

5. **No systematic/covariance treatment is described.** Even a formula that is structurally right needs the rigidity-scale and resolution-tail systematics (a scale shift must be propagated through migration, not just applied to the final spectrum), MC provenance validation (data/MC agreement with a metric and an action, not "reasonable agreement"), and a covariance that is not just per-bin percentage errors — adjacent bins are correlated through both the correction factor and shared systematics.

**Inputs that would change this verdict:**
- Confirmation the bin-by-bin factor is derived from and validated against a real response matrix (closure test result, not just "from MC"), with an explicit migration-negligibility check for this spectrum's steepness.
- The background ledger: which backgrounds are subtracted before `n_i`, by which estimator, with what control region and closure test.
- Whether "acceptance" already includes selection efficiency (effective acceptance with data/MC scale factors) or efficiency is applied separately.
- Whether livetime already encodes geomagnetic transmission, or that factor is missing.
- The systematic ledger (rigidity scale, resolution tails, MC provenance, geomagnetic model/safety-factor variation) and how correlations across bins are propagated.

None of this requires AMS-internal numbers to check — these are structural requirements from the general flux-measurement methodology, independent of which specific AMS proton analysis you're modeling this on.
