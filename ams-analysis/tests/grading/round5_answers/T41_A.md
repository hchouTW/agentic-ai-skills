**[Documented, C90, S01 — AMS Phys. Rept. review, "AMS on the ISS Part II," Section 16, full text]**

**The number:** the transition takes Δt = 830 ± 30 days, and AMS finds this transition time to be independent of energy (checked from 1–21 GeV).

**How AMS got it:** AMS built 3871 independent measurements of the positron-to-electron ratio R_e = Φ(e⁺)/Φ(e⁻), resolved jointly in energy and time, and fit them with a four-parameter logistic function per energy bin:

R_e(t, E) = R0(E) · [1 + C(E) / (exp(−(t − t½(E)) / (Δt/Δ₈₀)) + 1)]

with the constant Δ₈₀ = 4.39 chosen so that the fitted Δt corresponds to the 10%–90% transition time (not, e.g., a half-width or a 1σ width). Fits were made per energy bin over 1–21 GeV with χ²/d.o.f. ≈ 1 in all cases, and the fitted Δt came out statistically consistent with a single, energy-independent value of 830 ± 30 days.

The reversal itself is referenced to t_rev = 1 July 2013, which the review states is a stated choice — the center of the period during which the Sun's polarity was not well-defined — not a sharply measured reversal instant. The midpoint of each energy bin's transition, t½(E), lags t_rev following a power law: t½(E) − t_rev = τ·(E/GeV)^ρ, with ρ = −0.33 ± 0.04 (stat) (+0.08, −0.15) (syst) and τ = 580 ± 19 (stat) ± 136 (syst) days; t½ shifts by 260 ± 30 days between 1 and 6 GeV.

**Caveats to keep attached to the 830-day number:**
- The transition amplitude C(E) is largest (near 1) at 1 GeV, shrinks smoothly with energy, and is consistent with zero above ~20 GeV. That means Δt and t½ are only well-constrained by data at the lower end of the range; above 6 GeV the review does not refit them but instead fixes Δt to the 830-day value and uses the power-law form for t½. So "energy-independent at 830 ± 30 days" is really the review's determination at low energy, extended by assumption to higher energy rather than independently confirmed there.
- This is a descriptive fit the collaboration chose (a logistic curve) to characterize the transition shape, not a physical model of the underlying heliospheric-modulation mechanism — the review notes it is in qualitative agreement with drift-including solar-modulation models, which is a separate, model-level statement.
- No trial-factor or cross-bin correlated-error treatment is described in the text read, so treat the quoted statistical/systematic split as what AMS reports, without assuming further correction has been applied.
- This is a review-level (Phys. Rept. 894, 2021) summary; the individual electron/positron temporal-structure papers (S45, S46, 2023) are the primary AMS publications on this topic and may contain more detail than the review restates.
