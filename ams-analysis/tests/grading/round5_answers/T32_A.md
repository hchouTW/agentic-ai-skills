# Design: 27-day-binned e⁺/(e⁺+e⁻)-style ratio, 5–50 GV, solar cycle, searched for a 1-year periodicity

(Using "positron-to-electron ratio" as `e⁺/e⁻`; if you actually want the positron *fraction* `e⁺/(e⁺+e⁻)`, the denominators/covariance below change accordingly — say which.)

## 1. Estimand and variable

Target: `e⁺/e⁻` (or fraction) vs. rigidity `R` in 5–50 GV, in successive 27-day time bins, over a stated solar-cycle span; then a periodicity search at a fixed ~1-year period on that time series (per rigidity bin, or on an integrated summary). **Rigidity, not energy**, is your stated variable — note that this matches the *daily* electron/positron papers' binning (1.00–41.9 GV, rigidity) **[Documented, S45/S46]**, not the Bartels-rotation lepton paper, which bins in energy **[Documented, C48, S42]**. State this choice explicitly since it determines whether any comparison to AMS's own time-structure results is apples-to-apples.

## 2. Time binning: why 27 days, and what "Bartels rotation" does and doesn't buy you

A Bartels rotation is a fixed 27-day interval **[General method]**; AMS has built time-resolved *lepton* products both per-day (S45, S46) and per-Bartels-rotation, with 4-rotation (108-day) blocks used for deuterons/Li isotopes specifically **[Documented, C27/C29, S13/S14]** — that 108-day choice is those two analyses' statistics-driven decision, not a rule to reuse here. A single 27-day bin for a lepton *ratio* in 5–50 GV will be far more statistics-limited than the flux papers (leptons are a small fraction of the sample, and a ratio needs both numerator and denominator populated per bin per rigidity slice) — check expected counts per (rigidity, 27-day) cell before committing to single-rotation bins; if too sparse, either widen the rigidity binning or move to multi-rotation blocks (with the loss of time resolution that implies for the periodicity search). **[Proposal]**

Bin-edge rules that apply regardless of the count check: align bins so none straddles a hardware-era or reconstruction-version boundary (a version change invalidates the response/templates for whichever period straddles it); fix the binning, range, and stability metric before looking at the time dependence, and record any later change.

## 3. Per-bin ratio construction (electron/positron blueprint)

Each 27-day bin needs its **own** full extraction, not a single fit reused across bins — charge confusion, proton contamination, and efficiencies are all rigidity- and time-dependent:

- **Selection**: Tracker charge sign (only source of sign; TRD is not a charge-sign detector — invariant 2) + `|Z|=1`; TRD/ECAL lepton discriminants; `E/p` compatibility.
- **Backgrounds per sign, per bin**: proton contamination (dominant, rejected mainly via ECAL) and charge confusion (`e⁻→e⁺` migration via the Tracker tail — a **tail** phenomenon, never estimated from the resolution core per invariant 4). AMS's own e± papers extract the signal via a 2D template fit in (Λ_TRD, Λ_CC) with electron-data/MC-derived templates **[Documented, C24/C25, S04; C26, S05]** — a defensible template-fit approach to adapt per time bin, but that specific template construction is those two papers' choice, not necessarily what you'll have data/statistics for per 27-day slice.
- **Efficiencies**: conditional chain (trigger → selection → reconstruction), each measured or scale-factored per species/sign/rigidity/time; do not assume they cancel between numerator and denominator.

## 4. Ratio-specific cancellation classification (per effect, per bin)

Using the ratio blueprint's classification table, specialized to e⁺/e⁻:

| Effect | Likely correlation e⁺ vs e⁻ | Note for this design |
|---|---|---|
| Trigger efficiency | Partial | Same trigger, but efficiency can differ slightly by sign-correlated topology |
| Geometric acceptance | Largely common | Same fiducial, verify by MC |
| Charge confusion (e⁻→e⁺ tail) | Species/sign-specific | Not a cancelling effect — sizes the numerator/denominator asymmetrically |
| Unfolding/response | Correlated only if same response & R scale | Verify: is one response used for both signs, or separate? |
| **Livetime/exposure** | Common if same period, **but geomagnetic transmission differs with charge sign at low R** | 5 GV is close enough to typical cutoff-sensitive rigidities that this cannot be assumed common — compute transmission per sign, per bin |

Do not assume the ratio's acceptance/systematics cancel; propagate the joint covariance `Var(N/D) ≈ f²[(σ_N/N)² + (σ_D/D)² − 2ρσ_Nσ_D/(ND)]` (first-order; use toys if uncertainties aren't small/Gaussian).

## 5. Exposure, per bin, per sign

`Φ_k = (N_k − b_k)/(E_k ΔR)` with `E_k` accumulated *inside* the bin — never average fluxes/ratios of bins with different exposure, only combine through counts and exposures. Solar modulation is **charge-sign dependent** (heliospheric field polarity), so e⁺ and e⁻ (or the ratio itself) need identical time bins, rigidity variable and edges, but **sign-specific cutoff and charge-confusion handling** — this is exactly why the ratio, not just each flux separately, is the physically interesting quantity here, but it also means you cannot borrow one sign's exposure/systematics for the other.

If your solar-cycle span crosses the 2013 magnetic-polarity reversal, note that AMS's own Phys. Rept. review already reports a 4-parameter logistic fit of the e⁺/e⁻(or similar) time structure across that reversal, Δt = 830 ± 30 days, energy-independent, `t_rev` = 1 July 2013 as a stated choice **[Documented, C90, S01]** — useful as a comparison/cross-check functional form, not as a rule for your rigidity-binned, 27-day design, and not verified beyond the review's own restatement.

## 6. Covariance across (time, rigidity)

Classify each systematic as persistent (calibration, template shape, cross-section model — correlated across all 27-day bins), slowly varying (correlated between neighboring bins, e.g. a drifting calibration), or independent per bin (statistics). Build one covariance over the joint (time, rigidity) space with a documented ordering; a Kronecker factorization (time matrix ⊗ rigidity matrix) is valid only if you test that the correlation actually factorizes — don't assume it. Run `scripts/validate_covariance.py` on the assembled matrix before using it (symmetry, PSD, correlation bounds, conditioning). Normalizing every 27-day bin to one reference bin only cancels the persistent part of the systematics and correlates every bin's statistical error with the reference bin — carry that correlation if you do this.

## 7. The 1-year periodicity search

- **Predefine, before looking:** the exact period/frequency band around 1 year (not just "near 1 year" — state the tolerance), the observable (the ratio series itself, or per-rigidity-bin), the test statistic, and the significance threshold.
- **Uneven sampling / uneven exposure**: 27-day bins over a multi-year span will have bin-to-bin exposure and count variations (orbit precession, veto/quality-time changes, detector configuration changes); use a method that weights by per-bin uncertainty and tolerates uneven spacing rather than assuming a uniform-cadence FFT.
- **Trial factor**: even searching one nominal frequency, if you scan a band around 1 year (rather than testing a single pre-registered frequency) you incur a look-elsewhere effect — report local *and* global significance with a Gross–Vitells-style trial-factor treatment, calibrated with toys reproducing your real sampling/exposure pattern and correlated systematics. Do not use Wilks' theorem or a single-frequency p-value (invariant 6; also an explicit failure mode in `time-dependent-analysis.md`).
- **The critical confound for a 1-year signal specifically:** annual periodicities are a classic *instrumental/orbital* artifact — Earth's orbital motion modulates Sun-Earth distance and hence, indirectly, geomagnetic and detector thermal/seasonal effects; ISS attitude and pointing patterns can also carry annual components. Before treating any detected ~1-year signal as physics, run the identical search on (a) the exposure time series, (b) an efficiency time series, and (c) a control species (e.g. proton or the combined e⁺+e⁻ flux) built the same way. A coincident signal in any of these is evidence of an instrumental/orbital origin, not a discovery, per `time-dependent-analysis.md`'s explicit failure mode ("Apparent periodicities can come from exposure, orbit, season or detector-state cycles").
- AMS's daily-proton periodicity paper used a wavelet time-frequency method with significance from Monte Carlo red-noise (lag-1 autoregressive) surrogates **[Documented, C45/C52, S43]** — a reasonable methodology to adapt for your ratio series, but it is that paper's method for a different observable (daily proton flux) and its trial-factor treatment for periodicity/hysteresis significances is **not described** in the text read here **[Unknown/needs input]** — do not assume AMS's exact significance construction transfers; define and validate your own.

## 8. Joint model / reporting

`L = Π_k Π_i Poisson(n_ki | μ_ki(θ_shared, φ_k, η))` with shared spectral-shape parameters, per-period normalization/modulation parameters, and nuisances classified per §6; test the joint/shared-shape hypothesis against separate per-period fits with toys rather than asserting it. Report per-period ratios with their covariance and exposures (not just a single combined periodicity number), so the time structure itself remains visible and re-fittable.
