# Design: limit setting for a rare-antinucleus search, zero observed events

This follows the **rare-antimatter blueprint** — every step below is a **[Proposal]** design unless a primary AMS source is cited; AMS has not published an antihelium or antideuteron search/limit paper (the only AMS-authored antihelium result in the index is the 1999 AMS-01 limit, S22; conference/thesis material exists but is not a peer-reviewed result), so nothing here should be presented as "how AMS does it."

## 1. Define the signal topology before touching data

State the target species (antideuteron, antihelium-3/4, or heavier), the discriminating observables: **sign** from the Tracker (only source), **`|Z|`** from multiple subsystems (Tracker, TOF, RICH), and **mass** from `R` and `β` jointly (`m = |Z||R|/(γβ)`). Define the signal region in `(R, β, |Z|)` space and the mass window around the antinucleus hypothesis.

## 2. Blind the signal region

Mask it in plots, logs, and optimization before defining cuts; state in advance who may unblind and when, and what the unblinding checklist requires (candidate-level diagnostics below).

## 3. Background/misidentification paths to model and validate

- **Charge-sign confusion** of the far-more-abundant same-`|Z|` matter nucleus (e.g., He → "anti-He") via Tracker curvature-sign tails and bad hit assignment — this is the dominant concern and **must be validated in the tail, not the Gaussian core** of the rigidity resolution (non-negotiable).
- **`|Z|` confusion** from fragmentation/charge-changing interactions upstream.
- Secondary tracks, and interactions that themselves produce antiparticles.
- Wrong mass from `β` errors — recall the mass-resolution formula has a `γ²` amplification of `δβ/β`, so mass separation degrades sharply as `β → 1`; state the rigidity range where your mass resolution is actually usable rather than assuming full-range sensitivity.

**Data-driven tail measurement**: use an abundant matter sample (e.g., He or protons) under progressively loosened cuts to measure the sign-flip tail probability as a function of rigidity, then extrapolate to the tight selection actually used — and test that extrapolation's assumptions rather than taking them on faith.

## 4. Redundant consistency checks (before deciding signal region cuts)

- Independent partial-track fits (different layer subsets / upper vs. lower segments) must agree in sign, on both data and MC.
- Subsystem `|Z|` estimators (Tracker, TOF, RICH) must be mutually consistent within validated tails, before and after the magnet/material.
- Charge-changing interaction rates and secondary-track rates validated on abundant control samples.

## 5. Exposure

`E = A_eff(R) × ε(R) × T_live × (geomagnetic transmission)`, computed for the signal species and rigidity range (not copied from a matter-particle analysis without justification, since survival/interaction cross-sections differ by species). This is what turns a candidate/count limit into a flux limit.

## 6. Statistical treatment given zero observed events

With **zero candidates observed**, do not use `S/√B`, Wilks, or a Gaussian error — invariant #6. The construction depends on whether the background is exactly known:

- **[General method]** If background is treated as exactly known and `b = 0`: the classical one-sided Poisson upper limit on the signal mean at 95% CL is `-ln(1 − CL) ≈ 3.00` events. I ran `scripts/poisson_diagnostics.py upper-limit --n 0 --b 0 --cl 0.95` to confirm this: it returns `upper_limit_on_signal = 2.9957...`, matching `-ln(0.05)`.
- **[General method]** If `b > 0` and known exactly, the *classical* limit is `-ln(1−CL) − b` — it **shrinks** as `b` grows and can go to zero or negative (in which case the script reports "no limit," which is itself informative, not a result to report as-is). The **CLs** limit, by contrast, is `b`-independent and equals `-ln(1−CL)` regardless of `b`. These are different constructions answering different questions — do not call the classical zero-count bound "background-independent."
- **[General method]** In a real analysis the background is essentially never known exactly (it comes from a tail-probability/misidentification model with its own uncertainty), so the appropriate construction is **Feldman–Cousins or CLs with a profile likelihood and toys**, not the bare classical formula above — the classical number is a sanity-check anchor, not the final limit. Nuisance parameters to profile/marginalize: the sign-flip tail probability, efficiency, and exposure uncertainty. Validate the interval's **coverage** with seeded toys (`scripts/poisson_diagnostics.py coverage`) rather than assuming nominal coverage holds.
- Convert the count limit to a flux (or matter-relative ratio) limit through the exposure: `Φ_UL = N_UL / (E · Δx)`, propagating the exposure uncertainty (including any correlation with the background model) as a nuisance.

## 7. Reporting discipline

Distinguish **candidate** (an event passing full selection, subject to diagnostics), **excess**, **evidence**, and **discovery** with thresholds declared *before* unblinding. Zero observed events after unblinding is reported as an upper limit, full stop — never invent candidate counts, cuts, bins, livetimes, acceptances, or systematic sizes that no source provides (non-negotiable invariant #8), and never present this workflow as AMS's actual, unpublished search methodology.
