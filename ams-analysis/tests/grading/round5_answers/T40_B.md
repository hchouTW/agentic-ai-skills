No — a constant ratio is consistent with a common origin, but it doesn't prove one.

**What's documented.** [C77, S01 (AMS Phys. Rept. 894 review, full-text, main article; Section 6, Fig. 69)] Above 60 GeV the positron-to-antiproton flux ratio is consistent with a constant; a fit over 60–525 GeV gives `2.00 ± 0.035 (stat.) ± 0.06 (syst.)`, χ²/d.o.f. = 7.2/12. The review reads this as positrons and antiprotons having "nearly identical energy dependence at high energies" and says it "suggests a possible common source" — note the hedged language even from AMS itself. The source-index entry is explicit that this is the review's interpretation, not a proven conclusion, and adds important context: the review also states that antiprotons are *not* produced by pulsars (one of the leading positron-excess explanations), that a cutoff in the antiproton spectrum would be *expected* if high-energy antiprotons come from dark-matter annihilation (not that one is observed), and that continued data taking is needed to confirm the origin.

**Why this isn't proof.** [General method — statistical/interpretation logic, per the skill's result-interpretation rule: direct measurement vs. model interpretation are separate steps, and "agreement between measurements is consistency, not proof."]
- A constant ratio over a 60–525 GeV window is a single observable. Two species can track each other in that range for reasons other than sharing a source — e.g. if each has its own physical origin but happens to have a similar power-law index over that particular energy range, the ratio would look flat without any causal link.
- It's a necessary-but-not-sufficient condition: a genuine common source predicts a constant ratio, but a constant ratio doesn't uniquely imply a common source (the inference doesn't run backward without ruling out alternatives).
- The claim "common origin" is itself ambiguous — common origin under which mechanism? The result is in *tension* with a pulsar origin for the shared feature (since pulsars don't make antiprotons), and only *consistent with* origins that can produce both species together (e.g. certain dark-matter annihilation/decay channels, or exotic astrophysical sources) — and even for those, AMS's own review treats the antiproton-cutoff test as a still-open discriminator, not yet observed.
- Confirming a common origin would need independent, cross-checked observables (e.g. matching cutoff behavior in the antiproton spectrum itself, consistent anisotropy, agreement with a specific production/propagation model over an extended range) rather than a single ratio fit over a limited window — and the review explicitly says more data are needed before the origin question is settled.

**Bottom line:** treat the constant ratio as evidence compatible with (and interesting for) a shared-source hypothesis, not as proof of one. The measurement itself is solid; "common origin" is a model-interpretation step that AMS's own review does not claim to have closed.
