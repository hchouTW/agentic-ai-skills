**[Documented]** The Anti-Coincidence Counters (ACC) are scintillator panels surrounding the Tracker inside the magnet bore. Their job is to **veto particles that enter from the side** (through the magnet, rather than along the nominal downward-going acceptance) and to **reject events with extra activity** (e.g., a shower or secondary interaction) that would corrupt the track reconstruction.

**Mechanism:** a charged particle crossing the panels produces a light signal that is thresholded into a veto decision at the event level — if the ACC fires when it shouldn't (side entry, extra activity), the event is rejected.

**Trade-off to keep in mind:** a tighter veto removes more off-acceptance/shower-contaminated events but also loses some valid events from accidental hits or shower backsplash (e.g., from the ECAL at high energy) hitting the panels. That loss is properly an **efficiency**, not part of the geometric acceptance — the two must not be double-counted.

**Failure modes:** energy-dependent inefficiency from ECAL backsplash at high energy, time/orbit-rate-dependent accidental vetoes, and hardware inefficiency that lets false side-entering events through.

The ACC is a veto only — it does not measure charge sign, `|Z|`, `β`, or direction on its own (see the cross-subsystem matrix: it contributes "No" to all of those, and is the primary source only for the side-entry veto).

One documented performance figure, with its context: the ACC side-entry rejection efficiency is quoted as **0.99999** in the antiproton paper (AMS, PRL 117, 091103, 2016). This number is context-specific (that analysis's selection) and should not be treated as a universal constant.
