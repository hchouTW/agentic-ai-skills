**Positron cutoff: not 5σ — it's 4.07σ.** [Documented — C97, S01 (AMS Phys. Rept. 894 review, full-text, main article; Section 2, Eq. 5, Figs. 39–45)] Fitting the positron flux (0.5–1000 GeV) to a diffuse-plus-source parametrization with an exponential cutoff `E_s`, AMS finds `E_s = 810 (+310, −180) GeV`, with the no-cutoff hypothesis (`1/E_s = 0`) excluded at **4.07σ** (>99.99% C.L.). The source-index entry for this claim explicitly notes it is "the same 4.07σ" as the original positron paper's result and states plainly that "5 sigma is not supported." So the number you're citing should be corrected downward. Two caveats worth keeping: this significance comes from a six-parameter scan without a stated look-elsewhere/trials correction, and `E_s` is the cutoff of a fitted parametrized source term, not a model-independent spectral break in the data.

**Electron flux: no, it does not show the same cutoff.** [Documented — C99, S01, Section 3, Eqs. 8–9, Figs. 50–51] AMS tested this directly two ways:
- Fixing the positron's source-term shape (including `E_s = 810 GeV`) and fitting the electron flux with the source normalization `f` free: `f = 0.5 (+1.2, −0.6)` with no χ² improvement over either `f=1` (source present) or `f=0` (absent). In other words, the electron data **neither require nor exclude** a shared 810 GeV-cutoff source term — they simply don't have the discriminating power to say either way.
- Fitting the electron spectrum for its *own* cutoff parameter independently, AMS excludes any electron cutoff energy `E_s < 1.9 TeV` at 5σ. So on its own, the electron spectrum shows no evidence of a low-energy cutoff at all — its cutoff limit is more than a factor of 2 higher than the positron's fitted value.

**Bottom line:** the positron cutoff is a real, fitted feature at 4.07σ (not 5σ), and the electron flux does not display an analogous cutoff near 810 GeV — it's compatible with either a shared source term or none, and independently rules out any cutoff below ~1.9 TeV.
