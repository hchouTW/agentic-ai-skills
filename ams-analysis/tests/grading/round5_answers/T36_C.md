**No — the ~810 GeV positron cutoff is not a 5σ result, and the electron flux does not show the same cutoff.**

**Positron cutoff significance.** [Documented, C35, S04] In "Towards Understanding the Origin of Cosmic-Ray Positrons" (AMS, PRL 122, 041102, 2019), the abstract states the source-term energy cutoff is significant at "more than 4σ," and the article's final fit discussion gives **4.07σ**. A claim of 5σ is explicitly not supported by this paper.
[Documented, C97, S01 Phys. Rept. review] The later Phys. Rept. review (AMS, 2021) restates the same fit: `Φ(E) ∝ [diffuse term] + [source term]×exp(−E/E_s)` with `E_s = 810 (+310, −180) GeV`, obtained by scanning all six fit parameters to map the significance region; `1/E_s = 0` (no cutoff) lies at **4.07σ** (>99.99% C.L.) — consistent with C35, not 5σ. This significance is a chi²-based scan of a specific parametrized model over a stated energy range, not a look-elsewhere-corrected discovery significance.

**Does the electron flux show the same cutoff?** [Documented, C99, S01] No — the electron flux does **not** show an equivalent low-energy cutoff. Two related tests in the review:
1. Fitting the electron flux (41.61–1400 GeV) with a power law plus the positron's fixed source term (`E_s = 810 GeV` held from the positron fit), the source-term normalization is left free: the data are consistent both with the source term fully present (`f=1`) and fully absent (`f=0`), with no χ² improvement when `f` floats (`f = 0.5 (+1.2, −0.6)`) — i.e., the electron data neither require nor exclude a shared source term.
2. Fitting the electron flux directly with its own power-law-plus-exponential-cutoff form, the best fit gives `1/E_s ≈ 0` (no evidence of a cutoff at all in that range), and cutoff energies **`E_s < 1.9 TeV` are excluded at 5σ**. That is a *limit excluding a low cutoff*, not a detection — and it is well above the positron's 810 GeV, meaning the electron spectrum is inconsistent with a cutoff as sharp/low as the positron's.

[Documented, C98, S01] Separately, the electron flux's own transition/hardening fit (`E0 = 42.1 (+5.4, −5.2) GeV`, stated at 7σ) describes a spectral hardening around tens of GeV, which is a different feature from the positron's high-energy cutoff — the review states the nature of the electron excess is different from that of the positron excess.

**Bottom line:** the positron 810 GeV feature is a 4.07σ result (not 5σ), and the electron flux shows no corresponding cutoff — it is statistically consistent with no cutoff up to ~1.4 TeV and excludes a cutoff as low as 1.9 TeV at 5σ. These significances are model-dependent (specific parametrized fits) and not look-elsewhere-corrected.
