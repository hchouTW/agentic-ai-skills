[Documented, C67] The AMS Phys. Rept. review (S01, "AMS on the ISS Part II," 2021, section 1.3) reports a TRD-alone proton rejection **above 1000 at 90% e± efficiency, over 2-200 GeV/c**, which improves further if the e± efficiency working point is tightened to 65% (no specific higher number is given at 65%). 100 GeV falls inside that quoted momentum range, so "above 1000" is a defensible floor to assume for the TRD discriminant alone at that working point — but treat it carefully:

- **It's a lower bound for one specific selection**, not a universal number: the claim's own scope note says the value 1000 is a lower bound "for that selection and range only," measured on-orbit for the review's own TRD estimator working point. If your analysis uses a different Λ_TRD cut, efficiency point, or template-fit approach instead of a hard threshold, this number does not automatically transfer.
- **TRD is only one leg of the published e/p separation, not the whole rejection.** AMS's actual positron/electron papers (S04/S05) don't rely on TRD alone — they cut on an energy-dependent ECAL estimator Λ_ECAL first (which reduces the antiproton background in the negative-rigidity sample to <10⁻³ after the electron cut, per S05) and then run a 2D template fit in (Λ_TRD, Λ_CC). The review separately quotes an ECAL-only proton rejection of about a further factor of 3 (independent of TRD) when tightening from 90% to 65% e± efficiency (C72). So if you're assuming ">1000" as your *total* proton rejection for a positron analysis, that's an underestimate of what's achievable with ECAL added, but also not something you should assume without your own validation.
- **Don't just plug the review number in as your analysis's performance.** Charge/species misidentification is a tail phenomenon — the Gaussian core of any resolution doesn't set the mis-ID rate. You need a data-driven proton control sample to validate the actual tail rejection your specific selection achieves at 100 GeV, rather than importing this figure as a substitute for that validation.

So: **>1000 at 90% e± efficiency (C67) is a reasonable documented starting assumption for a TRD-only cut at 100 GeV**, with the understanding that it's a lower bound from a specific past on-orbit measurement/selection, it doesn't include ECAL's additional rejection, and it should be checked against your own control-sample tail validation before being used as an analysis input.
