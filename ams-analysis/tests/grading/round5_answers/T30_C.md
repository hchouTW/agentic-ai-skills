**Verdict: no — do not just clip the eigenvalues and move on.** You have at least two likely construction errors, and clipping only masks numerical (round-off-level) non-positive-semidefiniteness, not bugs. [General method]

**1. The -1.3 correlation is not a "slightly non-PSD" matrix — it's an impossible number.** A correlation coefficient is bounded in [-1, 1] by definition (it's a normalized covariance). Getting -1.3 out of `Cov_45 / sqrt(Var_4 · Var_5)` means the matrix entries themselves are inconsistent (e.g., `Cov_45²` already exceeds `Var_4 · Var_5`), which can't happen for a legitimately built covariance. This is upstream of eigenvalue clipping entirely — clipping fixes small negative eigenvalues from round-off, it does not fix an entry that violates the Cauchy-Schwarz bound. Before touching eigenvalues, find the construction bug. Common causes to check: mixed conventions (absolute vs. relative blocks summed as if consistent), a bin-ordering mismatch between the covariance matrix and the variance/mean vector, a subtracted term (e.g. background subtraction) whose correlation was estimated or guessed rather than propagated, a component added in twice, or unfolding-induced anti-correlation that was mis-normalized during propagation. Only once the entries are internally consistent does it make sense to symmetrize `(C+Cᵀ)/2`, take eigenvalues, and compare the most negative to the largest — round-off level (~machine precision × largest eigenvalue) is fine to clip; anything larger is a bug to rebuild, not clip. If it's a bug, do not repair by dropping correlations or clipping — rebuild from coherent systematic variations (or toys propagated through the full unfolding, whose sample covariance is PSD by construction).

**2. A response-matrix column sum of 0.85 has two legitimate readings, and you need to know which applies before you touch it.** Either (a) the matrix is normalized "conditional on the selected sample" and 0.85 reflects true inefficiency (events not reconstructed into the selected sample) that should already live in your acceptance factor, or (b) the matrix stores migration only within the tabulated reconstructed-bin range, and 0.85 reflects feed-out to un-stored under/overflow bins rather than inefficiency at all. Don't assume it's "the" efficiency without checking your matrix's declared metadata (does it store under/overflow rows? was it built with an efficiency-inclusive or efficiency-excluded convention?).

**3. The trigger-efficiency multiplication is the real red flag.** If your response matrix's <1 column sums already include events lost because they failed the trigger during MC generation (i.e., trigger loss is baked into how R was built), and you *also* multiply reconstructed counts by a separate trigger-efficiency correction, you are correcting the same effect twice — inefficiency must live in exactly one place (either inside `R`, or in the acceptance/exposure factor applied once), never both. Trace back how R was built: were events required to pass a simulated trigger before being histogrammed into R? If yes, drop the separate trigger-efficiency multiplication (or vice versa — pick one). This double counting would itself distort your unfolded flux and could easily be feeding into the pathological covariance too, since an incorrectly over/under-corrected normalization can produce spurious anti-correlation when propagated.

**What to actually do:** (1) fix the response-matrix inefficiency/under-overflow ambiguity and the trigger double-counting first — these change your central values, not just your errors; (2) rebuild the covariance from coherent variations or toys so it's PSD by construction, or at minimum locate and fix the entry that produces an out-of-bounds correlation; (3) only after both of those, if a small residual non-PSD-ness remains at the round-off level, clip it, record the change, and confirm your fit/χ² result is unchanged within tolerance.
