# Limit-setting design for a rare-antinucleus search, zero observed events

This is a symbolic design (proposal), following the rare-antimatter blueprint. It does not use, and does not need, any AMS-internal cuts, counts, or systematic sizes — none are assumed or invented.

## 1. Estimand
An upper limit (95% CL, one-sided, unless you want a different convention) on the flux (or count) of the target antinucleus species, in a defined rigidity/energy range, given zero candidates pass the full selection. State up front: species, rigidity range, time period, and whether the target is a flux limit or a limit relative to the matter counterpart (e.g., anti-X/X).

## 2. Signal definition and blinding (before touching data)
- Define the signal topology in `(R, β, |Z|)` space: sign (Tracker only), `|Z|` (multi-subsystem consistency), and mass region (from `R` and `β`).
- Define and mask the signal region before any optimization; state who can unblind and when. This must happen before background/efficiency tuning to avoid look-elsewhere bias on your own analysis choices.

## 3. Background model — even with n_obs = 0, you still need an expected background
Zero observed events does not mean zero expected background; you need `b` (or its distribution) to construct the limit correctly. Enumerate and estimate, **from data-driven tail studies on abundant matter samples (e.g., helium, protons) under loosened cuts, not from the Gaussian core of any resolution function**:
- Charge-sign confusion of abundant same-`|Z|` matter (e.g., He → anti-He) via Tracker tails/bad hit assignment.
- `|Z|` confusion from fragmentation/charge-changing interactions.
- Secondary tracks and wrong-mass reconstruction from `β` errors.
- Interactions producing genuine antiparticles as secondaries.
Cross-check with redundant partial-track fits (independent layer subsets) for sign consistency, and subsystem `|Z|` cross-consistency (Tracker/TOF/RICH) before and after material traversal.

## 4. Efficiency, acceptance, exposure
Build the rigidity-dependent exposure `E(R) = A_eff(R) · ε(R) · T_live(R) · (geomagnetic transmission)` from the MC generation record for this species (generation surface/solid angle, spectrum, truth definition, material/geometry model, reconstruction version). Carry its uncertainty as a nuisance — it enters the final limit multiplicatively.

## 5. The n_obs = 0 statistical construction
Distinguish two cases, and don't blur them:

- **If background is negligible / treated as exactly known (`b`):** the classical one-sided upper limit on the *signal mean* from `P(0 | s + b) = 1 − CL` is `s_UL = −ln(1 − CL) − b`. For `b = 0`, `CL = 0.95`: `s_UL = −ln(0.05) ≈ 3.00`. I confirmed this with the skill's script:
  ```
  scripts/poisson_diagnostics.py upper-limit --n 0 --b 0 --cl 0.95
  → upper_limit_on_signal: 2.9957322735539904   [General method]
  ```
  This classical limit **shrinks as `b` grows** and becomes zero/negative once `b ≥ −ln(1−CL)` — at that point it is not a valid result, and the script will say so rather than report it. Do not call this construction "background-independent": that property belongs only to a flat-prior Bayesian/CLs-style limit, which is `−ln(1−CL)` regardless of `b` but answers a different question.

- **If background is estimated with a real uncertainty (the realistic case here, since step 3's tail-based background estimate is never exactly known):** use a Feldman-Cousins or CLs construction with the background nuisance profiled or marginalized, and toys — not the classical formula. The skill's script is explicitly classical/known-background only and does not implement Feldman-Cousins/CLs; treat that as the boundary of what a deterministic check here can validate, and build the construction with the general-method references (Feldman & Cousins; Junk for CLs).

State explicitly which construction you used, the CL, one-sided vs. two-sided, and the nuisance treatment (profiling vs. marginalization vs. hybrid) — these are not interchangeable and the choice must be reported.

## 6. Convert the count limit to a flux (or ratio) limit
`Φ_UL = N_UL / (E · ΔR)`, propagating the exposure uncertainty (and, if relevant, the background-model uncertainty) as a nuisance rather than dividing by a point estimate and ignoring its error.

## 7. Sensitivity, coverage, and reporting
- Compute the **expected** limit (median and bands) from the background-only model *before* unblinding, so the observed limit can be compared to what was expected.
- If you quote coverage for the chosen construction, use seeded toys and scan across assumed true means — a single coverage number at one mean is a diagnostic, not a proof (`scripts/poisson_diagnostics.py coverage` does this for the Garwood interval specifically; record the seed, toy count, and CL alongside any quoted number).
- Report the result honestly under the skill's four-level policy: this design, by construction, produces at most a **limit**, never a candidate/excess/evidence/discovery claim — those require actual observed events plus predefined significance thresholds, which don't apply here since `n_obs = 0`.

## Open inputs needed to make this concrete
Target species and whether it's antihelium/antideuteron-class (note: no peer-reviewed AMS-02 result for either currently appears in this skill's source index, so if you intend to frame this as continuing a published AMS-02 search, that framing needs to be checked against current primary sources rather than assumed); rigidity/energy range; assumed or measured background level and its uncertainty; exposure and its uncertainty; desired CL and construction (classical vs. Feldman-Cousins vs. CLs) — the choice materially changes the number, and none of these should be filled with invented values.
