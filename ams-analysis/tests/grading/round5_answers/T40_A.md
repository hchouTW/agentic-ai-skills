No — constancy of the ratio is consistent with a common origin, but it does not prove one. That's true both as a matter of general result-interpretation practice and because it's explicitly how AMS itself frames this particular result.

**What's actually documented [C77, S01 — AMS Phys. Rept. review, Section 6, full text]:** the positron-to-antiproton flux ratio is consistent with a constant only above about 60 GeV; a constant fit over 60–525 GeV gives 2.00 ± 0.035 (stat.) ± 0.06 (syst.), χ²/d.o.f. = 7.2/12. The review reads this as antiprotons and positrons having "nearly identical energy dependence at high energies" and *suggests* a possible common source — but the ledger notes explicitly that **this constant-ratio result is a fit to that specific range and does not prove a common origin.** The review adds that antiprotons are not thought to be produced by pulsars, and that a spectral cutoff would be expected in the antiproton spectrum if high-energy antiprotons came from dark-matter annihilation — and says continued data taking is needed to confirm the origin. In other words, AMS's own presentation treats "common source" as an open interpretive question, not an established conclusion.

**Why constancy alone can't settle it [general method, source-policy result-interpretation]:** a constant ratio just means the two spectra have the same shape (same power-law index) over the measured range — it says nothing by itself about mechanism. Similar shape is compatible with a shared production/propagation process, but it's also compatible with different processes that happen to produce similar rigidity dependence over a limited energy window (e.g., secondary production of both species in cosmic-ray collisions with the interstellar medium, each shaped by the same propagation/diffusion physics, without requiring a single new "source"). Two spectra tracking each other is consistency with a hypothesis, not proof of it — you'd need an independent line of evidence (a shared spectral feature like a cutoff at a specific energy, or a model that uniquely predicts this ratio and excludes the alternatives) to move from "consistent with" to "evidence for."

**Also worth flagging:** don't over-extend "the ratio is constant" beyond 60–525 GeV — the review's earlier antiproton paper (C06) separately reports "nearly identical rigidity dependence" for p̄, p and e⁺ around 60–500 GV, which is a related but distinct statement (different quantity/range) and shouldn't be merged with the positron/antiproton constant-ratio claim above.
