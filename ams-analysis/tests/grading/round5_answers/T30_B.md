## Defects, in priority order

1. **A correlation of −1.3 is not a numerical artifact — it's mathematically impossible, and it proves a construction error.** Correlation coefficients are bounded to [−1, 1] by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality; there is no legitimate covariance matrix that produces −1.3 between two bins. This is a different (and worse) situation than "eigenvalues slightly negative at machine precision" — it tells you the matrix wasn't assembled correctly before you even get to eigenvalues. Likely culprits: mixed absolute/relative conventions, a subtracted term (e.g. numerator−denominator covariance for a ratio, or a background-subtracted count) with an implausible assumed correlation, double-counted components, or an unfolding-induced anticorrelation from an under-regularized response inversion. Clipping eigenvalues would hide this rather than fix it — the fix is to trace bin 4/bin 5's off-diagonal element back through its construction and rebuild it from coherent, individually-PSD variations (or from toy propagation through the full chain, whose sample covariance is PSD by construction).
2. **A response-matrix column sum of 0.85 needs a diagnosis, not an assumption.** A column sum below 1 has (at least) two legitimate readings: inefficiency for events in the selected sample (if inefficiency lives in the acceptance term `A_j` instead, the sum should be 1, not 0.85), or migration of true events into reconstructed bins outside the tabulated range (under/overflow not stored in the matrix). These have different implications and different fixes. Check the matrix's declared metadata/orientation before concluding anything from the 0.85 alone.
3. **Check for double-counted efficiency between the response matrix and the separate trigger-efficiency multiplication.** If the 0.85 column sum already encodes some efficiency loss (selection and/or trigger) inside `R`, and you're *also* multiplying counts by a trigger efficiency elsewhere in the chain, that efficiency is very likely being applied twice — once implicitly in `R`'s normalization, once explicitly as a separate factor. The rule here is that inefficiency must live in exactly one place (either folded into `R`'s column normalization, or in `A_j`/an explicit efficiency factor, never both). This is worth resolving before touching the covariance at all, since if it's double-counted it will also distort whatever covariance you're propagating.
4. **Only after 1–3 are resolved does "clip and move on" become appropriate, and even then it needs to be checked, not assumed.** If, after rebuilding the covariance correctly and resolving the response/efficiency question, you still see negative eigenvalues that are of order machine precision relative to the largest eigenvalue, then symmetrizing and clipping (or nearest-PSD projection) is the right numerical fix — but you should record the change and confirm your fit/χ² result is unchanged within tolerance. Clipping a matrix that has a −1.3 correlation in it, before fixing the construction error, will produce something PSD but still wrong.
