**Verdict: reject as described — one step is a hard rule violation, and a second is underspecified enough to hide a large systematic.**

1. **Charge confusion estimated from the Gaussian core of the rigidity resolution is rejected outright.** Charge confusion is a *tail* phenomenon: the rare events where curvature is fit with the wrong sign live in the non-Gaussian tail of the rigidity-resolution function, not its core. A Gaussian-core estimate will badly underestimate (often by orders of magnitude) the true confusion rate at the rigidities where it matters for a p̄ analysis, because the proton flux is many orders of magnitude above the antiproton flux and even a tiny tail fraction contaminates the antiproton sample. This needs a **data-driven or calibrated-simulation tail estimate**, validated on an abundant same-charge sample (protons) under loosened cuts, ideally cross-checked with redundant independent partial-fits (e.g., upper vs. lower track segments) for sign consistency, and expressed as a rigidity-dependent migration matrix `M(p → p̄)`, not a single number from the resolution width. This is the highest-priority defect — the rest of the analysis is not trustworthy until it's fixed.

2. **"Background from electrons is subtracted by MC" is underspecified and likely missing a validation step.** A background estimator needs: which control/sideband region validates the electron template, whether it's MC-only or MC-shape-corrected-with-data, a stated closure test, and a quantified data/MC comparison in the *tail* region of whatever discriminant separates electrons from antiprotons (not just "reasonable agreement" in the core). A pure-MC electron background with no data-driven cross-check risks absorbing a shape mismatch (e.g., TRD discriminant miscalibration) silently into the antiproton yield. State the estimator (sideband, template fit, MC-corrected) and its closure test explicitly.

3. **Selection described (negative-sign track + TRD cut) omits other standard discriminating information and doesn't establish a full background model.** Two things are missing from what you've described:
   - No mention of an ECAL-based or other complementary hadron/electron discriminant. TRD alone leaves residual electron background at rigidities where the TRD's e/p separation weakens (this is a proposal for a more complete design, not a claim about what AMS internally requires — but the physics reason is real: separate subsystems exploiting different physics give more robust rejection than one cut).
   - Charge-confused *protons* (positive protons reconstructed with flipped sign) are a distinct background from electrons and need their own template/estimator — your description only names electrons as "background," while charge confusion is treated as a standalone number rather than as a background component in a background ledger (with its own control region, transfer model, and closure test).

4. **No mention of the antiproton-side and proton-side (denominator) efficiency/acceptance/exposure, no response/unfolding step, and no systematic ledger.** Even once signal and background are correctly defined, you still need: conditional efficiencies for the negative-sign, TRD-passing sample (not assumed independent — check factorization bias); acceptance/exposure with geomagnetic transmission; a response matrix if you plan to unfold to a flux or p̄/p ratio; and a systematic ledger covering rigidity scale/resolution, TRD calibration drift, and MC provenance, with correlations tracked (this matters especially for p̄/p, where numerator and denominator share some systematics but not all — cancellation must be demonstrated, not assumed).

**Inputs that would change this verdict:**
- The tail-validation method and control sample used for the charge-confusion migration matrix (what replaces the Gaussian-core estimate).
- The electron-background estimator's provenance (MC-only vs. data-corrected) and its closure test / control-region definition.
- Whether ECAL or another subsystem contributes to the hadron/electron separation alongside TRD.
- Whether charge-confused protons are treated as a separate background component with their own control region.
- The efficiency, acceptance, exposure, and systematic ledger for both the antiproton numerator and (if a ratio is the target) the proton denominator.

None of the fixes require internal AMS numbers — they're structural: the Gaussian-core rejection and the background-ledger/efficiency requirements apply regardless of which specific selection thresholds you end up using.
