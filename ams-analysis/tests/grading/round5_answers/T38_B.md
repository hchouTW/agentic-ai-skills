Qualifiedly yes, but only with the caveat attached — you shouldn't quote it as a bare number.

**[Documented, claim C91, source S01 — AMS, "AMS on the ISS, Part II," Phys. Rept. 894, 1 (2021), Section 12]** In its nitrogen-flux analysis, the AMS review fits the nitrogen flux over its full measured range (2.2 GV–3.3 TV) as a weighted sum of a primary-like reference (oxygen) and a secondary-like reference (boron): `Φ_N = Φ_NP + Φ_NS`, with `Φ_NP = (0.092 ± 0.002) × Φ_O` and `Φ_NS = (0.61 ± 0.02) × Φ_B` (χ²/d.o.f. = 59/64). The review itself **does** describe the fitted coefficient **0.092 ± 0.002 as "the N/O abundance ratio at the source,"** and compares it to the Solar-System value 0.135 (+0.051, −0.047), calling the two consistent.

**The caveat that must travel with the number:** this labeling rests on an assumption, not on a propagation-free measurement. The decomposition treats oxygen as a purely primary-like shape and boron as a purely secondary-like shape (the review notes O has the lowest secondary content among He/C/O, and B is itself mostly produced from primary C and O). So 0.092 is *conditional on that modeling choice* — it is the coefficient of a two-template fit, not a directly measured source abundance. The fit's goodness-of-fit (59/64) tests whether the fit describes the data well, not whether the primary/secondary-template assumption itself is correct. In the skill's terms: this is the collaboration's **result interpretation** (a model step), not the **direct measurement** (the nitrogen flux itself); "reasonable agreement" with the Solar-System value is not by itself validation of the source-abundance interpretation.

**So:** you can attribute the number and the "source N/O" framing to AMS/this review (cite S01, claim C91, Section 12), but you should state the primary/secondary-template assumption alongside it rather than presenting 0.092 as a model-independent abundance measurement. If your use case needs a model-independent quantity, use the measured nitrogen flux and the He/C/O/B fluxes directly rather than this derived ratio.
