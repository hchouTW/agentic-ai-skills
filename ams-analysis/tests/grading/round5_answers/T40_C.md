No. A constant ratio is *consistent with* a common origin, but it does not prove one.

[Documented, C77] The claim itself is from the AMS Phys. Rept. review (S01, section 6): above 60 GeV the positron-to-antiproton flux ratio is consistent with a constant, with a fit over 60-525 GeV giving 2.00 ± 0.035 (stat.) ± 0.06 (syst.), χ²/d.o.f. = 7.2/12. The review reads this as the two species having "nearly identical energy dependence at high energies" and states it "suggests a possible common source" — but that reading is explicitly the collaboration's own qualitative interpretation, not a separate measurement, and the skill's own source ledger flags it directly: **"the constant-ratio result is a fit to that range and does not prove a common origin."** [Documented, C75] Likewise, the review's broader interpretive statements about antiprotons (spectral shape similarities, dark-matter-vs-collision model comparisons) are labeled by the collaboration as qualitative readings requiring better modeling before any definitive interpretation — not conclusions the ratio measurement itself establishes.

Why constancy alone can't get you to "common origin" [General method]:

- **A flat ratio only says the two spectra have the same energy dependence (same power-law index) over the measured window.** Two physically distinct production/propagation mechanisms can produce similar high-energy slopes — e.g., if both are secondary/tertiary products of the same interstellar propagation processes acting on primary cosmic rays, diffusive propagation can impose similar energy dependence on otherwise distinct production spectra without a shared source.
- **The ratio is degenerate with respect to mechanism.** The same 2.00 constant is compatible with (a) both species coming from the same astrophysical or dark-matter source, (b) both being secondaries from the same cosmic-ray population interacting with the interstellar medium (a shared "cause" upstream of either species' own production), or (c) coincidental similarity of otherwise-independent spectra over this specific finite range — the fit range (60-525 GeV) doesn't test behavior outside it.
- **A genuine common-origin claim needs more than a ratio.** The review itself only goes as far as noting a cutoff would be *expected* in the antiproton spectrum under a dark-matter-annihilation origin, and that this hasn't been established, plus explicitly notes antiprotons are not produced by pulsars (ruling out one but not all shared/independent-source scenarios) — i.e., discriminating between shared-source and coincidental-shape hypotheses requires matched spectral features (breaks/cutoffs at consistent energies), not just an overall constant ratio, plus continued data and model comparison, which the review says is still needed.

So: treat "ratio is constant" as a data point that a common-source hypothesis must be consistent with — not as evidence that establishes it. The collaboration's own published language stops short of a origin claim for exactly this reason.
