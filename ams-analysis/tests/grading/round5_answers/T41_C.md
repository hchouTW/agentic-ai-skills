**Transition time.** [Documented, C90, S01 Phys. Rept. review] AMS's collaboration-level fit finds the positron-to-electron ratio `R_e(E) = Φ(e⁺)/Φ(e⁻)` takes **Δt = 830 ± 30 days** to complete its transition across the 2013 solar polarity reversal, and this transition width is found to be **energy-independent** (fit over 1–21 GeV; above 6 GeV, Δt is held fixed at 830 days rather than independently re-fit).

**How AMS obtained that number.** [Documented, C90, S01, Section 16, Eqs. 17–18] AMS built 3871 independent measurements of `R_e` resolved jointly in energy and time, then fit each energy slice with a four-parameter logistic function:

`R_e(t, E) = R0(E) · [1 + C(E) / (exp(−(t − t_½(E)) / (Δt/Δ80)) + 1)]`

with `Δ80 = 4.39` fixed by construction so that `Δt` is defined as the **10%–90% transition time**. The four fitted parameters per energy bin are `R0` (baseline ratio), `C` (transition amplitude), `t_½` (midpoint time), and `Δt` (transition width); each energy-bin fit had `χ²/d.o.f. ≈ 1`.

Two further fitted relationships describe the transition's structure (context, not the Δt answer itself):
- The midpoint delay follows `t_½(E) − t_rev = τ·(E/GeV)^ρ`, with `t_rev = 1 July 2013` (a stated choice — the center of the period when solar polarity was not well-defined, not a measured date), `ρ = −0.33 ± 0.04 (stat) (+0.08, −0.15) (syst)`, `τ = 580 ± 19 (stat) ± 136 (syst) days`; `t_½` itself shifts by `260 ± 30 days` from 1 to 6 GeV.
- The amplitude `C` is close to 1 at 1 GeV, decreases smoothly with energy, and is consistent with zero above ~20 GeV — which is why `Δt` and `t_½` are only well-determined at low energy (where `C` is large) and `Δt` is fixed above 6 GeV rather than independently fit.

**Caveats on this number.** [Documented, C90] This is the collaboration's own descriptive four-parameter fit, not a derived physical model, and the review states it is only in qualitative agreement with drift-including solar-modulation models. `t_rev` is a stated modeling choice, so the delay parameters (`t_½ − t_rev`) depend on that choice. The text read does not describe a trial-factor treatment or a correlated-error model across the energy bins for this fit, so treat cross-bin correlations of `Δt` as **[Unknown/needs input]**.
