**[Documented]** AMS reports that the secondary-to-primary spectral index hardens by **Δ = 0.140 ± 0.025** across a boundary at **192 GV** (the review rounds this to "above about 200 GV"), and describes the significance of this hardening as **exceeding 5σ**. This is from the Phys. Rept. 894 review (2021), Section 10, and covers the average behavior of six ratios: Li/C, Be/C, B/C, Li/O, Be/O, B/O. Concretely: the model-independent spectral index of the secondary (Li, Be, B) fluxes was computed over non-overlapping rigidity intervals; a single power law was fit to each secondary/primary ratio in two adjacent windows, 60.3–192 GV and 192–3300 GV; and the difference between the fitted indices in those two windows, averaged across the six ratios, is the quoted 0.140 ± 0.025.

Physics reading given in the review (their interpretation, not part of the measurement): this pattern is consistent with the hardening arising from propagation in the Galaxy rather than from the injection spectrum at the source, since source-injection hardening would be expected to affect secondaries and primaries similarly, whereas a propagation origin affects secondaries more. The review also states no model correctly predicted the observed behavior of either the primary (He, C, O) or secondary (Li, Be, B) fluxes.

Caveats on the "how significant" part: the review's text does not detail how the 5σ figure was computed — e.g., whether it accounts for correlations between the six ratios sharing input fluxes, or whether any trial/look-elsewhere correction was applied for choosing the 192 GV boundary. Treat the significance value itself as [Documented, stated by AMS] but the computation behind it as **[Unknown/needs input]** beyond that quoted number.

For reference, this 192 GV two-region hardening test is distinct from the earlier single-ratio B/C paper (PRL 117, 231102, 2016), which reported a single power-law index for B/C above 65 GV of **−0.333 ± 0.014 (fit) ± 0.005 (syst)** — a different measurement (one ratio, one power law above one threshold, not a hardening test across 192 GV) and should not be conflated with the 0.140 ± 0.025 hardening figure above.
