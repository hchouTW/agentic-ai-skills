**Verdict: not sound as described.** The design has a genuine signal handle (Tracker sign) but three of its four pieces are each a known failure mode for antiproton analyses, and one target quantity is entirely undefined.

**Defects, in priority order**

1. **Charge confusion from the Gaussian rigidity resolution is invalid.** Charge confusion (a proton mis-signed as negative) is a *tail* phenomenon of the Tracker's rigidity reconstruction, not a core-resolution effect — this is a non-negotiable rule for this skill, and it is listed explicitly as a failure mode for antiproton/lepton analyses. Reading off a mis-sign rate from the Gaussian core will underestimate it by orders of magnitude, especially at high rigidity where the core is narrow but the tail (mis-measured/mis-associated hits) is what actually produces sign flips. It must instead be measured data-driven (e.g., from an abundant same-sign control sample under loosened cuts, or from redundant partial-track fits) or from MC explicitly validated against such data/test-beam tails — this is what AMS's own antiproton paper does (charge-confusion MC checked against 400 GV proton test-beam data).

2. **"Negative-sign tracks passing a TRD cut" is not a sufficient signal definition.** The TRD separates leptons from hadrons (electron/positron vs. proton/antiproton); it does not separate antiprotons from other hadron-like negative tracks, in particular charge-confused protons and negative pions/other secondaries. A TRD cut alone removes the electron background component but leaves the charge-confusion-proton and pion backgrounds essentially untouched, since both are hadron-like on the TRD. The documented AMS approach instead runs a template fit on the negative-rigidity sample with separate templates for antiprotons, charge-confused protons, and electrons/pions — cut-and-count on a TRD cut has no way to separate these components or quantify their leakage.

3. **"Background from electrons subtracted by MC" has no stated validation.** A bare MC subtraction is not enough: there's no control region, no data/MC comparison metric or threshold, and specifically no tail-region check, even though the electron background is largest exactly in the low-rigidity tail region where TRD/ECAL rejection is weakest. "Reasonable agreement" is not a validation criterion — state a metric (e.g., ratio/difference of yields in a proton-depleted, electron-enriched control) and the action taken if it fails.

4. **The measurement target is unstated.** Is this a `p̄` flux, or `p̄/p`? If it's a ratio, the proton denominator's own selection, efficiency, and acceptance need to be defined, and each nuisance (trigger efficiency, acceptance, charge confusion, etc.) classified as correlated/partial/independent between numerator and denominator rather than assumed to cancel.

**Inputs that would change the verdict**
- Evidence that the charge-confusion estimate is tail-validated (data-driven measurement or MC checked against a data/test-beam tail sample), not a resolution-core estimate.
- The actual discriminant/estimator used to separate `p̄` from charge-confused `p` and from `π⁻` (a template fit in a charge-sign-sensitive variable, per the antiproton blueprint), rather than a single TRD cut.
- A background ledger entry for the electron subtraction: control region, closure test, and a tail-region data/MC comparison with a stated tolerance.
- Whether the target is a flux or `p̄/p`, and if the latter, the correlation classification of shared systematics between `p̄` and `p`.

This is a proposal-level critique against this skill's general design rules; the published AMS antiproton method (template fit with electron/charge-confusion-proton/pion templates, test-beam-validated charge-confusion MC) is documented in *Antiproton Flux, Antiproton-to-Proton Flux Ratio...*, PRL 117, 091103 (2016) — use it as the point of comparison, not as a source for numbers on this specific setup.
