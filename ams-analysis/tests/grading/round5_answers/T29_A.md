# Measurement brief: ⁴He flux, 2 GV–3 TV

## Flag before anything else: the estimand as stated mixes two different measurements

"Helium-4" names an **isotope** (mass-separated), not the elemental He flux. Isotope separation needs a mass measurement, `m = |Z||R|/(γβ)`, using `R` (Tracker) and `β` (TOF/RICH). As `β → 1`, `γ²` amplifies the `β`-resolution term, so the ³He/⁴He mass split loses power at a rigidity set by detector resolution, not by statistics. AMS's own published isotope-resolved He measurement is bounded to **2.1–21 GV (⁴He), 1.9–15 GV (³He)**, May 2011–Nov 2017 **[Documented, C09/C28, S12]**. There is no evidence a mass-separated ⁴He flux exists, or is achievable, out to 3 TV. What AMS *has* published to 3 TV is the **elemental** He flux (both isotopes combined, Z=2 selection only): 1.9 GV–3 TV, first 30 months **[Documented, C59–C61, S07]** — not "helium-4," and not the full mission either.

So "2 GV to 3 TV" cannot be a single mass-separated ⁴He estimand. I'm proceeding with the only physically coherent design: a **two-regime estimand**, stated explicitly as an assumption (resolvable by you supplying a different scope).

- **Low-R regime** (rigidity range bounded by usable `β` resolution — of order the AMS isotope paper's range, exact edge is an analysis input, not assumed here): isotope-separated ⁴He flux via a template/mass fit.
- **High-R regime** (up to 3 TV): elemental He flux, with ⁴He attributed via an assumed ³He/⁴He composition and its propagated uncertainty (³He/⁴He is itself rigidity/time-dependent above 4 GV per **[Documented, C86, S01]**, `C(R/4 GV)^Δ`, Δ = −0.294 ± 0.004 — a review-level number, quoted only as context, not as an input to reuse without its own citation check).

## Estimand

Differential flux `Φ(R)` of ⁴He (isotope-resolved where velocity resolution allows; elemental-He-with-assumed-composition above that) at the top of AMS-02, `R` from 2 GV to 3 TV, over a stated public data period. **[Proposal]** for the range split; not an AMS-stated boundary.

## Target and level

`target = flux`; `level = flux` (efficiency/acceptance/response-corrected, not raw counts or object-level).

## Species

`He-4`: `abs_Z = 2`, `A = 4`, `charge_sign = +1` (only sign; Tracker/magnet is the sole source), `mass ≈ 3.7274 GeV` **[General method, nuclear rest energy; verify against PDG (S39) before precision use]**. Companion isotope `He-3` (`A=3`) enters only as the background/composition partner in the low-R regime.

## Variable, unit, range, bins

- Variable: rigidity, unit GV (per invariant 1: rigidity ≠ momentum ≠ kinetic energy; state `Z`,`A` on every conversion).
- Range: 2–3000 GV, split at an unresolved isotope-reach boundary (see above).
- Bins: not fixed here — bin width should scale with the local rigidity resolution (finer at low R, coarser at high R near the tracker's maximum detectable rigidity, where resolution and migration dominate). **[Proposal]**; no AMS bin edges are invented.

## Data/MC scope and period

`sample = public`. Neither documented He measurement's period matches an unstated "current" span: elemental He is first-30-months only (S07); isotope He is May 2011–Nov 2017 (S12). State your intended period explicitly; if it extends past either paper's window, you are extrapolating the *design*, not reusing AMS's actual result. MC: species- and regime-specific generation record (see `efficiency-acceptance-backgrounds`), matched in reconstruction/calibration version to the chosen data period.

## Subsystem roles

Tracker (rigidity, charge sign, |Z|=2 selection), TOF (β at low R, trigger, direction), RICH — NaF then aerogel radiator (β at higher R, isotope mass discrimination), ACC (side-entry veto). TRD provides |Z| support only, never charge sign (invariant 2).

## Public vs. user-supplied

Public: the fact that AMS has published both an elemental He flux and an isotope-resolved He measurement, with the ranges/periods/claim IDs above. Not public here: cut values, template definitions, resolution numbers beyond the review-level figures already cited, efficiencies, acceptance, or any bin-by-bin systematic size — these must come from you or be left symbolic.

## Open inputs (would change the design)

1. Do you actually want a single "⁴He" quantity across the full range, or are you willing to report it as elemental He above the isotope-resolved edge (with the composition assumption made explicit)?
2. Data-taking period and hardware era.
3. Track/subsystem configuration (e.g. inner-tracker-only vs. L1–L9) — affects acceptance and the achievable maximum rigidity.
4. Geomagnetic cutoff model and safety factor.
5. Response matrix (orientation, normalization, whether efficiency sits inside it).
6. β-resolution and the actual isotope-reach boundary for your track configuration.
