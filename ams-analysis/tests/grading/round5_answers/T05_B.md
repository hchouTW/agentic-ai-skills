## Verdict

As described, this is an ad hoc "count / (acceptance × livetime × bin width)" estimator with a single bin-by-bin MC correction bolted on. It's missing several things that are normally required for a proton flux, and the one correction you do apply (the "bin-by-bin factor from MC") is exactly the step most likely to silently bias the result for a spectrum this steep. I would not sign off on it as written — not because any single piece is wrong, but because none of the choices are justified or shown to be safe. [General method / skill design guidance below]

## Defects, in priority order

1. **The bin-by-bin MC correction is a migration correction, and its validity isn't demonstrated.** "Correct with a bin-by-bin factor from MC" is the diagonal-response estimator `Φ_i = (n_i)/(ΔR_i A_i ε_i T_i) × k_i`, which is only valid when off-diagonal migration between rigidity bins is negligible. For a proton spectrum this steep, that's not a safe default: abundant low-rigidity events migrating upward into higher bins can bias the flux even when the resolution core looks small. Before trusting this, show the response matrix and demonstrate migration is negligible at your binning/precision, or replace the correction with a proper response-matrix unfolding or forward fold.
2. **No background subtraction is mentioned.** `n_i` after cuts is not yet a proton count — the ratio expression has to be `(n_i − b_i)`, not `n_i`. For protons, likely backgrounds include charge-confused negatively-charged species faking a positive-rigidity proton, deuteron/helium leakage from the charge/mass estimators, and (at lower rigidity) secondary/albedo particles below the geomagnetic cutoff. If you believe these are negligible for your selection, that has to be shown (a background ledger with an estimator per component), not assumed by omission.
3. **"Acceptance" is underspecified, and this is where double-counting hides.** Is your "acceptance" the geometric acceptance `A_geo`, or the effective acceptance `A_eff` (geometric × selection/reconstruction efficiency)? If it's `A_eff` and your MC bin-by-bin factor *also* reweights for a selection or reconstruction effect, you may be applying the same effect twice. Conversely, if your "acceptance" is purely geometric, you need an explicit, separately-validated efficiency chain (conditional factors, not multiplied as independent unless shown to be) — it isn't visible in the description at all.
4. **Livetime vs. exposure isn't separated out.** "Livetime × bin width" isn't yet exposure unless it also carries the geomagnetic transmission (fraction of livetime each rigidity bin is above the cutoff, with whatever safety factor you're using) and any time-dependent acceptance. If livetime here just means calendar-time-live, the geomagnetic cut is missing from the whole chain.
5. **Possible circularity in the MC correction factor.** If the bin-by-bin factor is derived by comparing generated vs. reconstructed truth bins in MC, it depends on the *generated* spectrum shape in each bin. If that generated spectrum doesn't match the real (measured) spectrum, the correction is biased; this needs either reweighting-to-convergence or a demonstration that the correction is insensitive to reasonable spectral variations.
6. **No closure test, bin-center convention, or systematics are mentioned.** At minimum I'd expect: MC closure (recover truth by "measuring" MC with this exact procedure), a stated bin-center/bin-average convention for a steeply falling spectrum, and a systematic budget (rigidity-scale, acceptance, background, and — given point 1 — a migration/unfolding-method systematic).

## Inputs that would change the verdict

- **The response matrix** (or evidence migration is negligible at your rigidity binning) — this alone determines whether point 1 is a real problem or a non-issue.
- **Whether "acceptance" already means effective acceptance**, and what exactly the MC bin-by-bin factor corrects for (selection efficiency vs. migration vs. something else) — resolves points 1 and 3 together.
- **Your background estimate** (or an argument for why it's negligible in your rigidity range) for point 2.
- **Whether livetime already includes geomagnetic transmission** for point 4.
- **The rigidity range and binning**, since the severity of migration bias and the need for background subtraction both depend strongly on how steep the spectrum is in your range and how fine your bins are relative to resolution.
