**[Documented]** AMS measures nuclear inelastic cross sections in its own material using an **in-flight survival-probability method**, described in the Phys. Rept. review (Aguilar et al., "AMS on the ISS, Part II," Phys. Rept. 894, 1, 2021, §7, "Nuclear cross section measurements"):

**How it's done.** The material a cosmic-ray nucleus traverses in AMS's acceptance averages 73% carbon and 17% aluminum by mass (with smaller amounts of Si, O, H, Na, Au and others), path-length-averaged over the acceptance — this is not a general material budget, just what's seen along accepted trajectories. Before AMS, inelastic cross sections for He and C projectiles had only been measured below 10 GV. AMS exploits ISS attitudes where it points horizontally, so nuclei pass through the detector both from L8→L1 and from L2→L9. The seven inner tracker layers (L2–L8) identify and rigidity/charge-tag the particle, and the charge measured in L2–L8 is compared with the charge in L1 (or L8 vs. L9) to see whether the particle survived without interacting. The L2–L8 interaction probability (below 3%) is cross-checked using the upper/lower TOF charges, and combined with the L1–L2 and L8–L9 survival probabilities to give the full L1–L9 survival probability. A dedicated Geant4 simulation using Glauber-Gribov inelastic cross sections, varied by ±10%, is compared to the data, and the best-agreeing normalization is chosen. A few-percent discrepancy was found at low rigidity (below ~30 GV) for helium, which led to a modified Glauber-Gribov model tuned to the He data; below 30 GV the measured He rigidity dependence is then used to correct the model for heavier nuclei too. Survival probabilities were measured this way for Li, Be, B, C, N, O, Ne, Mg and Si; carbon-target inelastic cross sections averaged over 5–100 GV are found constant within the achieved accuracy, and (per the review) no other measurements of these cross sections exist except He-on-C and C-on-C.

**What values it got.** The review states the method and qualitative findings above, but the actual cross-section numbers are given in figures in that section, not transcribed as text in the parts of the review read for this index — so I can't hand you specific millibarn values here without misquoting a figure. What I can give you, from the individual flux papers where this correction shows up as a systematic:
- Helium (first 30 months, PRL 115, 211101, 2015): the He-material-interaction systematic (survival-probability method, Glauber-Gribov cross sections scaled 1.05–1.20) contributes about **1% below 100 GV, rising to ~2% at 3 TV** to the helium flux uncertainty.
- He/C/O (PRL 119, 251101, 2017): inelastic cross-section systematics of **below 2.2% (C) and 2.7% (O) up to 100 GV, rising to 3% and 3.5% at 3 TV**.
- Lithium isotopes (PRL 134, 201001, 2025) note the same survival-probability approach was used for Li specifically.

So: the *method* is well documented (in-flight survival probability from redundant tracker-layer charge measurements, calibrated against a Glauber-Gribov Geant4 model), and the *impact* on specific published fluxes is quoted as a systematic-uncertainty size, but the underlying cross-section values themselves are figure-only in the source I have access to — treat any specific millibarn number as [Unknown/needs input] unless you can pull it directly from the review's figures or a dedicated AMS cross-section paper (not separately indexed here).
