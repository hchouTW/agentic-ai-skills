# 27-day positron-to-electron ratio, 5-50 GV, and a one-year periodicity search

Labels: [Documented, C##] = public AMS source in source-index; [General method]; [Proposal]; [Unknown/needs input]. No AMS internal numbers (livetimes, efficiencies, event counts per bin, systematic sizes) are supplied; they stay symbolic.

## 0. Estimand and what is already public

- Target: R_k(R) = Phi_{e+}(R,k) / Phi_{e-}(R,k) for 27-day bin k (one Bartels rotation), in rigidity bins covering 5-50 GV, at the top of the instrument (not LIS). The positron fraction e+/(e+ + e-) is a different estimand with a different denominator; choose the ratio e+/e- as asked, and state it.
- [Documented, C36-C41, abstract level] AMS published daily and Bartels-rotation e+ and e- fluxes with recurrent 27-day, 13.5-day and 9-day structure. The daily electron and positron papers bin in rigidity, 1.00-41.9 GV (C40, C41). The Bartels-rotation lepton paper bins in energy, 1-50 GeV (C39, C48). So a 5-50 GV ratio is not a published range: 41.9 GV is the top of the daily-lepton rigidity range, and 50 GeV is an energy edge, not a 50 GV edge. Decide the variable first (rigidity vs energy) and state it. For leptons p ~ E, so R ~ E/1 for |Z|=1 to a good approximation, but the Tracker measures R (sign-carrying) and the ECAL measures E; the variable in which bins are defined sets the migration matrix and the energy-scale/rigidity-scale systematics. [General method]
- [Documented, C90, S01] The review fits a four-parameter logistic function to the positron-to-electron ratio across the 2013 polarity reversal (Delta t = 830 +- 30 days, energy independent; t_rev = 1 July 2013 is a stated choice). Descriptive fit, not a rule.
- [Documented, C07, S09, abstract level] Over an 11-year solar cycle (1.00-41.9 GV) antiprotons vary less than protons, electrons, positrons. Not a statement about the e+/e- ratio.
- Whether AMS applied any look-elsewhere correction to its periodicity significances is not described in the text read: [Unknown/needs input]. The AMS wavelet analysis (daily protons) used normalized power with 95% levels from lag-1 red-noise Monte Carlo surrogates (C45, C52). Choices of those analyses, not rules.

## 1. Design of the time series

### 1.1 Time binning [Proposal, with General-method reasons]
- Bins: Bartels rotations, fixed 27-day count. Bin edges are aligned to rotation boundaries and must also respect hardware-era and reconstruction-version boundaries (no bin straddles one). Fix bins, rigidity range, test statistic, and period band before looking at the time dependence; record later changes.
- A one-year period spans about 13.5 bins; the Nyquist period is 54 days, so one year is resolved. Caveat: 27-day bins average over the 27-day recurrent solar structure and the 13.5-day and 9-day structures, which then alias or suppress rather than resolve. Bin edges are exactly 27 days, so the sampling comb is regular apart from gaps and low-livetime bins; aliasing of a 13.5-day or 9-day signal into the annual band is not excluded a priori (e.g. 27-day sampling of a 27/k-day signal folds to low frequency). Say this in the analysis plan and test it with injected 13.5-day and 9-day signals.
- Span: the ratio covers the solar cycle, so the series length is about 10-12 years (about 150 bins at most); it contains the 2013 polarity reversal. The slow solar-cycle trend is much larger than any annual term, so it must be modeled (section 3), not ignored.

### 1.2 Rigidity bins
- Define bins no narrower than twice the relevant resolution, with unfolding for migration (the lepton flux analyses use bin widths at least twice the energy resolution, C25; a choice of that analysis). Coarse bins also fix the statistics problem at the top of the range: positrons are rare, and the number in the least populated (rigidity, time) bin must be adequate for the chosen inference. If counts are low in the 27-day high-rigidity bins, merge rigidity bins (for example into 5-10, 10-20, 20-50 GV groups) or use the Poisson-exact or toy-validated likelihood, not Gaussian errors. State the rigidity range where time resolution is supported. The user's range up to 50 GV needs checking against the 41.9 GV published ceiling; counts per 27-day bin at 30-50 GV are [Unknown/needs input].

## 2. Measurement of R_k per bin

For each time bin k and rigidity bin i:

Phi_{e±}(k,i) = (N_{e±,ki} - b_{e±,ki}) / (E_{e±,ki} dR_i), unfolded per time bin, with exposure E_k = sum over time in bin of A_eff x T_live x geomagnetic transmission, kept separate from acceptance, efficiency and livetime. [General method] AMS built its time-resolved fluxes this way, with migration unfolded independently in each time bin and the exposure per rotation from livetime-weighted seconds above the geomagnetic cutoff in normal conditions, within 40 degrees of zenith and outside the SAA (C43, C46, C48, C49). Those are the choices of those analyses.

- Signal extraction [Proposal, consistent with documented practice C24-C26]: Tracker gives charge sign and |Z|=1; TRD (Lambda_TRD) and ECAL (Lambda_ECAL, E/p) reject protons; template fit in (Lambda_TRD, Lambda_CC) with templates for correct-sign lepton, correct-sign proton, charge-confused lepton. Fit the positive and negative samples simultaneously with shared charge-confusion parameters. Templates must be validated per rigidity bin and for stability in time (TRD gas/gain drift changes the template shape). Do not use ECAL variables both to define the lepton sample and to derive the template that measures it.
- Charge confusion: a tail phenomenon, not the Gaussian core. Treat e-> e+ as a charge-sign migration matrix M(e+<-e-)(R, k). The e- flux is about an order of magnitude larger than the e+ flux, so e- charge confusion contaminates the numerator much more than the converse; at the upper end (30-50 GV) the contamination grows. Validate the tail on abundant samples with loosened selection, per time period.
- Proton background: dominates the positive sample by orders of magnitude; validate the transfer from control regions per time bin, per rigidity bin. Protons are also strongly time-modulated, so the background fraction changes with the solar cycle: the template/background model is time-dependent by construction.
- Geomagnetic cutoff: compute transmission per bin, not once. Low-rigidity bins (5 GV and a bit above) are the most sensitive, and sensitivity can differ between charge signs. Use the same safety-factor policy for e+ and e-, vary it as a systematic per bin and per sign. (Public analyses used factor 1.2 with scans 1.0-1.4 in several papers, C27, C31, C49; not a universal value.) At 5 GV the cutoff effect is smaller than at 1-2 GV but still not negligible for ISS orbit-dependent bins.

## 3. Ratio and covariance model

- Form R_k(i) = Phi_{e+}/Phi_{e-} bin by bin. Cancellation is not automatic; classify each effect (table):

| Effect | Treatment for the e+/e- ratio |
|---|---|
| Geometric acceptance | Largely common (same fiducial, same track configuration); verify with MC |
| Trigger efficiency | Partial; check sign dependence |
| Livetime/exposure | Common if identical intervals; cutoff transmission differs with charge sign at low R |
| Tracker charge-sign selection / charge confusion | Sign-specific; not cancelled |
| TRD/ECAL selection efficiency | Largely common for leptons, but data/MC corrections measured on the electron sample and applied to positrons must be shown to transfer |
| Proton contamination | Strongly sign-specific; not cancelled |
| Rigidity (energy) scale | Same scale, but the two fluxes have different local spectral slopes; scale shift is propagated through migration, not as a ratio rescale |
| Unfolding | Correlated only if the same response and scale are used |

- Numerator and denominator come from the same events/runs, so statistical errors are independent only if the event samples are disjoint (they are: opposite charge-sign samples) but systematics are shared. Build one covariance over (time, rigidity, species) with a documented ordering, classifying each systematic as persistent (calibration, template shape, cross-section model: correlated across all periods), slowly varying (correlated across neighbouring rotations), or independent per period; across rigidity as normalization, smooth shape, or independent. A Kronecker factorization of time and rigidity parts is valid only if tested. Check with `scripts/validate_covariance.py`. Do not combine unknown correlations quadratically. [General method]
- [Unknown/needs input] How AMS systematics are correlated across days or rotations is not in the text read. Do not supply it; build it from your own error ledger.
- Delta-method error for the ratio: Var(R) ~ R^2[(sN/N)^2 + (sD/D)^2 - 2 rho sN sD/(N D)] only when relative errors are small and Gaussian; use toys at low counts.
- Normalizing to a reference period cancels only the persistent systematics and correlates all periods' statistical errors with the reference; carry that covariance.

## 4. Periodicity search

### 4.1 The detrending problem comes first
The solar cycle (including the 2013 polarity reversal and the charge-sign-dependent drift modulation) produces a large, non-stationary multi-year variation in R_k. The e+/e- ratio depends on the heliospheric magnetic-field polarity (charge-sign dependent modulation), so it is not a stationary series. A Fourier or Lomb-Scargle periodogram of the raw series puts the power at low frequency and leaks into the annual band. [General method]

Therefore, before the search:
- Pre-specify the trend model and fit it simultaneously with the annual term where possible: a smooth baseline (spline with a pre-set knot spacing of several years, or the four-parameter logistic across the 2013 reversal, cf. C90, as a descriptive alternative) plus sinusoid(s) at the test frequency. Report the dependence of the annual amplitude on the baseline flexibility (knot spacing); an annual term that exists only for stiff baselines is not credible.
- Rigidity-dependence of the trend: the solar-cycle amplitude and the lag between e+ and e- vary with rigidity (hysteresis below about 8.5 GV between electron and proton and electron and positron, C40, C41; positrons modulated more than protons below about 7 GV, C41; do not merge the two thresholds). The lowest bins (5-8.5 GV) are therefore where the baseline is least simple; search per rigidity bin and in a combined bin, and present both.

### 4.2 Test statistic and frequency band [Proposal]
- Observable: the detrended ratio residual r_k = (R_k - baseline_k)/sigma_k, or fit directly the amplitude A and phase of cos/sin terms at period P in a weighted least-squares or likelihood fit with the full (time) covariance.
- Band: define the period band before the analysis. Because the null hypothesis is "one-year", the primary test is a single pre-specified frequency (P = 1 yr: about 365/27 = 13.5 rotations). Then there is no look-elsewhere factor for the primary test, but the answer must say that the primary-test p-value covers only that single frequency; any broader scan (for example, 2-3 harmonics, or a scan of 0.5-2 yr to see whether power peaks near one year) carries a trial factor. Report local and global significance (trial factors, Gross and Vitells, S37) and calibrate by toys that reproduce the sampling, the exposure pattern and the correlated systematics. Do not use Wilks or single-frequency p-values for a scan. [General method]
- Method options: weighted Lomb-Scargle or a sinusoid-plus-baseline likelihood (handles gaps and unequal errors); optionally a wavelet time-frequency analysis, since the annual signal may not be stationary (AMS used wavelets with normalized power and red-noise 95% levels for the daily proton flux, C45, C52; those choices are not rules). With only about 150 bins, the frequency resolution is limited: the annual peak has width about 1/T = 1/(10-12 yr), so neighbours at 0.9 and 1.1 yr are not separated. State this.
- Noise model: the residuals have red noise and the leftover solar structure; the null distribution must come from toys with a red-noise (lag-1 autoregressive or empirically matched) model, including the 27-day-recurrent solar power that is not removed by detrending. White-noise p-values will be anticonservative.

### 4.3 Confounders that produce annual or near-annual terms (the main physics risk)
Treat a coincident signal in any of these as a systematic, not a discovery. [General method]
- ISS orbit and exposure: the exposure pattern, geomagnetic transmission and livetime have seasonal and precession structure (the ISS orbit precession gives a roughly 60-day cycle and the solar beta angle gives an annual-scale modulation of thermal state and exposure). Apply the identical search to (a) the per-bin exposure series per rigidity bin, (b) the detector efficiency and livetime series, (c) a control species with the same selection (for example the proton or the positron-free combined e+ + e- flux, whose modulation is documented), and (d) the e- flux alone and the e+ flux alone. A signal appearing in the controls or in only one charge sign through a detector path is instrumental.
- Temperature and detector state: TRD gas, ECAL, Tracker and TOF conditions with seasonal thermal variation. Show a stability metric versus time of abundant control species' rates and of efficiency after livetime and cutoff correction, with a trigger for adding a period-split, scale factor with uncertainty, or systematic.
- Heliospheric physical annual effects: the Earth's heliolatitude changes by about +- 7 degrees through the year (a real physical source of annual structure; the interpretation, force-field or drift modelling, is a separate model step, not part of the measurement).
- Alias from the 27-day solar rotation: with 27-day sampling, the solar rotation is at the sampling frequency itself; and 13.5-day and 9-day structure aliases into low frequencies. Test by injection and by comparing with finer (daily or per-rotation, public-style) bins as a cross-check where statistics permit. Plus, annual beating of the 27-day solar rotation with the Earth-year orbit gives an apparent shift of the observed synodic period; keep this in mind when reading near-annual sidebands.

### 4.4 Injection and validation [Proposal]
- Inject sinusoids of known amplitude at P = 1 yr (and at nearby periods and the aliases) into the real exposure/count pattern, run the full chain (counts, templates, unfolding, ratio, detrending, fit) and report recovery and bias. State the detection sensitivity as the minimal annual amplitude recoverable, so a null result is interpretable as an upper limit on amplitude (a fraction of R) per rigidity bin.
- Metrics and triggers (no "reasonable agreement"): amplitude recovery within, say, a pre-set tolerance (set from the size of the effect on the result); if the recovered amplitude depends on baseline knot spacing by more than the statistical error, add that as a systematic or declare the baseline-limited result; if the control series (exposure, efficiency, proton) show a coincident annual term at the pre-set significance, classify as instrumental.
- Reporting: amplitude (with unit: fractional change of R), phase (to compare with the phase of the exposure and of control series), local and global significance, amplitude as a function of rigidity bin, and the full covariance used. Use the four-level policy for any significance claim (excess, evidence, discovery with predefined thresholds); a local p-value from one rigidity bin out of several is an excess at most.

## 5. Joint fit option [Proposal]
Shared-shape and period-specific parameters: L = prod_k prod_i Poisson(n_ki | mu_ki(theta_shared, phi_k, eta)) x constraints, with theta_shared for the spectral shape common to all periods, phi_k period-specific normalization or modulation parameters, eta nuisances shared or per period per the correlation classification. The annual term can then be a parametrization of phi_k. Test the joint model against separate fits with toys. Do not combine periods that use different definitions (rigidity ranges, track configurations, cutoff treatment, hardware eras).

## 6. Checklist of failures to avoid
- Averaging fluxes from bins with different exposure; using one cutoff transmission for every bin.
- Raw-series periodogram of a trend-dominated signal; annual power that is really baseline leakage.
- Single-frequency p-value after a scan; using white-noise toys for a red-noise series.
- Not running the same search on exposure, efficiency, control-species series.
- Cancellation assumed in the ratio; independent errors assumed across periods while systematics persist.
- Treating the 5-50 GV range as published: the daily lepton papers stop at 41.9 GV (C40, C41) and the Bartels-rotation lepton paper is in energy (1-50 GeV, C39).
- Quoting the 108-day (four-rotation) binning of the deuteron/Li analyses (C27, C29) as an AMS rule: a different choice; here 27 days is chosen for the annual-period resolution.
- Fabricating AMS efficiencies, counts, or correlation models.
