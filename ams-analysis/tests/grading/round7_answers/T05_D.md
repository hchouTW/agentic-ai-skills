**Verdict: not yet defensible as written. The skeleton is right, but it has four gaps and one likely double count, and none of the four gaps can be judged from a one-sentence description.**

I assume this is a public-style AMS proton flux at the top of the instrument, in rigidity. Please correct me if it is not. I use symbolic quantities throughout, and I do not supply any AMS numbers.

## What you describe versus the standard estimator

[General method] Your chain is `Φ_i = N_i / (A · T · ΔR_i) × c_i`, where `c_i` is an MC bin-by-bin factor.

[General method] The diagonal estimator is `Φ_i = (n_i − b_i) / (ΔR_i · A_i ε_i T_i)`. It is valid only if bin migration is negligible.

[Documented, C31] Public AMS flux papers write the flux as `N/(A ε ΔR T)`, with `N` corrected for migration by unfolding.

## Defects, in priority order

1. **Bin-by-bin MC factor in place of a response model (largest issue).**
   - A bin-by-bin factor is a diagonal correction. It silently assumes that migration is small and that the MC spectrum shape is right.
   - With a steeply falling spectrum, events from lower true rigidity bins feed higher reconstructed bins. The size of that feed depends on the spectrum you assumed in MC, so the correction is circular. It is also rigidity-dependent, and the rigidity resolution has non-Gaussian tails.
   - [Documented, C30] The migration correction is not small. For phosphorus in the AMS paper it ranges from about +25% at 3 GV to −21% at 1.2 TV. This is a different species and does not transfer to protons, but it shows the effect is not negligible in AMS-type spectrometers.
   - Fix: use the response `R_ij = P(R_meas in bin i | R_true in bin j, selected)` with tails included. Either unfold, or forward-fold with `μ_i = Σ_j R_ij A_j ε_j T_j Φ_j ΔR_j + b_i`. See `inference-and-unfolding`.
   - If you keep the bin-by-bin factor, you need an MC closure with several injected spectral shapes, and you must show that the factor is stable against the assumed spectrum. Otherwise state that it is a correction with a systematic, not a measurement-level estimator.

2. **Ambiguity over what the MC factor contains, which risks a double count.**
   - The factor may be "reco/true counts" in MC, in which case it already contains efficiency and acceptance. Then dividing by acceptance and applying separate efficiencies counts them twice.
   - The factor may instead be a pure migration correction. In that case the efficiency must be applied separately.
   - Decide which one it is. Record each effect in exactly one category: veto (exposure), calibration, efficiency, or response. Exposure already contains acceptance and livetime.
   - `scripts/audit_analysis_spec.py` uses one `effect_id` per correction, `response.includes`, and `estimator.applies` for this check. Fill those fields in as your checklist.

3. **No efficiency term, and no background subtraction.**
   - "Acceptance times livetime" omits `ε`. Effective acceptance `A_eff` is geometric acceptance times average efficiency. Say which one you use.
   - If you use `A_geo`, you need the trigger and selection efficiencies as conditional factors. Each needs a named denominator, and you must not multiply marginal efficiencies as if independent. You also need combined-efficiency closure and ordering closure (see `efficiency-acceptance-backgrounds`).
   - Data/MC efficiency scale factors are needed, and they must not be measured on a different species or period. [Documented, C31 and C25 context] AMS corrects acceptance for data/MC efficiency differences.
   - "Events after cuts" is not signal. You need `n_i − b_i` with covariance. For protons the contributions include `D`/`He` contamination, electrons depending on rigidity, and charge confusion (wrong sign or wrong |Z|).
   - Charge confusion is a tail phenomenon. Validate it in the tails, not with the Gaussian core of the resolution.

4. **Livetime and exposure are probably underspecified.**
   - Livetime is not calendar time. It excludes dead time and prescales, and it must be quality-approved.
   - Livetime must be combined with geomagnetic transmission per rigidity bin. [Documented, C31] Public AMS selections require rigidity above 1.2 times the maximum geomagnetic cutoff in the field of view. [Documented, C23 and C27 context] AMS then varies that factor as a systematic, for example 1.2 to 1.4 in the antiproton analysis and 1.0 to 1.4 in the deuteron analysis.
   - [Documented, C31] Public analyses also restrict to normal detector conditions, pointing within 40° of zenith, and ISS outside the South Atlantic Anomaly. Check that you have stated your equivalents.
   - Acceptance and livetime vary together in time, so integrate exposure over intervals. Do not take the product of two averages.
   - If you cannot state the cutoff model and safety factor, the low-rigidity bins are not reliable.

5. **Smaller items.**
   - The binning convention is unstated. For a steep spectrum, quoting the flux at the arithmetic bin center is biased. Give a prescription, or use the exact bin-average (`scripts/ams_kinematics.py` handles the Jacobian).
   - Survival and interaction against upstream material, with its cross-section model variation, is a separate correction from geometric acceptance.
   - Generated-spectrum reweighting must not be circular with the measured spectrum.
   - There is no systematic ledger, and no covariance across bins. Adjacent bins are correlated through unfolding and shared systematics.
   - The ledger should cover these families:
     - rigidity scale, propagated through migration and not as a rescale of the final spectrum;
     - efficiency and factorization bias;
     - material, hadronic and fragmentation model;
     - background and charge confusion;
     - response and prior;
     - cutoff factor;
     - time stability;
     - MC statistics.
   - Data and MC must use the same reconstruction and production version and the same hardware era.

## Required validation

State a metric and a trigger for every check. "Reasonable agreement" is not validation.
- MC closure with injected spectra, including wrong-shape priors.
- Stability of the flux against time, detector region, track configuration, and cut variations.
- Cutoff-factor scan.
- Comparison to published spectra as a plausibility check only. Agreement does not prove correctness.

## Inputs that would change this verdict

- Whether the MC factor is migration only or also includes efficiency and acceptance (the double-count question above).
- The rigidity range and the size of the migration in your MC. A small, well-closed migration in a limited range could make bin-by-bin tolerable with a stated systematic.
- Whether you have a response matrix with tails.
- Your background estimates and their covariance.
- The cutoff model and safety factor, and the time selection.
- The data period, hardware era, and whether the work is public-data-style or internal. Any internal numbers or cuts you supply I will treat as user-supplied and cannot verify.

[Proposal] Recommended path: define the estimand (flux in true rigidity, top of the instrument) and the response first, and only then choose bin-by-bin, unfolding, or forward-folding. Bin-by-bin should be justified by closure, not assumed.
