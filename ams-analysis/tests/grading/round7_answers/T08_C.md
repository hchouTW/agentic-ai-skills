## Verdict

Not sound as described. Each of the three pillars has a defect that would bias the result, and the first two break AMS-specific invariants. I treat this as a public-level design review with no internal numbers; every fix below is a [Proposal] or [General method] unless tagged otherwise. Antiproton estimand assumed: the flux or p̄/p versus |R|.

## Defects, in priority order

**1. "Negative-sign tracks passing a TRD cut" is not a signal definition. [General method / detector invariant]**
- Charge sign comes only from the Tracker and magnet. The TRD does not measure charge sign. It gives lepton/hadron separation through transition radiation, which depends on γ. It is a background-rejection tool, not a charge-sign detector.
- The signal needs all of the following:
  - Tracker sign < 0.
  - |Z| = 1 from the Tracker, TOF and others.
  - A hadron-like TRD and ECAL response.
  - β consistent with the rigidity.
  - Geomagnetic and primary selection.
- The TRD cut is only one piece of the electron rejection. The ECAL is the other. Their separate and joint efficiencies must be measured as conditional factors, not assumed independent.
- The TRD efficiency for antiprotons is rigidity dependent. It must be measured, for example from protons, with an explicit transfer to antiprotons.
- The signal is defined by what passes the selection. A hard cut also leaves a residual electron tail.

**2. Charge confusion from the Gaussian rigidity resolution is invalid. [Invariant 4]**
- Charge confusion is a tail phenomenon, set by non-Gaussian effects:
  - Large-angle scattering.
  - Bad hit assignment.
  - Interactions.
  - Alignment.
  - Low-rigidity failures.
- The Gaussian core says almost nothing about the sign-flip probability. It can be off by orders of magnitude.
- At high rigidity, the dominant background is protons that are positive-sign but reconstructed negative. These protons outnumber antiprotons by a large factor (user-checkable in the published sample sizes, S08, C06 [Documented]). A tiny tail probability therefore matters a lot.
- Required:
  - Take the tail from calibrated MC, and validate it on data. Use abundant proton samples with looser or alternative cuts, redundant partial-track fits, and hit-pattern checks. Alternatively use test-beam data.
  - Show the extrapolation from the validation sample to the tight selection is tested.
  - Treat it as a charge-sign migration matrix M(p̄←p) versus rigidity, with an uncertainty.
- [Documented, S08, C22] The published antiproton analysis models charge-confusion protons as a template and checks the charge-confusion MC against 400 GV proton test-beam data. It does not use the Gaussian core.
- Better still, fit it rather than subtract it. Use a charge-sign-sensitive discriminant, such as signed rigidity or a Tracker-tail estimator, with an explicit charge-confusion template.

**3. Electron background "subtracted by MC" is under-validated.**
- Electrons are a dominant negative-sign background at some rigidities.
- A pure MC subtraction depends on three things: the e⁻ flux model, the TRD/ECAL rejection in MC, and the data/MC agreement of the rejection tail. None of these is validated.
- Required:
  - Use a data-driven or template approach. [Documented, S08, C22] The published analysis uses a template fit to the negative-rigidity sample. The signal template comes from proton data. The electron, π⁻ and charge-confusion templates come from data and MC.
  - Define a control region that is electron-enriched and signal-depleted (for example reversed TRD or ECAL cuts). Measure the transfer factor, and check the antiproton contamination of that control.
  - Add finite-template-statistics nuisances (Barlow-Beeston style). Check template cross-contamination and identifiability.
  - Check closure and fit bias with injected signal.
  - Do not tune the template, binning or discriminant repeatedly on the fit sample.
- Also watch for a circular dependence. If ECAL variables define the sample and also the templates, the two are not independent.

**4. Missing ledger items.** The description lists only the signal and two backgrounds. The following are absent:
- Secondary and interacting particles, and π⁻.
- The p denominator, if the target is p̄/p, with its own acceptance and efficiency. Cancellation must be shown, not assumed.
- Exposure: acceptance × livetime × geomagnetic transmission, kept distinct and applied once.
- Efficiency conditional chains, with ordering and combined-efficiency closure. Each effect must be corrected in one place only, with no double counting between veto, efficiency and response.
- Rigidity migration. At a steep spectrum a diagonal bin-by-bin correction is unreliable. A response matrix or unfolding with closure and prior variation is needed.
- A systematic ledger and covariance. Classify each nuisance as correlated, partial or independent, and carry the joint covariance.
- Low-count behavior at the highest rigidities. Use exact or toy-validated intervals, not Gaussian errors or S/√B.
- Time stability and data-quality selection.

**5. Validation language.** "Estimated" and "subtracted" are not validation. State a metric (closure, pulls, coverage) and a trigger for changing the method or adding an uncertainty.

## What would change the verdict
- Data or MC level, and the rigidity range. The tail and background mix change a lot between low and high rigidity.
- Whether a template fit or a cut-and-count is intended. A fit is strongly preferred.
- What the control samples are, and whether they are signal-free.
- Whether the target is the flux or the ratio. Public or internal work.
- Any tail validation you already have. If it comes from test-beam or abundant-sample data, the verdict on item 2 would soften.
