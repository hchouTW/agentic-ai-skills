## 1. The 2013 paper is not the latest

[Documented, C03; S02] The 2013 AMS paper is the first result: "First Result from AMS on the ISS: Precision Measurement of the Positron Fraction in Primary Cosmic Rays of 0.5-350 GeV", PRL 110, 141102 (2013), based on 6.8x10^6 positron and electron events. Its verification level is abstract+metadata only. It is a valid citation for "first AMS measurement of the positron fraction", not for "latest".

It has been superseded in range and statistics.
- [Documented, S03] A 2014 high-statistics update is PRL 113, 121101 (0.5-500 GeV). The index records it at metadata-only level, so I quote no numbers from it.
- [Documented, C04; S04] The 2019 paper "Towards Understanding the Origin of Cosmic-Ray Positrons", PRL 122, 041102, reports 1.9 million positrons up to 1 TeV. It reports the positron flux, with a rise near 25 GeV, a dropoff above about 284 GeV, and a source-term cutoff at more than 4 sigma. The index records the positron flux there, not a fraction.
- [Documented, S01] The Phys. Rept. 894, 1 (2021) review covers the first seven years of data.

Publication date and data period differ. The 2013 paper was published in 2013, with data from the first part of the mission. The later papers extend to more years of data. Reading "latest" correctly means checking which question each paper answers (fraction vs flux, energy range, data period) and not just taking the newest date.

For "latest" the proper check is INSPIRE-HEP, the journal record and ams02.space, including supplements and superseding papers. I cannot browse here. The skill's index was last verified 2026-09-20, so I do not claim currency beyond that.
