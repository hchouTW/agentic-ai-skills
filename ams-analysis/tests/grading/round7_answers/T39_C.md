**Short answer:** AMS measures nuclear inelastic cross sections in its own material in flight, from the survival probability of cosmic-ray nuclei crossing the detector, and then adjusts a Monte Carlo cross-section model to match. The numerical cross-section values are only in figures in the source I have, so I cannot give them.

**Method** [Documented, C80; source S01, the AMS Phys. Rept. 894 review, Section 7. This is review-level only. The dedicated AMS cross-section paper it cites was not read, so the method is only summarized.]

- **Material.** Averaged over the path lengths in the acceptance, the material traversed is about 73% carbon and 17% aluminium by mass. Small amounts of Si, O, H, Na, Au and others make up the rest. These are path-length averages over the acceptance, not a material budget.
- **Orientation.** The ISS attitude lets AMS point horizontally. Nuclei then pass through the detector both from L8 to L1 and from L2 to L9.
- **Defining the particle.** The seven inner tracker layers, L2-L8, define the particle, i.e. its dE/dx charge and rigidity.
- **Survival fractions.** The charge distributions in L2-L8 are compared with those in L1 (or in L8 and L9). This gives the L1-L2 and L8-L9 survival probabilities.
- **Interaction probability inside the tracker.** The probability of interaction between L2 and L8 is below 3%. It is obtained by comparing the upper-TOF and lower-TOF charges.
- **Combination.** That probability is combined with the L1-L2 and L8-L9 survival probabilities to give the L1-L9 survival.
- **Model adjustment.** A dedicated Monte Carlo uses the Geant4 Glauber-Gribov inelastic cross sections, varied by ±10%. It is compared with the data, and the best-agreeing value is chosen.
- **Helium below about 30 GV.** A discrepancy of a few percent appeared at low rigidity. A modified Glauber-Gribov model was therefore matched to the data over the full rigidity range.
- **Heavier nuclei below about 30 GV.** The measured He rigidity dependence is used to modify the model.
- **Related analyses.** The helium flux paper used the same horizontal-pointing survival method, with Glauber-Gribov cross sections scaled by 1.05-1.20 [Documented, C60]. The Li isotope paper also used a survival probability measured with AMS cosmic-ray data [Documented, C29]. The review states that the Li result is consistent with its survival-probability results [Documented, C80].

**What was obtained** [Documented, C80]

- **Species.** Survival probabilities, and from them inelastic cross sections, were measured for Li, Be, B, C, N, O, Ne, Mg and Si.
- **Carbon target.** The inelastic cross sections on carbon, averaged over 5-100 GV, are constant within the measurement accuracy.
- **Prior data.** Before AMS, inelastic cross sections had been measured only below 10 GV, and only for He and C projectiles. The review says no other measurements exist except He-on-C and C-on-C.
- **Numbers I cannot give.** The cross-section values themselves are in the review's figures and are not transcribed in the skill's source index, so I do not quote millibarn values. The index does not support any.

**Use in flux systematics** [Documented, C60, C62, C44]

The inelastic-interaction uncertainty is a systematic in the AMS flux analyses. The sizes below are specific to each analysis and must not be carried over to other species or periods.

- **Helium, first 30 months:** 1% under 100 GV, 2% at 3 TV (C60).
- **Carbon:** below 2.2% to 100 GV, 3% at 3 TV (C62).
- **Oxygen:** below 2.7% to 100 GV, 3.5% at 3 TV (C62).
- **Ratios:** the cross-section systematic is treated as correlated between numerator and denominator (C85).
