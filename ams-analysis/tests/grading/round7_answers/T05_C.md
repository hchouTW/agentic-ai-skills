# Review: proton flux estimator

**Verdict: not acceptable as stated.** The formula is the right skeleton, but the description has one likely-wrong step (the bin-by-bin MC factor), two terms that are probably used loosely (acceptance, livetime), and missing pieces (background, cutoff, data/MC efficiency, systematics). Nothing below assumes internal AMS cuts or numbers. Everything is symbolic and labeled.

Your description: `Φ_i = N_i / (A · T_live · ΔR_i)`, then multiplied by a bin-by-bin MC correction `c_i`.

## Defects, in priority order

**1. Bin-by-bin MC factor in place of a response treatment [General method; Documented practice, C31].**
- A single factor per bin is the diagonal estimator. It is valid only if bin migration is negligible.
- Rigidity resolution has tails, and the spectrum falls steeply. Events from lower true bins feed higher reconstructed bins, so the bias is rigidity-dependent. It grows toward high R, where the resolution is worst.
- The factor is also **MC-spectrum dependent**. It is computed with some generated spectral shape. If that shape is not the measured one, you have a circular or biased correction. Iterating the MC spectrum toward the data does not remove the dependence unless you show convergence.
- Public AMS flux papers correct for migration by **unfolding**, not by a single factor (Documented, C31; the migration correction is not small, e.g. about +25% to -21% across the range for phosphorus, C30. That is a different species, so it only illustrates scale).
- Required: define the response `R_ij = P(R_meas in bin i | R_true in bin j, selected)`, with tails. Then either unfold or forward-fold: `μ_i = Σ_j R_ij A_j ε_j T_j Φ_j ΔR_j + b_i`.
- If you keep a bin-by-bin factor, state what it contains. Show the migration matrix, show the size of the off-diagonal terms, and show that the factor is insensitive to the assumed spectrum (reweighting test). Treat that as a bias estimate with a systematic, not as a correction.

**2. "Acceptance times livetime" is not exposure [Invariant 3].**
- Acceptance, efficiency, livetime and exposure are distinct. Exposure is `Σ_t A_eff(t) · T_live(t) · geomagnetic transmission`, which you cannot form from one A and one T.
- Check which acceptance you mean. If it is geometric `A_geo`, then selection and reconstruction efficiency `ε_j` is missing. If it is effective acceptance, `ε` is already inside it, and a separate `ε` must not be applied again.
- Livetime must be quality-approved and per-rigidity. It has to include the fraction of time each rigidity is above the local cutoff. A single calendar or DAQ livetime is wrong at low R.
- A and ε vary with time and rigidity. Integrate their product with the livetime over intervals, not as separate averages.

**3. Geomagnetic cutoff is not mentioned [Documented, C31, C20].**
- A primary-proton flux needs a stated cutoff treatment. Public AMS analyses require R above a safety factor (1.2) times the maximum cutoff in the field of view, then vary that factor as a systematic. Other papers use different scans, so there is no single AMS standard.
- State your field model, safety factor and scan range. Below or near the cutoff, secondary and albedo populations contaminate the sample.

**4. No background subtraction [General method].**
- Your numerator is "events after cuts". For protons that includes at least:
  - charge confusion (wrong sign or wrong |Z|), which is a tail effect;
  - light-nuclei and deuteron contamination, which depends on rigidity;
  - electrons or positrons at some rigidities;
  - secondaries and albedo near the cutoff.
- Use `N_i - b_i` with a background ledger: origin, estimator (template fit or migration matrix), control region, signal contamination of the control region, closure, and nuisance correlations. Charge-confusion rates must be validated in the **tails**, not extrapolated from the Gaussian core of the resolution (Invariant 4).

**5. Efficiency: MC alone, or data/MC-corrected? [General method; Documented, C25, C27 for practice].**
- Efficiencies must be conditional, each with a named denominator. Do not multiply marginal efficiencies unless independence is shown. Do a combined-efficiency closure in MC and an ordering test.
- Where MC and data differ (trigger, tracker/TOF matching, charge selection, interaction survival), apply data/MC scale factors with their own uncertainty. Measure them with tag-and-probe or orthogonal samples, with signal contamination of the control accounted for. State which of your ε values are data-driven and which are MC-only.
- Do not correct the same effect twice. A veto, calibration, efficiency and response correction each handle different things. A drifting detector period should be removed as livetime or carried as a systematic, not both.

**6. Smaller points.**
- State the bin convention. Quoting at the arithmetic bin centre is wrong for a steep spectrum. Use a stated prescription or quote the bin-averaged flux.
- Say whether bins are in rigidity (R_true for the flux). Do not mix R_meas and R_true.
- Interaction and survival probability in upstream material is a separate acceptance-type correction. It needs a cross-section model and its variation.
- Flux is at the top of the instrument. Do not present it as interstellar without a modulation model.
- Statistical errors: if you unfold, they are correlated between bins. Report the covariance, not only diagonal errors.

## What to add: systematics and validation

A systematic ledger, with each source tagged by its correlation across bins:
- trigger and selection efficiency, including factorization bias;
- acceptance, material, and interaction cross-section model;
- background and charge-confusion tails;
- rigidity scale and resolution. Propagate shifts **through migration**, not by rescaling the final spectrum;
- unfolding prior, regularization and response perturbation;
- cutoff safety factor and field model;
- time dependence and livetime;
- MC statistics and generator or transport model.

Combine covariances, not unexamined quadrature sums. Do not combine unknown correlations in quadrature (the AMS papers add independent sources in quadrature, C23, C25, only where independence is the stated assumption).

Validation needs a metric and a trigger, not "reasonable agreement":
- MC closure by injecting a spectrum with different slopes and recovering it. Name the tolerance that triggers an unfolding change or an added uncertainty.
- Stability of the flux versus time period, detector region, geometry, cut loosening, and track configuration.
- Comparison with published spectra only as a plausibility check.

## Recommended corrected form [Proposal]

`Φ_j ΔR_j` is obtained by solving `n_i = Σ_j R_ij · X_j · Φ_j ΔR_j + b_i`, where `X_j = A_j ε_j T_j` is the exposure in true bin j, including geomagnetic transmission and survival. Use forward folding or iterative unfolding. The diagonal form `Φ_i = (n_i - b_i) / (ΔR_i · A_i ε_i T_i)` is acceptable only after you show that migration is negligible for your bins and range.

## Inputs that would change this verdict

- Whether the MC factor actually contains the migration, or is only efficiency and acceptance. If it is only efficiency and acceptance, then item 1 becomes "no migration correction at all".
- Rigidity range and bin widths relative to the resolution. At low R with wide bins, the diagonal estimator may be adequate. At high R it is not.
- Whether your "acceptance" includes efficiency, and how livetime and the cutoff were applied.
- Public data or internal work, and the rigidity range. I treat all internal numbers as user-supplied and cannot supply cuts, bins or systematic sizes.
- Whether the MC spectrum was reweighted to the data, and how.
- How backgrounds and charge confusion were estimated.
