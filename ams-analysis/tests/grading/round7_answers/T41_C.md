**Short answer.** About 830 +- 30 days (roughly 2.3 years) for the 10%-90% transition of the positron-to-electron flux ratio, and in that fit the duration does not depend on energy. [Documented, C90, S01] This is a Phys. Rept. review (S01) result for 1-21 GeV, from the first seven years of data (the review's section 16, Eqs. 17-18). The number is the width of a descriptive fit function, not a directly observed event duration.

**How AMS got it.** [Documented, C90, S01; review-level, full text]

1. **Data.** Positron and electron fluxes were measured per Bartels rotation (27 days) in energy bins, from May 2011 to May 2017. From these the review forms R_e(t, E) = Phi(e+)/Phi(e-). In this ratio the short-term structures largely cancel, leaving the long-term, charge-sign-dependent evolution. The review attributes that dependence to particle drifts in the heliosphere (C89). The input is 3871 independent R_e measurements in energy and time. [Documented, C89, C90, S01]
2. **Fit function.** A four-parameter logistic (sigmoid) function at each energy: R_e(t, E) = R0(E) [1 + C(E) / (exp(-(t - t_1/2(E)) / (Delta t / Delta_80)) + 1)].
   - The parameters are R0 (baseline), C (transition amplitude), t_1/2 (midpoint time) and Delta t (transition time).
   - Delta_80 = 4.39 is a constant that makes Delta t the 10%-90% rise time. [Documented, C90]
3. **Result of the fits.** chi2/d.o.f. is about 1 for all fits over 1-21 GeV. Delta t is consistent with an energy-independent 830 +- 30 days. [Documented, C90]
4. **Midpoint delay.** The energy dependence sits in the midpoint, not the width: t_1/2(E) - t_rev = tau (E/GeV)^rho, with t_rev = 1 July 2013 (the centre of the period without a well-defined solar polarity), rho = -0.33 +- 0.04 (stat) (+0.08, -0.15) (syst), tau = 580 +- 19 (stat) +- 136 (syst) days. t_1/2 changes by 260 +- 30 days from 1 to 6 GeV. The amplitude C is near 1 at 1 GeV, falls with energy and is consistent with zero above 20 GeV. [Documented, C90]

**Caveats.**
- The logistic form is the collaboration's descriptive choice, not a physical model. The review notes qualitative agreement with drift-including solar modulation models, but that agreement is not a derivation of 830 days. [Documented, C90]
- t_rev = 1 July 2013 is a stated choice, so the delays t_1/2 - t_rev depend on it. The width Delta t does not.
- Delta t and t_1/2 are really determined only at low energy where C is large. Above 6 GeV the review fixes Delta t to 830 days and uses the Eq. (18) form for t_1/2, so "energy independent" is partly built in at high energy. [Documented, C90]
- The systematic uncertainties on rho and tau are large, so quote both stat and syst errors.
- [Unknown/needs input] The text I read does not say how systematic errors are correlated across energy bins and rotations, or whether any trial-factor correction was applied. Do not assume either.
