**No. A constant ratio is consistent with a common origin and suggestive of one, but it does not prove it.**

**What AMS reported**
- [Documented, C77] In the Phys. Rept. review of the first seven years, the positron-to-antiproton flux ratio above 60 GeV is consistent with a constant. A constant fit over 60-525 GeV gives 2.00 ± 0.035 (stat.) ± 0.06 (syst.), with chi2/d.o.f. = 7.2/12. The review reads this as nearly identical energy dependence of positrons and antiprotons at high energy, and says it "suggests a possible common source". It adds that continued data taking is needed to confirm the origin.
- [Documented, C06] The earlier antiproton paper (PRL 117, 091103) reports that the antiproton, proton and positron fluxes have nearly identical rigidity dependence at about 60-500 GV. That is a different variable and range from the ratio above, so the two should not be merged.

**Why it does not prove a common origin**
1. **The test is weak.** A constant-ratio fit that does not fail only shows the data cannot distinguish the two spectral shapes within the errors. It does not show the shapes are identical. Over a limited range (60-525 GeV) with finite statistics and systematics, many different physical pictures can give a flat ratio.
2. **Similar shape is not a shared source.** Two species with different production mechanisms could have similar high-energy spectra, for example through similar secondary production plus propagation. The review itself says the antiproton spectrum shape is not expected if antiprotons come only from cosmic-ray collisions with the interstellar medium [Documented, C75]. It also says the modelling uncertainties must improve before a definitive interpretation.
3. **The ratio is of fluxes in different variables and with different systematics.** Positron fluxes are defined in energy (ECAL and Tracker) and antiproton fluxes in rigidity. Acceptance, efficiency and charge-confusion systematics do not cancel automatically in a ratio of independently measured fluxes, so each must be classified as correlated, partial or independent. The quoted systematic should be understood in that light.
4. **Common origin is a model step.** AMS measures fluxes. Calling the pair "common-source" requires a model of production and propagation, with the secondary-production and propagation uncertainties varied as nuisance parameters.
5. **Conditional interpretation statements.** The review states that antiprotons are not produced by pulsars, and that a cutoff in the antiproton spectrum would be expected if high-energy antiprotons came from dark-matter annihilation. These are the collaboration's interpretive statements, not measurements. No such cutoff is claimed as observed in this ratio (C77).

**What would strengthen or weaken the case [Proposal]**
- Extend the range to higher energy and test whether the ratio stays constant or develops a cutoff or slope. Any shape difference falsifies "identical spectra".
- Fit a slope of the ratio, not only a constant, and quote its uncertainty.
- Compare the data with explicit secondary-production plus propagation models, with their uncertainties, and a source-plus-background alternative.
- Treat the electron, proton and positron data jointly and use all AMS species to constrain models (C75).

**Bottom line:** the constant ratio is a published observation, and the collaboration's wording is "suggests a possible common source", not a demonstration. Constancy within errors over 60-525 GeV is necessary for a common-origin picture, not sufficient.
