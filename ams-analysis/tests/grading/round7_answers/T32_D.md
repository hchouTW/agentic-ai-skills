# 27-day positron-to-electron ratio, 5-50 GV, and a one-year periodicity search

Labels: [Documented, C##] = public AMS source in the skill's ledger, at the stated scope only; [General method]; [Proposal]; [Unknown/needs input]. No AMS internal cuts, livetimes, efficiencies or systematic sizes are available here, so everything operational is symbolic. Nothing below states an AMS result for a one-year periodicity in the positron-to-electron ratio; I found no such claim in the ledger.

## 1. Estimand (fixed before looking at the time dependence)

- Target: `R_k(R_i) = Phi_{e+,k}(R_i) / Phi_{e-,k}(R_i)`, the ratio of fluxes (not the positron fraction `e+/(e+ + e-)`), in time bin `k` and rigidity bin `i`, at the top of the instrument. [Proposal]
- Variable: rigidity (GV), as the request says. Note the AMS time-resolved lepton products differ in variable. The daily e+ and e- papers bin in rigidity, 1.00-41.9 GV [Documented, C40, C41]. The Bartels-rotation lepton paper bins in energy, up to 49.33 GeV [Documented, C48]. So 5-50 GV is not covered by the daily-rigidity papers above 41.9 GV. State the variable, and do not copy their bins.
- Time bin: one Bartels rotation, 27 days, a fixed count [General method]. A Bartels-rotation lepton flux exists in the public record, but in energy [Documented, C48]. Other AMS analyses used 108-day blocks (four rotations) [Documented, C27, C29]; that is those analyses' choice, not a rule.
- Span: the full data period you have, from May 2011 onward, as user input. Cover the solar cycle and the 2013 polarity reversal.
- Charge-sign dependence: electrons and positrons modulate differently through drift effects [General method]. Public daily results show electron and positron variations differ distinctly, with a hysteresis below about 8.5 GV [Documented, C41]. Positrons are modulated more than protons below about 7 GV [Documented, C41]. Do not merge these two thresholds. Your 5-50 GV range straddles both, so the low end is expected to carry more time structure than the high end. [Proposal]

## 2. Rigidity binning (5-50 GV)

- [Proposal] Use about 4-6 log-spaced bins, for example edges near 5, 7, 10, 15, 25, 50 GV. Fix the edges before looking at the time series. The edges are a placeholder; set them from the counts below.
- Bin widths should be at least about twice the resolution if migration is unfolded. For the Tracker rigidity resolution, the review gives dR/R = 0.1 below 20 GV [Documented, C63]. It is a review-level number, so use it only as a scale.
- High-rigidity bins are the limiting ones. Positron counts per 27-day bin fall steeply with rigidity. Choose bin edges so that the least-populated (rigidity, time) cell supports the inference. Use Poisson-exact or toy-validated intervals, never `S/sqrt(B)` or Gaussian errors at low counts. [General method]
- Where a cell is too sparse, merge rigidity bins, or lengthen the time bin for that bin only. Do not quietly drop it. Report the rigidity range where the 27-day resolution is actually supported.

## 3. Measurement chain per (k, i)

Estimator per species `s = e+, e-`. [General method; the form follows the public daily and Bartels analyses, C43, C46, C49]

`Phi_s,ki = N_s,ki(unfolded, bkg-subtracted) / (A_s,ki (1+delta) eps_s,ki T_s,ki DeltaR_i)`

The positron-to-electron ratio is formed from the two fluxes. Keep these distinct: acceptance, efficiency, livetime, geomagnetic transmission (exposure), and response.

1. Selection. Downward particle, Tracker track, `|Z| = 1`, in normal operating conditions, outside the South Atlantic Anomaly, and above a cutoff safety factor times the maximum geomagnetic cutoff. Public lepton analyses used 1.2 times the cutoff in energy and a pointing cut of 40 degrees from zenith [Documented, C24, C48]; one daily proton analysis instead computed the cutoff from data [Documented, C51]. These are those analyses' choices, not a standard. The safety factor and pointing cut are analysis inputs [Unknown/needs input]. Vary the safety factor (public analyses scanned 1.0-1.4 [Documented, C49; C27]) and carry the variation per bin and per charge sign.
2. Charge sign comes from the Tracker and magnet only. TRD, ECAL, TOF and RICH do not measure sign. The TRD is not a standalone charge-sign detector. [Invariant 2]
3. Lepton identification. TRD estimator and ECAL shower and E/p variables suppress protons, which dominate at these rigidities (the proton-to-positron abundance ratio is large and grows with rigidity, so the positron proton-contamination is the dominant background in the high bins). [General method] Public lepton analyses used a two-dimensional template fit (Lambda_TRD vs a BDT charge-confusion estimator Lambda_CC) with correct-sign lepton, correct-sign proton and charge-confusion-lepton templates [Documented, C24]. A proposal for this analysis is to fit each (k, i) cell by template, with shared shapes across k and a per-cell normalization. The template choice, provenance and finite-template statistics are inputs [Unknown/needs input].
4. Charge confusion. Treat it as a tail phenomenon, not the Gaussian core of the rigidity resolution [Invariant 4]. It is a sign-migration matrix `M(e+ <- e-)`, `M(e- <- e+)` versus rigidity, validated on abundant samples (electron data under loosened cuts, test-beam e+/e-). The electron charge confusion matters most for the positron numerator, because electrons outnumber positrons: the confusion tail of e- into the e+ sample is the key background, and the reverse is a small fraction of the large electron sample. Its size per bin is [Unknown/needs input]; the review quotes a BDT-based electron charge-confusion fraction below 8% up to 1 TeV after selection, which is electrons only and not a general rate [Documented, C65]. Do not use it for the ratio without a validated per-bin estimate.
5. Migration. Unfold per time bin independently as the public time-resolved analyses did [Documented, C43, C49], or forward-fold. Migration is small at these rigidities for leptons but not zero; check with the response matrix and `scripts/validate_response.py` on a supplied matrix. [General method]
6. Exposure per bin. `T_ki(R)` counts livetime-weighted seconds above the (safety-factor times) cutoff, per time bin and per sign. At 5 GV the cutoff transmission varies across bins and between positive and negative charge at the low edge, so compute it per bin and per sign, not once. [General method; cf. C48, C51]. Average nothing across bins of different exposure except through counts and exposures.

## 4. Systematics and covariance (ratio, not just flux)

- Ratios do not cancel acceptance, efficiency and systematics automatically. Classify each effect per the table in the species ratio blueprint. [Invariant 5]

| Effect | Treatment in the ratio |
|---|---|
| Geometric acceptance | Largely common if the fiducial region is identical for both signs; verify by MC |
| Trigger and selection efficiency | Partial; lepton-sign-specific pieces (e.g. TRD, ECAL) are measured per sign |
| Proton contamination | Mostly in the positron numerator; independent per sign |
| Charge confusion | Sign-specific; enters numerator and denominator anti-correlated |
| Rigidity scale | Correlated; amplified by the local spectral index; propagate through migration |
| Cutoff and exposure | Per bin and per sign; differs at low rigidity |

- Time dependence of each nuisance: persistent (calibration, template shape, cross sections: correlated across all k); slowly varying (correlated between neighbouring k); independent per bin. Statistical errors are independent between disjoint-event time bins. [General method]
- Public AMS time-resolved fluxes separated time-independent and time-dependent systematics, adding the time-dependent ones (efficiencies evaluated each period) in quadrature [Documented, C44; S43 daily proton only]. How AMS correlated systematics across days or rotations is not described in the text read [Unknown/needs input]. Do not copy a covariance model from AMS; build one explicitly.
- Build one covariance over (time, rigidity) with a documented ordering. A Kronecker product is valid only if the correlation factorizes; state and test it. Check with `scripts/validate_covariance.py`.
- Numerator-denominator covariance carried through. First-order: `Var(R) ~ R^2 [(sigma_N/N)^2 + (sigma_D/D)^2 - 2 rho sigma_N sigma_D/(N D)]`; use toys at low counts. [General method]
- Normalizing every period to a reference period cancels only the persistent systematics and correlates all statistical errors with the reference; carry that covariance. [General method]
- Do not correct one drift twice (veto, calibration, efficiency or response). [Invariant 3]

Stability checks (metric and trigger, not "agreement"): abundant control-species rate (protons or electrons) versus time after livetime and cutoff correction, with a tolerance set by the effect on `R`; a drift beyond tolerance adds a period split, a scale factor with uncertainty, or an extra systematic. Bin edges must respect hardware-era and reconstruction-version boundaries; no bin straddles one. [General method]

## 5. The one-year periodicity search

### 5.1 Definitions fixed in advance [Proposal]

- Observable: `R_k(R_i)` per rigidity bin, plus (separately) a combined 5-50 GV statistic if you want one. Pre-state that the primary band is a single frequency, 1 cycle/year (period 365 days), and say what else is searched.
- Sampling: 27-day bins give about 13.5 samples per year, so a 1-year period is resolved well above the Nyquist limit (period 54 days). The 27-day bins cannot test 27-day, 13.5-day or 9-day structure; those are not in scope. Note also that a fixed 27-day spacing differs from the 365.25-day year, so the phase advances by a fraction of a bin each year; this is fine for a sinusoid fit but make sure bin centers are the exposure-weighted times, not nominal ones.
- Span: the number of years covered sets frequency resolution, roughly 1/T_span. A span of about a solar cycle resolves the annual line from the nearby low-frequency solar-cycle power only after detrending.
- Statistic: choose one before the analysis.
  - (a) Fit `R_k = trend(t_k) + a cos(2 pi t_k/P) + b sin(2 pi t_k/P)` at P = 1 yr by weighted least squares using the full time covariance, and test `a = b = 0` (2 degrees of freedom). This is a one-frequency test and is my recommended primary.
  - (b) A weighted Lomb-Scargle (generalized, floating-mean) periodogram over a pre-stated band, with unequal weights from the bin uncertainties, for the exploratory scan.
  - (c) A wavelet power spectrum if you want to localize in time. AMS used a Morlet wavelet with normalized power and 95% confidence levels from lag-1 autoregressive red-noise Monte Carlo surrogates matched to the variance and lag-1 autocorrelation, for the daily proton flux [Documented, C45, C52; scope: S43 daily proton only]. That is the AMS method for that product, not a rule for this one.

### 5.2 Detrending is the main design issue

- The ratio carries the solar-cycle trend and the polarity-reversal transition. The 2013 reversal shows up in the positron-to-electron ratio as a slow transition; the review describes it with a four-parameter logistic function, with a transition time Delta t = 830 +- 30 days, independent of energy [Documented, C90; descriptive review-level fit, and the reference time t_rev = 1 July 2013 is a stated choice]. A slow trend leaks power into a one-year band unless removed or fitted at the same time.
- [Proposal] Fit the trend and the annual term jointly: a smooth trend (logistic across the reversal, or a spline with knots spaced much longer than 1 year, or a high-order polynomial, with the choice fixed in advance) plus the sinusoid. Test the sensitivity of the annual amplitude to the trend model (alternative knot spacing, alternative functional forms); this is a systematic on the amplitude.
- Do not high-pass filter in a way that makes the annual component partly absorbed or cut. Never detrend with a running average of width near 1 year.
- The red-noise component. Solar activity and Forbush-like variations give correlated, non-white noise on all scales. A white-noise p-value for the annual term is too optimistic. Calibrate by toys that reproduce the red-noise spectrum (fitted from the data after removing the candidate line, or from a surrogate method as in C52), the sampling, and the covariance. [General method]

### 5.3 Significance and look-elsewhere

- If only P = 1 yr is tested, a local p-value is the global p-value, and you can say so. If you also scan a band, report local and global significance with a trial factor and calibrate by toys (Gross and Vitells, S37). Do not use Wilks or a single-frequency p-value for a scan. [General method]
- Whether AMS applied any trial-factor treatment to its periodicity significances is not described in the text read [Unknown/needs input]; do not claim it did or did not.
- Report the annual amplitude and phase with uncertainties for each rigidity bin, not only a p-value. With several rigidity bins, state the number of tests and either combine through the joint likelihood or correct for it. The rigidity-bin trials are correlated (shared exposure, shared systematics), so use toys.
- Use a toy generator that preserves exposure per bin, the correlated systematics, and Poisson statistics at low counts. Check coverage in the low-count bins. [General method]

### 5.4 Annual systematics to rule out before calling it physics

A one-year signal in a ratio need not be heliospheric. Run the same search on the following control series and treat a coincident annual signal as a systematic, not a discovery. [General method]

- Exposure per bin, livetime, and the geomagnetic transmission at the low rigidity edge (the ISS orbit, its pointing and attitude, and season-correlated sampling).
- Selection efficiency and the trigger efficiency versus time; ECAL and TRD estimator stability (the TRD gain and temperature-dependent response are time-dependent calibrations; the validation observable is the discriminant of an abundant control species versus time).
- The proton-contamination estimate and the charge-confusion estimate per time bin (template fraction versus time).
- A control ratio with no expected annual physics: for example the electron flux alone and the positron flux alone, each normalized to protons, to see which species carries a line. Public results say positrons track protons and differ from electrons [Documented, C41], so a line in the ratio that comes only from the electron flux is plausibly physical charge-sign modulation, while a line in both species equally is more likely instrumental. [Proposal; heuristic, not a test of its own]
- Higher rigidity bins, where little solar modulation is expected, serve as an internal null: an annual signal that is rigidity-independent up to 50 GV points to an instrumental or exposure origin.

## 6. Joint model alternative [Proposal]

If you prefer one fit to a two-step approach, use a joint Poisson likelihood over (k, i) with the shared-shape hypothesis tested against separate fits by toys:

`L = prod_k prod_i Poisson(n_ki | mu_ki(theta_shared, phi_k, eta)) x constraints`

`theta_shared` is common spectral shape; `phi_k` is per-period modulation; `eta` are nuisances shared or per period according to Section 4. The annual terms enter as parameters in `phi_k(t)`, with the null model `a = b = 0`. Report per-period results with covariance and exposures so that others can refit.

## 7. Cross-checks and failure modes

- Closure on MC with an injected annual modulation at the expected size; verify recovered amplitude and phase, and the false-positive rate on null injections with red noise.
- Alternate track configurations, loose and tight lepton selection, an alternate proton template shape, and a charge-sign split.
- Period splits (before and after the polarity reversal; even and odd years) to check amplitude stability.
- Alternate bin phase (shift the 27-day grid by about 13 days) to check the sampling does not create the line.
- Failures to avoid: averaging fluxes of bins with different exposure; one cutoff transmission for all bins; independent errors assumed across periods while systematics persist; a single-frequency p-value after scanning; "reasonable agreement" as validation (state a metric and a trigger); reading charge-sign differences as detector-independent physics without sign-specific handling; quoting AMS's 108-day or daily-bin choices as rules.

## 8. What is missing (inputs that change the design)

- Data versus MC, and public versus internal work. Everything here is public-level and symbolic.
- Data period and hardware-era and reconstruction-version boundaries inside it.
- Per-bin livetime, exposure and positron counts (these set the bin edges and where the 27-day resolution is supported).
- Template provenance, the charge-confusion estimate per bin, and the cutoff model and safety factor.
- The AMS covariance model across rotations and any trial-factor treatment: not in the public text read, so build your own and say so.
- Whether the goal is a descriptive result or a model-parameter fit; modulation or drift interpretation (force-field or other parameters) is a separate model step with its own assumptions.
