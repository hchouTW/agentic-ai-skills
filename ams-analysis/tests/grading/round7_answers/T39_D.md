**Short answer:** AMS measures the in-flight survival probability of nuclei through its own detector material, using cosmic-ray data taken with the ISS attitude such that AMS points horizontally. It then converts that to an inelastic cross section. The skill's ledger does not give numerical cross-section values, so I cannot quote them. What it records is which nuclei were measured and how well the cross sections agree with the model.

**Method [Documented, C80; S01 Phys. Rept. review, Section 7, review-level summary only]**

- **Material.** Averaged over path lengths in the acceptance, the material is 73% carbon and 17% aluminium by mass. It also contains small amounts of Si, O, H, Na, Au and others. These are path-length averages, not a material budget.
- **Pointing.** With horizontal pointing, nuclei traverse the detector in both directions, L8 to L1 and L2 to L9.
- **Defining the particle.** The seven inner layers L2-L8 define the particle, through dE/dx charge and rigidity. The charge distributions in L2-L8 are compared with those in L1 (or in L8 and L9).
- **Survival probabilities.** The L1-L2 and L8-L9 survival probabilities are measured this way. The interaction probability between L2 and L8 is below 3%. It is obtained by comparing the upper-TOF and lower-TOF charges. The three pieces are combined to give the L1-L9 survival probability.
- **Monte Carlo comparison.** A dedicated Monte Carlo uses the Geant4 Glauber-Gribov inelastic cross sections, varied by ±10%. It is compared with the data, and the best-agreeing value is chosen.
- **Helium.** A few-percent discrepancy at low rigidity (below about 30 GV) led to a modified Glauber-Gribov model matched to the data over the full rigidity range. Below 30 GV, the measured He rigidity dependence is used to modify the model for heavier nuclei.

**What was measured and the results [Documented, C80]**

- **Nuclei covered.** Survival probabilities were measured for Li, Be, B, C, N, O, Ne, Mg and Si.
- **Rigidity dependence.** The inelastic cross sections on carbon, averaged over 5-100 GV, are constant within the accuracy.
- **Prior data.** Before AMS, inelastic cross sections had been measured only below 10 GV, and only for He and C projectiles. The review says no other measurements exist except He-on-C and C-on-C.

**What I cannot give**

- **Cross-section values.** The numerical values (in mb) are shown in figures in the review. They are not transcribed in the skill's ledger, and the dedicated AMS paper behind the review was not read. I therefore give no numbers, and none should be attributed to AMS from memory.
- **Method scope.** The survival-probability approach is AMS-specific and is not a general recipe. The ledger only summarizes the method.

**Related uses of the same approach in AMS flux papers [Documented]**

- **Lithium isotopes (C29, S14).** The survival probability of Li in AMS material was measured with AMS cosmic-ray data. This is consistent with the review's Li result.
- **Helium flux (C60, S07).** The helium inelastic-interaction systematic used the survival-probability method with a horizontal-pointing sample. Glauber-Gribov cross sections were scaled by 1.05-1.20. The resulting systematic was 1% below 100 GV and 2% at 3 TV. This is a helium-flux systematic for the first 30 months, not a cross-section value.
- **He, C and O flux paper (C62, S11).** The inelastic-cross-section systematic was below 2.2% for C and 2.7% for O up to 100 GV, and 3% and 3.5% at 3 TV. These are flux-systematic sizes, not cross sections.
