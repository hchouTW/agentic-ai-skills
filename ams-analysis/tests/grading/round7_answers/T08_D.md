# Review: antiproton analysis design

## Verdict

Not acceptable as designed. All three components have a defect, and two are fundamental:

1. The "TRD cut" is described as part of the signal definition. It cannot define a negative-sign signal, and one cut will not separate antiprotons from electrons.
2. Subtracting the electron background with MC alone leaves the dominant background unvalidated.
3. Estimating charge confusion from the Gaussian rigidity resolution is invalid. It is the most serious defect.

These are in priority order below, followed by the inputs that would change the verdict. All items are [General method] or [Proposal] unless tagged.

## Defects, in priority order

### 1. Charge confusion from the Gaussian resolution (critical)

- Charge confusion (a proton reconstructed with negative sign) is a tail phenomenon. It comes from the non-Gaussian tail of the Tracker rigidity response: bad hit assignment, multiple scattering, interactions, and large-angle or secondary hits. The Gaussian core of the rigidity resolution says almost nothing about it.
- A Gaussian extrapolation to the sign flip at `1/R = 0` will usually underestimate the rate by orders of magnitude. The error grows at high rigidity, where `1/R` approaches zero and the proton sample is huge.
- The abundant positive protons mean even a tiny tail probability can rival the antiproton signal. This is a leading background at high rigidity.
- Required instead [Proposal]:
  - Build a charge-sign migration probability `M(p̄←p)` versus `|R|`. Calibrate it on data and validate the MC tail on abundant matter. Use positive protons under loosened cuts, with the extrapolation to the tight selection tested.
  - Use a charge-confusion-sensitive estimator (for example a BDT on hit pattern, or partial-fit/sign consistency between track segments) and take its template from validated MC.
  - Check the MC tail against test-beam protons at high rigidity.
  - Carry the tail probability as a nuisance in the fit.
- [Documented, S08, C22] The published antiproton analysis uses a charge-confusion proton template from MC checked with 400 GV proton test-beam data. That is a tail validation, not a Gaussian estimate. Do not present your recipe as AMS practice.

### 2. The TRD cut as the signal definition (critical)

- [General method] Charge sign comes from the Tracker/magnet only. The TRD does not measure charge sign. It gives the electron/hadron likelihood. The signal is "negative-sign, `|Z|=1`, hadron-like", so the sign comes from the Tracker and the TRD only rejects leptons.
- A single TRD cut gives a finite electron rejection. Electrons are the dominant negative-sign background over a wide rigidity range. In the TRD, a sign-confused electron looks exactly like an electron.
- Rejection is rigidity dependent. The TRD response depends on the Lorentz factor, so a fixed cut has a rigidity-dependent efficiency and rejection.
- The cut also shapes the templates. Applying it hard and then subtracting what leaks through is not a measurement.
- Proposal: a TRD estimator is one discriminant. Add an independent ECAL estimator, with shower shape and an `E/|p|` check against the Tracker. Then fit in a discriminant plane rather than cut-and-subtract.
- Check that TRD and ECAL are not both used to define the sample and also to build the template that measures it. They share information with the Tracker rigidity and, for the ECAL, the energy.
- State `β` and `|Z|` requirements (TOF/RICH/Tracker) so that pions and other negative particles are handled.

### 3. Electron background subtracted by MC only (major)

- A pure MC subtraction of the dominant background carries all MC mismodeling of the TRD and ECAL response into the antiproton count. Shape agreement is not validation.
- Proposal: use a data-driven or template-fit estimate of the electron component.
  - Fit a discriminant such as the TRD estimator (plus ECAL) in each rigidity bin.
  - Use templates for the antiproton signal, the electron, and the charge-confused proton. Take the signal template from proton data and the others from data/MC with provenance stated.
  - Include finite-template-statistics nuisances (Barlow-Beeston or Conway). Check cross-contamination and degeneracy.
- Validate on a control region: electron-enriched (reversed TRD cut), a signal-depleted region, and a lepton/proton-enriched sample on data. Estimate any signal leaking into the control.
- Test closure with injected signal and with alternative templates. Test the fit bias across the signal strength, including zero.
- [Documented, S08, C22] The published antiproton analysis uses a template fit to the negative-rigidity sample with the signal template from proton data and the backgrounds from data and MC. It does not do a pure MC subtraction.
- Also cover the other negative backgrounds: pion and other secondaries, and interacting particles. They are missing from the stated design.

### 4. Estimand and downstream chain not defined (major)

- No estimand is given: flux, `p̄/p`, or a count. Signal, acceptance, and efficiency for `p̄` and `p` are different. Interactions of antiprotons differ from those of protons in the detector material.
- Define the response: rigidity migration with its tail, and `p`/`p̄` charge-sign migration as explicit rows and columns.
- State orientation and normalization of the response, and put the inefficiency in only one of `R` and `A`.
- Efficiencies must be conditional factors, not a product of marginals. Do an ordering closure and a combined-efficiency closure in MC. Include a data/MC correction with its own uncertainty.
- For `p̄/p`, do not assume acceptance and systematics cancel. Classify each nuisance as correlated, partial, or independent, and verify.
- Unfolding or forward folding is needed in a steeply falling spectrum. Do not use a diagonal bin-by-bin correction without a migration study. [Documented, S08, C23] The published analysis uses iterative unfolding stopped when successive fluxes agree within 0.1%.
- Exposure includes acceptance and livetime and the geomagnetic transmission; do not apply any of them twice.

### 5. Validation and statistics (moderate)

- "Agreement" is not validation. State a metric and a trigger for changing the method or adding an uncertainty.
- At high rigidity the antiproton counts are low. Use Poisson/exact or toy-validated intervals, not `S/√B` or Gaussian errors.
- Avoid tuning cuts, templates, or binning repeatedly on the fitted data. Predefine them or blind.
- Systematics ledger needed: charge-confusion tail, template shape, TRD/ECAL efficiency data/MC differences, rigidity scale, acceptance and material interactions, and unfolding.

## Inputs that would change the verdict

- Rigidity range. At low rigidity the electron and sign-confusion issues differ from those at high rigidity, where the tail dominates.
- Whether this is public/MC-level work or uses internal data and calibrated tools.
- Whether there is a charge-confusion control sample (for example abundant matter or test-beam data), and whether it constrains the tail.
- The target: flux, `p̄/p`, or a limit.
- Whether the electron MC has been validated against data in the TRD/ECAL tail, and with which template provenance.
- A response matrix with its orientation, and a list of correlated nuisances. I can then run the response and covariance validation scripts.

If the design replaces the Gaussian charge-confusion estimate with a validated tail model, replaces the single TRD cut and MC subtraction with a template fit in independent discriminants, and defines the estimand and response, I would revise the verdict to "acceptable with listed validations".

No AMS-specific numbers are quoted other than the documented practice cited above (S08, C22, C23).
