# Ne, Mg, Si (PRL 124, 211102, 2020) versus Li, Be, B (PRL 120, 021101, 2018)

Both are Tier 1 AMS papers. The ledger holds each at the full-text level, main article only, read 2026-10-02. The Supplemental Material was not read, so I give only the numbers printed in the main text, and each is a per-source bound, not a total.

| Item | Li, Be, B (S54; data 2011-05-19 to 2016-05-26, 1.9 GV-3.3 TV) | Ne, Mg, Si (S56; data 2011-05-19 to 2018-05-26, 2.15 GV-3.0 TV) |
|---|---|---|
| Trigger efficiency | Above 98% for 3 <= Z <= 5 over the whole rigidity range; systematic below 0.5% [Documented, C132, C134] | Above 94% for the three nuclei over the whole range; systematic below 1% [Documented, C144, C146] |
| Inelastic cross-section systematic | Below 2-3% up to 100 GV; below 3-4% at 3.3 TV [Documented, C134] | Below 3.5% up to 100 GV; below 4% from 100 GV to 3 TV [Documented, C146] |
| Rigidity-scale error | Below 1% up to 200 GV, rising to 5-7% at 3.3 TV [Documented, C134] | Below 1% up to 300 GV; 6% at 3 TV [Documented, C146] |

- **Trigger efficiency.** The Ne, Mg, Si trigger is less efficient (above 94% against above 98%), and its quoted systematic is larger (below 1% against below 0.5%).
- **Inelastic cross section.** The Ne, Mg, Si error is larger at low rigidity (3.5% against 2-3% up to 100 GV) and similar at the top (4% against 3-4%). The Li, Be, B paper quotes the upper bound as a range, "2-3%" and "3-4%", across the three elements.
- **Rigidity scale.** The two papers are close. The Ne, Mg, Si error stays below 1% to a higher rigidity (300 GV against 200 GV) and reaches 6% at its top bin, while Li, Be, B reaches 5-7% across its three elements at 3.3 TV. These come from different top bins (3.0 TV and 3.3 TV), so they are not a like-for-like comparison.
- **Rigidity-scale components.** Both papers attribute the rigidity-scale error to residual tracker misalignment, and the Ne, Mg, Si paper adds the magnetic-field map and its temperature corrections as a second contribution (C146).
- **Treatment of the two sets of numbers.** I did not combine them or interpolate between the quoted rigidities, because the papers differ in species, period, range and quoting style.

## Where 1/30 TV^-1 comes from

- **What the figure is.** It is the residual tracker misalignment, expressed as an error on 1/R, and it is estimated by comparing the E/p ratio of electrons and positrons. It feeds the absolute rigidity-scale systematic and is why that error grows with rigidity.
- **Ne, Mg, Si paper.** It quotes the value from its Ref. [26], Berdugo et al., NIM A 869, 10 (2017) [Documented, C146]. It is a cited value, not something measured in that Letter.
- **Li, Be, B paper.** It gives the same 1/30 TV^-1 and attributes it to E/p of e+/e- (C134). The ledger row does not record a reference for it there. I would not claim a specific citation for that paper without checking.
- **Same figure in later papers.** Fe, F and Na/Al quote 1/30 TV^-1 (C153, C158, C165), but the sulfur paper quotes 1/34 TV^-1 (C171, S60).
- **Limit on this attribution.** The Berdugo paper is not itself a ledger source: S19 lists different 2017 NIM A 869 papers (pp. 29 and 110). The ledger therefore does not contain that paper, and I have not read it. The attribution rests on the Ne, Mg, Si Letter's own citation.
- **Not a pure magnet-alignment number.** The E/p-based estimate and the field-map/temperature term are separate contributions to the rigidity-scale error. In the Na/Al paper the field-map error is below 0.6% and the overall scale error is below 1% up to 200 GV (C165).
