**What the sources do say (qualitative, plus quoted numbers):**
- The Li, Be and B spectral indices are nearly identical to each other in magnitude and rigidity dependence. They are distinctly different from He, C and O [Documented, C136, C84].
- Above about 200 GV the Li, Be, B fluxes harden more than He, C and O [C136].
- The Li and B fluxes have identical rigidity dependence above 7 GV. All three fluxes (Li, Be, B) are identical above 30 GV [C135].
- Fits to a constant give Li/B = 0.72 ± 0.02 above 7 GV and Be/B = 0.36 ± 0.01 above 30 GV. Li/Be = 2.0 ± 0.1 above 30 GV [C135].
- The Be flux differs in rigidity dependence below 30 GV. The paper attributes this "most likely" to radioactive 10Be (half-life 1.4 Myr). This is the paper's interpretation, not a measurement [C135].
- Secondary-to-primary ratios (Li/C, Be/C, B/C, Li/O, Be/O, B/O) were fitted as Delta = d[log(Phi_S/Phi_P)]/d[log R] in two intervals, 60.3-192 GV and 192-3300 GV. The 2018 paper quotes an average hardening of Delta above about 200 GV of 0.13 ± 0.03 [Documented, C136]. The Phys. Rept. review quotes 0.140 ± 0.025 for Delta[192-3300] - Delta[60.3-192], stated to exceed 5 sigma [Documented, C84]. The review's own data set differs from the 2018 paper's, so do not mix the two numbers. How the 5 sigma was obtained (systematic correlations, interval-choice or trial correction) is [Unknown/needs input].
- For comparison, the review gives a high-rigidity index of -0.266 ± 0.022 above 192 GV for these ratios [C82, C84, review data set].

**What this says about propagation (interpretation, not measurement).**
- Secondaries are produced by fragmentation of primaries on the interstellar medium. The secondary-to-primary ratio therefore probes the grammage and rigidity dependence of escape from the Galaxy, and the source spectrum largely cancels.
- If the high-rigidity hardening came from source injection, primaries and secondaries would harden similarly and the ratio would not harden. A ratio that hardens by about 0.13-0.14 above about 200 GV is, per the papers, consistent with the hardening being a propagation effect [C136, C84].
- Both papers say that no theoretical model predicted the observed behaviour of the primary and secondary spectra.
- The Li/Be and Be/B shape differences at low rigidity point to the 10Be decay, i.e. a propagation-time (age) effect. This is the paper's "most likely" reading and is not a measurement.
- Caveat [General method]: these are empirical spectral shapes. Turning them into diffusion-coefficient parameters needs a propagation model, which the AMS papers do not provide. Cross-section uncertainties for Li, Be and B production are also needed. The rigidity-dependent survival probabilities and the 10Be fraction are model inputs.
- Ratios do not automatically cancel acceptance or systematics. The review's ratio tables treat the cross-section, unfolding and rigidity-scale systematics as correlated between numerator and denominator (C85).
