**Short answer:** I cannot give you per-interval numerical values of the Li, Be and B spectral indices. They are not in the skill's source ledger, and I will not make them up. The paper gives them only in figures and Supplemental Material tables, which were not read. What can be stated is the structure of the result, a few quoted numbers, and what they imply for propagation.

**What was measured** [Documented, C131, S54 = PRL 120, 021101 (2018), main article only]
- Li, Be and B fluxes from 1.9 GV to 3.3 TV, from 5.4 x 10^6 nuclei collected May 2011 to May 2016.
- The total error on each flux is 3%-4% in the 100-108 GV bin.

**Spectral-index definition and rigidity intervals** [Documented, C136]
- The index is gamma = d[log(Phi)]/d[log(R)], computed model-independently.
- The rigidity intervals are bounded by 7.09, 12.0, 16.6, 22.8, 41.9, 60.3, 192 and 3300 GV. That gives seven intervals: 7.09-12.0, 12.0-16.6, 16.6-22.8, 22.8-41.9, 41.9-60.3, 60.3-192 and 192-3300 GV.
- The Li, Be and B indices are nearly identical to each other, and distinctly different from those of He, C and O.
- Above about 200 GV, Li, Be and B harden more than He, C and O.
- Individual gamma values per interval appear only in the paper's Figs. 2-3 and the Supplemental Material tables. They are **[Unknown/needs input]** here. To get them, read those tables (or the HEPData/AMS data release) and quote them with their errors.

**Other numbers that are documented**
- Li and B fluxes have an identical rigidity dependence above 7 GV. All three fluxes have an identical rigidity dependence above 30 GV [Documented, C135].
- Fits to a constant give Li/B = 0.72 +- 0.02 above 7 GV, Be/B = 0.36 +- 0.01 above 30 GV, and Li/Be = 2.0 +- 0.1 above 30 GV [Documented, C135].
- The three fluxes deviate from a single power law above 200 GV, in an identical way. The same behavior is seen in the AMS He, C and O fluxes [Documented, C135].

**Secondary-to-primary ratios** [Documented, C136; C84 for the review]
- The ratios Li/C, Be/C, B/C, Li/O, Be/O and B/O are fitted as Delta = d[log(Phi_S/Phi_P)]/d[log(R)] in two intervals, 60.3-192 GV and 192-3300 GV.
- The average hardening above about 200 GV in the 2018 paper is 0.13 +- 0.03 [Documented, C136].
- The later Phys. Rept. review, with a larger data set, gives 0.140 +- 0.025, stated as above 5 sigma [Documented, C84]. This is a different data set and error, so do not mix the two numbers.
- The review quotes an index of about -0.266 +- 0.022 for the ratios above 192 GV [Documented, C82, quoted there as the high-rigidity index of the light secondary-to-primary ratios]. The per-ratio, per-interval Delta values are again in the figures and tables only.

**What this says about propagation** [Documented as the papers' interpretation, not a measurement of mechanism, C136/C84]
- Li, Be and B are secondaries produced by fragmentation of C, O and heavier primaries. The ratio Phi_S/Phi_P therefore tracks the grammage traversed, which depends on rigidity through Galactic propagation.
- The ratio hardens at high rigidity while both families harden. Both papers state this is consistent with the hardening being a propagation effect. If source injection alone hardened, primaries and secondaries would harden similarly and the ratio would not change slope. The review states that no model predicted the observed behavior.
- Be differs from Li and B below about 30 GV. The paper attributes this "most likely" to radioactive 10Be (half-life 1.4 My) [Documented, C135]. This is the papers' statement, not a measurement. AMS isotope measurements cover Li and He, not Be.

**Caveats**
- Elemental fluxes are not isotopic. Converting to kinetic energy per nucleon needs an assumed A. The paper uses 6.5, 8.0 and 10.7 for Li, Be and B [Documented, C134].
- The 5 sigma figure is the review's statement. I did not read how its systematic correlations or interval-choice effects were handled.
- The Supplemental Material of the 2018 paper was not read.
- No web access was available. The ledger was last verified 2026-09-20, with the S54 paper read on 2026-10-02. I did not check for later or superseding AMS papers.
