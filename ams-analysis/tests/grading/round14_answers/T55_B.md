Source: AMS, "Properties of Neon, Magnesium, and Silicon Primary Cosmic Rays Results from the Alpha Magnetic Spectrometer", PRL 124, 211102 (2020), source S56. The ledger covers the main article only, not the Supplemental Material. No web check was done, so I make no claim about whether it has been superseded.

**Sample sizes, range and period** [Documented, C143]
- The sample after background subtraction is 1.8 x 10^6 Ne, 2.2 x 10^6 Mg and 1.6 x 10^6 Si nuclei.
- The rigidity range is 2.15 GV to 3.0 TV, in 66 bins with the same widths for all three nuclei.
- The period is the first 7 years of ISS operation, May 19, 2011 to May 26, 2018. This is 120 x 10^9 cosmic-ray events, and the collection time reaches 1.74 x 10^8 s above 30 GV.
- The total flux error is about 5% at 100 GV for each nucleus.

**Charge purities** [Documented, C145]
- Purity is above 98% for Ne and Mg, and above 99.7% for Si.
- The residual background from heavy-nucleus interactions between L1 and L2 is below 0.3% for all three.
- The background from interactions above L1, taken from simulation, is below 2% for Ne and below 1.5% for Mg, and is negligible for Si.
- The overall background-subtraction uncertainty is below 0.5%.

**Ratio fits** (ratio = k (R/R0)^Delta for R <= R0, and k above R0) [Documented, C147]

| Ratio | k | Break R0 | Delta | chi2/d.o.f. | Fit range |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ne/Mg | 0.84 +- 0.02 | 3.65 +- 0.5 GV | 0.19 +- 0.08 | 42/64 | whole range |
| Si/Mg | 0.89 +- 0.02 | 86.5 +- 13 GV | 0.069 +- 0.005 | 29/53 | above 6 GV |

- The paper concludes that Ne and Mg have the same rigidity dependence above 3.65 GV.
- It concludes that all three fluxes have the same rigidity dependence above 86.5 GV.

**Is Ne-Mg-Si a different class from He, C, O?**
- This is the paper's own conclusion, not an independent result. The ledger says to quote it as the paper's statement [Documented, C148].
- The evidence is a fit of Ne/O, Mg/O and Si/O above 20 GV with a double power law broken at 86.5 GV. Above the break the index differences are -0.046 +- 0.010 (Ne), -0.049 +- 0.011 (Mg) and -0.040 +- 0.011 (Si). The average is -0.045 +- 0.008, more than 5 sigma from zero.
- He, C and O have identical rigidity dependence above 60 GV. Ne, Mg and Si are 0.045 softer in index above 86.5 GV.
- The O, He and C comparison data come from earlier AMS papers, not from this Letter.

What the data show is a small, significant spectral-index offset relative to O (and He, C). "Different class of primary cosmic rays" is the authors' interpretation of that offset. The fits are empirical descriptions, so they do not prove a production or acceleration mechanism. The 5 sigma figure is the paper's own, and the main text gives no trial-factor details.

Later AMS papers build on the two-class picture (Fe with He, C, O; S with Ne, Mg, Si). That is a separate matter from this Letter [Documented, C154 and C172].
