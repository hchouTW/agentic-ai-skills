AMS Li, Be, B paper (PRL 120, 021101, 2018). All items below are [Documented, S54, main article only, full text; Supplemental Material not read]. Source-index checked 2026-10-02, no web access, so I make no claim about currency or later papers.

**Data sample, range, period** [Documented, C131]
- 5.4 x 10^6 nuclei in total: 1.9 x 10^6 Li, 0.9 x 10^6 Be and 2.6 x 10^6 B.
- Rigidity range: 1.9 GV to 3.3 TV, in 67 bins. The bins are the same for all three nuclei.
- Period: the first 5 years on the ISS, May 19, 2011 to May 26, 2016. This was out of 8.5 x 10^10 cosmic-ray events collected in that time.
- The total error on each flux is 3%-4% in the 100-108 GV bin. Per-bin errors elsewhere are in the Supplemental Material, which I did not read.

**Background corrections from interactions above the first tracker layer (L1)** [Documented, C133]
- These come from the carbon-fiber and aluminum-honeycomb support structures above L1. They are estimated from simulation.
- Li, Be, B at 2 GV: 5%, 8%, 5%.
- Li, Be, B at 3.3 TV: 2%, 13%, 8%.
- The main text gives only these two rigidity points, not intermediate ones.
- The uncertainty on the fluxes from these background corrections is < 1.5% over the whole rigidity range.
- This is separate from the background from interactions between L1 and L2 (in the TRD and upper TOF). That one is found by fitting the L1 charge distribution with templates and is < 0.5% for Li and Be and < 3% for B.
- Charge confusion from non-interacting nuclei is negligible.

**Constants** [Documented, C135]
- Li/B = 0.72 +- 0.02 above 7 GV (chi2/d.o.f. = 51/53).
- Be/B = 0.36 +- 0.01 above 30 GV (chi2/d.o.f. = 27/35).
- The paper also gives Li/Be = 2.0 +- 0.1 above 30 GV.
- The Li and B fluxes have an identical rigidity dependence above about 7 GV. All three fluxes have an identical rigidity dependence above about 30 GV. The text prints these two boundaries with a tilde, meaning approximate.

**Explanation of the different Be rigidity dependence** [Documented, C135]
- The paper says the difference is most likely due to the significant presence of the radioactive isotope 10Be, which has a half-life of 1.4 million years.
- This is the paper's own interpretation ("most likely"). It is not a measurement, and the paper does not fit the 10Be fraction.
- Isotopes are not separated in this analysis. The average mass numbers used for the kinetic-energy conversion are 6.5 +- 0.1 (Li), 8.0 +- 0.2 (Be) and 10.7 +- 0.1 (B) [Documented, C134].
