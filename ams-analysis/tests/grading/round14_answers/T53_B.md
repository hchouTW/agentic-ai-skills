The paper is AMS, "Observation of New Properties of Secondary Cosmic Rays Lithium, Beryllium, and Boron...", PRL 120, 021101 (2018). The source index records it as read in full text, main article only, on 2026-10-02. The Supplemental Material was not read. The answers below are all from the main article.

**Data sample, rigidity range and period** [Documented, C131]
- The fluxes cover 1.9 GV to 3.3 TV, in 67 bins that are identical for all three nuclei.
- The sample is 5.4 x 10^6 nuclei in total: 1.9 x 10^6 lithium, 0.9 x 10^6 beryllium and 2.6 x 10^6 boron.
- The data were collected over the first 5 years, 19 May 2011 to 26 May 2016, out of 8.5 x 10^10 cosmic-ray events in that time.
- The total error on each flux is 3%-4% in the 100-108 GV bin. Errors in other bins are in the Supplemental Material, which I did not read.

**Background corrections from interactions above the first tracker layer (L1)** [Documented, C133]
- These are estimated from simulation and come from the carbon-fiber and aluminum-honeycomb support structures above L1.
- At 2 GV the corrections for Li, Be and B are 5%, 8% and 5%.
- At 3.3 TV they are 2%, 13% and 8%.
- The text gives only these two rigidity points, not intermediate values.
- The uncertainty on the fluxes from these background corrections is below 1.5% over the whole rigidity range.
- A separate background comes from interactions between L1 and L2 (TRD and upper TOF). It is below 0.5% for Li and Be and below 3% for B. This is measured by fitting the L1 charge distribution with Li, Be, B and C templates, and it is not the above-L1 correction.

**Li/B and Be/B constants** [Documented, C135]
- Li/B = 0.72 +- 0.02 above 7 GV (chi2/d.o.f. = 51/53).
- Be/B = 0.36 +- 0.01 above 30 GV (chi2/d.o.f. = 27/35).
- The Li/B constant starts at 7 GV and the Be/B constant at 30 GV. The two thresholds are different and the quoted constants do not cover the same range.
- The paper also quotes Li/Be = 2.0 +- 0.1 above 30 GV.

**The Be flux's different rigidity dependence** [Documented, C135]
- The Li and B fluxes have an identical rigidity dependence above about 7 GV. All three fluxes (Li, Be, B) share an identical rigidity dependence only above about 30 GV.
- Below 30 GV the Be flux behaves differently. The paper attributes this to the likely presence of a significant amount of the radioactive isotope 10Be, with a half-life of 1.4 million years. The wording is "most likely", so this is the authors' interpretation and not a measurement.
- I did not read an independent isotope separation in the main text.
