Source: AMS Ne, Mg, Si Letter, PRL 124, 211102 (2020) (main article only; Supplemental Material not read). No web access, so I make no claim about newer or superseding results (index last verified 2026-09-20).

**Sample, range, period** [Documented, C143]
- Sample after background subtraction: 1.8 x 10^6 Ne, 2.2 x 10^6 Mg and 1.6 x 10^6 Si nuclei. They come from about 120 x 10^9 cosmic-ray events.
- Rigidity range: 2.15 GV to 3.0 TV, in 66 bins with the same widths for all three nuclei.
- Period: the first 7 years of ISS operation, May 19, 2011 to May 26, 2018.
- Total error is about 5% at 100 GV for each flux.

**Charge purities** [Documented, C145]
- Purity is above 98% for Ne and Mg and above 99.7% for Si.
- Residual background from interactions between L1 and L2 is below 0.3% for all three nuclei. It is evaluated by fitting the L1 charge distribution with templates.
- Background from interactions above L1 is estimated from simulation. It is below 2% for Ne, below 1.5% for Mg and negligible for Si.
- Total uncertainty from background subtraction is below 0.5% for each.

**Fitted breaks** [Documented, C147]
- The fit form is ratio = k (R/R0)^Delta for R <= R0, and k for R > R0.
- Ne/Mg:
  - k = 0.84 +- 0.02
  - R0 = 3.65 +- 0.5 GV
  - Delta = 0.19 +- 0.08
  - chi2/d.o.f. = 42/64, over the full rigidity range
- Si/Mg:
  - k = 0.89 +- 0.02
  - R0 = 86.5 +- 13 GV
  - Delta = 0.069 +- 0.005
  - chi2/d.o.f. = 29/53, fitted above 6 GV
- The paper concludes that Ne and Mg have an identical rigidity dependence above 3.65 GV. It concludes that all three fluxes are identical above 86.5 GV.

**Is Ne, Mg, Si a different class from He, C, O?**
Yes, that is the paper's own conclusion [Documented, C148]. The paper does not prove it independently, though.
- The basis is fits of Ne/O, Mg/O and Si/O above 20 GV with a double power law, with the break fixed at 86.5 GV. The high-rigidity index differences are:
  - Ne/O: -0.046 +- 0.010
  - Mg/O: -0.049 +- 0.011
  - Si/O: -0.040 +- 0.011
  - average -0.045 +- 0.008, which is more than 5 sigma from zero
- The paper concludes from this that Ne, Mg, Si are a different class of primary cosmic rays from He, C, O. It notes that He, C, O have identical rigidity dependence above 60 GV.
- Caveats:
  - "Identical above R0" is a statement about a chosen functional form. R0 has large uncertainties, and the 86.5 GV Si/Mg break coincides with the fixed O-ratio break.
  - The 5 sigma figure is the average index against zero. I did not read the paper's treatment of correlated systematics.
  - "Different class" is the paper's interpretation, not a measured mechanism. "At least two classes" is a minimum number, not a count.
  - The O, He and C fluxes come from earlier AMS papers.
