Source: the AMS nitrogen Letter (PRL 121, 051103, 2018; source S55, main article only, no supplement).

**Sample, range, period** [Documented, C137]
- 2.2 x 10^6 nitrogen events, after background subtraction.
- Rigidity range: 2.2 GV to 3.3 TV, in 66 bins.
- Period: the first five years on the ISS, May 19, 2011 to May 26, 2016.

Do not mix this up with the 3.9 million nitrogen nuclei quoted for the same 2.2 GV to 3.3 TV range in the Physics Reports review (C91). That is the review-level figure and a different dataset and paper. It is not the Letter's count.

**Two-component fit** [Documented, C142]
- The flux is fitted over the whole rigidity range as a weighted sum of a primary and a secondary component: Phi_N = Phi_PN + Phi_SN.
- Phi_PN = (0.090 +- 0.002) x Phi_O, with oxygen as the primary reference.
- Phi_SN = (0.62 +- 0.02) x Phi_B, with boron as the secondary reference.
- chi2/d.o.f. = 51/64.
- The same fit above 60 GV gives (0.083 +- 0.005) x Phi_O + (0.66 +- 0.04) x Phi_B, with chi2/d.o.f. = 18/25.
- The secondary share falls from about 70% at a few GV to below 30% above 1 TV.
- The 0.092 / 0.61 values with chi2/d.o.f. = 59/64 are the review's (C91) refit. They are not the Letter's numbers.
- This is a descriptive two-component form, not proof of a mechanism. It assumes O is purely primary in shape and B purely secondary.

**Which quantities come from the nitrogen paper itself** [Documented, C142 and C137]
- From the Letter:
  - the nitrogen flux itself (2.2 x 10^6 events, 66 bins, 2.2 GV to 3.3 TV, 2011 to 2016);
  - the fitted coefficients 0.090 +- 0.002 and 0.62 +- 0.02 (and 0.083 and 0.66 above 60 GV);
  - the chi2/d.o.f. values;
  - the spectral index of N above 8.48 GV;
  - the secondary-fraction trend.
- Not from the Letter:
  - the oxygen flux Phi_O and the boron flux Phi_B, which are taken from earlier AMS papers (the Letter's Refs. [8] and [9]);
  - the He, C, O and Li, Be, B spectral indices used for comparison, from the same earlier papers.
- In short, the coefficients are new; the O and B shapes are inputs.
