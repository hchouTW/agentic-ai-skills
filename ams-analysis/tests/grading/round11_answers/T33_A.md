## 1. The 2013 positron-fraction paper is not the latest measurement

The 2013 paper is S02: AMS, PRL 110, 141102, "First Result from AMS on the ISS: Precision Measurement of the Positron Fraction in Primary Cosmic Rays of 0.5-350 GeV".

- [Documented, C03] It measured the positron fraction from 0.5 to 350 GeV on 6.8×10⁶ positron and electron events.
- Verification level: abstract and metadata only. The index records no data-taking period for it, so I won't infer one.
- The index flags it as superseded in range and statistics by S03.

It can be cited as the first AMS positron-fraction result, not as the latest. Later AMS results as of the last index check:

- **S04** [Documented, C04] gives the positron flux from 1.9 million positrons up to 1 TeV. It reports an excess starting near 25 GeV, a dropoff above about 284 GeV, and a source-term cutoff at more than 4σ.
- **S46** [Documented, C41] reports daily positron fluxes. Their time variations differ from the electron variations and resemble the proton variations. It also reports a positron-electron hysteresis below about 8.5 GV.
- **S09 and S47**, from 2025, are not positron papers.

These alternatives answer different questions. S02 and S03 are the positron fraction, S04 and S01 the positron flux, and S46 its time dependence. Publication date is not data period. The 2019 S04 paper used data from 2011 to 2017, and the later papers extend the data period or change the question.
