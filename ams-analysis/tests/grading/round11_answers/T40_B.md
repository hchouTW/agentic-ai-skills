**No. A constant ratio is consistent with a common source, but it does not prove one.**

**What AMS reported**
- [Documented, C77] In the Phys. Rept. 894 review (S01), the positron-to-antiproton flux ratio above 60 GeV is consistent with a constant.
  - A constant fit over 60-525 GeV gives 2.00 ± 0.035 (stat.) ± 0.06 (syst.), with chi2/d.o.f. = 7.2/12.
  - The two errors are absolute errors on the ratio value 2.00, not percentages.
  - The variable is energy.
  - The review reads this as nearly identical energy dependence of positrons and antiprotons at high energy, and it "suggests a possible common source". It also says continued data taking is needed to confirm the origin.
- [Documented, C95, C74] The review's samples behind this are 1.9 million positrons (up to 1 TeV) and 5.6e5 antiprotons (1-525 GV). These belong to the review, not to the earlier antiproton paper.
- [Documented, C06, S08] The 2016 antiproton paper separately reports nearly identical rigidity dependence of the antiproton, proton and positron fluxes at about 60-500 GV. That is a different paper, variable and range, so it is not the same claim as the constant ratio.
- [Documented, C75] The antiproton-to-proton shape similarity (in rigidity) is described as not expected if antiprotons come only from cosmic-ray collisions with the interstellar medium. This is a collaboration qualitative reading, and the review says modelling uncertainties must improve before a definitive interpretation.

**Why it is not proof**
1. [General method] A constant fit over a finite range says the data do not need a changing ratio within the stated errors. It does not show that the species were produced by one mechanism.
   - chi2/d.o.f. = 7.2/12 shows consistency, not uniqueness. Any hypothesis that predicts a nearly flat ratio there would fit equally well.
   - Candidates include a secondary-production origin for both species with similar propagation, or two different sources whose spectra happen to run parallel over this range.
   - Positrons are produced and lose energy differently from antiprotons. Equal slopes can arise from different physics, so the result does not discriminate among these scenarios without a model.
2. [General method] The statement holds only in 60-525 GeV and within the quoted uncertainties.
   - It says nothing outside that range.
   - Uncertainties at the highest energies are large, with a 56-61% statistical error on the last positron bin (C95).
   - Shared systematics are not shown to cancel automatically. Acceptance, efficiency and charge-confusion systematics must be classified as correlated, partial or independent. I have not seen that classification for this ratio in the sources read.
3. [Documented, C77] The review's own conditional statements point to what would test the hypothesis.
   - It states that antiprotons are not produced by pulsars.
   - It states that a cutoff in the antiproton spectrum is expected if high-energy antiprotons originate from dark-matter annihilation.
   - No cutoff is claimed as observed. A cutoff test or more statistics at the highest energies would discriminate between models. The constant ratio alone does not.
4. [Documented, C77, C75] The "common source" wording is an interpretation, labelled "suggests" and "possible" by the collaboration, not a measurement. The review states that more data and better models are required.

**Additional caveats**
- The constant-ratio fit is a fit result in energy. Do not mix it with the rigidity statements (C06, C75).
- [Unknown/needs input] I have not seen how the systematic correlations between the positron and antiproton flux errors are treated in the ratio fit. Supplemental Material was not read.
- No web access was available here. I cannot check for later papers or a superseding AMS result. The skill's index was last verified 2026-09-20, and a post-2025 status should be checked on INSPIRE-HEP, the journal and ams02.space.
- This answer rests on the review (S01, full text, only the sections cited in C74, C75, C77) and the abstract-level and full-text claims for S08, not on any newer paper.

**Bottom line:** the ratio being consistent with a constant is evidence compatible with a shared origin or shared energy dependence. It is not a demonstration of one, and AMS itself words it as a suggestion that needs more data and modelling.
