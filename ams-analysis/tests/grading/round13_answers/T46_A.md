**Paper to cite for 0.5-500 GeV:** the AMS high-statistics positron-fraction paper, PRL 113, 121101 (2014), "High Statistics Measurement of the Positron Fraction in Primary Cosmic Rays of 0.5-500 GeV" (DOI 10.1103/PhysRevLett.113.121101). Do not cite the two companion 2014 PRLs for the fraction. PRL 113, 121102 is the separate e+ and e- fluxes and PRL 113, 221102 is the combined (e+ + e-) flux. [Documented, C113; source index S03, main article read; the full tables were not read]

**Sample** [Documented, C113]
- All data from the first 30 months, 2011-05-19 to 2013-11-26.
- This is a factor 1.7 more statistics than the 2013 paper.
- The bin fits give 10.9 x 10^6 primary positrons and electrons, 3.50 x 10^6 protons, and 0.64 x 10^6 positrons.
- Over 41 x 10^9 events were analysed. [Documented, C114]
- Energy is the ECAL energy, not rigidity.

**Behaviour above 200 GeV** [Documented, C113]
- The positron fraction no longer increases with energy above about 200 GeV.
- Wording matters. The paper does not say the fraction falls or reaches a plateau. It says the increase stops.
- The slope fit gives E0 = 275 +- 32 GeV, with chi2/d.o.f. = 3.9/12.
- The minimal model (diffuse power laws plus a common source with exponential cutoff) gives 1/Es = 1.84 +- 0.58 TeV^-1. The version without a cutoff is excluded at 99.9% C.L.
- These fit results are model-dependent and are the collaboration's reading.

**Comparison with the 2013 first result** (PRL 110, 141102; source index S02, main article only)
- **Range and sample** [Documented, C03, C113]
  - The 2013 paper covers 0.5-350 GeV, with 6.8 x 10^6 primary positrons and electrons.
  - It used data from 2011-05-19 to 2012-12-10.
  - The later paper extends the range to 500 GeV with 10.9 x 10^6 events.
- **Behaviour at high energy** [Documented, C110]
  - Below 10 GeV the fraction decreases with energy, as expected from secondary production.
  - From 10 to 250 GeV it increases steadily. The paper says this is not consistent with only secondary production.
  - It also says the behaviour above 250 GeV needs more statistics.
  - So the 2013 paper reports a steady rise out to 250 GeV and leaves the highest energies open. The 2014 paper adds the finding that the increase ends above about 200 GeV.
- **Consistency** [Documented, C113]
  - The 2014 paper states that the earlier result is consistent with it.
  - It is an extension, not a contradiction. Cite 2014 for the full range and 2013 for the first observation.
- **Method** [Documented, C107, C114]
  - Both papers use the same selection approach.
  - Both fit templates in the [TRD estimator - log(E/p)] plane in each ECAL energy bin, separately for positive and negative rigidity samples.
- **Fit parameters** [Documented, C109, C113]
  - The minimal-model cutoff parameters are not directly comparable between the two papers. The 2013 paper quotes a cutoff energy of 760 (+1000, -280) GeV. The 2014 paper quotes 1/Es in TeV^-1.
  - Both are model-dependent.
- **Anisotropy limit** [Documented, C111, C113]
  - The dipole-anisotropy limit tightens from delta <= 0.036 (16-350 GeV) to delta <= 0.030 (above 16 GeV), both at 95% C.L.

**Caveats for quoting this as a pure-secondary test**
- **Systematics.** The acceptance cancels in the fraction because it is identical for e+ and e-. This is a property of the fraction only and does not extend to the fluxes. [Documented, C114] The proton contamination and charge confusion are shared systematics and do not cancel. The selection systematic comes from more than 1000 repeated analyses with the efficiency varied by up to 30%.
- **Interpretation.** Pulsar and dark-matter statements are the collaboration's interpretation, not measurements of an origin. The abstract-level claim, as the index records it, is that the data are inconsistent with pure secondary production only for the rise from 10 to 250 GeV in the 2013 paper. The 2014 paper lists interpretation classes and does not settle the origin.
- **Currency.** I had no web access. The skill's source index was last verified 2026-09-20, so I cannot confirm that nothing newer exists. I checked neither for supplements nor for papers newer than the index. Later AMS papers give the positron flux up to 1 TeV (PRL 122, 041102) and a 0.5-1000 GeV positron-fraction table in the Physics Reports review. If you need the latest positron-fraction values, cite those only after reading them. The index records their fraction table as untranscribed, so I give no values from it.
