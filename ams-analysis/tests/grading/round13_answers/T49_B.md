## Data sample, rigidity range and period

| | Iron (Fe) | Fluorine (F) |
|---|---|---|
| Sample | 0.62 x 10^6 Fe nuclei after background subtraction [Documented, C149, C151] | 0.29 x 10^6 F nuclei after all backgrounds are subtracted [Documented, C156] |
| Rigidity range | 2.65 GV to 3.0 TV, 49 bins [Documented, C149] | 2.15 GV to 2.9 TV, 49 bins [Documented, C156] |
| Period | 19 May 2011 to 30 Oct 2019 (first 8.5 years on the ISS) [Documented, C149] | Same period, 19 May 2011 to 30 Oct 2019 [Documented, C156] |
| Total flux error | 4.8% at 100 GV [Documented, C149] | 5.9% at 100 GV [Documented, C156] |

- Both papers state that the total AMS sample in this period is 1.50 x 10^11 cosmic-ray events. That is the total sample, not the Fe or F sample.
- Both papers say there were no earlier rigidity measurements of these fluxes.
- The total errors above are quoted at 100 GV only.

## F/Si versus B/O comparison [Documented, C160, C161]

- **F/Si fit.** Si is used as the characteristic heavy primary. The ratio is fitted over 28.8 to 2900 GV with a broken power law with the break at 175 GV.
  - C = 0.044 ± 0.001, Delta1 = -0.34 ± 0.02, Delta2 = -0.19 ± 0.07, chi2/dof = 13/16.
  - Above 175 GV the F/Si spectral index hardens by 0.15 ± 0.07.
  - The paper compares this with the earlier AMS hardening of the light secondary-to-primary ratios (Li/C, Be/C, B/C, Li/O, Be/O, B/O) of 0.140 ± 0.025.
- **B/O fit.** Same functional form, applied to the earlier AMS B/O data (not a new measurement in this Letter).
  - C = 0.097 ± 0.003, Delta1 = -0.405 ± 0.005, Delta2 = -0.26 ± 0.03, chi2/dof = 24/36.
  - The fit interval is not stated explicitly in the text.
- **Double ratio.** [(F/Si)/(B/O)] is fitted over the full rigidity range.
  - k = 0.39 ± 0.01 and R0 = 9.8 ± 0.9 GV.
  - The exponent is -0.055 ± 0.013 below R0 and 0.052 ± 0.007 above it.
  - Above about 10 GV a single power law R^0.052 ± 0.007 describes it, which the paper states is 7 sigma from zero.

## What it implies

If heavy secondaries (F, from Ne/Mg/Si) and light secondaries (B, from C/O) propagated the same way, the double ratio would be flat. It is not.

- **The paper's conclusion.** F/Si has a rigidity dependence distinctly different from B/O (and B/C). Heavy cosmic rays from F to Si propagate differently from light ones from He to O. Secondary cosmic rays, like primaries, fall into two classes. [Documented, C161]
- **Fe and Si as primaries.** The Fe flux follows the He, C, O class above 80.5 GV, and Ne, Mg, Si form a different class [Documented, C154]. The paper therefore already treats primaries as two classes.
- **Fluorine itself.** F deviates from a single power law above 200 GV, and its index in 175 to 2900 GV is similar to B's. The F/B ratio is compatible with a constant above 150 ± 60 GV. [Documented, C159]
- **Caveat on the interpretation.** The two-class reading and the 7 sigma significance are the paper's own statements. The text gives no trial factor or significance calculation. The model comparison with GALPROP and GALPROP-HELMOD is shown in a figure (Fig. 3a) that I did not summarize. These are results at the paper's level, not independent verification.
- **What this does not show.** A diffusion or grammage mechanism for the difference, such as energy-dependent propagation or a different Galactic region sampled by heavy nuclei, is an interpretation. The paper's conclusion is the empirical two-class statement.
