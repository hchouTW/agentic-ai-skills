## The AMS proton flux values

The databases are Tier 6 transcriptions and are not the source of an AMS number. The AMS values come from the AMS papers.
- The skill's ledger records the main proton paper (S06, Precision Measurement of the Proton Flux, PRL 114, 171103, 2015). It covers 1 GV to 1.8 TV, 300 million events, and a spectral index that hardens with rigidity [Documented, C05; C31 for the analysis, which used the first 30 months, 19 May 2011 to 26 Nov 2013].
- The ledger has no tabulated flux values to quote, so I'm not giving any. Take the numbers from the paper's tables and figures.
- Later AMS proton results exist, such as the daily proton fluxes (S43) and the Bartels-rotation fluxes (S41). They have different periods and ranges, and their numbers are not in the ledger either.

## How to run the cross-check

1. Fix the estimand and the variable. The AMS proton flux is in rigidity, so keep that axis.
2. Query CRDB on the native axis with conversions off: `combo_level=0&energy_convert_level=0&exp_dates=AMS`.
3. Map each returned sub-experiment name to its AMS paper and period. Do not merge the sub-experiments or average them. Time series are excluded by default.
4. Compare your values with the paper, not with CRDB. Where they differ, the paper wins. A CRDB systematic of 0 means "not separately quoted" and is not an AMS statement.
5. Record the CRDB version and the retrieval date. For the SSDC data, do the same once you can open the page, and note which dataset it holds.
6. Judge agreement with a stated metric and a trigger for action, not with "consistent".
