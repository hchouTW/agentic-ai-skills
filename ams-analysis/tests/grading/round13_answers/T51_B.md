**1. Antiproton-proton hysteresis significance, every rigidity bin below 11 GV [Documented, C178; C123]**

Table SA has six bins. Method: for each bin, two 13-rotation intervals with the same normalized proton flux (one before, one after 2014-2015) are chosen to give the most significant difference in the normalized antiproton flux. Ratio = antiproton flux in interval A / interval B.

| Rigidity bin (GV) | Phi_pbar^A / Phi_pbar^B | sigma_hys (pbar-p hysteresis, difference from 1) | sigma_diff (pbar vs e- ratio) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.00-2.97 | 1.22 +- 0.04 | 5.5 | 7.4 |
| 2.97-4.88 | 1.14 +- 0.02 | 5.8 | 4.4 |
| 4.88-5.90 | 1.12 +- 0.02 | 6.9 | 1.9 |
| 5.90-7.09 | 1.07 +- 0.02 | 4.0 | 2.8 |
| 7.09-8.48 | 1.10 +- 0.02 | 5.6 | 0.5 |
| 8.48-11.0 | 1.06 +- 0.01 | 4.2 | 1.1 |

The text says the antiproton ratio differs from unity by more than 5 sigma at 1.00-2.97 GV, decreasing to 4 sigma at 8.48-11.0 GV. Hysteresis is observed in every bin below 11.0 GV. Below 4.88 GV the antiproton-proton hysteresis differs from the electron-positron one by more than 4 sigma.

Caveats (mine, from the evidence notes):
- The significance method is only cited from an earlier AMS paper. Nothing is said about how the correlation between the chosen intervals or the "most significant pair" selection (a look-elsewhere effect) enters. These are the collaboration's numbers, not validated global significances.
- The ratio is not monotonic in the table (1.07 at 5.90-7.09 GV, 1.10 at 7.09-8.48 GV), although the text says it decreases with rigidity.
- sigma_hys is not monotonic either (5.5, 5.8, 6.9, 4.0, 5.6, 4.2). In particular, 6.9 sigma at 4.88-5.90 GV is the largest.

**3. Antiproton-electron slope k at 1.00-1.92 GV [Documented, C179]**

k(e-, pbar) = **1.78 +- 0.12**.

- Definition: V = (Phi - <Phi>)/<Phi> per Bartels rotation, with <Phi> the 11-year average. The fit is V_e- = k(e-, pbar) V_pbar (Eq. S1), done per rigidity bin.
- Trend: k decreases with rigidity to 1.31 +- 0.08 at 5.90-7.09 GV.
- Significance: k > 1 at more than 4 sigma below 7.09 GV, so the antiproton flux is modulated less than the electron flux.
- Comparison: for the positron-proton pair, k(e+, p) is below 1.10 between 1.00 and 2.15 GV, rising to 1.20 +- 0.03 at 5.90-7.09 GV.
- Caveats: the relation is compatible with linear in the six bins from 1.00 to 7.09 GV shown. Per-bin fit errors and chi2/d.f. (Fig. S6) were not read. The reading of the pbar-e- versus p-e+ difference as showing the importance of spectral shape is the collaboration's interpretation, not a transport-model result.
