**Cite the 2014 high-statistics paper: AMS Collaboration, "High Statistics Measurement of the Positron Fraction in Primary Cosmic Rays of 0.5-500 GeV", PRL 113, 121101 (2014), doi 10.1103/PhysRevLett.113.121101.** [Documented, C113]

**Sample** [Documented, C113]
- It uses all data from the first 30 months, 2011-05-19 to 2013-11-26.
- That is a factor 1.7 more statistics than the 2013 paper.
- The bin fits give 10.9x10^6 primary positrons and electrons, 3.50x10^6 protons, and 0.64x10^6 positrons.
- Over 41x10^9 events were analysed in total [C114].

**Behaviour above ~200 GeV** [Documented, C113]
- The paper states that the positron fraction "no longer increases with energy above about 200 GeV".
- A fit of the slope = c log(E/E0) above 30 GeV gives E0 = 275 +- 32 GeV.
- This describes the shape of the fraction. It does not by itself identify the source.
- The collaboration lists pulsar and dark-matter classes of interpretation and says the origin needs data up to the TeV region and the antiproton-to-proton ratio.

**Comparison with the 2013 first result (PRL 110, 141102)** [Documented, C03, C110]
- The range was 0.5-350 GeV, on 6.8x10^6 primary positrons and electrons plus about 7x10^5 protons.
- The data ran from 2011-05-19 to 2012-12-10, which is about 8% of the expected AMS sample.
- Below 10 GeV the fraction decreases with energy, as expected from secondary production.
- From 10 to 250 GeV it rises steadily, which the paper says is not consistent with secondary production alone. The slope decreases by about an order of magnitude from 20 to 250 GeV.
- The behaviour above 250 GeV was left open because it needed more statistics.
- The 2014 paper extends the range to 500 GeV, and the 2014 paper states that the earlier result is consistent with it [C113].
- The 2013 paper is superseded in range and statistics. Do not call it the latest measurement.

**Caveat for your "pure-secondary-production test" framing**
- Neither paper presents the fraction as a pure-secondary test.
- The collaboration's statement is that the rise from 10 to 250 GeV is inconsistent with secondary production alone [C110].
- The 2013 paper's "new physical phenomena" sentence is the collaboration's interpretation, not a measurement of an origin.
- The fraction ratio does not cancel all systematics automatically. Acceptance cancels within statistics, but the paper treats charge confusion, the reference spectra and the energy scale separately [C114].
