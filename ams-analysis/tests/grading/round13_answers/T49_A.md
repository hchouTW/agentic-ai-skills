**Iron (PRL 126, 041104, 2021) [Documented, C149]**
- Sample: 0.62 x 10^6 iron nuclei (after background subtraction; purity > 97%, C151) out of 1.50 x 10^11 total cosmic-ray events (the total is not the Fe sample).
- Rigidity range: 2.65 GV to 3.0 TV, 49 bins.
- Period: first 8.5 years, 19 May 2011 to 30 October 2019.
- Result: above 80.5 GV the Fe flux follows the He, C, O primary class (Fe/O = 0.155 +- 0.006 above 80.5 GV) [Documented, C154, C155].

**Fluorine (PRL 126, 081102, 2021) [Documented, C156]**
- Sample: 0.29 x 10^6 fluorine nuclei after all backgrounds are subtracted, out of the same 1.50 x 10^11 events.
- Rigidity range: 2.15 GV to 2.9 TV, 49 bins.
- Period: the same, 19 May 2011 to 30 October 2019 (8.5 years).
- Both papers state that no earlier flux measurement in rigidity exists.

**F/Si versus B/O [Documented, C160, C161]**
- Each ratio was fitted with a two-segment power law with the break fixed at 175 GV. For F/Si over 28.8-2900 GV, Delta1 = -0.34 +- 0.02 and Delta2 = -0.19 +- 0.07, a hardening of 0.15 +- 0.07 above 175 GV. For B/O (boron taken from the earlier AMS paper, not re-measured), Delta1 = -0.405 +- 0.005 and Delta2 = -0.26 +- 0.03.
- The double ratio (F/Si)/(B/O) is not flat. Above about 10 GV it follows R^delta with delta = 0.052 +- 0.007, which the paper states is a 7 sigma difference from zero. The overall fit gives k = 0.39 +- 0.01 and a break at R0 = 9.8 +- 0.9 GV.

**Implication, as the paper states it**
- The rigidity dependence of F/Si is distinctly different from that of B/O (and B/C). So the propagation properties of the heavy secondaries (F, from parents Ne-Si) differ from those of the light secondaries (Li, Be, B, from He-O parents).
- The paper concludes that secondary cosmic rays, like primaries, fall into two classes. This matches the primary split between He, C, O, Fe and Ne, Mg, Si [Documented, C154].
- The F/B ratio is compatible with a constant above about 150 GV, and the F spectrum hardens above about 200 GV like B [Documented, C159]. The hardening seen in B/O (0.140 +- 0.025) is therefore also seen in F/Si, but with a different rigidity dependence at lower rigidity.

**Caveats**
- Only the main articles were read; the Supplemental Material was not (C160, C161).
- The 7 sigma significance is the paper's own figure, with no trial factor given.
- The "two classes" reading is the paper's interpretation. It does not by itself identify a specific propagation mechanism.
