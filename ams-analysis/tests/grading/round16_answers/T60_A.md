**Short answer.** I can give you the interval boundaries and the qualitative and averaged results, but not the per-interval spectral-index values. Those values are not in my source ledger, so I will not quote them. They appear only in figures and Supplemental Material tables that I have not read.

**What AMS measured (Li, Be, B fluxes)**
- [Documented, C131, S54, PRL 120, 021101 (2018); full text of the main article only, Supplemental Material not read] The sample is 5.4 x 10^6 nuclei: 1.9 x 10^6 Li, 0.9 x 10^6 Be and 2.6 x 10^6 B. It covers 1.9 GV to 3.3 TV and was collected May 2011 to May 2016. The total flux error is 3%-4% in the 100-108 GV bin.
- [Documented, C135] The Li and B fluxes have the same rigidity dependence above about 7 GV. All three fluxes (Li, Be, B) share it above about 30 GV.
- [Documented, C135] Li/B = 0.72 +- 0.02 above 7 GV and Be/B = 0.36 +- 0.01 above 30 GV. Li/Be = 2.0 +- 0.1 above 30 GV.
- [Documented, C135] The paper says the different Be dependence at lower rigidity is "most likely" due to radioactive 10Be (half-life 1.4 My). That is the paper's attribution, not a measurement.
- [Documented, C135] All three fluxes deviate from a single power law above 200 GV, in the same way as the primary He, C and O fluxes.

**Spectral indices and the rigidity intervals**
- [Documented, C136, S54] The spectral index is gamma = d log(Phi) / d log(R). It was computed over intervals bounded by 7.09, 12.0, 16.6, 22.8, 41.9, 60.3, 192 and 3300 GV. That gives seven intervals:
  - 7.09-12.0 GV
  - 12.0-16.6 GV
  - 16.6-22.8 GV
  - 22.8-41.9 GV
  - 41.9-60.3 GV
  - 60.3-192 GV
  - 192-3300 GV
- [Documented, C136] The magnitude and rigidity dependence of the Li, Be and B indices are nearly identical to one another. They are distinctly different from the He, C and O indices, and above about 200 GV Li, Be and B harden more than He, C and O.

I am not interpolating or recalling them. To get them, take the tables from the paper itself or from the AMS data release.

**Secondary-to-primary ratios and what they say about propagation**
- [Documented, C136, S54; C84, S01 Phys. Rept. review] The ratios are Li/C, Be/C, B/C, Li/O, Be/O and B/O. The index Delta = d log(Phi_S/Phi_P) / d log(R) was fitted with a single power law R^Delta in two intervals, 60.3-192 GV and 192-3300 GV.
- [Documented, C136] In the 2018 paper the average hardening above about 200 GV is Delta[192-3300] - Delta[60.3-192] = 0.13 +- 0.03. This is the only quoted number; the per-ratio and per-interval Delta values are in figures and tables I have not read.
- [Documented, C84, S01] The review quotes 0.140 +- 0.025, stated to exceed 5 sigma. It uses the same intervals but a later, larger data set. Keep the two numbers separate. I do not know how the review's significance was computed (systematic correlations, interval-choice trials), so treat that as [Unknown/needs input].

**Propagation interpretation**
- [Documented, C136, C84] AMS states that the secondary-to-primary hardening is consistent with expectations if it arises from propagation in the Galaxy. The review adds that a source-injection hardening would give similar hardening for secondaries and primaries. It also notes that no model predicted the observed behaviour of He, C, O or of Li, Be, B.
- [General method] The ratio index Delta reflects the rigidity dependence of secondary production and escape (the diffusion coefficient), because a source-injection spectral change largely cancels in Phi_S/Phi_P. The data support propagation as the origin of the hardening, but they are an empirical power-law description. They do not by themselves fix a specific diffusion model. That step needs a propagation-model fit.
- [General method] Ratios do not cancel acceptance, efficiency or systematics automatically. The review's ratio tables treat the nuclear cross-section, unfolding and rigidity-scale systematics as correlated between numerator and denominator (C85), but the correlation coefficients are not given.
- The Be-Li difference below 30 GV and the 10Be reading are the paper's own attribution, and Be isotopes were not measured here.
