**Spectral-index definition and intervals** [Documented, C136]

**What the spectra show** [Documented, C135, C136, C131]
- The data cover 1.9 GV to 3.3 TV, with 5.4 x 10^6 nuclei in total (1.9 x 10^6 Li, 0.9 x 10^6 Be, 2.6 x 10^6 B), collected from May 2011 to May 2016.
- The magnitude and rigidity dependence of the Li, Be and B spectral indices are nearly identical to each other. They are distinctly different from He, C and O, which come from a different AMS paper.
- Above about 200 GV, Li, Be and B harden more than He, C and O.
- Li and B have an identical rigidity dependence above 7 GV, with Li/B = 0.72 +- 0.02.
- All three fluxes have an identical rigidity dependence above 30 GV, with Be/B = 0.36 +- 0.01 and Li/Be = 2.0 +- 0.1.
- The three fluxes deviate from a single power law above 200 GV in the same way.
- The different rigidity dependence of Be at lower rigidity is attributed by the paper to the radioactive 10Be isotope (half-life 1.4 My). The paper words this as "most likely", so it is an interpretation and not a measurement.

**Secondary-to-primary ratios** [Documented, C136]
- The paper defines Delta = d[log(Phi_S/Phi_P)]/d[log(R)] over two intervals: 60.3-192 GV and 192-3300 GV.
- It uses the ratios Li/C, Be/C, B/C, Li/O, Be/O and B/O.
- The only number quoted is an average hardening of 0.13 +- 0.03 above about 200 GV. The per-ratio, per-interval Delta values are in the figures and Supplemental Material, which I did not read.

**What this says about propagation**
- Secondary nuclei (Li, Be, B) are produced by fragmentation of primaries (C, O and others) in the interstellar medium. Their flux, relative to the primary flux, depends on the grammage traversed.
- A secondary-to-primary ratio that hardens at high rigidity means the secondaries harden more than the primaries. This is what a change in the rigidity dependence of propagation, such as less escape-time path length at high R, would produce.
- The paper states that the hardening is consistent with expectations when it is due to propagation properties in the Galaxy [Documented, C136]. That is the paper's own interpretation. The data constrain an empirical rigidity dependence and do not by themselves identify a mechanism. Choosing between diffusion-coefficient breaks, reacceleration, a halo-size change or other models is a model-fitting step. [General method]
- The measured ratios Li/B, Be/B and Li/Be are close to constant above 7 or 30 GV. This supports the common secondary origin of the three species and similar production and survival behavior, apart from the 10Be effect on Be.
- Caveat: these ratios do not automatically cancel acceptance, efficiency or systematics. Survival cross-sections differ by species.
